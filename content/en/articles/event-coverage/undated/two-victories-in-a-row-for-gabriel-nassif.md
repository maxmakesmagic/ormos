## 2009 Grand Prix Chicago

![](https://i.ormos.uk/36/58/3658d52c33804bc6c46fb3d5fce2d8605c22d7536de01ebdddb879e7d434374b.jpg)

He's done it again! Just one week removed from his Pro Tour-Kyoto victory Gabriel Nassif has done it again! He is your 2009 Grand Prix-Chicago champion. The title comes at the height of discussion amongst the world over who the best active player is. After a blistering past few months, the talk of the community has centered around Luis Scott-Vargas, but with a win over the American in the Kyoto finals and now a Grand Prix victory one week later, Gabriel Nassif lays a solid claim to the title.

From the beginning, Chicago was an exciting event. With over 1,200 players, it *smashed* the previous attendance records for North America as players were ravenous to take on the Legacy format. The deck most talked about on the weekend? Counterbalance/Top, a powerful combination of cards when joined by Force of Will and Tarmogoyf. But when all the smoke had cleared, the Top 8 was filled with multiple archetypes including Ad Nauseam Tendrils, Dragon Stompy, Threshold, and aggressive creature decks.

Of course, when the Top 8 had played out, it was Nassif's build of the CounterTop deck that emerged victorious. Congratulations to Gabriel Nassif, your 2009 Grand Prix-Chicago champion!

## Quarterfinals: Paul Rietzl vs. Brian Six

*by Blake Rasmussen*

Both Paul Rietzl and Brian Six have some experience on the Pro Tour and Grand Prix stage. This was Six's first Top 8 and Rietzl's third. Both players had come to Chicago with decks expected to crush the control decks of the format, with Rietzl sporting a nearly mono black list and Brian Six playing Red/Green aggro with a full boat of [Price of Progress](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Price+of+Progress). Unfortunately for both players, they didn't find themselves sitting across from a [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) deck, meaning the round really could go either way.

Both decks were capable of blisteringly fast starts. Rietzl was powered by [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual) and cheap disruption capable of throwing any deck off balance from the start, while Six was capable of dealing tons of damage before an opponent could get their legs under them. Rietzl, for one, didn't like his chances.

“I need you to mulligan, and then I Hymn, Hymn,” said Rietzl.

**Game 1**  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/4b/0e/4b0e174e22d2342940c2841ba18ca4b4ca614c57935e5c1c2588882145142305.jpg) Rietzl's words turned out to be prophetic as Six started off mulling to six, and Pietzl made it worse with a first turn [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual) into [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) and [Hymn to Tourach](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hymn+to+Tourach). Pietzl chose [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) and then hit [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt) and a land with the Hymn. Six could only play a [Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath) and pass.

Rietzl had the follow-up [Nantuko Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nantuko+Shade), which was promptly hit by [Magma Jet](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Jet). In turn, Rietzl's spent the next two turns destroying land while Six only had [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape), pretty lonely at 1/1.

Rietzl, begging his deck for a creature, came up with [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) one turn and [Hymn to Tourach](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hymn+to+Tourach) the next, prompting the quick concession.

“That was the way I can win, right there,” said Rietzl.

Paul Rietzl 1 Brian Six 0

**Game 2**  
 After Six stated his preference for playing first, Pietzl quipped “One more card for me to find Hymn. I think that's my best card in this match-up.”

Both players kept their openers, and Six led with [Taiga](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Taiga) and a [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape).

“Classic start,” said Rietzl.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bc/3c/bc3c824649b7d4e11d0266902adcf2e55a66a0ad3c69c68f685a2c4006a4cc7f.jpg) Rietzl had his own classic start with [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual) into [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) (taking [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer)) and a [Hymn to Tourach](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hymn+to+Tourach) to take Price of Progess and a land.

“Here we go again,” said Six.

Six had an attack and a second [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape), but nothing else.

Rietzl had a [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) to hold the fort while still at a healthy 16 life, and added a [Nantuko Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nantuko+Shade) as Six played multiple lands while doing nothing much else. This freed Rietzle up to attack with both his creatures. Six declined to block, taking six, and was punished by Rietzl's second [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf). Once again, Six was forced to pass with no play and no attack.

Rietzl again hit with [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual) into [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize), and Six responded with [Magma Jet](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Jet), hiding the top two cards on the bottom. The [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) then took Fireblast, leaving only [Price of Progress](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Price+of+Progress) in hand. Rietzl used the remaining two mana to pump his [Nantuko Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nantuko+Shade), which was double blocked by [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape)s. The [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf)s took Six to two, and facing a lethal army with few resources, he extended his hand.

Afterwards both players revealed their sideboard choices that, effectively, were nonexistent in this match.

Paul Rietzl defeats Brian Six 2-0

  

## Quarterfinals: Brian Kowal vs. James Mink

*by Bill Stark*

“Do you play a lot of Legacy?” Brian Kowal asked his opponent as they shuffled for their Quarterfinals match.

“Yeah, from time to time.” James Mink replied. He then drew his opening hand, but was forced to mulligan. When that hand didn't pan out either, he sent it back as well, dropping to an opener of just five cards. The five became four as his opponent played [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual), [Mesmeric Fiend](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mesmeric+Fiend) trapping a [Chrome Mox](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chrome+Mox) under the 1/1.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9c/8a/9c8a1c17b5716bdb1485ab6e8e8d2139a2b0166c4f78a3c70066e9f3f9654f22.jpg)*James Mink takes a turn in the Grand Prix spotlight.* James Mink's Dragon Stompy deck failed to serve him the goods. He had [Trinisphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trinisphere), [Taurean Mauler](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Taurean+Mauler), and [Rakdos Pit Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos+Pit+Dragon), but no mana to play them. A [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) from Brian nabbed a [Blood Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Moon) Mink had picked up since the Fiend, but Kowal was doing a poor job presenting a relevant attacker. His 1/1 Fiend was left doing all the work.

At three mana, the Madison standout used [Vindicate](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vindicate) to blow up his opponent's only land, and James Mink passed the turn at 17 life with no permanents in play. Brian significantly increased the size of his clock with a [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) and piled on playing a [Sensei's Divining Top](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sensei%27s+Divining+Top) in the same turn. James, trying his best to put up a fight, drew and played a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) before passing.

Brian attacked for 5, then dropped a piledriver: [Tombstalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tombstalker) for a discounted price thanks to a well stocked graveyard. The next attack from the Midwesterner left Mink at 2 life, and when he didn't find a miracle on his next draw step, he was down a game in the match.

Brian Kowal 1, James Mink 0

A mulligan was again in the works for James Mink to kick off the second game, but he was able to stay at six, then had first turn [Magus of the Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magus+of+the+Moon). A second copy of the *Future Sight* card was forced out of his hand as Brian Kowal opened on [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize). James sent the version he had in play to the red zone, then played a [Powder Keg](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Powder+Keg).

Kowal started working on a Sensei's Diving Top to find himself some mana he could use, finally cashing it in for a free card. He played [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual), then had exactly enough cards in his graveyard to play a [Tombstalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tombstalker) with only three lands in play. Mink checked the math to make sure the 5/5 could be played for so cheap, then nodded his head grimly. He played a [Blood Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Moon) to make sure non-basics stayed [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain)s.

James got a bit of help in the form of a [Gathan Raiders](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gathan+Raiders). At 12 life, Brian was forced to trade his [Tombstalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tombstalker) for the face-up morph, then played [Mesmeric Fiend](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mesmeric+Fiend) in an effort to start getting ahead. It wasn't working, however, as the Moon effects wreaked havoc on his manabase and he furiously spun his Top trying to find a green source of mana. A second [Mesmeric Fiend](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mesmeric+Fiend) hit the board hiding an [Arc-Slogger](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arc-Slogger) from Mink's hand, but when Brian had [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) the following turn James showed he had the second one. Short a mana to play it, James turned to his deck for some help.

Help he got, as a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) slid off the top to the table, and he flopped the 4/5 onto the board. Brian nodded with a frown and scooped his cards up. The Moon effects had kept his mana sufficiently tied up, and the players were on to the rubber game.

Brian Kowal 1, James Mink 1

Both players kept their openers for the final game, and Brian Kowal opened on [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize). [Trygon Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trygon+Predator) was the next creature on the board for Nassif, and it was a threatening sight for James Mink, whose mana was coming in part from a [Chrome Mox](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chrome+Mox). Ripping a second [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), James attempted [Seething Song](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seething+Song), but Nassif used [Daze](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daze) to counter. Nassif all-ined on attacking, playing a second [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) after bashing for 5 and blowing up the Mox, and James could only watch, helpless to protect himself.

The second [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) ensured the next attack dropped Mink to 2, and he was drawing dead. After picking up his last card, he realized it and conceded.

“That [Daze](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daze) man. It's all about that [Daze](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daze)...” Mink said, reaching for his sideboard.

James Mink 1, Gabriel Nassif 1

On the play, Mink mulliganed his opening hand, before shipping his six-carder for five. Gabriel Nassif was clearly excited by the turn of events, but the explosive possibilities of Dragon Stompy meant he could still be staring down a first-turn Moon effect that could put him out of the game. It wasn't to be, however, as Mink simply played a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) and passed.

Nassif dropped [Polluted Delta](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Polluted+Delta), then passed, waiting to see what his opponent would do. James dropped [City of Traitors](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=City+of+Traitors), then played a morph. Gabe allowed the 2/2 to resolve, but when Mink tried for [Magus of the Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magus+of+the+Moon) the following turn, Gabriel was ready with [Hydroblast](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hydroblast). [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) then hit the board for Gabe, and as a 4/5 it dominated the board. Nassif didn't even hesitate when James pulled the trigger on a [Blood Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Moon), allowing the enchantment to hit.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/43/7a/437a0eddb3de2c1a76968cb3956e5d6240f2475e31b416688ecba4429bc256cf.jpg)*Gabriel Nassif's Top 8 adds another variable to the ‘best player' discussion.*[Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation) came down next allowing the European to steal his opponent's morph, which naturally was a [Gathan Raiders](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gathan+Raiders). Gabe took a deep breath, however, when Mink played [Seething Song](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seething+Song). Devoid of any counterspells, Gabriel couldn't stop an [Arc-Slogger](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arc-Slogger) from hitting the table, and the 4/5 would actually be quite good at the moment. Instead, it was [Rakdos Pit Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos+Pit+Dragon) that hit play, something much easier for Nassif to contain.

After much consideration, and multiple readings of the Pit Dragon, Gabe turned his team sideways the following turn. When James opted not to block, the Pro Tour champion discarded [Energy Flux](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Energy+Flux) to [Gathan Raiders](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gathan+Raiders) to unmorph it. That meant his 2/2 became a 3/3 and his [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) got +1/+1 from the extra card type in his graveyard; Mink fell to 6 life.

Looking for help, James drew for the turn, then quickly checked his lands. He played a second [Seething Song](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seething+Song), exactly enough mana to play [Gathan Raiders](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gathan+Raiders) with hellbent, making it a 5/5. That forced Gabriel to carefully examine the board before figuring out attacks. Ultimately he decided on [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) and Sower, dropping Mink to 4 and trading his Goyf for the Pit Dragon. When James wasn't able to draw a removal spell for the Sower, the 2/2 flyer was evasive enough to swing for the win.

Gabriel Nassif 2, James Mink 1

  

## Finals: Gabriel Nassif vs. Andy Probasco

*by Blake Rasmussen*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ce/d6/ced624b079f7b47ff6809591cccb6cbc214a274aeb6ec040c19d4862b6be4c6a.jpg) For the second weekend in a row, Gabriel Nassif found himself in the finals of yet another high-level event. Standing in the way of a second straight title was Eternal specialist Andy Probasco. Both players came to game with [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance)-fueled decks and wound their way through both a massive field and a hate-filled Top 8.

**Game 1**  
 Nassif won the all important die roll and started on [Tropical Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tropical+Island), while Probasco made a [Sensei's Divining Top](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sensei%27s+Divining+Top).

Nassif resolved a [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) through [Force of Will](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+of+Will) with a timely [Daze](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daze). Both players had half their combo, and the race was on to complete it first. Nassif tried to take the lead in that particular race by running out [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant).

Probasco attempted to draw first “blood” with his [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance), but Nassif had the lucky two on the top of his library to stop it.

“Random two for the win,” said Nassif. “Must be nice.” He then followed up with a [Sensei's Divining Top](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sensei%27s+Divining+Top) of his own. Must be nice indeed.

Resigned at facing down a stiff lock, Probasco merely played draw go while Nassif activated Top, drew cards off Confidant, and attacked freely, taking Probasco to 13. Lock in place, Nassif was content to pass the turn. When Probasco attempted to [Krosan Grip](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Krosan+Grip), Nassif had his own on top to once again halt any gains from the Atlanta native.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9b/3b/9b3b11eb3026358b603704b0d81bcd4128d0c8016e8527862f38841522c05d63.jpg) Nassif also cast and resolved a [Trygon Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trygon+Predator), hoping to lock things up even further (if that was possible). When Sower of the Temptation was countered by its twin off a [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance), Probasco conceded Game 1.

“That's good man,” he said while scooping his cards.

Gabriel Nassif 1 Andrew Probasco 0

**Game 2**  
 On the play, Probasco led with a [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder), while Nassif mirrored the play. Probasco resolved his [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) first, while, once again, Nassif did exactly the same (it resolved).

Nassif was able to dodge the [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) flip with a [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), who only had [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder)s and [Polluted Delta](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Polluted+Delta)s to feed him. The following turn, he dodged it again with Divining Top as Probasco was forced to reveal [Krosan Grip](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Krosan+Grip) on the top of his library. This allowed Nassif to plan ahead, stacking his deck with the [Trygon Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trygon+Predator) that helped counter the popular Split Second spell.

Probasco returned the favor the next turn when [Trygon Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trygon+Predator) fell victim to a revealed [Trinket Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trinket+Mage). That did, however, grow [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) to 4/5, allowing it to drop Probasco to 11.

The [Trinket Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trinket+Mage), in turn, was countered by a revealed [Threads of Disloyalty](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Threads+of+Disloyalty), which had jumped from Nassif's hand to the top of his library via [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm) the previous turn.

Probasco tried another mirror-buster in [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation), but seemed resigned to his fate when Nassif played [Force of Will](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+of+Will). Another [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) attack took Probasco to five, and a second [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), resolving through [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210922104223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) once again, looked to end the game the next turn.

Probasco knocked on his deck, as was his custom, checked the top card and...

...extended his hand.

Gabriel Nassif defeats Andy Probasco 2-0 and is the Champion of Grand Prix Chicago!
