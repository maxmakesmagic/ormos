### A Tribute To Some Blue People In Seattle

* * *

For the present however, those trickster wizards still have a little magic up their sleeves. In general, the goal of this deck is to play a Wizard on turn two and follow it up with some sort of permission on turn three, hopefully in the form of Patron Wizard. Originally, I had black in the deck for Aphetto Exterminators and Cabal Interrogators, but with Patron Wizard being such a crucial turn-three play, I decided it was best to go monoblue. Besides, the alternatives to the Interrogators and Exterminators are arguably better.

This deck follows a basic blue plan of trying to stabilize. It starts off by playing early Wizards and countering a spell on turn three if it has no Patron. On turn four you can start chucking your Wizards to Voidmage Prodigy until you have enough mana to act more freely. Aether Bursts will help you get out of a pinch--and you will most likely be in one as it's hard to stop the onslaught with little Wizards. 

The inherent problem this deck has to overcome is that of threats versus answers. This deck is full of answers--very reactive answers at that. While many decks are content with playing threats that double as weak answers ("My answer to your Wild Mongrel is my own Wild Mongrel."), this deck plays strong answers that only work at specific times. This puts a strain on your mana and slows you down to the point where a first-turn Basking Rootwalla may put you in danger. Strangely enough, this means one of the most synergistic cards in this deck isn't even a Wizard. I am, of course, talking about Spiketail Hatchling. This little critter has the strangest effect on games--I have seen Masques Block decks beat Standard decks because of this card alone! It may help you win, but the time you need it is when you can't get a Patron into play on turn three.

### Tips On Playing The Deck

<table><tbody><tr><td><ul><li> Don't just throw all your critters out there--leave mana up for countermagic if at all possible. Overall, if you're playing Wizards, you will probably do well against Control decks and poorly versus aggressive decks. Wizard tribal is and interesting deck because it breaks the mold a little. Usually, Aggro-control is weak versus Control and strong against pure Aggro decks. Wizards is the opposite because it's "Control" element is strictly countermagic and isn't generally very aggressive. The result is a mid-speed deck that is good against Control but will be fighting an uphill battle against all the Rootwallas in the world. (Roooot-walla!) This is a definite metagame choice, indeed.
