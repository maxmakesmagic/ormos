Worlds Week, held on the first week of December, was a week full of high-level competition from [the world's top players](/en/events/2014/legacy-daily-2014-11-22/legacy-daily) and exciting [battles between nations](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/2014-world-magic-cup) ending with a dramatic topdeck finish.

It was a week packed with everything *Magic* is all about.

Of course the decklists were no different.

Each deck, built and developed for this very day by national top pros, was a deck you would want to try out at your local [FNM](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/friday-night-magic-event-types-events-2).

This week, I would like to talk about such Standard decks.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Heliod%27s+Pilgrim)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Heliod%27s+Pilgrim)

First off, the RW Beatdown Sam Black brought to the World Championship.

I have already introduced the prototype of this deck developed by Brad Nelson: it's constructed of prowess creatures and token-generating spells, backed up by burn and [Chained to the Rocks](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chained+to+the+Rocks).

However, Sam added his own essence to the deck, bringing it to the next level.

Surprisingly, he removed arguably the best creature in the format, [Goblin Rabblemaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rabblemaster), and added [Heliod's Pilgrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Heliod%27s+Pilgrim).

Perhaps he predicted matchups against [Sylvan Caryatid](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sylvan+Caryatid) and [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix). Rabblemaster is not very good against green creature decks. The Goblin tokens simply rush to their own doom. So I feel choosing a different card altogether is not a bad decision.

[Chained to the Rocks](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chained+to+the+Rocks) and [Eidolon of Countless Battles](https://web.archive.org/web/20150113000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eidolon+of+Countless+Battles) are what the deck achieved in exchange.

This creates the option of going large in addition to the original tactic of going wide; creating a whole different dimension to the deck.
