One of only 3 undefeated players coming in to the final day of Grand Prix: Kuala Lumpur, Shouta Yasooka is no stranger to the top draft tables. Going into a *Theros* draft, his natural preference is for more controlling, U/x decks, with a preference for a removal-heavy, evasion-creature-laden U/B deck if he can assemble the necessary pieces.

Yasooka's draft table held the other two undefeated players, Yuusuke Iwasaki and Jack Teo, as well as his long-time drafting compatriot, Makihito Mihara. With a challenging draft ahead of him, Yasooka cracked open his first pack.

**Pack 1**

Xenagos, The Reveler stared out at him for his first choice, facing off against a [Wingsteed Rider](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingsteed+Rider) and some scattered removal. The Planeswalker was a relatively straightforward first pick, but Yasooka took his time in the second, eventually settling for an [Ordeal of Thassa](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ordeal+of+Thassa) over some mediocre R/G cards.

 ![Xenagos, The Reveler](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Xenagos,%20The%20Reveler&options=)

Yasooka's third pick was more interesting, having to choose between a [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad), [Magma Jet](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Jet), and a couple of aggressive Red creatures. The [Magma Jet](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Jet) won out, and Yasooka's next picks of [Dragon Mantle](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Mantle), a [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad) to replace the one he'd passed, and a [Satyr Rambler](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Satyr+Rambler) started to push him down the road of an aggressive Red-based deck.

**Pack 2**

Yasooka opened a second pack that was, on the surface, a little underwhelming. A [Thassa's Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thassa%27s+Emissary), a [Rageblood Shaman](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rageblood+Shaman), a Feral invocation and a [Nylea's Disciple](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nylea%27s+Disciple) stared back out at him. Yasooka opted for straight-out power and chose the Emissary, clearly willing to move back into Blue if necessary at this early stage in the draft.

 ![Thassa's Emissary](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Thassa%27s%20Emissary&options=)

The next few packs yielded an [Ill-Tempered Cyclops](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ill-Tempered+Cyclops), a [Wavecrash Triton](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wavecrash+Triton), and a [Pharika's Cure](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pharika%27s+Cure). Then, just as Yasooka's preferred colours seemed to open up, he was forced back to his original R/G build, picking up a bunch of low-cost favourites like [Sedge Scorpion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sedge+Scorpion), [Akroan Crusader](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Akroan+Crusader), and [Two-Headed Cerberus](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Two-Headed+Cerberus). Yasooka managed to wheel his [Feral Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Invocation), upping the power of his aggressive and heroic smaller drops.

At the end of the second pack, Yasooka was firmly committed to Red, but looked undecided between a potential Blue splash and a bunch of solid dudes in Green.

**Pack 3**

Another disappointing pack, with a bevy of powerful Black and White cards, but nothing in Yasooka's colours more impressive than a [Nessian Courser](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nessian+Courser). The second pick had no R/G cards at all, Yasooka shaking his head and picking up a [Vaporkin](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vaporkin).

 ![Spearpoint Oread](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Spearpoint%20Oread&options=)

The next few packs saw him nab a [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad), another [Dragon Mantle](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Mantle), and a [Spearpoint Oread](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spearpoint+Oread). The wheeling packs, again, held no particularly interesting choices, with Yasooka spending his last picks rounding out a solid if low-curved creature base out of boosters that seemed determined to punish his colour choices in Pack 1.

**Building the deck**

It's always hard to measure the thoughts of Japanese Pro's, who tend to combine heroic levels of stoicism with an inscrutability that borders on the Buddha-like. Nevertheless, Yasooka seemed less than pleased with the result of the draft.  
  
 After tossing up whether it could be worth splashing for a third colour, Yasooka instead opted for a tightly focussed, if not particularly powerful, aggressive R/G deck. His strategy would be to try and run out games as quickly as possible with his aggressive low-costing creatures, and then strategically pump them with [Dragon Mantle](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Mantle)s and [Feral Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Invocation)s to push through the remaining points of damage. Without creatures with natural evasion, it looked like an uphill struggle for the former Player of the Year.
