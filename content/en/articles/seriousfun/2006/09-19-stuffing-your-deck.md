For those of you who haven't seen today's preview card… well, how the hell did you get here? Anyway, here it is:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2c/cf/2ccf39ebe6745d5f60ef37f2c57c92f719ea6848ebaf6e95c70bf146d09ed3a0.jpg)

When Serious Fun first began, Mark Rosewater and Aaron Forsythe suggested to me that this was the "Timmy" column. Timmy, the player prototype who generally enjoys large creatures and showy effects, loves alternate formats that make it easier to play such cards. Mark and Aaron weren't too hard-and-fast about it; but they did ask me to make sure I could keep that demographic interested from time to time.

I think Timmy has a lot to like in Stuffy Doll; but at this point, I want Timmy to hang back a bit and let Johnny take a look at this card. (Spike might consider this card for Limited play; but short of a combo with a *Time Spiral* card I haven't seen, it's not making it in Constructed tournament play. We all know that by now, right? Good.)

Stuffy Doll can fit into just about *any* multiplayer deck. You don't have to build a deck around it – heck, if you have a generic 59-card deck that's looking for an alternate path to victory, you can do a lot worse than plugging Stuffy Doll in the hole and seeing what games it pulls out for you.

But if you want to be more deliberate with your Stuffy Doll strategy, may I suggest the following? The list is not exhaustive – just a few ideas to get you started.

For each category of cards, I nominate a "Super-Cool in Class" award. Feel free to nominate your own on the message boards. I also put a "Slight Digression" in each section. Of all my writing quirks, I think the senseless digression is the most symbolic. What better way to close out our time together?

### Combat Schemes

When you have an indestructible blocker that you want to see take damage, combat can become critical. You might even, on occasion, wish to attack! The following cards will allow for a more effective combat phase with Stuffy Doll:

*   Arcum's Whistle
*   Blaze of Glory
*   Imp's Taunt
*   Incite War
*   Righteousness
*   Rimehorn Aurochs
*   Valor Made Real

And the super-cool in class award goes to…Entangler. It makes absolutely clear to everyone that you can handle – and expect – a full attack. With a mere two cards and nine mana, you can see a single opponent dead simply from the full-scale attacks you'll entertain.

*Slight Digression*: Righteousness is a suggestion that harks back to one of my early fun decks – a Mogg Maniac, Warrior en-Kor, Soltari Guerrillas deck. (It worked slightly better at generating infinite damage, under pre-*Sixth Edition* Rules.) It really throws opponents off when you play Righteousness on their creature. What are they going to do, argue?

### Damage Schemes:

The following cards are among *many* capable of manipulating the doll's damage redirection ability with a bit of flair:

*   Brawl
*   Contested Cliffs
*   Cyclone
*   Laccolith Titan
*   Magmatic Core
*   Pandemonium
*   Seedborn Muse

And the super-cool in class award goes to…Living Inferno. It goes something like this: Inferno taps and deals all 8 damage to the Stuffy Doll (or spreads it out a bit more, if there are other creatures on the board). Stuffy Doll, while passing on the damage effortlessly, deals 0 back to the Inferno. Both cards survive to do it again the following turn.

*Slight Digression*: I would like to take a moment to sing the praises of Magmatic Core. Our play group tried out a little Two-Headed Giant draft (all *Coldsnap*) recently, and I got three of them in a near-mono-red deck (splashing black for two Deepfire Elementals and Minion of Leshrac). They were stunning. In Two-Headed Giant, you're almost never going to have trouble finding appropriate targets. The card (actually, two copies of such) was essential in our team's effort to staunch some horrific bleeding that had brought us down to a 3-32 life disadvantage. We ended up winning that game, in part because of some great play by my teammate Michael Mills, and in part because the Cores don't let the other team operate well at all. For those of you still playing *Coldsnap*3 Limited formats, play enchantment destruction! There are too many good ones, and Core may be the best.

### Retargeting Schemes

The following cards are among many capable of pulling a Stuffy Doll from play, so that it can return to select a new target:

*   Equilibrium
*   Flicker
*   Flickerform
*   Kami of Twisted Reflection
*   Parallax Wave
*   Peel from Reality
*   Withdraw

And the super-cool in class award goes to…Dual Nature. With two Stuffy Dolls, you can target four different opponents. Neat! Of course, it's unlikely you'll get many people to attack you; so you may want to resort to global damage spells (any variant of Earthquake will do).

*Slight Digression*: cheap bounce and flicker effects can do an awful lot of good in many multiplayer formats. One format I'd like to share with you, which our group has enjoyed recently, is called Freespell. It works like this:

*   All spells are free to cast.
*   Decks are minimum size 100, with only one copy of each card per deck, and no lands allowed.
*   Starting and maximum hand size is four.
*   No player may play more than three spells per round (round = everyone takes a turn).
*   Mana rules: X always equals 0. No mana costs for any abilities (including kicker, echo, etc.) can be paid.
*   Banned list: Cards that suggest payment of mana (e.g., Mana Leak). Myojin of Night's Reach. Biorhythm. Feel free to add your own; but you may be surprised how few you need to list. Our group also limits library search cards to two per deck, for the sake of speed.

You get a lot of huge attacks, uneven battles, and just plain riotous fun – without it ever really coming across as patently unfair. Games go pretty quick. Mass removal is important, but so are occasional easy tricks like Tangle – or Parallax Wave, or Peel from Reality. (Note that Withdraw, Flickerform, and a few of the others would be banned since, they provide for mana costs in their rules text.)

It's good fun. I've played horrendously badly in it so far, but that's never been a criterion for what I recommend. (If I shut out every format where I did badly, this column would have ended four years ago.) And best of all, the rules are easy to learn and remember, if you've played **Magic** for a little while. 

And with that last alternate format – the ultimate Timmy format – we really do come to the end. Thanks to all for reading. Congratulations and good luck to The Ferrett.
