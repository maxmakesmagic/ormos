Jabba is back! That's right folks, former World Champion Carlos Romao is back, and he is now the proud owner of the Grand Prix-Phoenix trophy. And he had to beat a helluva field to get there.

In a Top 8 that included *four* Pro Tour Champions (Gadiel Szleifer, Shu Komuro, Geoffrey Siron, Romao), one Hall of Fame candidate (Raphael Levy), 2005 Worlds Top 8 member Andre Coimbra plus Sam Stein and sixteen year old Sean Inoue, it was the kid that almost took home the title. Inoue squeaked out a quarterfinals victory over Shu, then pipped Sam Stein's ridiculous blue-red concoction with topdecked [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) in games 1 and 3, on his way to the finals to face an assured star in the finals. This was after Inoue was the last undefeated player in a tournament that featured much of Magic's best and brightest from around the world, a great start for someone who just started driving this year.

On the other side of the bracket, Romao was busy taking down Geoffrey Siron in his quarterfinals and then edging Raphael Levy in the semifinals. He then fell behind Inoue in the finals on a mistake, giving the kid hope that he'd take home the trophy from his first ever Grand Prix. Romao wasn't going to let it happen though, demolishing Inoue with a serious deck advantage in games 2 and 3 that included a pair of [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) s. Inoue's hand in game 3 was particularly slow, and it didn't take long before Jabba was shaking his hand, ecstatic to hoist another trophy in a game he thought he quit not too long ago. Congratulations to Carlos Romao, the 2006 Grand Prix-Phoenix champion!

## Sunday, Sept 3: 7:57 p.m. - Quarterfinals - Gadiel Szleifer vs. Sam Stein

![](https://i.ormos.uk/28/d2/28d24f4c08190918e1912d68af1f24ac9b8e99d1d095f46256a714b736c64e3f.jpg)*My name is Sam Stein you betta recognize!*

Sam Stein just missed a Top 8 at U.S. Nationals. A good friend of team members Luis Scott-Vargas and Paul Cheon, Stein making that Top 8 would have given them an off chance of all participating in Worlds together. Neither Luis nor Paul made it to Day 2 here, but Sam finally got his Top 8 and drafted an insanely hot blue-red number with 5 Frost Raptors, multiple Skreds, Krovikan Whispers, and 2 Rimefeather Owls (because one is never enough). Stein's opponent is none other than Magic's precocious wunderkind Gadiel Szleifer. In a Top 8 that included 4 Pro Tour winners and a Hall of Fame candidate, Szleifer might still be considered the best player of the bunch. However, it remains to be seen whether his heavy green, red-splash deck can handle Stein's flying monstrosity.

Sam Stein won the coinflip and chose to play. Szleifer had to mulligan his first hand, but built up quickly after that, casting Sound the Call plus Frostweb Spider and Boreal Druid to Stein's lone Frost Raptor. Ronom Serpent from Stein quickly clogged down the middle, pushing the game into a stall. A sixth land and Aurochs Herd from Szleifer threatened to change that, especially since it tutored up a second Herd, but Rimefeather Owl came down on Stein's side of the board, looking very menacing.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/75/e9/75e924825086a6c0154211779a33c0de90ea9b2b8601ac29d77fe6a8254b273e.jpg)*Gadiel Szleifer*

Krovikan Whispers stole Gadiel's Spider, thus clearing the way for the Owl. Two swings later Gadiel was shuffling up for game 2.

Stein 1 - Szleifer 0

Game 2 was a battle of land and air, with Stein providing the air power and Szleifer attempting to race on the ground. Stein had plenty of removal and chump blockers, and it doesn't take long to kill a man when you are swinging through the air for five a turn.

Stein 2 - Szleifer 0

  

## Sunday, Sept 3: 9:06 p.m. - Quarterfinals - Carlos Romao vs. Geoffrey Siron

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d6/42/d64259e3575a01074465a245df9fa864bc86910d123bedcfd174babba24bc24e.jpg)*Quick, act casual!*

In a quarterfinal match of a northern American Grand Prix, a PT Champion from Belgium is facing a World Champion from Brazil. Geoffrey almost didn't make the trip to Phoenix, but another Top 8 competitor, Raphael Levy, convinced him to get a ticket when Geoffrey was having a bad day.

Carlos drafted a decent red/black deck but like most black/red decks it had too many four-drops and too few two-drops. ## Sunday, Sept 3: 9:20 p.m. - Semifinals - Carlos Romao vs. Raphael Levy

### Game 1

I tuned in a little late, and saw a board position of [Boreal Druid](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boreal+Druid) , [Ronom Hulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ronom+Hulk) , [Martyr of Spores](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Spores) and [Hibernation's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hibernation%27s+End) on one for Raph, while Carlos had [Rimebound Dead](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rimebound+Dead) , [Disciple of Tevesh Szat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disciple+of+Tevesh+Szat) and [Phyrexian Ironfoot](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Phyrexian+Ironfoot) , but he was already at 8. [Hibernation's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hibernation%27s+End) found Raph a [Boreal Centaur](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boreal+Centaur) on his next turn, and [Ronom Hulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ronom+Hulk) came through to put Carlos at three. He only had three [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) s and [Mouth of Ronom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mouth+of+Ronom) , with a hand of only red cards and a [Grim Harvest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Harvest) , and couldn't deal with the [Ronom Hulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ronom+Hulk) .

### Game 2

Carlos played first and started with six cards. He played a third-turn [Goblin Rimerunner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rimerunner) and Flametongue Kavu-ed Raph's [Bull Aurochs](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bull+Aurochs) with [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) , and followed up with [Thermopod](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thermopod) while Raph tried to slow things down with [Simian Brawler](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Simian+Brawler) and a [Thermopod](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thermopod) of his own. It didn't help too much though, as Carlos emptied his hand on his next turn: [Chill to the Bone](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chill+to+the+Bone) for the Brawler and [Goblin Furrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Furrier) , and he swung in for seven with the help of [Goblin Rimerunner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rimerunner) . Raph only had [Boreal Druid](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boreal+Druid) and a virtually useless [Hibernation's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hibernation%27s+End) , and Carlos drew the lethal [Soul Spike](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soul+Spike) , so we were on to Game 3. He slowrolled it a little though, as he didn't want Raph to know that he had it, but he ended up needed it because Raph was coming back in the game.

### Game 3

![Martyr of Spores](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Martyr+of+Spores)

No mulligans this game, and Raph lead with [Martyr of Spores](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Spores) and [Bull Aurochs](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bull+Aurochs) while Carlos had [Krovikan Scoundrel](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Krovikan+Scoundrel) and [Phyrexian Ironfoot](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Phyrexian+Ironfoot) , but no red mana again. Raph played [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core) and Carlos had [Zombie Musher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zombie+Musher) , but he was running out of spells that he could cast. [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core) 's first trigger made short work of Carlos' Scoundrel, and things were not looking good at all for the Brazillian World Champion, as he couldn't cast any more spells without finding his red mana source, and [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core) was getting ready to take out his Musher and Ironfoot. Raph added [Orcish Bloodpainter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orcish+Bloodpainter) to his squad, but he didn't have any real power on the board yet which might give Carlos some more time to draw into a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) . Raph's [Goblin Rimerunner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rimerunner) on the next turn put some pressure on Carlos though, and he traded his Ironfoot for the Bloodpainter and a [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core) trigger.

On his next turn, Carlos found his [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) and the game was on again. The board was now [Zombie Musher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zombie+Musher) and [Mouth of Ronom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mouth+of+Ronom) for Carlos, and [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core) , Rimerunner and [Bull Aurochs](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bull+Aurochs) for Raph. [Goblin Furrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Furrier) for Carlos slowed down Raph's attack, but it got shot by the Core as well. Carlos found a second [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) on his next turn though, and [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) dealt with the Rimerunner, but it too got shot by the Core while Raph added a second [Bull Aurochs](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bull+Aurochs) . Carlos returned it with [Grim Harvest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Harvest) and played [Gutless Ghoul](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gutless+Ghoul) , and Raph finally sacrificed his Core to make room for a [Ronom Hulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ronom+Hulk) that Carlos currently didn't have a solution for. He had a lot of cards to work with though, and both players weren't at that healthy a life total, so his plan was to chump block the Hulk a couple times and try to race it. [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) took care of another [Bull Aurochs](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bull+Aurochs) , and Carlos also played [Earthen Goo](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Earthen+Goo) to chump-block [Ronom Hulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ronom+Hulk) on the next turn. Raph added an [Aurochs Herd](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aurochs+Herd) (finding another copy), but Carlos had the [Mouth of Ronom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mouth+of+Ronom) for it, putting Raph at two with an unblockable [Zombie Musher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zombie+Musher) in play. Raph was dead next turn and didn't find anything, and extended the hand.

Raph Levy didn't manage to win his exactly 50th GP, and Carlos Romao moved on to the finals!

  

## Sunday, Sept 3: 9:34 p.m. - Finals - Carlos Romao vs. Sean Inoue

![](https://i.ormos.uk/09/11/091193040e9eac840dd9f340aa4f668c192229c53dc32039f57215f04eecf06e.jpg)*Inoue was undefeated on Day 1 and played an excellent Day 2 to land him here*

In a Top 8 featuring 4 Pro Tour winners, 1 Hall of Fame candidate, and a Top 8 member from 2005 Worlds who also made the Top 8 at the last Grand Prix format, in all of that, 16-year-old Sean Inoue finds himself in the finals. His black-white deck doesn't appear to be anything special, but it does have an aggressive bent to it and 5 [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) that he seems to draw on cue whenever he needs them. His opponent in the finals is former World Champion Carlos Romao. Romao said earlier in the day that he tried to give up the game in the last couple of years and has now come back with a vengeance. Inoue and Romao were the last two undefeated players in the event and now they are the last two men standing, pitted against each other in a battle of cardboard paper cuts that will go straight to the pain. If I had to pick a favorite here, I'd definitely give Romao the nod, though Inoue has managed to defeat nearly all comers thus far.

Inoue cast the first creature of the game in [Stromgald Crusader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stromgald+Crusader) , while Romao smashed back with [Goblin Furrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Furrier) before casting [Orcish Bloodpainter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orcish+Bloodpainter) . The Bloodpainter fell to [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) and Inoue followed up with another attack and [Krovikan Scoundrel](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Krovikan+Scoundrel) .

Romao made a major misplay with [Martyr of Ashes](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Ashes) , only revealing one card when it was clear that he had multiple red cards in hand (including a [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) Inoue had just seen on top of Romao's deck via [Mishra's Bauble](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mishra%27s+Bauble) ). This let Inoue cast [Kjeldoran War Cry](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kjeldoran+War+Cry) to save his men, getting an extra four damage out of the affair. A second Martyr cleared the board a turn later, but by that time Romao was at 4, with [Zombie Musher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zombie+Musher) picking away at him. Romao could not find an answer and fell behind in what clearly looked like an advantaged matchup for him.

Inoue 1 - Romao 0

Romao dominated early in game 2, at least partially due to some early mana screw for Inoue and partially due to a mistake Inoue made regarding [Goblin Furrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Furrier) 's special drawback and [Squall Drifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Squall+Drifter) . That was embarrassing.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8d/fe/8dfe79d734ff0ebb90dfba6fafd876ed1d42aabbe44b372d733eab512cc30408.jpg)*Carlos Romao showed why he is a champion.*

Inoue's mana screw remained in effect for multiple turns while Romao built up his forces and whittled away Inoue's life total, though he was fighting through [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) all the while. About the time Romao started to run out of gas, Inoue started to draw land, making for an interesting, back-and-forth game in what originally looked like a blowout. [Stromgald Crusader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stromgald+Crusader) and [Surging Sentinels](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Surging+Sentinels) were suddenly facing down [Orcish Bloodpainter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orcish+Bloodpainter) , [Gutless Ghoul](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gutless+Ghoul) , and [Goblin Furrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Furrier) . [Chill to the Bone](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chill+to+the+Bone) killed the Sentinels, but Inoue struck back with a turn of [Gelid Shackles](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gelid+Shackles) , [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) , and [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) to stabilize. Inoue's luck finally ran out and [Goblin Furrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Furrier) plus [Earthen Goo](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Earthen+Goo) finished him off to even the match at one game a piece.

Inoue 1 - Romao 1

[Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) played a big part in game 3, which should tell you right away which player likely won. Inoue kept a mediocre draw and managed to use some cantrips to draw into creatures, but Romao's men were all better than Sean's and for once, Inoue's [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20220814060204/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh) fetish seemed to have deserted him. Further fat plus the obligatory attack steps from the Brazilian, and Carlos Romao was once again on top of the world, or at least on top of Grand Prix-Phoenix.

Romao 2 - Inoue 1
