Honestly, I don't think this card requires a ton of analysis. But I don't want to disappoint those of you who tune in every Tuesday looking for some inspirational thoughts grounded in theory—I mean, I *am* pretty darn smart, or so my reflection insists every morning. (And he's so good-looking. Who am I to argue with him?) So here's this week's deep thought on Seedborn Muse: 

*The more players there are in the game, the more times your stuff will untap.  
—Anthony Alongi, Analyst Extraordinaire*

There, I even signed it, so you can attribute my unique insight appropriately. Feel free to copy and paste it into your message board signature. 

Let's get right to some decks, shall we? 

One of the most fluid decks will be blue-green, simply because both colors contain common and uncommon creatures that do wonderful things when they tap. The following is a list of blue and green commons and uncommons any one of us could come up with in a few seconds: 

*   Cephalid Broker
*   Cephalid Looter
*   Disruptive Pitmage
*   Nantuko Disciple
*   Nullmage Advocate
*   Puppeteer
*   Rootwater Hunter
*   Seeker of Skybreak
*   Vodalian Illusionist
*   about a half dozen other pingers
*   about 1,352 mana Elf variations

Of course, you'd also put in Squirrel Nest and whatever else. But I don't want to walk you folks through decks you can probably build yourselves in a few seconds. 

For my first deck, I'd like to explore something darker—say, using black. And Kamahl.

The idea here is to set up a moderate-sized army, ranging from Thrull tokens to the amazing Silklash Spider (which can sweep the air every turn), until Kamahl comes into play. This expensive Druid Legend needs plenty of mana to do his dirty work, and the Seedborn Muse is a handy aid. Each turn, you can animate lands to ping them with the Witches or Whip, force other players' creatures to attack (and they're unlikely to come after you because you can pump +3/+3 on your entire army), and be otherwise annoying. Volrath's Stronghold helps recover from the inevitable removal (you can spend an entire round restacking your deck!), and you can substitute the uncommon Haunted Crossroads if you don't have it. The cycling lands have virtually no drawback now, because with a Muse out, they'll untap on your first opponent's turn. 

Tangle is there just to wreck the opponent that finally manages to punch through your defenses. It complements the "I untap more than you" theme of Seedborn Muse beautifully and is one of those demoralizing cards that gets burned into your opponents' memories.

If you're worried about your creatures with tap abilities getting killed before your next turn, you could try Concordant Crossroads. And wouldn't Demonic Hordes look pretty swell in that deck, too? But the rare count is already a bit high. 

Note that when you activate the Witches, you choose a target—and so does the opponent of your choice. That opponent doesn't have to be the one you ping (or whose creature you pinged). Perhaps you'll activate Kamahl to animate a third player's land (and let it resolve) before activating the Witches. Naturally, you can't expect the opponent you choose to be wholly cooperative every time, but there's no harm in exploring. Just open the door, and see if your chosen opponent walks through. (Please don't beg or barter—it doesn't become you!) 

Don't make the Coffin Queens bear too much weight—the idea here is just to find a way to remove particularly troublesome creatures (Incarnations, for example) from the game. You pull them out, you untap, and—"aw, gee, I forgot I could leave her tapped, I guess Genesis is gone for good."
