# Today's Must

1.  [*Magic* Math—Should You Play Two Chandra with Four Oath of Nissa?](http://www.channelfireball.com/articles/magic-math-should-you-play-2-chandra-with-4-oath-of-nissa/) | ChannelFireball | Frank Karsten

It's the article we've all been waiting for since Raphaël Lévy [won a Grand Prix](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-manchester-2016) with two [Chandra, Flamecaller](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Flamecaller)s that could only be cast via [Oath of Nissa](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oath+of+Nissa). Frank Karsten is basically the community's math teacher, and in this article Karsten uses such phrases as "using a simple hypergeometric distribution," and "conditional probability with land counts and card selection." But don't feel daunted—read on anyway and feel all the smarter for it.

# After Those, Read, Watch, or Listen to These

1.  [Playing Pauper: Rebels](http://www.mtggoldfish.com/articles/playing-pauper-rebels) | MTGGoldfish | Jake Stiles

This is how you analyze and show off a new deck in any format, period. It just happens to be that this excellent analysis is of the Rebels Pauper deck. Inside, Stiles has a video deck tech, multiple video matches, plus text analysis, tips for playing and beating the deck, and possible new directions to take the list. It has everything you could possibly want in a deck analysis.

If I could start a slow clap on the internet, I would.

This was inevitable. We've heard a lot about cards missing from *Eternal Masters*, and this is that put in video form. I mean, don't get me wrong, I'm mad about some missing cards too. No [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter)? What is that?

Here's a different twist on the Top 10 *Eternal Masters* list shtick. Alex and Ben talk about ten cards from *Eternal Masters* that, if introduced into Modern, they feel would shake up and improve the format. [Mana Crypt](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Crypt) is probably on that list. Right guys? Right? Anyone?

# Top 25

1.  Insane Hayne and the Rookie move on up

Alexander Hayne is a lot of things. He's a Pro Tour Champion. A Platinum Pro. The great Canadian hope. A stylish dancer. And now the No. 7-ranked player on the planet after a Top 8 at [Grand Prix Prague](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-prague-2016). Thanks to that fantastic finish, Hayne now counts himself among the Top 10, knocking Joel Larsson to No. 11.

But just as exciting is Oliver Tiu, the current leader for the Rookie of the Year (by a country mile), entering the rankings at No. 25. The wunderkind knocks Kentaro Yamamoto off the rankings and, given his tear this year, isn't likely to go anywhere.

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Standard Temur Traverse](/en/events/2016/competitive-standard-constructed-league-2016-06-14/competitive-standard-constructed-league)

I'll admit, looking through Standard lists for innovation is a little daunting right now, as the metagame is pretty settled while we wait for *Eldritch Moon* to release whatever horrors it has in store for us.

But that doesn't mean innovation is dead. *Magic Online* user GBRUNO recently went 5-0 in a Competitive Standard League with a Temur [Traverse the Ulvenwald](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Traverse+the+Ulvenwald) deck that plays a bunch of interesting cards.

First, there are some of the more obvious choices if you're going to build a Temur deck. [Tireless Tracker](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tireless+Tracker) is a format staple, [Roast](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Roast) is great removal, [Jace, Vryn's Prodigy](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Vryn%27s+Prodigy) is a powerful card that helps get delirium, and [Sylvan Advocate](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sylvan+Advocate) might be the most-played card in Standard.

But then there's [Lightning Axe](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Axe) with no madness cards. Main deck [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate) and a pair of [Brutal Expulsion](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brutal+Expulsion)s, which you don't really see anywhere. Plus, the deck actually goes as deep as playing [Wastes](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wastes) just to pack three copies of [Thought-Knot Seer](https://web.archive.org/web/20160615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thought-Knot+Seer).

I have no idea if this deck is good, medium, or poised to break out. All I know is that one player managed to go undefeated with the list, so there has to be something to it.
