Katsuhiro Mori is the 2006 Japanese National Champion!

Mori had hoped for a scenario where he and his good friend, Pro Tour Charleston winner Tomoharu Saito, could have made the team together. They tried to jockey for position in the closing rounds of the Swiss but their maneuver backfired and they squared off in the quarterfinals ensuring that one of them would be frustrated in their attempt. When Mori defeated Saito, his friend made the former Rookie of the Year promise to win the tournament. Mori did not disappoint him.

Mori was piloting Structure and Force, a three-color control deck that abused [Sensei's Divining Top](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sensei%27s+Divining+Top) in conjunction with both [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) and [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance), that was designed by Saito's Charleston teammate Tomohiro Kaji. After besting Saito's beloved Sea Stompy in the quarters he went on to dispatch Ishimaru Ken's BUG Aggro in the quarters. After Ishimaru fell to Hidenori's Rakdos deck in the third place playoff, Mori took down another Sea Stompy deck -- this one piloted by Shouhei Yamamoto.

The Japanese National Team for 2006 World Championships will be Katsuhiro Mori, Shouhei Yamamoto, and Hidenori Katayama with Ishimaru as the alternate and qualified for Worlds.

## BLOG

  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/b1/ee/b1ee578f776fcfcbc4ef5300d8b083f30169a3a60f11189a38cc2df9484a8251.jpg)*Katsuhiro Mori back when he was the 2001 Rookie of the Year*

There was a small tear in my objective journalistic eye when I saw this match-up for the Quarterfinals. This was a match-up of two of the best players in the elimination rounds. Katsuhiro Mori is a former Rookie of the Year, becoming the first Japanese player to earn an international title. While everyone knows that Tsuyoshi Fujita was the first Japanese to reach the Top 8 it was almost Mori who achieved that honor when he finished ninth at Worlds 2001. Fujita would go on to reach the finals of Pro Tour Tokyo during the following season and Mori would go on to become the World Champion several seasons later. The deck he was playing this weekend was a black-white-control deck that had a card drawing/control suite of [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance), [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant), and [Sensei's Divining Top](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sensei%27s+Divining+Top).

His opponent was his long time friend Tomoharu Saito going all the way back to the days of Saito's win at the Finals in 1999 with his Stompy deck. Before his win at Charleston, with Kajiharu80, Saito was best known as the creator of Sea Stompy (ever since that long ago Finals win, Saito has named all his decks with green in them "\[something\]Stompy"), which he debuted at Pro Tour Hawaii. He almost played a different deck this weekend - Erayo Ninjas - but could not resist one more chance with his beloved Ninjas; especially with [Ohran Viper](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ohran+Viper) to add to the mix.

Saito and Mori shared a room for this weekend and had made a vow that they would both reach the National team. In the final round of the Swiss, Saito conceded to his opponent because he thought it would not only help Mori's tiebreakers but also allow them to dodge each other at the start of the elimination rounds. Even if they had met in the semis, the third/fourth playoff would allow them to both make the team. While they were not happy about playing each other they looked around at the considerably less experienced players surrounding them and made a follow-up vow; whichever one of them won this match would become the 2006 Japan National Champion.

### Game 1

Tomoharu Saito bluffed at mana problems after leading off with Llanowar Elf. He offered up [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) on turn two and Mori countered with [Remove Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remove+Soul) - a card I never thought I would see in a high level event but which has proved to be quite formidable this weekend. Saito actually had the second land and was able to play Jitte unopposed. Mori absorbed a Jitte'd Elf with his [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant). Saito resolved his next Ape and that one went all the way - six, six, six - with the artifact in hand.

### Game 2

Saito was bluffing at mana issues this game. He mulliganed a one land hand and kept a [Shivan Reef](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shivan+Reef)/[Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape)/Ninja draw but never found the second land to turn it on. With al the time in the world, Mori found [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) and Jitte and Saito scooped.

### Game 3

After another mulligan for Saito he smiled at he looked at his next six - it was almost the same six-card hand as last game. This time he sent it back and went to five. Just like that he was down to four cards. He gave Mori thumbs up and a big smile when he decided to keep. He was down to two cards when he played [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest) and Bird. He did not have the Ninja for turn two though and passed the turn sheepishly. When he passed third turn he looked at Mori and asked, "Gasp?"

Mori killed the Bird and untapped to play a Divining Top. Saito had nothing but lands and could do nothing but sulk. [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) came down to get max value out of the Top. Saito hopefully offered up a Bird looking for a [Remove Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remove+Soul) but Mori had no play. He untapped to summon [Court Hussar](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Hussar).

Saito sent in his [Birds of Paradise](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Birds+of+Paradise) and swapped it out for a [Ninja of the Deep Hours](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ninja+of+the+Deep+Hours). His attempt at a Jitte was [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare)d. Mori had a Jitte all his own. "Nice card," frowned Saito. "Concede."

### Game 4

"This deck is my girlfriend and my girlfriend does not like me right now," laughed Saito as he mulliganed yet another hand. He obviously did not want to keep the next hand but could not bring himself to ship it back without a long internal debate. Finally he sighed, slapped himself in the face - perhaps a little harder than usual - and went to five.

[Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) on turn three was met with [Remove Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remove+Soul). He had no play on the subsequent. He began slapping on the top of deck - domestic abuse? - as he drew each card. He was able to [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) a [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant), which tapped Mori out so he could play [Meloku the Clouded Mirror](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Meloku+the+Clouded+Mirror).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/12/1e/121e56435fa5eb1d1ba822b73f80c271607e46af636e5052a0053fc9e4c24b28.jpg)*Tomoharu Saito*

Mori attacked with Bob and Saito chose not to block. Mori had plenty of mana but only one tenuous source of white and he had to burn a Tendo Ice Bridge in order to have his [Court Hussar](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Hussar) stick around (meanwhile Saito was fishing around for pigs to use as flying tokens) Mori wrapped up the turn with a Top.

Saito attacked with Meloku, made a token, and replayed land. "[Rumbling Slum](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Slum)?" Mori peeked at his top three and had to allow it to resolve. It seemed surprising when Mori took a pain from [Underground River](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Underground+River) to play a second [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) but it became clear when he tapped his two remaining black sources to [Last Gasp](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Last+Gasp) and really-I-mean-it-this-time [Last Gasp](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Last+Gasp) Meloku. Saito made a second token.

The freshly cast Bob stepped in front of the hemi-powered Elemental. Saito erected another Slum: "Maybe I win this one?"

Mori had Meloku but Saito had Jitte and the battle of broken legends was on: "No [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare)?" The score was 13 to 8 in Saito's favor. He tried to figure out the optimal wielder of the artifact and began walking through Mori's potential plays. In the end he simply put it on one of his Slums and sent them both in. Mori made a token - returning his long ago expended Tendo to no doubt cast [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn) next turn. He put Bob and the token in the way of each monster.

Mori just fiddled with the top of his deck on his turn and replayed his Tendo. He fell to six on Saito's upkeep and braced for impact. Saito seemed excited to actually be playing lands and spells this game and smiled, "This is a nice game." After careful consideration he once again attacked with only the Slums. Mori had the [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn) but with only his Tendo for white, [Remand](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remand) was like a hard counter. Mori made a token - hoping for better luck with the [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn) next turn - and pushed it and the [Court Hussar](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Hussar) into oncoming traffic. Mori considered countering a Bird of Paradise with [Remove Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remove+Soul) but realized there were bigger things he was frightened of than mana. Saito made a big note on his scorecard to remind himself for next turn: "CONDEMN".

Mori agonized over his Top decisions at the end of turn. He had plenty of options and was even close to being able to steal the game with Meloku and [Orzhov Pontiff](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orzhov+Pontiff). Mana was an issue in that regard so he stole [Birds of Paradise](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Birds+of+Paradise) with [Threads of Disloyalty](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Threads+of+Disloyalty) and passed the turn. Saito removed four counters to kill the legend at end of turn and Mori considered how to respond. Now he would not be able to use his Tendo for white on Saito's turn and reset it. He made one token and contemplated making additional…he made a second. Finally he realized what he had to do and quickly scooped up all his untapped land except for the Tendo and made four total tokens. "I may be losing this one."

![Umezawa's Jitte](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte)

Saito thought for a while before moving the Jitte to one of his two illusion tokens. He kept his [Rumbling Slum](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Slum)s back this turn - Mori was at four and they would finish the World Champion in two turns. Mori [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn)ed one token and decided to let the other one through. "NINJA!" announced Saito as he cut the number of turns Mori had remaining in half.

"What happens if this game ends in a draw?" Mori asked the table judge.

"We go onto the next game."

Mori used the Birds to cast the Pontiff and attacked for eight with the illusions and they were both at two at the end of turn. The [Rumbling Slum](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Slum)s put them both on zero and the game was a draw. Despite having eight white sources in the deck, Mori never found a second source that would have allowed him to win. With another [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn) lurking on top of his deck, he could have attacked, put damage on the stack, and then [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn)ed one of his own attackers to survive Saito's upkeep. Hidenori played his fifth land and a [Shard Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shard+Phoenix). Ken still had no fourth land, and held back again, but didn't choose to block when Hidenori pressed in - attacking with both creatures. Hidenori pressed his advantage even harder with a [Shock](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shock) and [Volcanic Hammer](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Hammer) directed at Ken, and then finished him off the following turn with a attack and a [Seal of Fire](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seal+of+Fire).

Hidenori won the playoff 3 - 1 and earned a spot on the 2006 Japanese National team that would be looking to repeat at the World Championships in Paris at the end of the year.

  

**Sunday, Aug 27: 5:18 p.m. - Finals: Katsuhiro Mori vs. Shouhei Yamamoto**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/71/af/71af78c82c4eab1513b4ceb759a58d96ef41b630c0c096bc0c3fd380ee49a75f.jpg)*Katsuhiro Mori's poker face*

Are you familiar with the story of Jeff vs. Goliath?

How about George vs. Goliath?

Bob vs. Goliath?

The reason you have not heard of those stories before is because Jeff, George, and Bob are just three of the hundreds upon hundreds of guys that Goliath slew before David got lucky on some given Sunday. Katsuhiro Mori was clearly a confident Goliath in this match while the relatively inexperienced Yamamoto was here to determine if he was David or just another Jeff, George, or Bob.

To make the challenge all the more daunting, Goliath had help on this one. Mori is easily one of the better deck designers in the whole world. His decks vied with Itaru Ishida's for the attentions of Kenji Tsumura en route to Kenji winning the Player of the Year and it was iteration of GhaziGlare that carried him all the way to the World Championship title last season. Yet, he was playing Structure and Force, a deck designed by Tomohiro Kaji (who finished 16th at this tournament). Basically it wasn't just David-hopeful vs. Goliath… It was David-hopeful vs. Goliath and Goliath's pal who might be even bigger than Goliath.

Ah, well -- any given Sunday and all that… onto the action!

### Game 1

Yamamoto sent back his first eighteen cards but at least he was on the draw. Mori wanted to put a little more distance between their hand sizes and had Top/[Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) going. Yamamato was trying to close that gap with [Ninja of the Deep Hours](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ninja+of+the+Deep+Hours) but Mori played a second Bob to block the Ninja. [Llanowar Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Llanowar+Elves) made a reappearance and when Mori sent the Invitationalist into the red zone, Yamamoto blocked, leaving him only two lands. Mori was happy to be the one doing the land destruction.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6a/70/6a70a080f6c40885d686907763cb2704f6722ee202b555bec76fdbc71b7fc063.jpg)*Shouhei Yamamoto*

[Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) hit the board and Mori had established control - something pretty easy to do when your opponent triple mulligans and gets stuck on two land. Mekoku came down for the mop-up detail while Yamamoto gamely played on. Mori did not need to waste any cards on any of Yamamoto's attempts since he has Top going and was floating a variety of two and three casting cost cards which he revealed with [Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance).

### Game 2

[Counterbalance](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterbalance) had done its job. Now it was off to the sideboard in favor of board control cards like [Orzhov Pontiff](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orzhov+Pontiff). While Yamamoto was taking mulligans number four and five of the match, Mori had to make his own decisions. He had no black sources in hand and multiple cards that required it. He kept it and Shizo Death's Storehouse showed up on turn two to power out [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant). [Trygon Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trygon+Predator) was the first play for Yamamoto. He untapped and added [Giant Solifuge](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Solifuge). Mori had no blocks for the hasty creature. He killed the [Trygon Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trygon+Predator) that had hung back with [Last Gasp](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Last+Gasp) and untapped to Pontiff away the Solifuge. Yamamoto followed up with Llanowar Elf and Jitte.

Yamamoto tapped out for Meloku on the next turn only to have it [Remand](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remand)ed. [Grand Arbiter Augustin IV](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grand+Arbiter+Augustin+IV) hit play for Mori. Llanowar Elf picked up the Jitte but elected not to attack. Jitte cleared Yamamoto's Jitte and Bob traded with the Elf in combat. Mori transmuted [Muddle the Mixture](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muddle+the+Mixture) for [Remand](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remand) and passed.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9f/96/9f962ff9818f6630aa3855b51b0b5a5a4ccece3e02a254f813618798c3b239fc.jpg)

Bird for Yamamoto allowed him to try a [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) but that only drew Mori a card. Mori showed him the [Threads of Disloyalty](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Threads+of+Disloyalty) he was going to draw and Yamamoto moved onto Game 3 - hoping to start one of these games with his hand a full strength.

### Game 3

Yamamoto decided he liked this set of seven and he kept it. Mori was not so happy about his one-lander but kept it based on having a Top - he always keeps one land hands if he is holding Top. Yamamoto led off with a full size [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) and had Jitte on turn two. Mori was holding [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare) but had been forced to open on Top. He flipped the Top for his second land. Yamamoto had [Stone Rain](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stone+Rain) to send him back to one mana.

[Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) picked up the Jitte and attacked but that one mana was good enough to [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn) it. Mori replayed his Top. Yamamoto had another monster but Mori had the mana necessary to [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn) the [Rumbling Slum](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Slum). Yamamoto offered up a Predator and Mori used his Top to draw [Remand](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remand) - and in turn draw his Top.

Mori was still only working on three lands and tapped out to play [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) and the Top. Yamamoto took advantage of the opportunity to play two Predators. Mori reluctantly decided to Top on upkeep. He revealed land with [Dark Confidant](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Confidant) and hoisted his Top onto his deck and played his fourth land.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/79/7e/797ea711775ffc1b476dc3f5a729cdec73cb942132acd0b9e7b7dee2a41ad40e.jpg)*The World Champion*

Yamamoto made his move. He tapped out to summon [Giant Solifuge](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Solifuge), equip a Predator, and attack with the team. Mori quickly shoved Drak Confidant in the path of the Solifuge, Gasped one Predator and [Condemn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condemn)ed the other. Yamamoto was down to one card with four lands, Bird, and Jitte in play.

Yamamoto played another [Rumbling Slum](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Slum) after Mori had summoned [Court Hussar](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Hussar). Mori played a Jitte and invoked the Legend rule. The score was 25 to 8 in Yamamoto's favor with the Slum bearing down on him. He transmuted [Muddle the Mixture](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muddle+the+Mixture) to find another copy of everyone's favorite artifact and passed the turn pants down. [Stone Rain](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stone+Rain) hit [Godless Shrine](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Godless+Shrine) leaving Mori with only four mana. He played the Jitte and Top hoping for the best. The Slum dropped him to 6 on Yamamoto's upkeep but the Sea Stompy player had no attack.

Mori found a white source in [Adarkar Wastes](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adarkar+Wastes) and went three cards into his deck with [Court Hussar](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Hussar). He fell to 4 on Yamamoto's turn from the wear and tear of the Slum. Slum number two was countered with a Legends edition [Remove Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remove+Soul) (available at fine singles vendors everywhere).

Mori equipped one of his Hussars and it went toward the red zone - Yamamoto blocked and the Slum was felled with [Last Gasp](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Last+Gasp). Grand Arbiter Augustin joined the fray and it seemed like Yamamoto was out of gas. He attempted [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte) but Mori was ready with [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare). Meanwhile, the counters on the Hussar continued to amass. One more [Remove Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918211615/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remove+Soul) later and Japan had its 2006 National Champion

Katsuhiro Mori - the reigning World Champion - won the finals of the 2006 Japanese National Championships in a fast three game set. He will lead the National team of him, Shouhei Yamamoto, and Hidenori Katayama at the 2006 World Championships in Paris. In addition to defending his Worlds title the team will also defend their title in the Team Competition.
