<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 11:07 a.m.: Teaming With Opportunity</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Bill Stark</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>It seems to happen at every tournament. You work for weeks preparing, you buy an expensive plane ticket, you fly hundreds of miles for your chance to win fame and glory, and inevitably you find yourself stuck paired against your friend and teammate who went through the exact same steps with you to get to where you're at.</p>&#10;      <p>Sigh.</p>&#10;      <p>U.S. Nationals was no different and as the competitors sat down to their second draft pod of the tournament today, two high-profile pairings stuck out. Former National Team members Luis Scott-Vargas and Paul Cheon were drafting at the same table, as were San Diego Sliver Kids Jacob van Lunen and Chris Lachmann. We had the chance to talk to them about what that was like.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/89/ef/89efa4648788cd1e4aa5e958e37165479a1abdcc9e738bc8d874e97ea2415dac.jpg">&#10;      </div>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>&quot;Sitting next to each other means we can send good signals,&quot; said current U.S. National Champion Paul Cheon. &quot;Our colors don't overlap at all.&quot; When asked if that meant he <i>wanted</i> to be sitting at the same table as his teammate, though, he added an addendum: &quot;We want to sit at different tables, but if we're at the same table, hopefully we're sitting next to each other.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Chris Lachmann agreed, saying &quot;It would have been beneficial [to sit next to Jacob] because we know how we draft and would have been able to send strong signals.&quot; Only a single player, Brandon Scheel, separated the two from one another, but that can make a world of difference.</p>&#10;      <p>Why did the pros want to sit next to each other if they were at the same pod? Luis Scott-Vargas explained, &quot;Sitting next to Paul means we're not hating each other late. We had an <a>Imperiosaur</a> come around seventh that I would normally cut. Since I was sitting next to Paul I shipped it.&quot; While Paul wasn't in green, he did have the opportunity to choose whether he wanted the card if he was in green, or to cut it from the rest of the table.</p>&#10;      <p>How does sitting at a table with your friend affect your chances? Cheon predicted a 2-1 finish for himself, while Vargas felt a 2-1 was a baseline goal with 3-0 a real possibility &quot;Though anything can happen…&quot; he added. Lachmann predicted a 3-0, saying &quot;That's what I need, so it's what I'm predicting,&quot; while teammate Van Lunen was more comfortable with his chances: &quot;I'll be real disappointed with this deck not getting a 3-0. It's the best deck I've drafted in weeks.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>For these four players, then, being stuck at a table with friends is simply a case of making lemonade out of their lemons…</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 11:44 a.m.: Half and Half</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Bill Stark</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>With the constant need for tech in an ever-changing metagame, it's easy for players at home to forget that half of each Nationals tournament, including the ones running over the course of <b>Magic</b> Weekend in the U.S. and Britain, are Booster Draft. The first draft pod Stateside finished yesterday and when the smoke had cleared, 16 players had managed sparkling 4-0 records at their pods. We spoke with a few of them to get their perspective on the impact of 40 cards at this level.</p>&#10;      <p>Grand Prix-Columbus champion Steve Sadin and Grand Prix-St. Louis runner-up Zac Hill felt that the Draft was a key element in their bids for the National Team. Said Zac &quot;My Standard deck is a week old and I haven't tested in a few days. I feel I need to 7-0 the Draft to Top 8.&quot; That merited an eye-roll from Sadin, who maintained that <i>everyone</i> wanted to 7-0 the Draft in order to make the single elimination rounds. &quot;I figure if I have a good Limited performance, like X-1, I can Top 8,&quot; he added.</p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/25/c7/25c70c2ddb97bba903952dd346ae60c86bdc27d46eec87b58d2915074b6fe407.jpg">&#10;      <p>Luis Scott-Vargas, a 2007 U.S. Nationals team member, said &quot;Draft is more important than Standard. If you do well in the Draft the final rounds of Standard don't matter as much.&quot; Vargas felt that a solid record going into the last rounds of Standard was important, allowing you to draw some of those rounds instead of risking bad matchups. Antonino De Rosa, a former U.S. Nationals Champion, disagreed, claiming Standard was more important for winning the actual tournament as you have to play it during the elimination rounds on Sunday. He added, however, that he liked having Draft in the middle of the day: &quot;Sometimes it's not your day [with a Standard deck] and if I 1-2, I like being able to put the deck aside to draft. By the time I come back to it on Day 2, it may be good again.&quot; He clarified what he meant by that, explaining that a deck geared to beat the decks he projected the pros would play might struggle against a large field of amateurs during the first three rounds of competition Friday. After drafting for seven rounds, however, many of the pros De Rosa expects to beat float to the top of the standings, and come the final four rounds of Standard, his deck finally gets to face the matchups he had been hoping for.</p>&#10;      <p>Current Nationals champ Paul Cheon, who X-0ed the entire Draft portion last year in addition to this year's first pod, added his thoughts: &quot;Half of the tournament is Draft, and people forget that. A lot of players come in from Regionals with their Standard decks, mostly Constructed players, and 2-1 or 3-0, only to lose in the Draft portion.&quot; When asked if he felt pros ever did the same but in the reverse formats he said, &quot;Sometimes, but the pros know to test.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Will these players be able to remain undefeated? Stay tuned to the coverage to find out.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 12:39 p.m.: Lounging Around</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Bill Stark</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>This weekend isn't called &quot;<b>Magic</b> Weekend&quot; for no reason, and in addition to the U.S. National Championships happening right now, another tournament is taking place: the culmination of this year's <b>Magic</b> Scholarship Series. It almost goes without saying that with every tournament for juniors comes an additional grouping of supporters, the parents.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/bb/93/bb9338f8b3621fbd6d06b29b38ff02363fcdf262760b4809e86f2b1f0f04c76e.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>From left: Stan, Barbara, and Jeff, Magic parents.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>This year's MSS Championship, like any other, features a Parents' Lounge, and we caught up with a few of these oft-forgotten but oh-so-important parts of the <b>Magic</b> scene. Stan Muszynski is from Tennessee, and is here supporting his son Stan III. He was happy to have been able to take the trip after his son's friend failed to qualify. &quot;He was originally going to come with a friend, but that friend didn't qualify and I didn't want Stan riding the rail system alone from the hotel to the site.&quot; Mr. Muszynski was definitely glad Baltimore was cooler this time around; in 2005, the last time Baltimore hosted the Championship, temperatures went as high as 100 degrees!</p>&#10;      <p>Barbara Winn is in town from Louisville, Kentucky to support her grandson Tyler. The family's name has certainly paid dividends for the 16-year-old as Barbara said he managed to win $500 in scholarship money last year, despite only having been playing the game for a few years. When I asked the parents if they enjoyed the traveling portion of their youngsters' exploits, Barbara ecstatically exclaimed, &quot;Yes! I volunteered for this 'job!'&quot; Her couchmate Jeff Kronenberger, in town from Cleveland to support his 15 year old son Chris, nodded his head in agreement. &quot;Jeff's been playing for four years so…&quot; He shrugged. Traveling was certainly nothing new for him.</p>&#10;      <p>When I asked whether or not their charges ever shirked responsibilities at home in pursuit of their favorite card game, Stan was quick to respond. &quot;It doesn't cause problems. There are a lot of worse things they could be doing and it keeps them out of trouble.&quot; Barbara and Jeff nodded in agreement, and Stan added &quot;Plus it's a good social tool for the kids.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Barbara pointed out that the Parents' Lounge allowed her to meet other parents in her situation. &quot;You meet people from all over,&quot; she said, while Stan added, &quot;Last year I talked with some folks all the way from Hawai'i!&quot; They also agreed that the Parents' Lounge was a huge relief to have, a space to sit down and relax before hearing reports back on how Stan III, Tyler, and Chris were doing each round.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;It's definitely nice,&quot; concluded Stan.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="5"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 2:10 p.m.: We Are the (Draft) Champions</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Brian David-Marshall</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/ae/19/ae19b94e5de29c88dc7eb9b6e5483af80d16c8dddc2d7c92be0e8c0729576fe2.jpg">&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Seven rounds of Nationals are in the books, and the Standard Draft champions are Jim Davis, Steven Sadin and Brad Nelson.
