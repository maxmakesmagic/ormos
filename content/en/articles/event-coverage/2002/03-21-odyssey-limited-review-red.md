This is the fourth installment in my series looking at the cards in Odyssey and their value in limited play. The rest of the series can be found [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### RED

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5b/6b/5b6b474c3848b5501b6e27203310f273e33eb6e46df2ee70ff9d2af41277d7d2.jpg)With the internet failing to define Limited environments the way it does their Constructed counterparts, its seldom that the **Magic** community comes to a consensus with regards to 40 card decks, but everyone and their sister seems to agree that Red is *Odyssey's* weakest color.

It isn't all bad. Red offers two of the top five cards in the set in Shower of Coals and Kamahl, Pit Fighter, and one of the set's best commons in Firebolt. It also is one half of what may be the most popular color combination in *Odyssey* Limited: red-green, while not experiencing the drop off white and green do in *Torment*, but that seems to be around where the kudos end. 

Red has the weakest creature base in the format and very little in the way of staying power, let alone a way to combat green's fat creatures and creature producing sorceries, meaning that when drafting it, you have little choice but to try and finish your opponent off before they get started. When you draft red, draft hard or go home: if you don't you'll go home after the first round.

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/96/f8/96f8f7e9665cd3f8f7754bf3d7665a2185cc5e7bb0e9b2801370970fa3aa7c56.jpg)**Acceptable Losses**  
 It seems like R&D did everything they could to make this card mediocre. Its costs a lot for a targeted burn spell, can't hit players, is cast at sorcery speed, forces you to use two cards regardless of whether it resolves and has a potentially catastrophic random discard. I'm almost surprised that it doesn't cost ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif). With all those problems though, the simple truth is that red has a lot of trouble dealing with large creatures, often making this a necessary evil. You'll probably play it if you get it, but don't get too many and don't pick Acceptable Losses too high. A 6th - 8th pick. 

**Anarchist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It's interesting how far we'll go to get card advantage at times. Anarchist asks us to pay five mana in addition to a sorcery's casting cost and the only benefit we get in addition to that sorcery is the 2/2 body that this card provides, and yet because of card advantage, that can turn into a very good deal. Anarchist's main function seems to be the optimizing of Shower of Coals (As if casting it once isn't enough), but whether it's Shower, Overrun, Morgue Theft or what have you, Anarchist will be good as long as your other resources are able to buy you enough time to take advantage of that card advantage. Don't force this card, as Sorceries generally aren't as strong as instants and red is unpopular, meaning you'll get these late, but you'll likely want Anarchist if one of those great game-winning sorceries happens to be in your deck. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Ashen Firebeast**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Eight is a lot of mana to pay for any card, but this monster pays dividends. Paying seven mana for a generic 6/6 seems acceptable, so adding one more for a very strong special ability doesn't feel outrageous. Once in play, the Firebeast completely controls the table, killing just about any non-flyer that might stand in its way. Of course, when faced with the option of drafting this big guy, you'll have to decide if your deck can buy you time to cast it, but know this: if you can do so successfully, you're probably going to win. A 5th - 7th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a6/6a/a66a3fabccb05e6545f0e948f669bce5411fbeaf185071dcaafc3c65ed770c42.jpg)**Barbarian Lunatic**  
 Red's common creatures just aren't good. They're either too fragile or too expensive, and none of them really fulfil a deck's needs as far as a strong creature base goes, so when the red mage comes across a creature that would be playable in any color, they have to scoop it up when they can. Barbarian Lunatic falls into an elusive mana slot, providing a solid body for its cost (though that body wouldn't be sufficient except...) and a strong special ability that can often lead to card advantage. Yes, it costs a lot to activate, but in this color, you won't find yourself doing much better as far as creatures go. A 3rd - 5th pick. 

**Bash to Bits**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The best artifact in this format may be a 0/1 creature that taps for colorless mana. You'll never need to kill two. Keep in mind this card's value may change when *Judgment* comes along, depending on the cards in that set, but for right now, you won't need to sideboard Bash to Bits in very often. A 10th - 12th pick.

**Battle Strain**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It's possible you might want to sideboard this in against decks that rely on walls and the like to stop your aggressive red-green beatdown deck, but that will only happen in extreme situations. An 11th - 13th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/dc/3adcc5ca9411ce5856c803ec44c27e6281c173e3558acf87888cdd8f8bef1e1a.jpg)**Blazing Salvo**  
 When *Odyssey* first came out, I went on record saying how much I liked the Salvo. Now that I've had a chance to learn the environment a little better, I see that it isn't quite as good as I originally thought, but I still like it a lot. The key here is the fact that the spell, regardless of whether it kills a creature, only costs one mana, making either result incredibly mana efficient. Obviously, doing five points of damage won't do too much in a passive or defensive deck, but thrown in with some aggressive creatures, I think you'll find the Salvo is very good at what it does for what it costs. A 5th - 7th pick

**Bomb Squad**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This is a tough one. Bomb Squad is a card that will make your opponents panic. They'll know that their immediate priority is to kill it, as failure to do so will mean the end of their creatures and eventually their own demise. At the same time, it's four mana for a 1/1 that takes three turns to kill a creature. In the end, it comes down to your deck and how capable it is of winning on the strength of one card. If you can protect it, it should be pretty good. By the way, remember to activate it in upkeep once its second ability has gone on the stack. Makes things go a lot faster. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Burning Sands**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This just isn't a Limited card. Five mana is just too much to make land destruction a major issue for your opponent. A 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ca/06/ca06b83f85276cbf157a6791f958851d20aaae7cd90d59a6b8e2e6a27ff3ddf3.jpg)**Chainflinger**  
 In writing about Hallowed Healer in the white installment of this series, I mentioned that I'd heard people call this the best common in the set, and now I'm here to tell you they're wrong. A very solid card, the Flinger isn't the best common in red, and for some draft styles doesn't rank second or third. Red has two modes: beatdown, which works best with white or green, or control, for which blue and black are red's better-suited partners. As far as control goes, the Flinger is almost as good as Firebolt assuming you can eventually get threshold, but for those beatdown decks, it's a very slow, very expensive card. I'll always play it if I get it, but in your more aggressive decks I wouldn't draft it too highly. A 2nd - 4th pick. 

**Chance Encounter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There's only one other coin flip card in *Odyssey*. It's just as bad and just as rare as this one. A 15th pick.

**Demolish**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This is pretty cut and dry. The artifacts in this format come up lacking, with only the rare Mirari ever meriting destruction, so Demolish won't see too much use there, but it's nice to have in the sideboard anyways to get rid of those nasty enchant lands. Obviously Earth Rift does a better job of land destruction, but the added versatility is a nice little bonus. A 9th - 11th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/41/f5/41f5d1eddabedcd68a4c83141c10f3c135cc1cfd7474d458e76f06f3775493c8.jpg)**Demoralize**  
 If you're red-green, open Overrun. If you don't, try grabbing one of these later on. Demoralize is a good way to finish opponents off with your beatdown decks, but its potency really does depend on your ability to get threshold. If you have Wild Mongrel, Lithatog or the like, you should be fine. Just make sure your deck can do the first twelve points of damage: Demoralize should take care of the rest. This card seems to go really late, so don't draft it too high. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Dwarven Grunt**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If you're low on one or two drops and have four Ember Beasts, this might be sideboardable against red. Doesn't feel great though. Red-green decks need one-drops, and if you can't get any of the higher quality varieties, the Grunt will do. A 11th - 13th pick.

**Dwarven Recruiter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There are essentially two Dwarven Recruiters: the one that you play because it gets you Dwarven Strike Force or Bomb Squad, and the one that provides you the room temperature 2/2 body with no special abilities. Odds are that if it's the former to you, it will be the latter to everyone else, so you should never be picking it too high. Think of the search ability as a bonus when picking this card: In the end, it's rarely not a 2/2 for three. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Dwarven Shrine**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Maybe in the six Scorching Missile deck...nope. A 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/08/7b/087b4d23830ffafe4252f6def9818f0443d517c53533751abc996a4eab27caa7.jpg)**Dwarven Strike Force**  
 This guy I like a lot. Yes, it's annoying that the discard ability comes at random, but in this case, it's usually worthwhile. The Strike Force always has first strike as far as your opponent's blocks are concerned, meaning you often won't have to activate the ability, and at the same time, you can use it to gain threshold with excessive lands and useless spells going to the graveyard. Cast the Dwarf, activate it four times, attack with my 5/5 Springing Tiger, my 4/4 Werebears and my regenerating Krosan Avenger sounds pretty good to me. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Earth Rift**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I've seen decks drafted that ran four or five of these in the main (Don't ever plan on this. On average, there are 2.3 - 2.4 copies of each common in any given draft if there are three packs of the base set) and in the end, the players lost matches. Simply, if you replace threats with land destruction, you quite often won't have enough threats remaining in your deck to capitalize on their mana problems. Sideboard the Rift against decks with poor mana bases or problematic enchantments like Caustic Tar, but don't main deck it. Not in draft. A 9th - 11th pick 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/28/69/286971fe519ac970d1f981024ec3862ef02a29d72e0f01c85b0cee2ca8c7c6bc.jpg)**Ember Beast**  
 Wow, was I wrong about this card. Originally, I didn't like the Ember Beast too much, with its limitations outweighing the benefits of its size in my mind, but as I've continued to play with it, I've seen the folly of my ways, as before threshold is reached, you'll seldom find a larger creature, and if you do it will cost five or six mana. A crucial card in beatdown decks, Ember Beast may be red's best common creature. It definitely is if you're going aggro. Just make sure you have some other fast creatures so you can really capitalize. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Engulfing Flames**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I really like this card. Whether you're killing a Cephalid Looter or preventing Dirty Wererat from regenerating, you can rest assured that you'll get to do it again. A one-mana spell that kills some important creatures, Engulfing Flames would be very playable if it didn't come with the flashback bonus. That it does takes it from solid to strong. It isn't a card that's crucial for any deck, but it will make a nice addition to any deck already playing mountains. A 3rd -5th pick. 

**Epicenter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This is NOT Armageddon. Armageddon is an efficient spell that can be cast any time to devastating effect. Epicenter is a slow card whose threshold ability is good at getting cards in the graveyard, but whose pre-threshold ability is useless. I used to play this in my main deck. I don't any more. A 9th -11th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/77/67/7767d544abaf71ee5807fb15adfddd92d442ba9c5770d8dc21afbb64abccb165.jpg)**Firebolt**  
 Ah, now this is the good stuff. There are a lot of reasons to not play red in this set, but Firebolt is the best common reason to do so. Firebolt kills a two or less toughness creature for one mana, populates your graveyard helping you get threshold, eventually comes back when you want it to, and does all this with easy to use casting and flashback costs. Kamahl and Shower of Coals get picked ahead of Firebolt if you're already red, but you won't say the same for much else. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Flame Burst**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In a format with so many three-toughness creatures, Flame Burst isn't perfect, but cheap efficient kill is hard to find, and when it comes at instant speed, you should take it more often than not. Sure, Flame Burst has the potential to do more than two points of damage, but I wouldn't put much stock in that when evaluating draft value: Even if you get two Bursts or Burst and Pardic Firecat, it's pretty unlikely that you'll draw them all together. Just take the card at its initial value. It should be good enough. A 2nd - 4th pick. 

**Frenetic Ogre**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Five mana can get you Shower of Coals, it can get you large flyers, it can get you card advantage creature kill spells like Second Thoughts or it can get you...this. 2/3 is not a substantial enough body for the five mana investment, regardless of the Ogre's pumpability. That aspect only coming in tandem with a random discard seems especially weak: that you have to pay mana also just makes it bad. The Ogre can find a place in your weaker decks, but I'd really try to avoid that eventuality. A 9th - 11th pick. 

**Halberdier**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 For a creature that's so easy to kill, for a creature with a toughness of one, for a creature in the weakest color in *Odyssey* and for a creature that effectively costs the same as Springing Tiger or Elephant Ambush while obviously not comparing, Halberdier can be a little annoying. That's a lot of negatives though, so don't pick it too high: There are plenty of other four casting cost creatures that can fill your deck just as effectively, if not more so. A 5th - 7th pick. 

**Impulsive Maneuvers**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Build your eighty card multiplayer decks around this: don't do the same with your forty card draft decks. A 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7a/4c/7a4c5e17f9d344d07d6e69a9580c7d8432bd45b90d33d23c2ccf5aae350ed3b5.jpg)**Kamahl, Pit Fighter**  
 Kamahl...or Shower...Kamahl...or Shower? The correct answer, of course, is 'who cares as long as I get one of them.' Kamahl is insane, either killing a creature or dealing six points of damage the turn he comes into play. The worst case scenario has the legend getting Afflicted, making for an even trade, but the more consistent scenario sees him kill everything in sight then ride along with your other creatures to victory. Shower is the more consistent of the two cards, but if you want to win a game by tapping your mana once, Kamahl is the man. A 1st pick. 

**Kamahl's Desire**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Who needs willpower when you're doing three damage a turn? All I know is that you don't need first strike when your opponent has no creatures. This could be sideboardable against green decks without tricks, but it will only hold off long enough to build up a sizable enough swarm to finish you off anyways. A 12th - 14th pick.

**Lava Blister**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I don't see many nonbasic lands worth saving on this list, but if you need to destroy one, don't give your opponents options: use Earth Rift, it does the job a lot better. A 13th - 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f5/77/f57701c8f5a42c85203bc101187ed8179978483b075284c0f8fa7f25395d4c58.jpg)**Liquid Fire**  
 I seem to like this card more than most people. The cool thing about Liquid Fire is that you only assign damage on resolution, forcing your opponent to do all their fast effects before you make your damage distribution decisions. Throw in the versatility that allows you to kill Cabal Patriarch or throw five points of damage to the head (assign zero to the creature) and you have a spell which, while expensive, always finds itself useful. A 3rd - 5th pick. 

**Mad Dog**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The two drop is a necessary evil for a properly constructed draft deck, with the low powered spells filling out one's mana curve so that they might get off to a fast start that overwhelms the opponent before they get started, and Mad Dog is constructed in such a way that it will usually do that and only that. A strong companion to Ember Beast, the Dog's value to your deck will come down to how aggressive you are and how many two drops you have. Don't pick the pup too high: he won't be your best friend. A 6th -8th pick.

**Magma Vein**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Another card I seem to like more than most, Magma Vein offers you three major benefits: mass creature kill, a high degree of board control and a way to get your lands into the graveyard to help with threshold or black's graveyard draining creatures and spells. Yes, it's a slow card and really requires that you play a decent amount of red to make it effective, but it doesn't actually have to be used to be a threat once in play. I'm not telling you that this is a high pick; it isn't. I'm just saying that if you have it, it's useable, and if you don't main deck it, it will be a great sideboard option. A 6th - 8th pick.

**Magnivore**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Even with haste, it's very seldom that you'll have enough sorceries to make this rare viable. You may find reason to sideboard it in if your opponent has more than you can count, but I doubt it'll happen. An 11th - 13th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/14/cf/14cf0ed4f7a42458b6367d89c267813306b8960faaf7a37a0fb6e6bef4634285.jpg)**Minotaur Explorer**  
 I'm not a big fan of any card that forces a random discard. Either you run the risk of losing something early, or eliminate the effectiveness of a low casting cost card by waiting until your hand has been depleted of cards of value to cast it. In other words, to make this card effective, your deck had better value speed and power over economy. A great compliment to Ember Beast. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Mine Layer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If you're going to play a 1/1 for four mana, it had better produce an effect that has an immediate and profound effect on the game, and unfortunately, Mine Layer doesn't meet these requirements. A solid sideboard option against Chamber of Manipulation or Squirrel Nest, this Dwarf is probably insufficient against important lands played by red or black decks that can kill it easily, at least compared to Earth Rift or Demolish. However, if your opponent is playing a slow deck that takes eons to take control and kill, this may be your answer. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Molten Influence**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 No! A 15th pick.

**Mudhole**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 An excellent sideboard card against the deck with four Terravores. Terravore is a rare. A 13th - 15th pick.

**Need for Speed**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card has a lot going for it. It offers an opportunity for haste, a valuable commodity in this set. It gives you a way to get your lands in your graveyard, providing in turn a quick, effective method for threshold. It's casting cost is extremely low, its activation cost requires no mana, and all that adds up to a lot of plus side for what this card does. Unfortunately, the best time for haste is in the early game, when you'll often be unable to afford the loss of a land. If you think you can withstand it though, this becomes a very solid card. Don't pick this too high, and get Reckless Charge instead if you can, but in your aggressive decks, the Need could give you the staying power required to finish your opponent off. If nothing else, they won't attack too aggressively when you have no creatures and they're at four life. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Obstinate Familiar**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 1/1's for one mana don't have enough impact on the field of play unless they provide an important special ability. We've all heard the jokes about the Familiar 'ruining' Stasis, but the reality of this card is that its ability really won't apply itself in Limited play. A 13th - 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/91/b1/91b14010a3865bce876ef0a15f6c9bef0129c02dbd90dcd0329aa5d8df834f9f.jpg)**Pardic Firecat**  
 The Firecat offers little to get excited about. It's only 2/3 for four mana, which leaves it pretty far down the totem pole on its own, but the contributions it makes to deck synergy are much greater than one would think. Again, not as good as Reckless Charge, the haste option makes possible the start your opponent just can't deal with: There are very few decks out there that can survive 2nd turn Mongrel, 3rd turn Ember Beast, 4th turn Firecat because of the sheer speed of the draw. There are bigger creatures and definitely better ones, but few will keep your deck running as smoothly. A 6th - 8th pick.

**Pardic Miner**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Miner offers little to get excited about. Unlike the Firecat though, that's pretty much all I have to say about that. A 15th pick.

**Pardic Swordsmith**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Pardic comes from the Latin for 'weak'. This guy couldn't forge a sword made out of cotton candy. A 15th pick.

**Price of Glory**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Obviously a little more applicable in Constructed, the Price is not without its merits as a potential sideboard card. There are certain cards in the *Odyssey* environment like Second Thoughts that are only really effective on your opponent's turn, and when your opponent's deck is relying on those spells to maintain control of the table, the Price disrupts those spells to such an extent that they become unusable. You won't find yourself siding it in very often, but if you get Price of Glory, keep an eye on what your fellow drafters are doing: it could end up being very strong. A 11th - 13th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/66/32/663287fe228543623a2a9f777014e3f75aa46e8e8f1b094d26541f8199d12b6c.jpg)**Reckless Charge**  
 Through the time that's elapsed since the set's release, my estimation if this card's strength has risen non-stop. Reckless Charge is very similar to one of my favorite draft cards of all time, Seething Anger, as it gives one of your more aggressive creatures a huge boost more than once, but while the Anger was better as far as sustained assaults go, the Charge's initial infusion is topped by none. +3/+0 and haste completely throws off all math involved in defensive decisions made by your opponent on their previous turn. This is the engine that makes the red-green train go. You'll pick other cards higher, but you definitely want to get your hands on one or two of these. A 4th - 6th pick, moving up. 

**Recoup**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It seems like most of the sorceries you want to flashback have flashback already, and for those few that don't, Anarchist does the job better, and Anarchist is nothing special. An 10th - 12th pick.

**Rites of Initiation**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In some ways, this card is like Overrun without a safety net. Overrun, you cast the spell and attack, and if your opponent doesn't die, your creatures live to fight another day. With the Rites, while your guys don't trample, there is the element of surprise, but with the discard being random, if you don't kill your opponent you're left with an empty hand and very likely little enough on offense to do significant damage in the near future. The randomness and uncertainty make this a card I prefer to not use except in a fast red-green, but there it'll be a game winner. A 7th - 9th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/66/f8/66f8652f9fa587e7ad7acea527426dea47b110429a0ebea2b57f8ecfa0403a80.jpg)**Savage Firecat**  
 BIG kitty. The Firecat is a very simple card in that you play it and close your eyes in the hopes your opponent can't handle it. If they appear able, the Firecat has to go away pretty soon, as otherwise you'll get outraced. Fortunately for the red player, 7/7 tramplers are pretty difficult to deal with, making this a high pick. I think I take Firebolt over it, but its close. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Scorching Missile**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When you're playing extreme aggression, there are some cards that defy the rules for success in Limited play, or rather you have to redefine those rules. Scorching Missile defies those rules as concerns card advantage and interaction, but rules are made to be broken, and when you're playing that super aggressive deck that is entirely capable of dealing the first 16 points of damage (Don't count on flashing this back), you can use Scorching Missile to finish them off. Don't pick this over anything you might be using in your deck, but when you get it 10th or 11th consider it as a viable option if you're red-green. A 10th - 12th pick. 

**Seize the Day**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I've heard players retell their Seize the Day stories where casting the card wins them the game, but frankly I just haven't found the situation where it's made that much of a difference. Pretty good with either massive creatures or creatures with more powerful tap abilities, if you have either in play, you're more likely to be winning than not, making the Seize expendable. If it cost less, I might be more inclined to try abusing this card's ability, but as is, I don't see it being played in my decks. An 11th - 13th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/48/a1/48a1f690c09026ee73bc095689e541ce6b5869a30f61294279333324df85bcac.jpg)**Shower of Coals**  
 For pure power, this is the best non-rare in the set, and it rivals the best rares as well. Shower of Coals is nuts, offering versatility, early and late game strength and finishing power. When you get Shower of Coals, you have the best reason I can think of to play red. Don't look for reasons not to. A 1st pick.

**Spark Mage**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Filling your casting cost slots is important for aggressive decks, but its important to hold the creatures you use to a standard of playablity. The Mage gives you a one-drop, but everyone else is concerned enough with getting their two- and three- casting cost creatures that when you get to cast this on first turn, it usually won't amount to much. Cast any other time, the Mage will find itself staring up at much bigger game. Sounds like a recipe for failure to me, though Overrun and Rites of Initiation can make everything ok. An 11th - 13th pick. 

**Steam Vines**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Another red solution to annoying enchant lands. I'd play Earth Rift or Demolish first unless your opponent's deck is a lot slower than yours, but this is a suitable answer to Squirrel Nest or Chamber of Manipulation. An 11th - 13th pick.

**Thermal Blast**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 dependable creature kill is hard to find, but when you find it, you'd really prefer it cost less than five mana. Thermal Blast is a good, not great, solution to your opponent's better creatures, but its slow, plodding nature keeps it from attaining high pick status. You could definitely do worse, but if you're taking these with your first or second picks, you're either doing something wrong or very unlucky. A 4th - 6th pick. 

**Tremble**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Don't ever play this card in Limited. It won't accomplish anything for you. A 15th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/43/d5/43d5118f955ab746b0054eb204a6cc3b209b09d966a2d84a036d5e7b5f836ded.jpg)**Volcanic Spray**  
Cephalid Looter, Hallowed Healer, Painbringer and the like are creatures that could conceivably be killed by Volcanic Spray, and with the flashback you can get some massive card advantage out of it. Once your opponent has seen it, the surprise value will be gone, usually leaving the Spray to act as a one for one card with some mediocre one toughness creature, but that seems fine as far as two mana flashback abilities. Especially fun when your opponent has done some massive blocking that barely keeps each of their creatures alive, the Spray is a solid card that will find itself proving useful most of the time. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Volley of Boulders**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Over the last few years as I've done these articles, I've voiced time and again my distaste for cards that cost more than six mana. With those sentiments in mind, there are exceptions like Amugaba and Iridescent Angel that definitely make the grade, but with the Volley costing nine mana with a next to impossible flashback cost, it doesn't get into my decks. Leave it in the pack for someone more willing to die with eight land in play, you won't regret it. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Whipkeeper**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I had high hopes for this 1/1 at first, trying time and again to make it work in unison with Chainflinger and Psionic Gift, but in the end, it's frailty proved to be its predominant feature. Simply, you need to lock the table down to make this ability really applicable, but it is so easy to remove that by the time it can get really abusive, the Whipkeeper is dead. Pretty good as a sideboard against white-blue decks full of 1- and 0-toughness defensive creatures. A 9th - 11th pick.
