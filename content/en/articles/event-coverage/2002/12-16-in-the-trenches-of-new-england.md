For a long time, I’ve run most of the PTQs in New England. A lot of the great players have played in my events. I remember when Billy “Baby Huey” Jensen first qualified for the Pro Tour at one of my PTQs and then really impressed me by winning a PTQ next season before the first Pro Tour he qualified for had even happened. When Darwin Kastle fell off the gravy train, he requalified at the first PTQ he played. The mighty Sean “Hammer” Regnier must have qualified at my events a dozen times. 

The big name famous players are great to talk about. And we can find those stories all over the *Sideboard*. But what about the common PTQ players? 

I’ve watched *their* story unfold more times than I can count. The players who are fighting against incredible odds to make their dream happen: to win the slot, to play on the Pro Tour.

This PTQ season, I’ve decided to get these player’s stories and share them with the rest of the **Magic** community. I’ll be doing this through a series of articles on the New England PTQs. In this series, I’ll give you the player’s decklists and strategies, but I’ll also try to provide a glimpse into their view of the game (what have they done in the game, how they prepare, and what they want to get out of the game).

The first New England PTQ of the season was not one of mine. It was run by Tom Shea at his store, TJ’s Collectibles (Tom and I often work together on events, and will be jointly running Grand Prix-Boston in February). At first glance, TJ’s appears to be a comics and collectibles shop with a large inventory, but no play space. But descend the stairs into the basement and you’ll find a different story. There are game tables to seat 150 players and a wall lined with arcade games. TJ’s is home to the New England Championships tournament series and the biggest casual **Magic** gatherings you’ll ever see. They get about fifty players every week for their Saturday 12–3 PM casual Type 1.5 tournament.

I tried to interview a good cross section of players for this event. Some of the players I spoke to had impressive **Magic** resumes, others were total unknowns outside New England.

Paul is a young, talented player who has qualified for the Pro Tour twice (PT-Osaka and PT-Boston), but has yet to make money on the Tour. When he’s qualified for the Pro Tour, Paul tests with YMG, but for the PTQs, he tests with his non-pro friends. Because he’s in school right now, his group did most of their work for this PTQ online. It mostly took the form of looking up decklists and discussion. 

Rob: “How did you choose this deck?”

Melissa worked with the YMG Providence team for the PTQ. They built copies of all the popular decks and played them against each other. Brian Lynch built this two-color version of Gary-Oath two days before the PTQ.

Melissa:“Just play an Oath. Your opponent never plays guys. You win with your Faerie Conclaves and Treetop Villages. Oath is a very good deck for the field. It’s great against Rock and goblins. It’s a very good deck to play right now.”
