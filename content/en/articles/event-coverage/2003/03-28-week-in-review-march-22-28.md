### Pro Tour-Venice

Little Timmy couldn't possibly be happier.

Angels and dragons battling it out in Pro Tour Top 8 matches. Kai Budde summoning Silvos. The return of the monored goblin decks. Darwin Kastle playing with eleven dragons – not counting the one on his polo shirt. This Pro Tour had all those things, and I am sure marketing people at Wizards of the Coast are ecstatic.

Tournament players shouldn't be unhappy either – this was probably the most diverse and interesting Block Constructed Pro Tour in many years. Far from being dominated by one or two archetypes, there were more than a half dozen viable decks. Six different archetypes made Top 8, with several more appearing in Top 16.

Among the most popular decks were Astral Slide, Beast Bidding and goblins. All of the decklists should be appearing on the *Sideboard* over the next few days. Look forward to seeing any number of creative decks sprinkled in the metagame. Cleric and zombie tribal decks made a minor appearance, but many of the more interesting archetypes were all about much larger creatures. Explosive Vegetation powered a number of strategies, helping summon huge beasties like Akroma and Kilnmouth Dragon.

Despite a metagame that actively persecuted Astral Slide decks, Osyp Lebedowicz navigated this archetype into the finals, managing some very impressive upsets on the way. The final match was easy by comparison, as he faced his best possible matchup – a goblin deck. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptven03/805.jpg)Osyp Lebedowicz made a name for himself at Pro Tour-Osaka, where he placed seventh. A year later, Osyp once again proved his mastery of the Block format with this victory. Osyp is a member of TOGIT-CMU – a conglomerate that has become a major force on the Pro Tour over the last year and a half. The Only Game in Town is a store in Somerville, NJ that has been home to a number of pro players including Eugene Harvey and Gerard Fabiano. TOGIT's co-owner Patrick Sullivan also won a 230-man overnight PTQ held at Pro Tour-Venice.

The other man in the finals was Finland's Tomi “Jelly” Walamies. Well known for his deck construction skills, this member of Team Punisher has been talking about retirement for a while now. Despite his Top 8 finish in New Orleans, he had a poor year and even fell, or was about to fall, off the gravy train. This tournament finish has reinvigorated not only Walamies's career (as he is now qualified for all Pro Tours next year) but also his interest. Walamies vows to be back at Worlds.

In third place was Jordan Berkowitz, a virtual unknown from United States. He managed to keep at the top of the standings throughout most of the tournament with a very sturdy beasts build. He was ranked number one after Day 1 and easily made his way into the Top 8, but eventually fell to Walamies. In contrast to Berkowitz, the other Top 4 finisher is a true veteran. William “Baby Huey” Jensen added another impressive tournament finish to an already long list. Like Budde and a number of other high profile pros, Jensen played a green-white control deck with huge monsters.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptven03/808.jpg)Darwin Kastle was the only Your Move Games representative in Top 8 this time, but we had a very solid showing nonetheless, with a total of four players in Top 32. Kastle played The Claw – a red-green deck featuring eleven dragons and capable of summoning an 11/11 monster on turn 4, with a perfect draw. Woe is the tale of teammate Rob Dougherty who played the same deck but ran into SEVEN goblin decks during the seven rounds of Day 1. Even with that being The Claw's worst matchup, Dougherty still managed an impressive-under-the-circumstances 4-3 record, but it was not enough for him to make Day 2. Another teammate, Justin Gary, became the fifteenth **Magic** player to break the $100,000 lifetime winnings mark, thanks to his Top 16 finish.

France's Gabriel Nassif has a very impressive season last year, but was not heard from much this season. He changed that in Venice, as one of the goblin players to make the Top 8. The other was Sweden's Mattias Jorstedt. 

Finally, there's Japanese rookie Akihiro Kashima. I know little of this player, but his deck was both unorthodox and interesting. Japanese players did very well this Pro Tour with Kashima's Top 8 finish highlighting. Kazuhiko Mitsuya's ninth place and a total of eight money finishers, on top of PS2's win in the Masters showed, once again, that Japan is a **Magic** power.

Notably absent from this tournament was Dave Price. Despite being qualified, he did not attend. Could it be that Dave is done with competitive **Magic** for good? Time will tell.

### Venice Team Masters

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptven03/348.jpg)It is time to brag a little bit. Illuminati – Justin Gary, Zvi Mowshowitz and I – have been within one match win away from the Team Masters our entire career. At Pro Tour-New York, we lost in the Top 4 to Les Plus Class, missing a Masters invite. We were in on rating, but right before Masters, there was a mad rush with all the highly rated teams grabbing a few extra points. We played in a few small tournaments too, but the tournaments did not get processed/reported on time and we had to sit that one out, frustrated. For this current Masters, we played in Grand Prix-New Jersey, where the winning team would qualify, and made finals, but lost to The Jokas. With the Masters program being eliminated as of next year, this was our last chance.

Illuminati went through the Gateway, having to defeat our own teammates Your Move Games among others in the process. We were one of two teams to win the Gateway, along with Norway's Outland featuring Nicolai Herzog, Eivind Nitter and Bjorn Jocumsen. Outland almost earned both spots in the Gateway – Team Outland 2 lost to us in the final match of the other bracket.

Other teams in the last Team Masters included Phoenix Foundation, Courtney's Boys, 2020, Panzer Hunters and The Jokas. Panzer Hunters managed an upset, defeating the strongly favored Phoenix Foundation in the first round. They were themselves defeated by PS2 in an all-Japanese semifinal, while 2020 took down Courtney's Boys. In the end, PS2 emerged triumphant, adding to the recent Japanese string of major tournament accomplishments.

### Semi-Premier Magic Events

Over the course of the last year or so, a new and very exciting trade has been growing in competitive **Magic**. More and more stores and tournament organizers are running what I'd like to refer to as semi-Premier events. These are large tournaments with significant cash prizes ranging from $1000 to $5000, which often require a player to win an invitation. Spring is a great time to host these events, as there is a significant gap in tournament play, without much going on between the end of this round of PTQs and Regionals.

### Texas Magic League

Spearheaded by **Magic** columnist Fletcher Peatross, a number of stores in Texas banded together to run the $5000 Texas Magic League. The setup will be similar to that of the NEC I described last week. Players will compete in qualifying tournaments and then qualifier winners will play it out for a total of $5,000 in prizes, with the first place prize being $1000.

For more information on this and future events by the League, please check out [www.texasmagicleague.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20030328000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://%20www.texasmagicleague.com&origin=sideboard_wir_20030328).

*This took place during round 2 of the Legions Prerelease. It was down to the final game and I was feeling the pressure, mana hosed for the second game in a row. I was barely surviving as my opponent got six lands in play and was holding three cards in hand. I cast Blackmail, which I boarded in and, sure enough, got his Phage. A few turns later, I got up to five mana and played my trump card: Patriarch's Bidding, naming legend. He looked at his graveyard not even noticing, probably assuming I had discarded a legend earlier and named zombie. He returned all his zombies to play and I said “you forgot someone…” He then noticed Phage is a legend and muttered “doh”!*
