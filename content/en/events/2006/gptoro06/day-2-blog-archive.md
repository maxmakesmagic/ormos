<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, June 4: 12:17 p.m. - Drafting With Adam Chambers</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <i>Draft One: Pod One</i>&#10;        <br> Jonathan Sonne<br> Adam Chambers<br> Ben Lundquist<br> David Rood<br> Josh Breau<br> Billy Moreno<br> Mark Vizzacco<br> John Fiorillo</p>&#10;      <p>Adam Chambers best finish came during last season when he LCQ'd into Pro Tour Atlanta with Don Smith and Andrew Pacifico as We Add. The team would make the most of their last minute invite by reaching the semifinals before getting knocked out by the French team with the long name in a funny language. </p>&#10;      <p>He has earned a reputation as an unorthodox drafter with card valuations out of synch with public perception. He has also earned a well-deserved reputation as a super tight player with an unflappable demeanor. He was sitting in between Jon Sonne to his right and Ben Lundquist. Coming into the draft Adam was adamant that he did not want to go green-white or red-white because &quot;those color combinations never win&quot; but he opened up a first pack with Tolsmir Wolfsblood and was unwilling to pass it up in favor of the ever popular Peel From Reality. </p>&#10;      <p>His second pack had <a>Cleansing Beam</a> and an unexciting array of green mid-range picks and he opted for <a>Vedalken Dismisser</a>. No doubt thinking about the lack of removal in green-white-blue, Adam set himself up to be able to play black cards with a third pick Elves. As much as he wanted to resist the siren call of Selesnya, a fourth pick Evangel made it impossible. The Ravnica pack rounded out with him picking up another <a>Elves of Deep Shadow</a> and a <a>Golgari Signet</a>. He looked like he was going to be green-white-blue with plenty of fixing should he steer toward black for removal.</p>&#10;      <p>Guildpact offered him <a>Pillory of the Sleepless</a> and <a>Angel of Despair</a>. Despite the strain it would put on his mana Adam can never turn down the more expensive card and he took the rare. <a>Douse in Gloom</a> over Teysa was his second pick - the Pillory and the Teysa went straight into Jon's picks at the next seat. With the third pick Adam had a choice between <a>Ghor-Clan Savage</a>, <a>Ghost Warden</a>, <a>Gristleback</a>, and <a>Harrier Griffin</a>. The easy rule of thumb with Adam is to look for the highest casting cost and that will be his pick - he took the Griffin. His subsequent picks were <a>Shrieking Grotesque</a>, <a>Orzhov Basilica</a>, and <a>Ghor-Clan Savage</a> - all of which he seemed quite pleased to see. </p>&#10;      <p>In <i>Dissension</i> Adam first picked <a>Rakdos Carnarium</a> over <a>Plaxcaster Frogling</a> and <a>Protean Hulk</a>. He almost second picked <a>Azorius Chancery</a> but instead opted for the flexibility and power of Supply//Demand. I was gape-jawed as <a>Stormscale Anarch</a> wandered pitifully around the table looking for a home. The card is a certifiable bomb rare and it looked like it was going to end up a sixth or seventh pick. I was reminded of the 10th pick Ghost Council from Grand Prix Richmond. Adam was equally astounded as he found himself the beneficiary of a fifth pick <a>Sky Hussar</a>. Even more stunning to him were a pair of extremely late pick karoos - including the Chancery he considered as a second pick.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Wow. Just wow,&quot; muttered Adam as he came to the Feature Match area to construct his deck. &quot;This is the best deck I have ever drafted in this format. I have bounce lands, not too much removal but some spells, and I have some great creatures. I am four colors but I have bounce lands in all my colors and some fixing. I don't have to play any bad cards - which is new for me.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>After pack two Adam looked like he was in danger of falling into the green-black-white trap where you don't have any good guild options but his plan was always to play four colors. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I was trying to just splash the Tolsmir but a fourth pick Evangel with nothing else in the pack? I just had to take it. Tolsmir is just a bomb rare. He makes a game so easy. You just play him and you win. I had the Dismisser and I was always planning on playing it. Going into the third pack blue-green cards were what I was hoping for but I ended up getting Supply//Demand and <a>Sky Hussar</a>,&quot; he shrugged happily. He was especially surprised and pleased with the late mana fixing he picked up - something that had not happened for him in his practice drafts. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I got the Chancery 10th and a Growth Chamber 6th - I first picked a double land that I am not even going to have to play. I don't even like playing this format without double lands - it's just no fun. When I was practicing at Ken's I would never ever see them - ever!&quot;</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <div>&#10;                Adam Chambers&#10;
