<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>April 24, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f0/9f/f09f4d2a172231ed02bcfadb60e0264389ebd0a5d2bbaaf40ad6a927154a8f0e.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: In <i>Onslaught</i> Sealed Deck, do you choose to play first or draw first, and why?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Gary Wise</b>: I thought playing first would be the right call, but sealed isn’t nearly as tempo based as draft and you get a lot of standoffs, so I‘m starting to think drawing could be right, especially when you’re three colors. <br><b>Gerard Fabiano</b>: I play first. This format is all about tempo. If I won game one, then I board into <a>Elvish Pioneer</a> so I gain back the tempo with the extra land play. <br><b>Dirk Baberowski</b>: So far, I have almost always played first, but with the introduction of <b>Legions</b>, the format is more about drawing your broken cards like <a>Timberwatch Elf</a>. Also, the top tables are generally decks with 5+ removal spells, so at the last Grand Prix, I chose to draw first twice. <br><b>Olivier Ruel</b>: Play first. 99% of the time, the morph advantage is too strong. <br><b>Alex Shvartsman</b>: Although many would disagree, I still choose to draw, as I would in other sealed deck formats. Almost everyone is playing a three-color deck and sealed decks are generally slower than draft decks. Thus, the advantage of having an extra card to start with while denying that same card to your opponent outweighs the tempo advantage of going first in my mind. <br><b>Ken Krouner</b>: You absolutely have to play first. Tempo is the most important thing in this format. You need your mana untapped during the morph-on-morph fights.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
