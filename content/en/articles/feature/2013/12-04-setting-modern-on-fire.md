Travis Woo is not one to shy away from crazy decks, and he would be first on my list if I had to guess who made a deck with [Worldfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Worldfire), [Spellweaver Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellweaver+Helix), and [Reforge the Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reforge+the+Soul) (much less four of all of them). 

![Worldfire](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Worldfire)

[As Travis explains in his article](http://www.channelfireball.com/articles/woo-brews-cheap-modern-toys/), he built this list to be budget friendly, as Modern is a difficult format to get into. I don't know if his stated goal was to go as deep as possible, but he may have succeeded. The two main engines in the deck are [Pyromancer Ascension](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyromancer+Ascension) and [Spellweaver Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellweaver+Helix), which are inevitably the centerpieces of any deck they are a part of. 

Both these cards work with [Faithless Looting](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Faithless+Looting) and [Wild Guess](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Guess), as well as [Reforge the Soul](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reforge+the+Soul), although it's more accurate to see Ascension as the engine that ideally gets you to the [Spellweaver Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellweaver+Helix) activation. You can easily trigger Ascension with all the card draw, four-ofs, and Rituals, plus [Flame Jab](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Jab)s for extra consistency. Once Ascension is going, casting [Worldfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Worldfire) can be trivial, after which all you need is a single [Flame Jab](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Jab) to finish things off. 

[Spellweaver Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellweaver+Helix) is a way to shortcut much of that process, and when combined with all the discard, makes it fairly simple (as simple as a [Spellweaver Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellweaver+Helix) deck is capable of being, at least) to cast [Worldfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Worldfire). Discard [Worldfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Worldfire) and any cheap spell, Helix your opponent, then cast another copy of the cheap spell and you've caught the world on fire. Once again, [Flame Jab](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Jab) is the preferred method of finishing things from there. 

A big part of this deck's strategy is how it plays after sideboarding. The twelve cards that Travis plans to bring in frequently are four each of [Young Pyromancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Young+Pyromancer), [Empty the Warrens](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Empty+the+Warrens), and [Goblin Bushwhacker](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Bushwhacker). Swapping out the [Worldfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20131204000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Worldfire)-Helix plan for the token plan can catch opponents by surprise, and change the angle of attack entirely. The deck may still be vulnerable to anti-storm cards, but it now dodges graveyard cards, artifact removal, and enchantment removal. A swarm of Goblins and Elementals is not hard to generate, and a Bushwhacker later and the match might be over.
