Commander progenitor and Judge Emeritus Sheldon Menery writes a column on StarCityGames.com where he details his Commander decks. One of the most popular commanders out there is [The Mimeoplasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Mimeoplasm), and Sheldon recently took the opportunity to share his build and how an average game might play out.

This take on the green-blue-black commander isn't as focused on quickly killing the opponent with [The Mimeoplasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Mimeoplasm) as much as it is focused on using [The Mimeoplasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Mimeoplasm) for incremental advantages. While some players might try and do something like [Buried Alive](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Buried+Alive) into [Skithiryx, the Blight Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skithiryx%2C+the+Blight+Dragon) and a huge creature, Sheldon is a champion of having a fun, interactive game rather than trying to kill people quickly. The result is some really fun turns, using [The Mimeoplasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Mimeoplasm) on players around the table!

Because of how this deck likes to play on other players' graveyards, the deck even contains [Archive Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Archive+Trap) and [Mindcrank](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindcrank) to load up your opponents' graveyards. [Dreadwaters](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreadwaters) works in a pinch to either mill yourself or your opponent, letting you do whatever will help your chances more.

Of course, in a pinch, the deck can just end games. Searching up [Lord of Extinction](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lord+of+Extinction) with [Fauna Shaman](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fauna+Shaman) or [Survival of the Fittest](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Survival+of+the+Fittest) allows you to [Rite of Consumption](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rite+of+Consumption) it away, often being enough to immediately defeat one player!

[The Mimeoplasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20120524000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Mimeoplasm) is a lot of fun to play, and Sheldon's version is no exception. Take a look at his decklist, and, if you want to read it in action, go [check out his article](http://www.starcitygames.com/magic/commander/24106_The_Mimeoplasm_PlayByPlay.html) where he details how a game played out!
