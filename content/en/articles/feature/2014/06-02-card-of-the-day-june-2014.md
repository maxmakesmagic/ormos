![Pernicious Deed](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Pernicious+Deed)

  
[Pernicious Deed](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pernicious+Deed) – *Conspiracy*. English picked up *pernicious* in the early fifteenth century from Middle French, which took it from Latin's *perniciosus* ("destructive"), which in turn developed from *pernicies* ("ruin"). Both parts of the word almost certainly came from [Proto-Indo-European](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language). *Per-* just meant "forward" or "through," but *necis* and its originator *nex* probably look familiar to **Magic** players, as they gave us [nausea](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=174915) and [necro-](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=382317), [for example](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=194977).

![Grenzo's Rebuttal](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Grenzo%27s+Rebuttal)

  
[Grenzo's Rebuttal](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grenzo%27s+Rebuttal) – *Conspiracy*. We are perhaps more familiar with the legal and political form of *rebut*, meaning to contradict or refute, but that meaning traces back only to the early nineteenth century. Originally, rebut had a more violent meaning. From Old French through Middle English in the fourteenth century, it originally meant "to drive or beat back," or "to repulse."

![Bite of the Black Rose](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Bite+of+the+Black+Rose)

  
[Bite of the Black Rose](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bite+of+the+Black+Rose) – *Conspiracy*. Short words that describe concrete, observable phenomena (like *bite*, *black*, and *rose*) tend to be pretty old. Both *bite* and *black* trace back easily via Old English and [Proto-Germanic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) to [Proto-Indo-European](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language). *Rose* traces back via Latin and Ancient Greek to Persian (which, in turn, might have taken it from [PIE](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language)).

[Æther Tradewinds](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86ther+Tradewinds) – *Conspiracy*. [This Arcana](/en/articles/feature/2014/03-21-in-ther-words) explains *Æther*. *Trade wind* originated in the mid-seventeenth century. As explained by the OED (*Oxford English Dictionary*), it is "from the phrase *blow trade*," which means "blow steadily in the same direction." The OED continues: "Because of the importance of these winds to navigation, 18th-century etymologists were led erroneously to connect the word *trade* with 'commerce.'"

![Custodi Soulbinders](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Custodi+Soulbinders)

  
[Custodi Soulbinders](https://web.archive.org/web/20140602000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Custodi+Soulbinders) – *Conspiracy*. The Custodi were an order of priests who served King Brago. They unnaturally prolonged his life to preserve their own power, with [unintended consequences](/en/articles/feature/2014/05-14-betrayal). *Soul* has held its meaning since roughly Old English, which got it from [Proto-Germanic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language). *Bind* also has pretty consistent meaning all the way back to [Proto-Indo-European](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language).
