### 

It’s been a month since Pro Tour-Chicago, a month until Venice and, as a result of the Tribal review fiasco, I started doing a color-by-color analysis in Wise Words. Seems like it’s time to look at the red cards of *Onslaught*.

### Red

Okay, when the set first came out, black, red, green and white each had their advocates as potential best color in the format and 75% of them were naturally wrong. Red is the best color in *Onslaught*. To debate it is to show ignorance and it starts with the best common, the best uncommon and two of the three best rares in the set.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/50/02/5002f0b54d0fbee8a11abd9565e91501d5637907decf1b5468c32c3490a6b1b1.jpg)Sparksmith is insane. There are only two uncommons you should be picking over it first pick, first pack and those are Lightning Rift and Slice and Dice, both red. Slice and Dice and Lightning Rift are both insane and there are only a small handful of rares you should be taking over them first pick, first pack and if you poll pros as to which their most desired rare to open is, roughly two thirds will say Rorix or Starstorm, with Visara providing the exceptions. Visara is the best individual card, but the added bonus of drafting red is too much to ignore.

Red’s commons go deeper than the 1/1. Shock and Solar Blast are both better first picks than any common outside of red and with red-blue being the most powerful archetype in the format, Lavamancer’s Skill falls into that same category for many. In other words, we’re looking at *Urza’s Saga* Black all over again. Red is the stone cold nuts, people. Don’t force it, but if the packs offer, gladly accept.

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ac/12/ac1227b5054735ef24dbab3f36fa6661386a9808066941e99106cc42d8c8230b.jpg)**Rorix Bladewing**  
 Sure, he’s big, tough, breathes fire, flies, has haste, wins you the game when you draw him and is named as much as any card when pros are asked what they want to open, but his breath is positively awful. Therefore, Rorix is not the sociable choice. Pass him to me.

**Starstorm**  
 Some rank this card, the best mass-kill spell for Limited play to come out in years, ahead of the pit fighters, but the bog boys give you a win method, so I place it third. Really though, it doesn’t matter. If you open any of them, pump the fist, preferably in the face of your first round opponent.

**Slice and Dice**  
 More mass kill, Slice gets the nod over Sharpshooter because the goblin can die pretty easily and casting either will likely win you the game.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d5f2e9d34cf37a652a43615ea45b006d0d52be604a9ceb6bbae6cf2ebd7c80.jpg)**Goblin Sharpshooter**  
 Yes, it’s better than Sparksmith. Yes, it’s true, you should almost never pass this card. Simply, the Sharpshooter’s casting cost makes it splashable and its power level means not taking advantage of the opportunity is a mistake. It just dominates the table.

**Menacing Ogre**  
 I’ve taken the Ogre over Slice and Dice before and every time I’ve done so, I’ve been pleased with the results. You should usually do that in red-blue, where the color combination is most likely to dominate the table with little men.

**Lightning Rift**  
 First pick first pack, the Rift moves up, making some people’s top five list, but unlike the other cards this high on the list, Rift forces you to play suboptimal cards in your deck. In this company, one strike is enough.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7b/74/7b7454007df289315e844cca118e312e16ab3488ec3df2ba52cc4bb9a041df6f.jpg)**Butcher Orgg**  
 Same deal as the Slice and Dice/Sharpshooter debate: Smith dies, Orgg doesn’t and when it doesn’t, you win the game. Yes it’s costly, but it’s so worth the seven mana.

**Sparksmith**  
 It’s just wrong that a card this good is 8th on this list, but that’s testimony to how powerful red is, not the strength of the goblin. Sparksmith is incredible, dominating the table like few cards can for just two mana from a common slot. It was probably a mistake to make this card with the black rarity icon.

**Insurrection**  
 As powerful as this card is, it falls short of ‘Smith for two reasons: a) eight mana is a ridiculous amount for any spell and b) it’s contingent on there being enough creatures in play to make it lethal. There’s little point to casting it otherwise, unless you have Nantuko Husk.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/05/6a/056aeff1d8b2461a7c4faa299c482b4667791ddc50862e03f800dc33f2ab3042.jpg)**Dragon Roost**  
 Bombs everywhere! Roost is even more expensive than Insurrection, requiring a grand total of thirteen mana for that first token, but if you spend nineteen mana for the second, the game’s over. Even still, it barely makes the top 10!

**Tephraderm**  
 It’s just a big beast, right? Well, not so fast. First off, 4/5 for five mana on its own is a very solid deal, especially when it’s going to take two or three blockers to kill it and they’ll all get theirs. Throw in the easiest of casting costs to abuse and you have a great all-around card. Just try not to Slice and Dice with it in play: It’ll live, but it’ll hurt. Ah forget it, take the pain, a 4/5 after Slice and Dice is hard to beat.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/85/fa855dc3ad9c9522fcd7a57d94ed4968b6165c4880f9b360cfc83808e9dfa8b6.jpg)**Shock**  
 Cheap, efficient kill. It’s that which often separates the bad decks from the good, and in this format, it’s deadly. If you can play a creature on turn two and morphs on turns three and four along with Shock with your extra mana, you’re going to find that it’s really difficult to lose. It’s the ability to cast it the same turn as another spell that makes Shock so strong.

**Solar Blast**  
 There’s a gap here, but Solar Blast is still an excellent card, in both of its capacities. The cycling isn’t as potent as that of Death Pulse, but it makes up for that in its ability to go to the head when you’re just a few points from victory. The fact is, though, when compared to Shock, you’re paying three extra mana for one more point of damage.

**Lavamancer’s Skill**  
 If you’re blue-red, Skill falls right behind Sparksmith in the common order and some players advocate forcing that color combo from the start, but I prefer to leave my options open and Skill just isn’t very good in other decks. It’s about here on the list that it’s worth the risk.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0d/3c/0d3c35f545ee7a68e0e8f201dbaee89d181e6a3ff8e076c40fef8fbb77c03891.jpg)**Gratuitous Violence**  
 A very powerful card, its cumbersome casting cost and reliance on your creatures surviving are reason enough for it to fall behind cheaper, more efficient spells. That said, be it flyers or fatties, your men are going to be very tough to beat in a race. If you want fun, try it with Goblin Sharpshooter.

**Words of War**  
 Never card advantage, against some decks, Words of War doesn’t need to be. If you can get ahead in the race for control, you aren’t going to lose with this in play, but when you’re behind, it’s best not to bother paying that activation. Remember, if you opponent uses preventative measures on the creature you’ve targeted, you can re-activate, choose a new target and not waste your turn. It’s a replacement effect.

**Chain of Plasma**  
 If Chain of Plasma cost one more mana, it would move way down this list, but in a color without many two casting cost spells, this is an efficient one and if you cast it turn two, there’s seldom a good target to return it to. 

**Thoughtbound Primoc**  
 It’s obviously not too good against blue players (I’d suggest siding it out against them), but against everyone else, it’s just so efficient and when you start out red, you have a decent chance of being blue anyways. Once you have the Primoc, lean a little more forcibly towards that option.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6e/2a/6e2a78952e26b49b5a1b5f32a3aa5ecf0b6264b9c1aa0c5c9069330a31460b6f.jpg)**Skirk Commando**  
 No, Craghorn is not better. No, people do not always block and if they do, there are ways of disabling their ability to do so. Give your Smith a goblin and get your card advantage even without the 1/1… seems pretty good to me.

**Goblin Machinist**  
 Providing an extraordinarily large posterior, the Machinist turns into… well… a machine in the late game, hard to kill, hard to survive. If you have nothing better to do with your mana, activate it just to learn the order of your library.

**Erratic Explosion**  
 Use your head when drafting it. If you already have a few one-drops you plan on playing, pass it along. If you get a couple and want them to be effective, choose higher casting cost spells. Best in red-green.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/59/9c/599cfb7f97e9d74309c4266376785845c03980572c70f511a2cdb0df78a81325.jpg)**Blistering Firecat**  
 I laugh when people say the Firecat isn’t very good in Limited. How can it be bad? Forgetting the Searing Flesh effect for four mana, it either trades with a very large man or does so with a small one while also dealing five to the head. Seems pretty good to me…

**Pinpoint Avalanche**  
 It’s nice that it’s an instant, nicer still that it deals four, but Pinpoint Avalanche is very slow, often killing a creature that costs less and was picked later. That said, it’s the closest thing red has to a bomb solution.

**Searing Flesh**  
 This game is a lot easier when you only have to do thirteen to win. The trick is surviving long enough to do so, but with morphs trading left and right, that’s seldom a problem.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5e/ce/5ecef9b2615cd9673efd39a8164baf127e957c44522f56e5dd79c48dfa4a8073.jpg)**Avarax**  
 It loses some value with the coming of *Legions* and the going of that third *Onslaught* pack, but Avarax is still a solid citizen. Haste is a strong ability, making the card the equivalent of a turn faster, and if the board happens to be clear when the beast is roaming free, things get ugly fast.

**Snapping Thragg**  
 I think I like the Thragg more than most, despite being voted “least likely to be played as a morph” among his class. Just pay five and make your opponent cope. A lot of the time, they won’t be able to.

**Battering Craghorn**  
 While it’s no Commando, the Craghorn is still pretty good, possessing the potential to hold off opposing armies, despite those rickety legs. Always a strong choice for your deck.

**Embermage Goblin**  
 For the same reason Lavamancer’s Skill isn’t that great in the format on a non-wizard, the Goblin is a bit of a disappointment without a second and gambling on another one is a lot like playing against the house; it’s a bad bet. It’s always playable, just understand you aren’t getting anything special.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/78/c8/78c8a081d62d56eed8c5b7a91b65422dcf69db2c297ef5f3d02ef2eba946cc49.jpg)**Skittish Valesk**  
 Moves up quite a bit in the blue-red deck, where beef is a scarce commodity and the Valesk provides it. Don’t take it too high in red-green, you’ll want to use your mana on morphs that don’t revert to form.

**Goblin Sledder**  
 This little guy is deceptively powerful, protecting your creatures through threat more than action. Their place in your deck improves the Commando so much, because your opponent usually won’t block the morph, then once face up, the 2/1 can be sacrificed for a bonus. We call that synergy on the Pro Tour.

**Charging Slateback**  
 Just a big dumb guy. Doesn’t like to help out at home, but it sure hits hard. I like hitting hard. It often ends the game quickly.

**Threaten**  
 Some people have had better experiences with this card than I have and rate it higher as a result. Amazing with Phyrexian Husk, it may be the only card that makes Accursed Centaur playable, but I think you would need four of them.

**Commando Raid**  
 Another card that I undervalue a bit, the Raid has a job and does it decently, but it requires that you have unblocked attackers. Doesn’t that usually mean you’re winning anyways?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/01/7f/017f3f1527e095a55dd498b1e17aa00366a8e3fe5c185f59b842b773c5a8a94a.jpg)**Thunder of Hooves**  
 This one keeps moving up for me and is almost an automatic main decker in my red-green decks. Once you’ve gotten a few three- and four-for-ones, you’ll understand why. I seldom cast it for more than two.

**Goblin Taskmaster**  
 Not my favorite main deck option, Goblin Taskmaster gives you the all important second goblin that makes Sparksmith truly vicious, while getting in a couple of points in the early going. This is the line you don’t want to cross.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bf/f5/bff53d990d5277240e0f1fc31049b8e72cccebb6a7080a541952ed6159642933.jpg)**Wave of Indifference**  
 Some people stand by this card, but I prefer just to run people over. Of course, things change when you have Skirk Commandos and Snapping Thraggs in your deck.

**Airdrop Condor**  
 2/2 for five huh? Too good to be true. I’ve never been a big fan of the 2/2 flyer for four mana, so you can understand my disdain for this little birdy. Solid in red-blue if you have Taskmasters and Illusions.

**Goblin Piledriver**  
 The good news is you can sell it for three ***Magic** Online* Event Tickets. The bad news is that’s because it’s only good in Constructed. 1/2 creatures don’t have enough impact on the table and if you do manage to attack with another goblin, your opponent’s going to have a bad morph to trade anyways.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cd/a3/cda3031b40dc797bf965e381295ce2cba4234b47084cb7670143e133fea8d787.jpg)**Spitfire Handler**  
 Easy to kill and ineffective on defense, the Handler can be played, but it’s never optimal. If I had ‘Smith and no other goblins, I’d make this sacrifice for the good of the deck.

**Goblin Pyromancer**  
 A great sideboard card against ‘Smith, the Pyromancer can be a lot of fun if you have Imagecrafters in your deck. I wouldn’t run it main too often though.

**Shaleskin Bruiser**  
 With apologies to Morgan Douglass, the card’s biggest advocate, the Bruiser generally doesn’t give you what you want to get for seven mana. If you’re desperate for a big man though, you could do worse.

**Aggravated Assault**  
 This card is a lot of fun once it gets going, but it so seldom does that I just don’t see it as worth having in a deck. If you have five thousand pinging effects… maybe.

**Reckless One**  
 often a good sideboard card in the red mirror, Reckless One should get better with *Legions*. You’ll have to give me a few weeks to figure out how much though.

**Dwarven Blastminer**  
 It’s a morph. Pretty simple. Oh, and it gets rid of Contested Cliffs.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d0/71/d071a22500ab5795b4515d13a3a236d05f96811825aa2e859377840c88b82c6f.jpg)**Crown of Fury**  
 I liked the Crown at first, but time and again it proved either futile or an enabler of card disadvantage. Still a solid choice in white-red, the Crown gains a lot if you happen to have Commandos, Thraggs or a Butcher Orgg, though really, at that point, YOU HAVE A BUTCHER ORGG.

**Fever Charm**  
 I don’t like maindecking Fever Charm unless I have two Imagecrafters, but this is obviously one of those sideboard cards you really want to have. Please don’t play turn two Taskmaster and give it haste. Someone did that against me once… I somehow managed to pull that one out.

**Lay Waste**  
 It cycles, it deals with the almighty Cliffs and occasionally, Lay Waste locks your opponent in the depths of mana screw. 

**Flamestick Courier**  
 The weakest of the Couriers, the targets are tiny, you can’t afford a decent creature’s casting cost and the activation cost and it’s just not very good. This is the beginning of the not-good-enough-to-play-just-to-have-a-goblin-to-support-Sparksmith line up.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e3/49/e349a3091b9355715a2f8f5f076575c4204b5daf2661cebcb1f7f4ef0fe97975.jpg)**Nosy Goblin**  
 People, don’t just randomly activate Nosy Goblin when your opponent has a morph and eighteen untapped lands. It forces me to involuntarily bash my head against the wall several times. And that hurts.

**Spurred Wolverine**  
 The YMG guys seem to like this card in their red-green decks, but we all saw how they did in Chicago. Sealed deck huh? Maybe you need some card re-evaluation (I’m going to get it for this one).

**Aether Charge**  
 Becomes playable with eight or nine beasts, but still isn’t optimal. Five mana for a card that doesn’t interact with the field of play is a lot.

**Custody Battle**  
 I guess if you fear the turn two ‘Smith, you can bring the Battle in, but please don’t put it in your maindeck. More often than not, it just doesn’t do enough because of the choice it gives your opponent.

**Skirk Fire Marshal**  
 Fire Marshal Bill’s a good man, but he’s not cutting it as a Limited card. If you get five or six Slice and Dices though…

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f1/db/f1dbd5099ffd533c566b2f81bd4499af827b689f28626d00d75697215a12adda.jpg)**Grand Melee**  
 It looks powerful, but I’ve never made it work. The advantage is that your opponent has to abide by its dictums first.

**Goblin Sky Raider**  
 Evasion is good, sure, but a power of one with no activation ability simply means it isn’t doing enough.

**Kaboom!**  
 That’s the sound your hopes of winning will make when you choose to play it.

**Risky Move**  
 Oh, man… far too many jokes for this space…the internet has only so much room.

**Mana Echoes**  
 Excellent with Sliver Queen. JUDGE!

**Skirk Prospector**  
 The one time I played this was in a monored deck I drafted in a practice draft in Chicago. I laughed and laughed and laughed and lost.

**Brightstone Ritual**  
 In honor of Robert Bayardo, this card stays out of the cellar.

**Break Open**  
 Of course, even Robert knew not to play this one.

Have a good week.
