One of the more interesting decks I saw from RPTQ Weekend was Victor Yee's Mardu Planeswalkers. This deck brought back some memories for me because I played a very similar deck at Pro Tour *Khans of Tarkir*. While the deck was really fun to play, it did have a major flaw: The deck was great at keeping the game under control, but wasn't so good at finishing a game in a timely manner.

If you take a look at this list, it's pretty low on win conditions. [Sarkhan, the Dragonspeaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sarkhan%2C+the+Dragonspeaker) aside, the win conditions it does play aren't exactly fast ones. Things have changed a lot in the past six months. We are in a new timeline and dragons are now ruling Tarkir. Mardu Planeswalkers are actually a really well positioned metagame, full of five- and six-mana Dragons.

[Silumgar, the Drifting Death](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silumgar%2C+the+Drifting+Death) is one of the hardest cards to remove from the battlefield right now due to hexproof. With Esper Dragons being one of Standard's top decks, it makes spells like [Crackling Doom](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crackling+Doom) pretty appealing. Typically, an Esper player will tap out for a Dragon on turn six, meaning that [Crackling Doom](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crackling+Doom) is pretty easy to resolve. Besides the three mana edict, this Mardu list is full of hard-to-deal-with Planeswalkers that gain you card advantage every turn, giving you inevitability against a control deck.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Crackling+Doom)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crackling+Doom)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sarkhan%2C+the+Dragonspeaker)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sarkhan%2C+the+Dragonspeaker)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sorin%2C+Solemn+Visitor)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sorin%2C+Solemn+Visitor)

The options for creature removal in this deck are great. [Hero's Downfall](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hero%27s+Downfall) and [Bile Blight](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bile+Blight) are some of the most powerful spells in Standard and a great reason to play black, and [End Hostilities](https://web.archive.org/web/20150506000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=End+Hostilities) is good at sweeping a horde of Goblin tokens. It made me sad that Mardu Midrange hadn't seen much play since Pro Tour *Khans of Tarkir* as it was one of my favorite decks, and it's good to see it climb back to a reasonable place in the metagame.
