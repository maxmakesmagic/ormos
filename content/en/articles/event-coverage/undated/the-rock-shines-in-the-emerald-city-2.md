**Ernie Marchesano is the Grand Prix Seattle Champion!**

Several different players commented before the tournament that they thought anything was viable in this format, with the exception of maybe Rock or Tog. Those same players were watching the finals between Ernie Marchesano with Rock and Taylor Putnam playing his aggro Tog deck. Ernie took down the match in two games after [Cranial Extraction](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cranial+Extraction) took out his opponent's [Psychatog](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Psychatog)s and his [Volrath's Stronghold](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volrath%27s+Stronghold) prevented Taylor from being able to implement his [Brain Freeze strategy](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brain+Freeze+strategy).

Before facing off against Taylor, Ernie dispatched the tournament's top-seeded player in Grant Struck and Goblin-wielding Max McCall. The Philly native was an amateur before he crossed the threshold of the Top 32 this weekend. In addition to his $2,400 payout for his victory this weekend, Marchesano will receive an additional $1,500 as the tournament's top amateur, bringing his payout just a hundred clams shy of $4,000.

Finalist Taylor Putnam was one of three non-amateur players in the Top 8 and his [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush)-a-Tog deck served him well as he took down Tsuyoshi Fujita and Shuhei Nakamura -- each for the second time this weekend -- before falling to Ernie. Fujita's deck was one of the most buzzed-about decks of the tournament. It was a super-fast [Sneak Attack](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack) deck that featured four [Blazing Shoal](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blazing+Shoal)s to ensure [Dragon Tyrant](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Tyrant) was legal every time it sneaked into play.

The Top 8 was a nice cross section of Extended decks with two Goblins, two Rock variants, [Sneak Attack](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack), Tog, [Aluren](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aluren), and Cephalid Breakfast. In the end, nothing beat Rock -- or at least Ernie's version of the once-thought extinct Extended staple.

## Sunday, March 6, 6:18 p.m. - Quarterfinals Recap

The gigantic hall was split into two large groups of people. Half of the players were playing in the twilight rounds of the PTQ, or vying for an unopened booster of Summer Magic.

The other group was watching the top 8, and specifically, the Tsuyoshi Fujita/Taylor Putnam match. Tsuyoshi's blisteringly fast [Sneak Attack](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack) deck was certainly the talk of the tournament. More people were gathered around that table than the other three matches combined, perhaps hoping to see another fabled Turn-1-Kill.

![Psychatog](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Psychatog)

Things started out well for Tsuyoshi. An early [Sneak Attack](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack) resolved when Tsuyoshi had the [Desperate Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desperate+Ritual) to pay for Taylor's [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak). A [Gamble](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gamble) was played fetching a [Serra Avatar](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Avatar), but held off by Taylor's untapped [Psychatog](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Psychatog). Unfortunately for Tsuyoshi, Taylor played another 'tog, which allowed the first to start swinging in. Tsuyoshi kept drawing mana sources, and soon enough the Togs were lethal.

Taylor Putnam-1 Tsuyoshi Fujita-0

The other big story of the tournament had to be John Ripley. John just missed out on 3 byes by losing in the quarters of the Grand Prix Trial. John then snuck into Day 2 at exactly 64th place, one away from the cutoff (sorry Mr. Saunders). From starting out dead last in standings, John clawed his way all the way up to top 8, where he faces off against the other Goblin deck, piloted by Max Mc[Call](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Call).

Game 1 was an interesting affair. John started off with the fabled Turn 1 [Aether Vial](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aether+Vial), which led into cheap goblins, but no hand re-fillers. Max traded the little red ones, then played Turn 3 Warchief and Turn 4 [Siege-Gang Commander](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege-Gang+Commander). John was forced to [Sparksmith](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sparksmith) the Commander, costing himself 6 life in the process. Another necessary [Sparksmith](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sparksmith) activation brought John to 5. Max played his 2nd Siege-Gang, John had no answers.

The second game was quite similar, and seems to be a common theme with the Goblin deck mirror. How much life are you willing to lose to get card advantage? John again had an early start, where Max was stuck on too few lands. Gems like Siege-Gangs, Ringleaders and Matrons waited anxiously in Max's hand, hoping and praying that Max could rip three mountains IN A ROW. Max did exactly that, and once again John found himself out of gas. Max's Ringleader for 4, followed by the double Warchief and double Siege-Gang was enough to earn the 2-0

Max Mc[Call](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Call)-2 John Ripley-0

In the second game of the Fujita/Putnam match, Tsuyoshi took a rough beat when his [Gamble](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gamble)d for [Sneak Attack](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack) immediately hit the bin. Didn't they ban [Entomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Entomb)?

With a [Foil](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foil) in hand, Taylor felt confident casting his [Psychatog](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Psychatog) on turn 3. Tsuyosi kept drawing mana sources, accumulating enough to hard cast [Dragon Tyrant](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Tyrant). It was countered of course, and a [Fact or Fiction](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fact+or+Fiction) later, Tsuyoshi's popular deck was retired for the evening.

Taylor Putnam-2 Tsuyoshi Fujita-0

Elsewhere in the top 8 was Ryan Cimera([Aluren](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aluren)) versus Shuuhei Nakamura(Cephalid [Life](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Life)). Ryan had lost earlier in the tournament to Shuuhei, and was not feeling confident about his chances. Shuuhei was feeling confident, which the Japanese certainly had reason to do this weekend.

Ryan and Shuuhei both kept their openers. Ryan started off with a double Therapy on Shuuhei, but had very few actual combo pieces in hand. Shuuhei's had was excellent enough to shrug off the two Therapies. The [Illusion](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Illusion)ist on turn 3 stuck, and on turn 4 Shuuhei had a large graveyard, a large Ghoul, and a Game 1 win.

The last quarterfinal matchup was Grant Struck versus Ernie Marchesano. Both were playing Rock decks, but with vastly different styles. Grant was with "MaceyRock", a much more aggressive version of the Green/Black deck, packing [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor)s and efficient 3 drops. Ernie's version was the controlling build, with multiple Deeds and Recurring [Eternal Witness](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eternal+Witness)es.

It's generally agreed that the MaceyRock version is better suited to this Extended format. However, Ernie's control version is absolutely brutal against Grant's aggressive type. Recursing [Pernicious Deed](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pernicious+Deed)s and [Smother](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smother)s is a rough situation for Grant.

Game 1 went according to type. Ernie had the [Smother](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smother)s for early [Birds](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Birds) and Elves. A [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor)ed [Mongrel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mongrel) from Grant got some hits in, but traded with Ernie's [Ravenous Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravenous+Baloth), killing Grant's last threat and erasing some of the damage in the process. Ernie started getting silly with [Eternal Witness](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eternal+Witness)es, and Grant soon had to pack it in.

Ernie Marchesano-1 Grant Struck-0

The Nakamura/Cimera matchup was far more fun to watch than two G/B decks siding in Edicts, so over there I went. The interesting thing about this matchup is how far fewer turns both players take than other matches, and how each of those turns take so much longer.

![Aluren](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Aluren)

Cimera was looking in trouble in the second game, with Nakamura with a number of cards in hand and a [Shuko](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shuko) on the table. Cimera simply had one [Cavern Harpy](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cavern+Harpy) in hand. In a nice move by Ryan, he proceeded to draw [Aluren](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aluren) and [Raven Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Raven+Familiar) as his next two draws, winning a game that did not look favorable.

Ryan Cimera-1 Shuuhei Nakamura-1

The Marchesano/Struck match ended with the sound of many, many of Struck's creatures dying under a hail of [Smother](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smother)s and Deeds and Edicts. Ernie's 4th [Smother](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smother) of the game earned a wry chuckle from Grant. With Grant out of threats, Ernie was free to swing in with a Witness and a Baloth, ending the match 2-0

Ernie Marchesano-2 Grant Struck-0

The final game of the quarterfinals was over in true Extended fashion. Cimera double mulligans into a fairly strong hand. In his 2nd turn, he has enough tutors to assemble the entire combo, assuming Nakamura gave him the time. Shuuhei did not however.

Shuuhei's game 3: Turn 1 Vial Turn 2-[Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm), end step put a Nomads en-kor into play Turn 3-Tap Vial for [Illusion](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Illusion)ist, mill library, play [Exhume](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Exhume) and win.

Shuuhei Nakamura-2 Ryan Cimera-1

Turns played by Shuuhei in the entire 3 game match:13 Turns played by Ryan in the entire 3 game match:12

## Sunday, March 6, 6:33 p.m. - Semifinals: Taylor Putnam vs. Shuhei Nakamura

Taylor Putnam is developing something of a reputation as an APAC slayer. His [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush)-a-Tog deck is well-equipped to deal with the fast, mana tight combo decks like Cephalid [Life](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Life) and Sneaky-Go because of his "free" [Foil](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foil)s and [Cunning Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cunning+Wish) targets like [Coffin Purge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coffin+Purge). The young man from Calgary, Alberta in Canada had already dispatched three of Japan's best on the weekend. On Day One he defeated Shuhei Nakamura and Masashi Oiso -- both playing the Cephalid [Life](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Life) deck -- and today he defeated Tsuyoshi Fujita twice.

![Coffin Purge](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Coffin+Purge)

When questioned about his match-up yesterday, the finalist from Pro Tour Columbus sighed and shook his head. "I went to [Reanimate](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reanimate) and he [Cunning Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cunning+Wish)ed for [Coffin Purge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coffin+Purge) and [Purge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221003210753/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Purge)d."

Taylor smiled at the memory, "Yeah we played yesterday."

Shuhei sighed and shook his head again as he had to send back his opening draw on the play. Although he had many of the puzzle pieces he needed to assemble for a quick win he only had one land -- which is why the rest of his hand looked so good, of course. He pondered his next six and decided to keep. Taylor stuck by his opening seven.
