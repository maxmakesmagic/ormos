Our last edition introduced *Eighth Edition* in a first pick-first pack situation. With several playable black cards and solid representation from three of the other colors, we will explore color preference based on the format and the pack in question. No card dominated the public vote. Death Pit Offering received about a quarter of the votes, edging out Phantom Warrior, Gravedigger, and Trained Armodon. Unsummon, Deathgazer, and Hill Giant also received a relevant number of votes. Opinions varied in the professional poll as well.

**Kai Budde**: I would take Phantom Warrior. I would not take Death Pit Offering because there are three playable black cards in the pack, and black is pretty weak in general.

**Antonino de Rosa**: Gravedigger and Death Pit Offering are probably the best cards in the pack but the lack of green cards in the pack and the sheer power of Trained Armodon would make me lean toward taking it. Taking a black card is a gamble since the pack has three good black cards. The other option is to take Hill Giant or Phantom Warrior. The problem with those cards is that they are not first-pick quality and you're only picking them because you will hopefully not fight with your neighbors. Trained Armodon is the way to go.

**Bryan Hubble**: Any experienced drafter will realize the immediate dilemma upon opening this pack. You've got very few real power cards and a really nice playable black rare, along with three more playable black cards in the pack. If you pick the Pits and pass all that black, you're sending mixed signals. Suppose the three players to your left each pick one of these black cards in the belief that the players to their right don't prefer this color. Come pack two, you've gotten yourself into a sticky situation.  
 Now compound that possible situation with the chance that a number of players to your right could also have gotten themselves into black early. Since you don't know any of the players around you or their color preferences, this could be a likely possibility. If you stick with your first pick Death Pits your deck will indeed be the pits. So what's the correct pick here? Quit being paranoid, just pick the Pits! So what if you're passing all that black; it won't hurt you until pack two and that's why you choose a second color. The rest of the nonblack cards in this pack aren't too great to begin with, so you can always hold out and see what comes your way. If nothing great comes along on the black side, you can abandon your first pick with no regrets. But let's say you get passed really good black from here on out. You've set yourself up for pack three as well, and you'll be picking all those great black cards while the people to your left that also went black will only get your dregs. In effect by running the old 'pass and cut' on black you've made the decks to your left a lot weaker and therefore will have a much better chance of winning the draft.

**Ken Krouner**: There are three questions about this pick: Is Death Pit Offering good, can we effectively cut off black if we take it, and are there any other cards that measure up to Gravedigger? I believe the answers are yes, no, and maybe. DPO is good for sure, but how good I don't know. Any card in which you can draw it and have it be dead is questionable. Of course land falls into this category, so that isn't a huge strike against it.  
 The next problem is can black be cut off if you take the Gravedigger or the DPO. This could be difficult. Since even Deathgazer is considered good by some people, it is very possible the two people to your left will take black cards making the cut off tough. The next two best cards are Trained Armodon and Hill Giant. Green is probably the worst color in 8-8-8 and this Hill Giant is not the powerful piece of removal you want to start going into red for.  
 Thinking about all these things, I would say take DPO. It is the most powerful card in the pack, and there is a chance someone to your left will grab the Hill Giant in an attempt to cut red. 

**Brian Kibler**: The two most powerful cards in this pack are Death Pit Offering and Phantom Warrior. I haven't had a chance to play with 8th Edition in Limited very much, but 2/2 evasion creatures for three mana are good in almost any format, and DPO was one of the most underrated cards in Masques Block, with the potential to dominate any given game completely. While the Offering is likely the more powerful card, the presence of Gravedigger in the pack - and the lack of any other really relevant blue cards from a signaling standpoint - would lead me to pick the Phantom Warrior.

**Matt Ranks**: The most powerful card in this pack is obviously Death Pit Offering. Such diverse responses cement the idea that this choice will boil down to determining the correct color rather than the correct card. In this type of decision, the first step is to narrow the field down to one card of each color. Phantom Warrior is easily better than Unsummon. Provided that you are amply devoted to blue, Phantom Warrior is an even better aggressive threat than cards such as Wind Drake or Diving Griffin, all of which are better than Unsummon themselves. The Warrior is a two-power threat for three mana that can only be stopped by a removal spell. It's everything you want a Wind Drake to be, but succeeds where the Drake fails against fliers or green spiders. The ability to block opposing fliers is not nearly as important as the assurance that the card will perform its primary task. While Unsummon represents a great deal of utility, it does not directly kill a creature and you should be taking the best creatures over it.

Deathgazer is strictly worse than Gravedigger, which has more potential power and greater consistency. Deathgazer's cost:stats ratio make it too slow to be an aggressive threat, limiting it to a wall against large non-black, non-flying creatures. Gravedigger will shine against creature removal or creature trades, which are much more consistent needs. Gravedigger can even play Deathgazer's role against a green deck by recouping the card lost from a double block. Unsummon, Boomerang, Raise Dead, or even another Gravedigger abuse its triggered ability even further, while Deathgazer will always be average. Gravedigger and Death Pit Offering are closer in power, but DPO is better. Gravedigger offers more consistent power, but DPO can be far better and its cost is more manageable than it seems at first glance. If you have DPO in your hand, you can either trade or slow play creatures as appropriate. To take this one step further, if you have DPO in your deck, you will be more prone to trade creatures or slow play ineffectual ones. DPO can be utterly useless if drawn at a late stage in the game, but this same problem plagues another Magic staple: land. Despite its heavy cost and the susceptibility to enchantment removal, +2/+2 to all of your creatures is worth the risk and it is the best black card in the pack.

Once left with Death Pit Offering, Phantom Warrior, Hill Giant, and Trained Armodon, you should examine the merit of each color based on this pack and the Eighth format. With an abundance of black and a shortage of white in the pack, it is likely that the second pack will bring the opposite. This is obviously a drawback in taking DPO, but as Noah Weil points out, it is also a deterrent where the Armodon is concerned. Moving into white is more likely than normal here, and green-white should be avoided if possible. Trained Armodon is also a suboptimal way to start drafting green, as it requires a heavy color commitment while trailing Nantuko Disciple, Spined Wurm, Craw Wurm, Giant Growth, and Giant Spider in power. It does not provide the sheer size, utility, or flying prevention of these cards, all of which are more important than tempo in this format. Though Noah's theory on Trained Armodon led him to take Hill Giant, it would lead me to take Phantom Warrior. As Kai points out in his Eighth Edition primer, red and black both have several phenomenally good commons, but are very shallow afterwards. Since you know you're accepting this tradeoff to play black or red, you want an incentive such as Volcanic Hammer, Shock, Dark Banishing, or Vicious Hunger to make up for the declining card quality. Despite black's weakness in the format and presence in this pack, Death Pit Offering is still a temptation given its potential to win a game on its own. Nevertheless, the most consistent option is to take Phantom Warrior and go blue-white if the opportunity arises.
