### Grand Prix-Prague

![](http://www.wizards.com/grandprix/images/NEW_GP.jpg)

When traveling to Czech Republic, I did not expect an easy tournament. After all, Germany is just around the corner and the country is centrally located in Europe. However, I certainly did not expect to be a part of the largest Grand Prix ever held in Europe, or anywhere outside of Japan.

When the long registration lines finally cleared up, well over 900 competitors were signed up for this last Grand Prix feeding Pro Tour-Yokohama. Players from all over Europe came to play. Most of “the usual suspects” from Germany, France, the United Kingdom and Denmark were present, but so were plenty of players from all over Eastern Europe – Czech Republic, Slovakia, Poland, Russia, Hungary and Ukraine. Due mostly to the financial hardship of traveling to the west, we see few of those players in other Grand Prix around Europe – but it was easier and more convenient for them to attend in Prague, and they took full advantage of that, helping to establish it as a history-making tournament.

After nine grueling rounds of Swiss, only a handful of those players who lost twice made it into Day 2. Interestingly, most players with three byes missed out, while a number of amateurs with a 7-2 record got to draft on Sunday. This is a strong argument against those billing the bye system as too harsh – though perhaps the additional round simply diluted the value of a bye enough to make this happen.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a1/9d/a19d3728b65706dcf0f14c4a8aed1d2ad7ee57289dce17b87364b2385ac892d8.jpg)

Only two American pros crossed the pond for this event – Dave Williams and I. Both of us were in a tight spot. Williams was not qualified for Yokohama and this was his last chance to make it in. I was q’ed for Yokohama, but needed 2 pro points to ensure myself an invite to Nationals and Worlds. Unfortunately for Williams, he opened one of the worst sealed decks I have ever seen in any format, and failed to make Day 2. I was more fortunate, entering Day 2 with a 7-1-1 record and going 4-2 to place 13th, earning the two points I needed and ensuring my place on the Pro Tour for the next six months. South America was also well represented. Carlos Romao and Victor Galimbertti failed to pass the Day 1 gauntlet, but Argentina’s Jose Barbero placed 10th. 

The Top 8 featured Raphael Levy and Gabriel Nassif of France, Jelger Wiegersma of the Netherlands, Anton Jonsson of Sweden, and Thomas Gunderson of Norway. They were joined by David Jensen (Denmark), a pair of Austrian players – Stefan Jedlicka and the only amateur in Top 8 – Armin Birner. Austria has been featured a lot more prominently on the pro **Magic** scene recently, with players like Thomas Preyer, Markus Joebstl and Helmut Summersberger. With his first Grand Prix win, over Raphael Levy in the finals, could Stefan Jedlicka be on his way to joining them? Jedlicka has had some success on the Grand Prix circuit previously, but Yokohama will be the first Pro Tour he will attend.

### Coming Up: *Magic Online* Qualifier for World Championships

![](https://i.ormos.uk/33/b5/33b53ff2126311f6ae8d4fbf87ec929e8d1324dc3d3d556728bb7eaf788fef2f.jpg)

For the first time ever, one lucky player will be able to qualify for a premier event via ***Magic** Online*. In a controversial move, the DCI allowed one Worlds invite to be handed out online. While a lot of players are excited about the possibility of qualifying online, there is also a lot of skepticism regarding this tournament. A number of top players will be helping their unqualified friends. The common joke is that Kai’s mom will be competing in Worlds this year. 

Even with all the pro help one might get, it will be a difficult endeavor. There will be a total of thirty two qualifier tournaments this weekend, one starting every few hours. The Top 8 players from each of these tournaments will be eligible to play on Sunday. Each of these tournaments will allow up to 256 players to sign up – and I won’t be surprised if many of them are completely filled. On Sunday, up to 256 players will compete in the final tournament, the winner of which will receive an invite and paid airfare to Berlin.

### Coming Up: Event Horizons Invitational

One of the forefathers of the “semi-professional events” I’ve been discussing in this column, the Event Horizons Invitational is an annual tournament hosted by Texas tournament organizer Tim Weissman and his company Event Horizons. This is the fourth time the event will be hosted. It features a variety of creative formats. A number of top players receive invitations based on DCI rankings and Pro standings, but anybody can play if they win one of the qualifier tournaments.

The sixteen players have been selected, and the finals will take place over the course of this weekend. *Sideboard* will be covering the event (Brian-David Marshall will travel to Houston to do professional coverage), so expect to learn more about this tournament sometime next week.

### Coming Up: Academy Games Invitational

Formerly known as M’s Collectibles, Michelle Cove’s store in Ayer, MA has moved to a new, larger location and changed names. She is celebrating the Grand Re-Opening – by giving away $2500 in prizes! To ensure that the event is of interest to Magic community at large, a number of players have been auto-invited. Among them are Rob Dougherty and the rest of the core YMG crew, Osyp Lebedowicz, Mike Pustilnik, the OMS brothers, and many others. Hosted just a week before Regionals, this Standard tournament may help a number of those still deciding upon a deck to make their final choices.

Pros aren’t the only ones with the shot at the money. The Invitational will take place on Sunday, while on Saturday Academy Games will host a qualifier, where the Top 20 players will make it into the event. You can learn more [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20030425000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.magicsingles.com/articles/article032503invitational-list.html&origin=sideboard_wir_20030425). 

This event will also be fully covered on *Sideboard*. Toby Wachter will be writing the coverage due to show up next week.

*“At the JSS at Stoney Brook, I played Joshua in the 3rd round. He was playing ‘Tog and I was playing red-green with Living Wish. In game 3, he was at low life, so he cast Delusions of Mediocrity, raising his life from 8 to 18. I attacked for a few points and he flashed back Deep Analysis twice, returning to 8 life. He pretty much had the game if he used the 2 Edicts in his hand, but instead he chose to play it out the usual way 'Tog wins - via Upheaval.
