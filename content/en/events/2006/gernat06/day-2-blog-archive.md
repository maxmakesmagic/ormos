<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, August 12: 11:24 a.m. - Drafting with Harald Stein</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Harald Stein, one third of PT Charleston's fifth-finishing team &quot;Schere, Stein, Papier&quot;, saw his hopes of Top 8 melting like Terisiare under Freyalise's Thaw. He went into the <i>Coldsnap</i> draft with 18 points, but: &quot;I can go 1-2, maybe 2-1, but I don't think it's that good.&quot; The players said Harald was one of the more experienced drafters in the field, and his confidence while picking spoke to that. That being said, the draft did just not go the way he wanted.</p>&#10;      <p>Harald came into the draft gunning for a white weenie strategy backing it up with red. His plan would have worked, had the other drafters and the packs cooperated. As it were, the draft showed all signs of randomness - and this was on table one!</p>&#10;      <p>First pack, first pick saw Harald take a <a>Skred</a> over <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a>, <a>Boreal Druid</a>, <a>Kjeldoran War Cry</a>, Jötun Owl Keeper, <a>Balduvian Fallen</a> and a <a>Snow-Covered Swamp</a>. The red-white plan continued to look good in pick two, with a <a>Kjeldoran Outrider</a> over another War Cry, another <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a>, a <a>Boreal Griffin</a>, an <a>Ohran Yeti</a> and a <a>Surging Might</a>.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Skred" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Skred&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Pick three brought the first agonizing decision that Harald had to face: Do I take a second <a>Skred</a> or the <a>Aurochs Herd</a> that's still in there? <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, <a>Thermopod</a> and a <a>Snow-Covered Forest</a> just didn't seem as good, and he took the removal spell <a>Skred</a> over the finisher. Pick four presented him with yet another <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, a <a>Chilling Shade</a>, <a>Frostweb Spider</a>, <a>Brooding Saurian</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Plains</a> and a <a>Thermopod</a>. He took the 4/3 haste guy, which fits well into his planned strategy.</p>&#10;      <p>Then, pick five showed the first signs that the decision to stick with the plan might not have been the best. If <a>Chill to the Bone</a> isn't an incentive to go black, maybe a <a>Stromgald Crusader</a> is? No, <a>Snow-Covered Swamp</a> won this pick over the jump knight, <a>Grim Harvest</a>, and <a>Rimewind Taskmage</a>. Pick six brought an incredible array of black to Harald: <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a>, <a>Chill to the Bone</a> #3, <a>Zombie Musher</a> and <a>Herald of Leshrac</a>. Hello? At least two of those shouldn't be in the pack anymore in pick six. However, Harald decided to take <a>Kjeldoran War Cry</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>After the draft, he commented: &quot;I should have taken black. I didn't, I was too afraid it would dry up.&quot; He could have had <a>Blizzard Specter</a> in pick seven, too, where he took a <a>Snow-Covered Plains</a> instead, also missing out on <a>Boreal Griffin</a> and <a>Orcish Bloodpainter</a>. Pick eight had him shaking his head: <a>Into the North</a>, <a>Goblin Furrier</a>, <a>Stromgald Crusader</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Island</a> and <a>Goblin Furrier</a>. He takes - the Furrier. Fits his deck, but he was not happy about the draft at this point. The last picks of pack one brought <a>Survivor of the Unseen</a>, two <a>Frostweb Spider</a>s, a <a>Boreal Griffin</a>, a <a>Tresserhorn Skyknight</a> in pick 13 and <a>Ronom Serpent</a> in pick 14. Blue was clearly underdrafted at the table.</p>&#10;      <p>Pack 2, Harald opened <a>Deepfire Elemental</a>, <a>Squall Drifter</a>, <a>Greater Stone Spirit</a>, Phyrexian etchings and <a>Ronom Hulk</a>, taking the Elemental with a mighty shake of his head. Pick two, <a>Goblin Rimerunner</a> over <a>Darien, King of Kjeldor</a>, <a>Gutless Ghoul</a>, <a>Drelnoch</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Plains</a> and <a>Tresserhorn Skyknight</a>. Another Etchings was in pack three, where Harald took <a>Skred</a> #3 over <a>Phobian Phantasm</a>. The rest of pack two was unspectacular, although both black and blue were pretty much open. Harald passed a <a>Chill to the Bone</a> fifth, pick eight featured <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, <a>Deathmark</a>, <a>Zombie Musher</a>, <a>Ronom Serpent</a> and <a>Diamond Faerie</a> (Harald took the Chill).</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat06/stein.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>The plan didn't work the way he wanted, as Harald Stein let the Black slide past.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>He got two more <a>Kjeldoran Outrider</a>s, two more <a>Goblin Rimerunner</a>s, a fifth-pick <a>Snow-Covered Mountain</a> and a <a>Greater Stone Spirit</a> for his deck. Of note was the pack with <a>Dark Depths</a> and <a>Ronom Serpent</a>, both of which made it around the table. Harald had to decide in pick 12 which one to take and chose the Serpent.</p>&#10;      <p>In Pack 3, there was more of the same, only less black going around. Harald's first pick was a <a>Goblin Rimerunner</a>, showcasing how incessantly he stuck to his plan. The pack also contained <a>Phyrexian Snowcrusher</a>, <a>Ohran Viper</a>, <a>Frostweb Spider</a>, <a>Thermopod</a>, <a>Arctic Flats</a>, <a>Ronom Unicorn</a> and Fest of Flesh. An <a>Ohran Yeti</a> made his pile over <a>Highland Weald</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Plains</a>, another Rimerunner, another <a>Feast of Flesh</a> and <a>Earthen Goo</a>, which he shortly contemplated taking.</p>&#10;      <p>Pick three Harald looked at a <a>Ronom Hulk</a>. That's right, a <a>Ronom Hulk</a> that nobody took! And Harald also shipped it in favor of <a>Ohran Yeti</a>. Pick four, <a>Snow-Covered Mountain</a> bested another Rimerunner. And then, pick five, Harald's deck got just a tad better: He windmill-picked <a>Lovisa Coldeyes</a>. Rimerunner is a Warrior, as is <a>Goblin Furrier</a>, and they get boosted by the Berserker lady. Rimerunner #4 came pick six, then a <a>Squall Drifter</a> at seven, Rimerunner #5 at eight and the final Rimerummer #6 at pick ten. A twelfth pick <a>Balduvian Warlord</a> and a thirteenth pick <a>Goblin Furrier</a> (also a Warrior) complemented the deck.</p>&#10;      <p>Harald was not really content, although he did have the six Rimerunners, which helped him win the first match of the draft. The draft table clearly had many signs of the players not knowing the format. Harald thought he didn't have enough snow for his three <a>Skred</a>s to work correctly, with four Snow-Covered Lands. His three <a>Kjeldoran Outrider</a>s are slightly out of place because he doesn't really play enough white to make the work as they should, and they aren't snow. He hopes to clinch two matches to preserve his chances for Top 8, and the deck can do that on the strength of <a>Skred</a>s and Rimerunners. Regardless of his performance, the one thing he really wanted to say was: &quot;Many greetings to my awesome girlfriend Mara.&quot; And how can we not acquiesce that request? Looks like German player's girlfriends are closely following the coverages at home!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, August 12: 12:01 p.m. - Vot dos zis card do?</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Language shouldn't be a problem at a National championship. But since you can play where you live, make sure that you at least understand some of it. Amiel Tenenbaum, a Frenchman living in Germany, was not too happy when he realized that today's <i>Coldsnap</i> draft would be a &quot;Kälteeinbruch&quot; draft, played with German cards. Amiel hadn't drafted <i>Coldsnap</i> before and didn't know just what the cards exactly did. Yesterday evening, he was looking to the judges for help. But what should they do? Give English boosters to his draft table while everybody else was drafting German cards? That wasn't going to happen.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat06/amiel.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Frenchman Amiel Tenenbaum and the helpful translation guide.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>I took pity on Amiel and handed him my <i>Coldsnap</i> player's guide from the Fat Pack, which has a picture of every card in English. Amiel sat down yesterday evening and took a good look at the cards, and had the guide ready while playing the <i>Coldsnap</i> rounds this morning. &quot;Without it, I wouldn't have been able to understand my first pick&quot;, Amiel said, &quot;but I would have picked it anyway: It has lots of text, so it must be good, right?&quot; The card he was talking about was the blocker-switching, already confusing <a>Balduvian Warlord</a>, or rather the Balduvianischer Kriegsfürst.</p>&#10;      <p>Headjudge Philipp Schulz had allowed Amiel that guide: &quot;The alternatives would have been that he calls over a judge every two turns, or we have to have a judge extra for him to read all cards for him. As long as he uses the guide only for looking up <i>Coldsnap</i> cards, we're ok with it&quot;, he said with a smile. Amiel won his first round with that very first-picked Warlord, stating: &quot;I think it's a good card, now that I know what it does!&quot;</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, August 12: 1:13 p.m. - Don't look the wrong way</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat06/headjudge.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Headjudge Philip Schulz keeps watch over it all.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Drafting holds many perils. For one player, the icy wastes of Terisiare led to downfall, as he was caught peeking at his neighbor's cards during the <i>Coldsnap</i> draft. One of the floor judges caught him peeling and alerted a second judge to verify his observation. Both judges saw the player looking at his next man's cards repeatedly, and that lead to a disqualification from the tournament.</p>&#10;      <p>As headjudge Philip Schulz told me, the player admitted that he had been looking at the cards, but didn't think anything of it. He was informed that his actually falls under cheating. Headjudge Schulz: &quot;When you can see another player's cards during the draft, don't look that way. If he holds his cards so that you can't avoid it, call a judge and tell them.&quot; So once again, the old rule holds true: When in doubt, call a judge.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, August 12: 2:38 p.m. - Round 10: Amiel Tenenbaum vs. David Brucker</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Amiel had some trouble with the German cards, as featured before. Nevertheless, he stands at 16 points, while David Brucker has 18 points. Before the match, Amiel (who won the die roll), showed David the <a>Thrumming Stone</a> he wasn't playing. He just didn't have enough multiples. This event was David's third draft, and Amiel hasn't had much more experience than that.</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 1</h3>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/3a/27/3a27ae827251d4cb359ea718a6ffcb208148b4d07855a3b51e09a5590d778e89.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Amiel Tenenbaum bent over the player's guide, looking up Karplusan Minotaur.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Amiel won the die roll, and he kicked off the game with a <a>Bull Aurochs</a> and <a>Surging Might</a>. Ripple hit nothing but <a>Skred</a>, <a>Sound the Call</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Forest</a> and <a>Ohran Yeti</a>… pretty bad to lose. But Amiel kept it up with a <a>Karplusan Minotaur</a>, saying &quot;I don't know this card, but I like it!&quot;, before looking it up in the player's guide. The first coin flip went in his favor, and <a>Surging Flame</a> from Amiel took out David's <a>Boreal Centaur</a>, rippling away <a>Shape of the Wiitigo</a>. David gained two life with <a>Gutless Ghoul</a> and the Ghoul died as a blocker. <a>Chill to the Bone</a> killed the coin-flipping Minotaur. David had <a>Aurochs Herd</a>, but Amiel put down <a>Ohran Yeti</a> and played his second <a>Shape of the Wiitigo</a> on the Aurochs. That made David scoop.</p>&#10;      <p>Amiel Tenenbaum 1 - 0 David Brucker</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 2</h3>&#10;      <p>The Frenchman started off with a mulligan. &quot;This format is ridiculous&quot;, said Amiel, as he geared up for his second trip to Paris. Many Pros don't like <i>Coldsnap</i> draft because it has so many random effects, and the high number of multiples make the decks pretty strong without much skill necessary. At least that's the argument. Amiel kept his five, and David opened up with the Boreal couple of Druid and Centaur. The mana elf died to Amiel's <a>Skred</a> and the Frenchman put down <a>Balduvian Warlord</a>. Guess what? David had to pick it up and read it, to which Amiel offered the player's guide.</p>&#10;      <p>David's Centaur and Amiel's Warlord traded and Amiel replaced it with <a>Frostweb Spider</a> and <a>Sound the Call</a>. <a>Chill to the Bone</a> from David killed <a>Orcish Bloodpainter</a>, and he made <a>Gutless Ghoul</a>: The perfect complement to next turn's <a>Gristle Grinner</a>. Amiel pointed to the Ghoul: &quot;Combo!&quot; Staying alive became difficult for the Frenchman. David also had <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a> and <a>Resize</a>, against which Amiel's two <a>Sound the Call</a> tokens just went to the graveyard. <a>Thermopod</a> from the Frenchman didn't help, and Amiel scooped them up.</p>&#10;      <p>Amiel Tenenbaum 1 - 1 David Brucker</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 3</h3>&#10;      <p>Amiel was on the play with turn three and turn four <a>Sound the Call</a>s, David had <a>Krovikan Scoundrel</a> and <a>Garza's Assassin</a>. The Assassin took down a token, but Amiel had yet another <a>Sound the Call</a> plus a <a>Boreal Centaur</a>, bringing a potential 11 damage to the the table. David's <a>Gutless Ghoul</a> and <a>Krovikan Scoundrel</a> looked tiny in comparison.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat06/brucker.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>David Brucker heard the call and succumbed to its sound.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Amiel checked out his guide again and thought long and hard. What he was looking for, we don't know, but what he had was a <a>Thermopod</a> with haste. Two 4/4 Wolves and the 'Pod smashed into the red zone, and David double-blocked one token with both Ghoul and Scoundrel. That took care of one token, and <a>Chill to the Bone</a> removed the other one. The lifegain from the Ghoul canceled the <a>Thermopod</a> damage, and David recovered his Assassin to deal with <a>Thermopod</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Amiel kept up the pressure with <a>Ohran Yeti</a> joining his <a>Boreal Centaur</a>, and David at six life had to bring more than just <a>Bull Aurochs</a> and <a>Gutless Ghoul</a> to come out of this game alive. Nobody can resist the saucy opportunity to work &quot;trips to Paris&quot; into conversations, trash talk and all the other banter going on at a Magic tournament. But what actually is a &quot;mulligan&quot;? Here's what I found:</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Stew made with whatever's available&quot;, 1904, hobo slang, probably from a proper name.</p>&#10;      <p>So we all wish for our opponents to have left-overs stew at the beginning of a match?</p>&#10;      <p>The name is from Gaelic Maolagan, or Irish Maelecan, a double diminutive of mael = &quot;bald&quot;, hence &quot;the little bald (or shaven) one&quot;, probably a reference to a monk or disciple.</p>&#10;      <p>Mr. Mulligan, would you pray for me if I put some copper coins in your bowl?</p>&#10;      <p>The golf sense of &quot;extra stroke after a poor shot&quot; (1949) is sometimes said to be from the name of a Canadian golfer in the 1920's whose friends gave him an extra shot in gratitude for driving them over rough roads to their weekly foursome at St. Lambert Country Club, near Montreal.</p>&#10;      <p>Thanks, Canada! The extra try at getting a better hand of cards comes closest to this origin of mulligan. So whenever you have your next stew, think of that Canadian golfer monk who goes by the name of Mulligan!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="9"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, August 12: 6:15 p.m. - Round 13: Stefan Meewes vs. Maximilian Bracht</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat06/bracht3.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Bracht had designed last year's winning deck, with Ninjas, and has the chance to become the next Ninja champion.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>It was Sea Stompy, piloted by Stefan Meewes, against Erayo-Ninja, built and piloted by Maximilian Bracht.
