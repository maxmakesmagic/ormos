### 

Ever wonder what it's like playing on camera in Top 8 of a Pro Tour? Curious about the origins of a word like "mise"? Interested in learning more about how a major team prepares for a tournament, or what the pro players think about the metagame? [Just ask them!](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/11-14-ask-the-pros)

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 23, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f0/9f/f09f4d2a172231ed02bcfadb60e0264389ebd0a5d2bbaaf40ad6a927154a8f0e.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: What was the most interesting deck that you saw at Pro Tour-Houston?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Gary Wise</b>: I thought Angry Hermit II, designed by Tsuyoshi Fujita and played by the Dutch, Neil Reeves and Bob Maher was very clever and something of a deck building triumph. At the same time, I loved the Draco-Explosion deck. It just seems like winning with it would be so much fun! <b>Patrick Mello</b>: Draco-Explosion.<br><b>Tomi Walamies</b>: I saw a few players running some kind of a blue-green deck that used an enchantment to triple its mana. It seemed incredibly powerful and lots of fun.<br><b>Frederico Bastos</b>: Draco-Explosion! A friend of mine was talking with me at the <i>Onslaught</i> Prerelease about Erratic Exploding a Draco and we both laughed. Suddenly, there's a player at the Pro Tour winning money with it!</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 20, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f0/9f/f09f4d2a172231ed02bcfadb60e0264389ebd0a5d2bbaaf40ad6a927154a8f0e.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: Who is your Pro Tour nemesis? What's your record against them?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Eric Froehlich</b>: My arch-nemesis on the Pro Tour is Broken Jens Thoren. Not only did he steal my teammmate's nickname - the Silent Assassin - previously attributed to Kamiel Cornelissen, but he is 2-0 against me with the wins coming in the Top 8 of Pro Tour-San Diego and the Top 4 of Masters in Houston. The minimum amount of extra money I would have earned by defeating him those two times was $15,500. Additionally, he cost me a spot in the <b>Magic</b> Invitational this last time!<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: I think saying anyone but Ryan Fuller here would be a mistake. Ryan and I haven't played too many times, which is good, because things get really ugly whenever we're in the same room. We have… certain differences in opinion. The funny thing is that we've only played twice that I can remember, at 2001 Pro Tour-New York, where Ryan beat me as ABU did Potato Nation and the Sydney Invitational, where I beat him in Solomon Draft.<br><b>Patrick Mello</b>: Alexander Witt. He beat me in Houston and at Worlds recently. He even took me out in a PTQ, back in the day. I have not won a match against him yet.<br><b>Tomi Walamies</b>: I used to be 0-4 against Noah Boeken in Pro Tours and Grand Prix, but then I proceeded to maul the punk savagely at Worlds in Sydney. Mutilate is no good against Tireless Tribe.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 19, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c7/c1/c7c1fe5cf49f58117fbd931561f1b60bd3183ad6276cf758a8fd3c7d6702fe27.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: How much time does it take you to prepare for a Constructed tournament?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Eric Froehlich</b>: We begin working on Constructed formats right after the Limited Pro Tour is completed. Sometimes, we even start before. While this rarely includes hard testing, it does involve bouncing ideas off one another and trying to figure out the potential metagame before really digging into the format - this goes doubly for Pro Tours with unexplored formats. The hard testing period generally lasts about five or six weeks.<br><b>John Larkin</b>: It depends on what Constructed format it is and, if it is an explored format, whether there are any major changes to take into account. A Constructed format with an established metagame can be pretty easy to prepare for; the difficult thing is dealing with a brand new format, such as the new Extended, or Block Constructed. New formats require weeks and weeks of testing to establish the top decks and then to figure out what beats them.<br><b>Diego Ostrovich</b>: My team and I start working around a month and a half, sometimes two months before the tournament if all the sets are available.<br><b>Frederico Bastos</b>: It depends on the tournament and my time to playtest. There have been tournaments where I received a deck the day before and others where I have playtested for months.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 18, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/50/6a/506a81655d6f8e73a751a1459805e125bbc0f9a665920c3a74b8f7a7a16a7928.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: With <a>Morphling</a> not even making an appearance in the Top 8 of Pro Tour-Houston, what do you think the best creature in Extended is? </p>&#10;<p><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/fc/d3/fcd303e216c832a96e5657bf356203e657af86193465daa8f87aac71517049b4.jpg"><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Mike Long</b>: The best creature in Extended looks to be: <a>Morphling</a>. Don't count the guy out because he took tournament off. Next thing you know, we'll be wondering who is better than Kai. <br><b>Dirk Baberowski</b>: <a>Psychatog</a>.<br><b>Peter Szigeti</b>: Pound for pound, the best creature is <a>Psychatog</a>. However, it might not be utilized as it should be, because of the current metagame.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 17, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/d5/77/d577174465a6216c4498875eafb3da2b3211ea99f3f114f05b0965ac4275f693.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: Which deck had the most impact on its environment?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Kai Budde</b>: Sligh. Whenever it was around, it was good enough to make people dedicate both main deck and sideboard slots to beat it.<br><b>Mike Pustilnik</b>: Trix, before <a>Necropotence</a> was banned in Extended.<br><b>Mike Long</b>: Necro changed <b>Magic</b> forever in 1996. It was the unbeatable nemesis at Constructed's high water mark. Since draft has supplanted Constructed as the most popular format, I don't think this will ever change.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 16, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/24/ed/24ed5be5578a04d4bd33e38ab1834ea7e497fbcee8f490f096ac18a1ccfd9f54.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question: If you could go back into the past and stop any one card from being created, which card would it be?</b></p>&#10;<p><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/cd/e6/cde68f21f19dd707b0c9132a54a964f67481f3d62ca2d38be249b1cbe081e49f.jpg"><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Adrian Sullivan</b>: <a>Dark Ritual</a>. I can't think of how many stupid games happened because of this card.<br><b>Mike Long</b>: <a>Flametounge Kavu</a>, followed closely by <a>Ramosian Sergeant</a>. Both were bad cards, Flametounge certainly had the worst effect on the game as a whole, preventing lots of cool cards from seeing Constructed play and ruining as many drafts as appeared in. I personally hated the Sergeant, but that's just because I couldn't really figure out what it was. <br><b>Kai Budde</b>: <a>Mountain</a>.<br><b>Dirk Baberowski</b>: <a>Balance</a>. There's not one format, including all kinds of multiplayer formats, where <a>Balance</a> doesn't take ALL of the fun out of a game. In 500-card highlander multiplayer for example, <a>Ancestral Recall</a> doesn’t even come close to it in terms of power - whereas in any serious tournament format, they're both ridiculous. So, in that sense, <a>Balance</a> is the most broken card ever printed - it doesn't belong anywhere.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 12, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c7/c1/c7c1fe5cf49f58117fbd931561f1b60bd3183ad6276cf758a8fd3c7d6702fe27.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: Which rare do you want to open most in <i>Onslaught</i> sealed?</p>&#10;<p><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/7e/8c/7e8c3232208ff831970530dcc5354f223701975890c0faef7d45cac350835d3f.jpg"><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Mike Long</b>: Probably <a>Silklash Spider</a> or <a>Visara the Dreadful</a>. <table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>December 11, 2002</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/50/6a/506a81655d6f8e73a751a1459805e125bbc0f9a665920c3a74b8f7a7a16a7928.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: I think it might be a bit disturbing to play a <a>Shadowmage Infiltrator</a> against Jon Finkel or a <a>Meddling Mage</a> against Chris Pikula. Is it strange playing with cards created by Invitational winners? </p>&#10;<p><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/8c/79/8c790dcd10c74051fa2f0acabe8ab5ec8f131ad0fb459c58df3042fa8a656f1a.jpg"><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Mike Long</b>: I lost to a <a>Rootwater Thief</a> at Pro Tour-New Orleans and I can tell you, it was weird. It’s just a little bit of flavor that makes things occasionally more interesting.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
