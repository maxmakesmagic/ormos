### Foreword

Before we get into my latest article, I would like to clarify for some of you what I am doing. I have received a few emails that can all be categorized the following way: "I have sent you a bunch of decks. When are you going to look at them?" While I appreciate that you all want me to help with each and every deck, it simply isn't feasible. Here is how the process works: I take a quick look through the list of decks and find one that looks like it will make a good article. I then go over what is in the deck, and let that gel in the back of my mind. From there, I load it into **Magic** Online, acquiring all the cards I need, usually through trading. I spend between 8 and 16 hours playing the deck over the next 3 or 4 days, slowly coming up with improvements. After this, I usually spend about 2-3 hours writing the actual article. It general takes me a day to get somebody to proofread it. After that, I send it to *Sideboard*, where they must do their part. (Stuff like additional editing, converting to html...) As you can see, it takes me the better part of a week to complete an article. Considering that I have received almost a 1,000 emails so far, it would take me almost 20 years to get through them all.

### On to the Nantuko Shrine

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2f/43/2f43fd6c2d111feaef34a8c32112b77cbfb73f49a5dd3188d8fde2e416a5c85f.jpg)Ok now that we have that out of the way, we can proceed to my latest operation. This deck is a deck based around Nantuko Shrine, an interesting card that seems like it may be useful. I first heard about people trying to build Shrine decks about a week before Osaka. It was an interesting concept that I simply didn't have time to explore then, but that I packed away to try at a later time. I received four different submissions about Shrine decks, and here is one of the more interesting ones.

### Comments

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c1/ba/c1bac84c650a69747a4ca1911626fb86495e330316b83ab0996c7d0c5ecf3297.jpg)This deck is basically a Standard version of Frank Hernandez's Nantuko Shrine deck (posted here at the *Sideboard* under the [OBC: Week 3 article](https://web.archive.org/web/20020513000000/https://www.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=sideboard/strategy/sb20020308b)). Since there is a Standard tournament coming up in the area and I'm looking to have some fun, so I decided to create a Standard version of the "Scrambled Eggs" deck. Essentially, the deck uses a lot of cheap cantrip-esque effects to dig through the deck and fill up the graveyard. It then uses Nantuko Shrines to start pumping out squirrel tokens at an alarming rate.

I originally tried to make this deck with Chatters and Call of the Herds, and it worked fairly well then too, but the deck has a problem of coming to a complete stop when you hit land while running through the deck with your cantrips (hence why it only has 16 lands) so I replaced those flashback spells with more cantrips.

The deck has a very low mana curve (with 36 of the spells costing a single mana) and with the 12 blue cantrip spells, its mana base works well.

The deck does fairly well in testing, but it is extremely vulnerable to a variety of spells and creatures, which is why I'm here presenting you with this deck today. Any suggestions on how to improve the deck? The sideboard is a little off. The Mongoose I think do belong in there, but I'm unsure about the majority of the rest of it.

Frank's Diner

### Analysis

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/12/3a12c80ee6f3d6a8124dbef0789a1e918377f5dc221c39224bdddc479ab8ec2a.jpg)This deck is definitely a combo deck. Combo decks traditional work one of two ways. The first is to be extremely fast. These decks win on turn 1 through 4, sometimes as late as turn 5. Being this fast, there isn't enough time for a lot of decks to get set up to deal with them. The second way is to have a lot of redundancy in their design, coupled with a lot of defense to keep them alive long enough for the combo to go off. Our deck doesn't fall into category 1 as it can't make the Shrine until turn 3. On turn 4, it is just making its first creatures. It also doesn't fall into category 2. Almost all the cards are part of the combo, very few are defensive in nature, and there is no redundancy on Nantuko Shrine. You simply must find one, and if the opponent Duresses or Counterspells the Shrine, you are in trouble. You might be asking if it has some redundancy because of how many cards it draws. While this could be reasonable, combo decks need to have ways to get the combo. The card drawing is a standard part of combo decks. Overall, I am not that hopeful for the deck.

### Playtesting

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1a/27/1a275d71ffce5133383406a163500c71a6df53749dcec1e566754693f1ea0f07.jpg)I probably did more playtesting on this deck than any other so far. The original deck could beat monogreen players, and not much else. In addition to losing to Counterspells and Duresses, I also lost to Worship, Burn, Pernicious Deed, and after sideboards, enchantment removal. The first block of modifications I did, I shifted to a green base, and tried much the same as the comments mentioned. However, I gave it redundancy by adding Call of the Herd and Beast Attack. I also added Careful Study to help with the land flood issue. This stopped me from being reliant on the Shrine to win, slowed way down most of the creature decks, and seemed to help a great deal. I continued hoping to get a high quality deck. However, this line died when I realized that I needed to take out a Nantuko Shrine to make the next improvement. Since having the Shrine combo is the point of the deck, I decided this was unacceptable. Two big developments came out of this playtesting that would make it to the final deck. Both of them were found when the opponent played them against me. They were cards that I hadn't thought of on my own. The first was Temporal Spring. While I am not a fan of the Spring, it has the ability to deal with almost any permanent that the opponent plays. Cards like Worship were no longer as scary. Also, the fact that it went back on the top of the deck interacted well with the Shrine. I could draw the Springs, cast it, gain squirrels, and know exactly how many squirrels his next card would give him. Often, this was zero. The other card was the card that gave me direction for my second line of testing.

### Howling Mine

![](https://i.ormos.uk/38/e8/38e8c08f2831a936c603b0d9566bfb6a45498a83e941c10279ca92a3e307bbf9.jpg)So here I was, depressed that testing the deck was going so badly, when somebody played Howling Mine on me. Oh boy was that game fun. I think I decked myself, but it didn't matter, because I realized that Howling Mine definitely belonged in the deck. In the past, every time I needed to add more defense, I had to remove Eggs. The deck had slowly lost its card-drawing engine, and with it, the point of the Shrine slowly diminished. Also, even in the original deck, you could start up the engine, and die due to a large pocket of land that piled up in your hand. By adding the Mines, I would be assured that I would have lots of cards to play. I would be able to burn though land much easier, and the drawing power of the Mines would allow me to remove many of the Eggs as I would now have plenty of card drawing without them. The big question was if the cards that Howling Mine gave to my opponent be too much for me to handle.

### Playtest Session 2

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ec/97/ec977a710deb2083544847b97b3ee6f42953869803da7cb62643fbef8fa58705.jpg)Starting over from scratch, I made a new revision. Since I still needed some cantrips to fuel the Shrine, I decided to keep Opt, Sleight of Hand and Chromatic Sphere. Because these cantrips were blue, I took the attitude that the new deck would be base blue, and that the cantrips and the Mines would allow me to find the green mana for the Shrines. I then added some countermagic to help deal with all my opponent's answers to the Shrine, since they would definitely draw their important spells for the matchup from the Howling Mines. The cards I chose for this were Counterspell, Syncopate and Memory Lapse. Counterspell was obvious for its guaranteed removal. Syncopate worked well, as early Syncopates helped keep the opponent's graveyard from growing, limiting their use of my Shrines. Memory Lapse worked in much the same way. Although it still has the annoying effect of giving the opponent the same spell next turn, when you are giving them extra cards every turn of the Mine, you sort of expect them to be able to do something nasty. Also, if it stops a Counterspell, you often don't care that they draw it again, as long as the spell you just got through was Nantuko Shrine. Finally, there was a really cool interaction with countering you own spells late in the game. You could cast a spell with 3 copies in the graveyard, counter it, cast Opt, cast it again. In some strange situations, this allowed you to cast a specific spell multiple times to trigger the Shrines.

As the number of cantrips had fallen dramatically, I also needed to add a few more lands. This is in contrast to where I started out in Build 1, keeping the lands steady at 16.

Finally, the deck really needed a kill card to bring games to a crashing halt. This card had been used in many Shrine decks, so it wasn't particularly hard to find: Overrun. Yes, I realize that it requires a lot of green mana to cast. However, by the time you need it, you really should have the mana. This version seemed the most successful I had made to date, and although the Howling Mines sometimes helped the opponents too much, I found I really needed the cards it gave me.

### Sideboard

Having spent so much time on the main deck, my time left for the sideboard was very limited. However, fortunately for me, there was an obvious tenet that I need to follow for tuning the sideboard. Due to the effects of Nantuko Shrine, there should only be 4 spells in the sideboard, 3 with 4 copies and 1 with just 3. The scariest part of the sideboard I put together was its colors. Here are the cards I chose and why:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bf/70/bf709c1a49050926582279fb0351784321ab1b5adbbb074e94f68024d6f81381.jpg) **Creeping Mold:** I want something that can kill enchantments. I also need something to destroy Cabal Coffers. With very limited sideboard slots available, I took a hit on its speed, and thus the ability to deal with Opposition to gain both effects.

**Circular Logic:** For when I need to counter more spells. Bringing me to 16 counterspells, even with some of them substandard, my card advantage spells should get me high enough that I can fight head to head with counter decks.

**Call of the Herd:** A good general-purpose spell. Sometimes you need to take out the Howling Mines and this is a good filler regardless of the deck you are playing against.

**Compost:** If any spell in the sideboard loses a copy, it should be the one that doesn't get you tokens.

### Summary

This deck started out winning very few games. It has come along way without losing its theme. However, even with all the progress made, I would only rate this one as tier 2 at best. The original author noted that he built the deck for fun, and I hope that he still finds this one fun, as he will have to work a little harder when playing it. Should he be willing to take the extra effort, the deck will pay off in a much better win ratio. If not, then I hope that he can at least use the idea of Howling Mines to fuel his deck.
