It seems like the World Championship was just yesterday but Pro Tour *Fate Reforged* in Washington, DC, is already next week.

The format is *Khans of Tarkir*/*Fate Reforged* Booster Draft and Modern.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Karn+Liberated)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Karn+Liberated)

[Last year's winner](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/pro-tour-born-of-the-gods-coverage-events) was Shaun McLaren with his Jeskai control deck. Who will take the trophy this year?

This week, I would like to introduce some Top 8 decks from [Grand Prix Omaha](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-omaha-2015), the Modern tournament prior to the Pro Tour.

First off, Andrew Elenbogen's Red-Green Tron.

Once [Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Urza%27s+Mine), [Power Plant](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Urza%27s+Power+Plant), and [Tower](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Urza%27s+Tower) are in play, the power is yours. Usually, this starts with something like [Karn Liberated](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Karn+Liberated) destroying lands.

If your opponent begins flooding the board, [All Is Dust](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=All+Is+Dust) comes in handy. Although not possible on your third turn, [Oblivion Stone](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oblivion+Stone) is another choice.

The deck finishes off with utilizing its overflowing mana to call [Emrakul, the Aeons Torn](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Emrakul%2C+the+Aeons+Torn) from the [Eye of Ugin](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eye+of+Ugin).

Urza Tron has always been around, since it begins produce seven mana on turn three with green cards like [Sylvan Scrying](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sylvan+Scrying) and [Ancient Stirrings](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancient+Stirrings) played via [Chromatic Star](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chromatic+Star).

[Relic of Progenitus](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Relic+of+Progenitus) is a third cheap artifact that doubles as a cantrip. It matched well with the field full of [Treasure Cruise](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasure+Cruise)s at the time. Mainboard [Pyroclasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyroclasm) clearly illustrates what matchups the deck was prepared for.

Now might be the time to switch to [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout) for potential [Wild Nacatl](https://web.archive.org/web/20150126000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Nacatl)s.
