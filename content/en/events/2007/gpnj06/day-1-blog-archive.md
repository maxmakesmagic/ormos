    *by Noah Weil*
  

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, Nov 11: 12:50 p.m. - Sealed Deckbuilding with Mark Herberholz and Tomoharu Saito</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Noah Weil</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpnj06/saito1.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Tomoharu Saito</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Tomoharu Saito and Mark Herberholz are two pros who have consistently proven their talent with <i>Time Spiral</i> limited, most recently at Pro Tour Kobe. For the Grand Prix, these two find themselves with three byes, and deep in the running for player of the year. With Herberholz coming in at 40 Pro Tour points and Saito at 45, a big score here would go very far for taking the title.</p>&#10;      <p>Tomoharu and Mark both started their construction in the same way: verifying their registration sheets to their physical cards. Pretty smart, as they both know how disruptive it is to call a judge midway through deck construction. After the card pools checked out, the two pros got to work. Mark started off sifting through the White cards. Finding nothing to pique his interest, those got quickly became sideboard fodder. Green was a little more interesting with <a>Durkwood Baloth</a> and double <a>Gemhide Sliver</a> [<a>Gemhide Sliver</a> pic]. Black also looked like quality with <a>Strangling Soot</a>, <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a>, <a>Vampiric Sliver</a>, and a <a>Plague Sliver</a>. Red was the real winner though with some removal and slivers across the board, including the extraordinarily powerful <a>Sedge Sliver</a>. Suddenly Mark's deck took on a whole new tenor as he laid out every single sliver he opened across the table. </p>&#10;      <p>Tomoharu's deck nearly built itself. His red was excellent with great creatures and removal, including <a>Firemaw Kavu</a> and double <a>Lightning Axe</a>. His white consisted of <a>Witch Hunter</a> and nothing else, and his black was simply a blank. His only major decision was whether to pair red with green or red with blue. The green offered <a>Durkwood Baloth</a> and <a>Verdant Embrace</a>, but in the end it was just a collection of creatures. Blue, on the other hand offered flexibility in <a>Snapback</a>, <a>Wipe Away</a>, <a>Errant Ephemeron</a>, and a very sexy <a>Psionic Blast</a>. Interestingly, when Saito was sorting out his cards by mana cost, he put both bounce spells in the four-slot. An insight into how Tomoharu plays those cards? Tomoharu's final decision was <a>Eternity Snare</a> versus <a>Chromatic Star</a>. It was a close call, and Saito went back and forth for a good five minutes as he began to register everything else. Finally, with a declaration of &quot;sideboard&quot;, <a>Chromatic Star</a> made maindeck rank. The deck ended up looking very good, and Tomoharu felt confident going into the fray.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpnj06/herber1.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Herberholz focuses on the slivers</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Mark ended up dedicating his deck to the sliver theme. At this point <a>Plague Sliver</a> took the axe, but in the end Mark pulled off a surprisingly consistent-seeming sliver deck, impressive for sealed.</p>&#10;      <p>Herberholz: &quot;I like the deck. It was nice getting so many slivers that worked so well together.
