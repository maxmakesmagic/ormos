I tracked down Frank Karsten to see what he made of his card pool. Karsten is one of the top Dutch players and finally broke through the bubble to earn a quarter-final finish at Pro Tour Nagoya earlier this year.</p>&#10;      <p>Players register a card pool and then pass it to the player opposite, who then checks the deck has been registered correctly. Frank got to check a deck that had some very strong red including double Earthquake in the form of Ryusei and Jiwari, the Earth Aflame.        <a>Stampeding Serow</a>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>ARTIFACTS</b>&#10;        <br>&#10;        <a>Nine-Ringed Bo</a>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>For anybody wanting tips on how to build sealed decks it is worth following an approach similar to Frank's. First he went through the cards and laid out all the playables in each color. Then he divided those further into the good cards and the filler. In this case Karsten was clearly lumbered with an awkward sealed deck. The best rare was Reverence and that could hardly be classed as a bomb.</p>&#10;      <p>Karsten likes consistency so his aim was to try and find a two colour deck. With his pool this was difficult as each colour was barely reaching nine playables. He went back into the reject pile to try and scrape up some more possibilities. It's never a good sign when <a>Stone Rain</a> crawls into consideration.</p>&#10;      <p>Karsten decided to ditch the red as aside from a Sokenzan Spellblade, <a>Yamabushi's Flame</a> and Houndmaster there wasn't a lot going on.</p>&#10;      <p>He took the remaining four colours and lined up the creatures by mana cost to see if the curve would help. In this case the curve looked reasonable in all colours. Again not much help.</p>&#10;      <p>He tried building blue-white.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I have to play white if I want to go two colour, it has 12 playables,&quot; Frank decided.</p>&#10;      <p>He tried out green and black before moving back to blue. This seemed to be the most synergistic combination. The blue added fliers and a good curve as well as the Petals for card drawing that would allow him to best use cards like the Kitsune Bonesetter. This combination would give him a fairly good blue-white control deck with fliers and some useful combat tricks.</p>&#10;      <p>The last card to deliberate over was <a>Nine-Ringed Bo</a> or Oppressive Will. He looked over the Saviors decklist to see if the artifact would be better or worse with the addition of the new set. Although he considered the <a>Nine-Ringed Bo</a> more of a sideboard card he was worried that fliers like <a>Teller of Tales</a> might cause him problems, whereas with the pinger he could at least trade equally with one of his two power fliers. In the end he opted for the more general use card and took the Oppressive Will.</p>&#10;      <p>He felt the deck was underpowered, but would hopefully be consistent enough to give him the 3-2 record he needs to advance to tomorrow.</p>&#10;            <br>&#10;          </div>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, June 11: 5:22 pm - <b>Feature Match Round 4: Julien Nuijten vs. Jelger Wiegersma</b></b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Sometimes it can be a struggle to pick interesting feature matches in the early rounds. Today was sort of the same problem, but for different reasons. This all Dutch clash between the current world champion Julien Nuijten and GP juggernaut Jelger Wiegersma seems an obvious one. On the other feature match table there is an exciting all Belgian clash between Geoffrey Siron and Bernado Da Costa Cabral, while in the mass British Pro Sam Gomersall is up against Wilco Pinkster, winner of the largest GP ever in Paris last year.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpbolo05/b2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Nuijten, left, vs. Wiegersma</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Nuijten shipped back his first purely because of his outrageous mana base playing up. He kept the next six and busted out first an <a>Elder Pine of Jukai</a> and then an Ember Fist Zubera and a <a>Frostling</a>. Wiegersma curved out a <a>Cruel Deceiver</a> and then <a>Kabuto Moth</a>. His fourth turn brought in a <a>Honden of Night's Reach</a> and a sigh from Nuijten.</p>&#10;      <p>Nuijten equipped the Zubera with a <a>Manriki-Gusari</a> and charged in. Once the dust settled Nuijten had shifted the Moth for the loss of his Zubera and <a>Frostling</a>. He discarded a forest in Wiegersma's upkeep.</p>&#10;      <p>Wiegersma's deck appeared to have hit a big stall as he skipped a couple of turns without a play. Meanwhile Nuijten stepped up the offence with a <a>Skyfire Kirin</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Another turn passed with no play from Wiegersma as he stared at a hand full of blue spells and no island. He wasn't completely defenseless, a <a>Horobi's Whisper</a> with a <a>Soulless Revival</a> tacked buried the Kirin while simultaneously fetching back the <a>Kabuto Moth</a>. When he followed up with an <a>Island</a> and then a <a>River Kaijin</a> to stall up the ground it looked like he might take control of the game.</p>&#10;      <p>Nuijten was still up for scrapping. His Elder Pine was drawing a succession of lands to feed the black Honden and a <a>Pull Under</a> removed the annoying Kaijin. Nuijten attacked to put Wiegersma to 3 life. Then he summoned a <a>Hearth Kami</a> and wasn't too chuffed to see a <a>Strength of Cedars</a> go by as the Elder Pine fetched another land for him.</p>&#10;      <p>Wiegersma was back on the defensive, summoning a <a>Soratami Cloudskater</a> and leaving his troops at home.</p>&#10;      <p>Nuijten had to stay home also, and was now down to zero cards from the Honden.
