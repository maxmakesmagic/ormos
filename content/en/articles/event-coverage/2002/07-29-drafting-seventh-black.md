Please be sure to read [part 1](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/07-19-drafting-seventh-part-1) of this column first. 

I received a number of emails asking for a more detailed description of the commons. For example, a lot of folks did not care for the "bottom of the barrel" approach and wanted an explanation of how some of the cards ended up there. Thus, I am going to go with a card-by-card approach for this article.

This might be the most interesting color in the set. Beyond a couple of very obviously powerful cards, black has many over- and undervalued cards that will be discussed here. 

**Blood Pet**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Definitely "bottom of the barrel" material here. Accelerating your mana by one turn will never be worth throwing away a card, and 1/1 for ![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) is not worth playing unless the special ability warrants it. I have drafted many a basic land over this card in **Magic** Online because hey, basic lands are actually useful.

**Bog Imp**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Another very poor card. Flying hardly makes up for playing with a 1/1 creature for two mana that has no interesting abilities. Some folks have argued that he is necessary in the beatdown red-black or blue-black decks and is a fine target for Howl from Beyond. My answer is that even in such a deck, you are reaching pretty deep if you have to play this guy. The aggro decks work fine without him and do not need him.

**Corrupt**  
 A top tier card as long as black is not a splash color. Its casting cost may be high, but it will be quite powerful by the time you can actually cast it - usually able to take out a creature with a 3 or 4 toughness if you are playing a two-color deck. The life swing is also very important. Although cards that do nothing but gain you life are horrible (as I will discuss with Stream of Life), being able to gain a few life points in addition to a great effect as with Corrupt or Staunch Defenders is a significant boon in Limited play. Corrupt and Gravedigger vie for the title of the second best black common in *Seventh*.

**Dark Banishing**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
**This** is the best card in *Seventh*, no matter who you talk to. The best creature removal spell available, it may not take out black creatures but monoblack is extremely improbable as an archetype in this format, so there will always be some first-pick quality creatures for it to kill.

**Drudge Skeletons**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Black certainly does not have the best fighting creatures in the set, so this little guy is extremely important. His job is to hold down the fort against Trained Armodons and Spined Wurms while your Razortooth Rats or another evasion creature is sneaking damage past the opponent's larger creatures. Although you should not have to pick Skeletons too early, you definitely want one or two in your u-b or r-b decks (not so much in g-b).

**Duress**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 One of very few commons that are actually much better in Constructed than Limited play, Duress is a card you **might** play, but you certainly do not **have** to. Trouble with it is that in this format you will have a very high fizzle percentage (as in, you will Duress an opponent and find nothing you can take). Duress is a good card to draw in your opening hand but becomes progressively worse as the game goes on and your opponent plays out his or her cards. Ostracize is even worse - while your opponent will often hold back on a trick or two, he will almost certainly play out all of his creatures, making Ostracize a dead draw after turn five or six.

**Fear**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Creature enchantments are tricky. Some are certainly good enough to play. Some are even good enough to first pick, such as Serra's Embrace. However, a creature enchantment needs to be super, extra good in order to be picked. The reason is its built-in loss of card economy any time an opponent finds a way to deal with the creature it enchants. Unsummon or Shock on a creature enchanted with Fear is the "risk vs. reward" scenario where "risk" definitely wins out. Even if your opponent does not have a removal spell, Fear will be a dead card if they are playing black, or even have a decent artifact creature in play. That is why Fear is at the bottom of the barrel.

**Giant Cockroach**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card is horrendous in an aggro deck (for four mana it gets blocked by a Bear), but its quite reasonable in a control deck (it trades with a Hill Giant or Spined Wurm in combat). Generally you should always pick a Hill Giant or a Giant Octopus over this card. On the other hand, that rule makes the Cockroach available to be picked much later in the pack, when he becomes worth taking. Draft him if you plan to do a lot of blocking on the ground, especially in u-b, but be sure to still pick Drudge Skeletons first.

**Gravedigger**  
 Although it shares the same casting cost as Giant Cockroach and is even worse in combat, Gravedigger's ability makes it one of the best black commons available. In *Invasion* and *Odyssey* block, cards with built-in card economy abound, but in *Seventh Edition* there are very few ways to gain a card on your opponent. This is a very high pick - in fact, I will often first-pick Gravedigger in the second or third pack if no better black card is in sight.

**Hollow Dogs**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A solid pick, unlike most black creatures, attacking is the Dog's specialty. It is especially good in a black-red deck that can clear the path for the Dog. In general, a lower pick than any Hill Giant type card, but better and larger than the other black creatures and therefore often necessary.

**Howl from Beyond**  
 It could be a game finisher, but I tend to rate the card relatively low because it has only a limited effect on the games that do not come down to a race. If you are overwhelming your opponent, you do not need the Howl, and if he is overwhelming you, it will let you take out a larger creature by losing card economy two-for-one in most cases. You can occasionally pull off neat tricks by combining Howl with regenerator or a creature with first strike but it will not happen often enough to elevate the card above a level of 10th pick or so.

**Looming Shade**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Some people swear by this card and some hate it. I try to stay in the middle on this issue, but certainly lean to the latter. Like Whispering Shade, Looming Shade sucks early in the game and is very vulnerable to red removal. True, every once in a while it will be an awesome monster that wins you the game by itself, as it attacks as 7/7 creature or so - but it will not happen often enough for me to pick the Shade very early. It is a reasonable sixth or seven pick in my book and I pretty much never want to play more than one of them in a deck.

**Mind Rot**  
 A reasonable if unspectacular spell. A fairly late pick, it can nevertheless make the cut, and will occasionally catch your opponent unawares, stealing a pair of really good cards from their hand. Although Mind Rot provides card economy, it does not influence the board in any way and so will be bad in games where an opponent has an upper hand early, or where your creatures are racing each other for the win. Those factors devalue the card to a level of eighth to tenth pick, but it is playable if you need filler for your deck. It also becomes a lot better if you have a number of bounce spells like Boomerang or Unsummon.

**Nausea**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The quality of this card depends on your own creature roster. If you drafted a ton of Razortooth Rats and Drudge Skeletons, the only reason to draft Nausea would be to keep it out of hands of your opponents. If you have few one-toughness creatures however, Nausea is a very useful trick. It is especially good against white mages with their hordes of Angelic Pages and Samite Healers.

**Ostracize**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 See Duress.

**Plague Beetle**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As I wrote above, 1/1 for 1 is hardly exciting. I would never maindeck the Beetle and the only time I would sideboard it is in the black-red on black-red matchups. In that archetype it becomes necessary to kill anything at all that is capable of dealing damage every turn, and so the Beetle will draw fire away from better cards - after hopefully having done a few points of damage.

**Raise Dead**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Playable, but not very good. In order to make this card good you need to a) draw a creature, b) have it die, and c) have that creature be so good that it was worth adding ![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) to its casting cost to play it. Raise Dead becomes better in a deck with Gravediggers, or if some of your creatures are extremely powerful - like Western Paladin or Stronghold Assassin.

**Razortooth Rats**  
 As you already gathered from this article, black is much better at killing creatures and holding off the enemy hordes than it is at attacking. This makes Razortooth Rats very good. If your opponent is also playing black, it is just another 2/1 for 3. If he is not, it is one of the few modes of victory against green decks. I will often pick Rats as high as 4th.

**Scathe Zombies**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A very mediocre creature and only worth playing if you are dangerously low on men.

**Serpent Warrior**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Is paying three life worth getting a 3/3 creature out early? While Serpent Warrior is far from exciting, I will usually play one (though not multiple). If your opponent has an aggressive draw, dropping a 3/3 on turn 3 may save you those same points of damage in combat over a turn or two. If you are the aggressor of the matchup, you can even things out by swinging for three before your opponent has anything bigger than a 2/2 in play. Of course, there are also nightmare scenarios of getting Warrior bounced to your hand repeatedly and such, but black has always been about taking higher risks with your spells.

**Spineless Thug**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Mediocre as all other bears, Thug may nevertheless be necessary in order to fill your mana curve.

**Unholy Strength**  
 Unlike Fear, this enchantment is powerful enough to play - though not to pick too highly. The best possible scenario will see you enchanting a Drudge Skeletons with it to create a very annoying monster, but giving the boost to Razortooth Rats or any other creature that will not easily die in combat is worthwhile. Try not to enchant Giant Cockroach with it very often.

### Archetypes

Black works well with most colors. I am not a fan of black-white, but the other three combinations are all solid. Black-red is a little shaky because you need a number of things to click into place to make it work - you have to have a solid mana curve, plenty of removal, and a bomb or two. However, when you do have all those things it is a fearsome archetype. This is where cards like Razortooth Rats and Spineless Thug work best.

Black-blue is a lot easier to pull off. You will want blue evasion creatures to combine with black removal and ground stall. This archetype is one of the strongest in Seventh far as I am concerned (though I definitely have a tendency to draft blue too often).

Finally, there is black-green. You want very little black here, almost splashing it for removal and a handful of creatures that help fill out your mana curve. Dark Banishing and Befoul are key here, with Gravedigger and Raise Dead perfect support cards.
