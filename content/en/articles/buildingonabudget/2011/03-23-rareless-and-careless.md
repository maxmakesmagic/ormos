I've had a number of readers ask for a Standard deck that plays no rares. To be honest, I wasn't sure if this was even possible. Cards like [Jace, the Mind Sculptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+the+Mind+Sculptor) are so powerful that it becomes very difficult to compete while confining oneself to commons and uncommons.

I decided to do a [Gatherer](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Default.aspx) search of Standard [including only commons and uncommons](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?action=advanced&rarity=%7C%5BC%5D%7C%5BU%5D&format=%7C%5B%22Standard%22%5D) to see what colors offered the most powerful spells with the rares and mythic rares out of the picture.

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration/images/magic/daily/boab/boab135_bolt.jpg)  

Red was a clear winner here. [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt), [Arc Trail](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arc+Trail), [Staggershock](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Staggershock), [Searing Blaze](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Searing+Blaze), and [Forked Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forked+Bolt) all seemed like cards that had the power level to compete in a world of [Stoneforge Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoneforge+Mystic)s and Jaces.

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration/images/magic/daily/boab/boab135_5Cards1.jpg)  

I didn't just want to make a red burn deck, though. That would risk becoming a boring down version of the red deck played by Patrick Sullivan at the recent StarCityGames.com Standard Open in Edison, NJ.

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration/images/magic/daily/boab/boab135_commando.jpg)  

[Neurok Commando](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Neurok+Commando) is a card that has the ability to completely dominate a game. It's especially strong when paired with cards like [Arc Trail](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arc+Trail) and [Forked Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forked+Bolt) that can clear the way and let the card advantage really pile up. One thing that's especially nice with [Neurok Commando](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Neurok+Commando) in the current Standard is how often your opponent will leave back just a single [Squadron Hawk](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Squadron+Hawk) back to block. "You already used two burn spells. Could you really have another one?"

![Neurok Commando](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Neurok+Commando)

  

The truth is, yes, of course you have another one—if your deck plays twenty burn spells!

I already wanted to play all the good commons and uncommons that red has to offer. [Kiln Fiend](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kiln+Fiend) seemed like a perfect fit to give this deck some really explosive draws. [Kiln Fiend](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kiln+Fiend) has the potential to let you end games very early. It works especially well when you have multiple one-mana burn spells or a [Staggershock](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Staggershock).

![Kiln Fiend](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Kiln+Fiend)

  

[Æther Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86ther+Adept) may not seem like the best card, but I've been pretty impressed with its ability to bounce creatures wielding [Sword of Feast and Famine](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sword+of+Feast+and+Famine). It's also nice to bounce your opponent's blocker when you have a [Neurok Commando](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Neurok+Commando) waiting to net you some extra cards.

![Aether Adept](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Aether+Adept)

  

I played a few games with the deck and found myself wanting some amount of countermagic. [Deprive](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deprive) seemed pretty good in a deck like this, and [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) is always a welcome addition. I decided to play a relatively even split and see which card did better for me in actual game play.

![Deprive](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Deprive)

  

As with other red decks I've worked on lately, I need a sideboard plan to deal with [Kor Firewalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Firewalker) and [Baneslayer Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baneslayer+Angel). This time around, though, it's much easier. Blue gives us access to [Narcolepsy](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Narcolepsy), and even if they're still gaining the life from the [Kor Firewalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Firewalker), it's much more important that we have a way to force our [Kiln Fiend](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kiln+Fiend) and [Neurok Commando](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Neurok+Commando) through their team.

I wanted extra countermagic in my sideboard for matchups like [Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Valakut%252C%2Bthe%2BMolten%2BPinnacle). I'll play a full set of [Flashfreeze](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flashfreeze). I'm also a fan of [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20110323000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) against a lot of different decks that don't play many creatures.
