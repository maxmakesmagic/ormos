**Magic** tournament environments are mirrors that reflect back on themselves. Players look to results of the last big event of a format to get an idea of what they should play, and what they are likely to face. This causes the round 1 field of a PTQ or GP to look a lot like the top tables of the last Pro Tour.

The upcoming Extended season will be no exception. A look at the Pro Tour-Houston deck lists will reveal much. The most obvious revelation is the large number of different deck types proven to be viable in that latest trial by fire. Wizards of the Coast has to be happy with the apparent health and diversity of the new Extended.

Everyone loves a winner. Players interested in off the shelf decks naturally look to the highest ranking deck from the Pro Tour that fits their play style or card collection. That tendency makes a top down list from the Pro Tour’s Top 32 a good preview of things to come.

The three color version (containing black for Pernicious Deed and Engineered Plague) Justin played is strictly superior to the two color counterpart. I expect nearly all Oath players in the upcoming season to be running the Deed.

It won the Pro Tour, and it’s an incredibly powerful deck. So, what’s to keep it from being the deck at every mage’s fingertips? The same thing that’s been keeping Oath of Druids off the banned list: Oath decks are hard to play. It often has long, Complicated games with dozens of make or break decisions. To make matters worse for the aspiring Oath mage, it’s a pain to playtest. 

Oath loves nothing more than to put out an Oath on turn two and sit. With its opponent afraid to play creatures, it goes for the turn one hundred and twelve kill with Treetop Village. Exciting! Try getting your playtest partner to sit down to more than two games of that fun.

Despite its finer qualities, I predict Oath will be only moderately popular. It’s a fantastic choice for those who can play it well. For everyone else, consider adding some Dust Bowls to whatever you *are* running.

Benzo’s overwhelming hand destruction followed by an early giant monster makes for a lot of quick easy wins. Its wide variety of low casting cost spells does allow for a lot of decision making in the first few turns of the game, but its raw power makes it fairly forgiving of those who make the wrong choices.

The sideboard of this version of Benzo makes it *much* more resistant to hate than its predecessor. It shrugs of Coffin Purges, Gilded Drakes, Planar Voids and the like as it boards in Negators and Butchers.

Between Benzo, u-b and Angry Ghoul, fans of Reanimation do have a wide variety to choose from. I can’t see playing u-b over monoblack. The two-color version weakens its mana base for access to blue spells that are of equivalent power level to the black spells it gives up. Angry Ghoul, however, provides an attractive alternative. It’s faster, more powerful, and less flexible than Benzo.

**Magic** cards may not be as easy to get a hold of in Australia as they are in the U.S. and Europe, but they did seem to have plenty of Jackal Pups and Ball Lightnings there. I got my teeth kicked in by three Sligh decks in a row. Moral of the story? Never underestimate peoples desire to play mountains in Extended. This is especially true for events with a high casual player content.

That’s not to say Sligh is not a powerful option. It is to say that due to its popularity, you can expect to see more of it than its power level and PT performance would justify. You’ve been warned.

### My Predictions

There were a dozen different deck types in the Top 32. The following is a listing of what I feel will be the most to least popular among them:

1.   The Rock
2.   Psychatog
3.   Benzo
4.   Angry Ghoul
5.   Turbo Oath
6.   Sligh
7.   Aluren
8.   Suicide Black
9.   u-b Reanimator
10.  Enchantress
11.  u-g Madness
12.  Draco Explosion

Remember, that’s a listing of popularity, not power levels.

Notable for its absence from the Top 32 was Tinker. Despite its poor showing at the PT, I don’t think Extended players are ready to give up on it yet. I’d put Tinker at number 10.5 on the above list.

If there is one thing we can learn from the post-PT Extended seasons of the past, it’s to expect the unexpected. The most powerful decks of each season saw their introduction at GPs, not the PT.

Remember the Michelle Bush special: Illusions Donate? How about last season’s Miracle Grow? If something new and broken doesn’t rear its ugly head this time, it will be the exception, not the rule.
