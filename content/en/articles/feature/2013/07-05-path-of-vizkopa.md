* * *

  

Black/White decks based around lifelink are something I try to build as each new set comes out. With ***Magic** 2014*, I think there’s a chance I might finally have the cards to do it, thanks to Archangel of Thune, Fiendslayer Paladin, and Path of Bravery.

What I really want to do is use [Vizkopa Guildmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vizkopa+Guildmage) with [Rhox Faithmender](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rhox+Faithmender) to turn lifelink into a huge damage source that ignores blockers. One problem I always run into is that [Vizkopa Guildmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vizkopa+Guildmage) isn’t that powerful of a card on its own, but there might finally be enough support to justify playing it in context.

Another challenge is finding the best one mana plays, since most creatures don’t really fit with the theme. I’m curious as to whether there’s enough support to make [Trained Caracal](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trained+Caracal) playable—It’s dangerous to play too many cards that are individually underpowered, like [Trained Caracal](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trained+Caracal) and [Vizkopa Guildmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vizkopa+Guildmage), but Path of Bravery and Archangel of Thune potentially do a lot to make the card relevant.

I’d prefer a [Soul Warden](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soul+Warden) type card, of course, but that’s not an option in Standard right now. I could play Soulmender if I’m worried that [Trained Caracal](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trained+Caracal) will get trumped by larger creatures and have to sit on the sidelines, but [Trained Caracal](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trained+Caracal) plays much better with Path of Bravery, so I’d rather start there.

As for support cards, I think [Brave the Elements](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brave+the+Elements) is extremely important, since we’re trying to build a critical mass of creatures while attacking every turn to keep Path of Bravery going. In this deck it protects everything but [Vampire Nighthawk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vampire+Nighthawk), but [Vampire Nighthawk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vampire+Nighthawk) can usually survive an attack without help.

There’s a problematic glut at 5 mana between Archangel of Thune, [Blood Baron of Vizkopa](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Baron+of+Vizkopa), and [Obzedat, Ghoust Council](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Obzedat%2C+Ghoust+Council), but I think Archangel of Thune is the best in the maindeck because it’s so explosive with Path of Bravery—when you attack with other creatures the turn you play it, everything gets +1/+1 for each of them, then they all get bigger again after lifelink happens when the deal damage. Against B/W decks, [Blood Baron of Vizkopa](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Baron+of+Vizkopa) is borderline unbeatable, and [Obzedat, Ghost Council](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Obzedat%2C+Ghost+Council) is the best card against control decks, so I think it’s worth having access to all of them, so that you can choose the proper top end of the curve, since each is quite different.

The sideboard is mostly aimed toward beating control decks, since I’m hoping the life gain will be good enough against aggressive decks. There’s a real concern that where you’re weakest is against big creatures that attack for enough that it doesn’t matter how much life you have, like [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk) and [Angel of Serenity](https://web.archive.org/web/20130705000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Serenity), but that’s where ***Magic** 2014*’s Lifebane Zombie shines, and I expect that card to be absolutely crucial in the format at large for keeping decks like Junk Rites in check moving forward.
