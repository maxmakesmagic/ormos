Tung Yi Cheng is probably best known in *Magic* for drawing a [Bonfire of the Damned](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bonfire+of+the+Damned) against Team USA at the World *Magic* Cup two years ago. We even included an animated .gif of it in the coverage yesterday, but posting it a second time might be unnecessarily cruel to Brian Kibler, so we'll leave it be, for now. 

Cheng came into the second draft at 10-2 and needing at least two wins and a draw to make top 8.

Pick 1, pack 1, he was faced with the following:

*   Feral Incarnation
*   Forge Devil
*   Gravedigger
*   Spirit Bonds
*   Triplicate Spirits

  

Almost everyone we spoke to here this weekend agreed that [Triplicate Spirits](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Triplicate+Spirits) is the best common in this set for limited play, and Cheng clearly seemed to agree with them, moving it quickly to the front of the pack. However, [Spirit Bonds](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spirit+Bonds) also drew his attention, which created a dilemma: shipping one or the other downstream might suggest to that player that white is open, and create a conflict of interest for Cheng in pack two. In the end, he decided on the [Spirit Bonds](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spirit+Bonds), and followed it up with a [Welkin Tern](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Welkin+Tern). 

Pack 1, pick 3 had no white or blue cards of note, causing Cheng to grimace visibly in his chair. I mean, I was standing behind him, and I could still tell his face was a picture of pain. He selected a [Flesh to Dust](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flesh+to+Dust) over an [Inferno Fist](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Fist), and followed it with a [Typhoid Rats](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Typhoid+Rats), and a [Rotfeaster Maggot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rotfeaster+Maggot), each of which were drafted quite quickly and decisively. 

I asked Cheng after the draft about his move into black, and he explained that Rats and the Maggot were key in stabilizing a game. The Rats are functional removal, and the Maggot often produced a noticeable life swing as it entered play, as well as being the right size for keeping enemies at bay.

The second pack was not kind to Cheng, who first picked a [Heat Ray](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Heat+Ray) that ended up in his sideboard. By the time the players were readying to crack their third packs, Cheng's deck was in need of a miracle. 

But if anyone is good at Miracles, it's the guy who has an animated .gif of himself drawing one. Pack 3, pick 1 gave Cheng a Spectral Ward, followed by a [Soul of Theros](https://web.archive.org/web/20140727000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soul+of+Theros). 

As Cheng assembled his deck, I asked him if he thought it was capable of getting him to the top 8. "If I'm lucky," he replied with a smile.
