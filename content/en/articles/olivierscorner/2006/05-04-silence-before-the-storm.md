P*T Prague starts tomorrow. After nearly one week of intensive playtesting, we are about to get into the real deal.*  
  
  
  
 In the first five days I’ve been drafting 16 times, with a global record of 24-22. Nothing great, but you have to consider every draft table was worth a PT table 1.  
 Julien’s house crew is the following, between brackets, the standings in the Pro Player of the year Race 2005:  
 Kamiel Cornelissen (39th)

 Frank Karsten (8th)  
 Rogier Maaten (21st tied)  
 Julien Nuijten (21st tied)

 Antoine Ruel (11th)

 Jelger Wiegersma (30th)

With 22 PT Top 8s, 62 GP Top 8s and global lifetime winnings of *1,169,229* million US dollars, the team presents a decent Curriculum Vitae. Before the week started, I estimated that with 60% wins I’d have pretty good chances to make Top 8.

With 52%, I’m doing ok but I’m still a bit far from the results I was hoping for. On the last 7 drafts, the rate goes up to 71%, definitely encouraging.  
![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/ads/Be%20Oc%20Gathering.jpg) As you can notice, it’s hard to draw conclusions from these results, except for one maybe: whenever I went UW, I did badly.  
 Surprisingly, I drafted two times two colors, which seems to be an aberration, as I use only one guild out of ten. Both times I went 3-0, essentially for two reasons:  

*   The less guilds you play, the stronger the synergy between cards is.
*   The less colours you’re running, the less mana problems you’ll find on your way.

The plan of this week was to practice a lot to know the format better than the other players. But did we break the format? Hard to say…  
 We didn’t find any super secret draft tech, there is probably none in this format, for one good reason: signals are nearly useless. Indeed, in *Ravnica*, players will often simply take the best card in every pack and then see what happens.  
 If there was a key in this format, I’d say it’s adaptability. It is crucial to be able to switch colors anytime in the first two packs.  
 In my opinion, and not everyone agrees in Julien’s house, playing four or five balanced colors should be an option to keep when your deck isn’t looking good or when you get infinite fixers. But it’s better to go for a more stable three color deck. I expect, there will be more mulligans and mana problems in Prague than in any PT before. If you can avoid them, do so. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/ads/Be%20Oc%20Spectral.jpg)

At first I was unhappy about my drafts, and even when I liked them, I had mana problems. It’s easy to blame bad luck in this situation but at some point I had to open my eyes, there was something wrong about my drafting techniques. Being 9-16 (36% win) couldn’t be bad luck only. Then what changed? Why did my results get better? I think it’s essentially the result from experience. The experience of these first 9 drafts of course, but also the experience of these years spent drafting which developed my ability to adapt in a new environment.  
 It took me three days, but I finally feel like, despite not drafting perfectly, I can have good decks whatever happens on a draft. 

Most of the players at Julien’s agree on one thing, their decks are just getting better and better, it actually looks more like constructed playtests. In most draft formats, drafting bad gives neighbours better chances to have a good deck. The two most important things in draft are picking the right cards and reading the signals.  
 Here, surprinsingly, we often saw six bad decks a draft in the beginning. The point of the all thing is not to know which card to take, but how your cards will fit together. As Roel said “*You just never draft the same deck*”. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/ads/Be%20Oc%20Siege.jpg)  
  
 Some card values changed a lot with the introduction of *Dissension*:  
Siege Wurm, Conclave Equenaut and all the Convoke spells are just way less good than before, as they don’t have any synergy with the guilds from pack two and three. Even Selesnya Guildmage is not as broken as it used to be. The same applies for Vedalken Entrancer and the mill spells, except for Glimpse the Unthinkable which is good if you can get Izzet Chronarch or Izzet Guildmage.  
 The fixers took some value, as more and more decks draft 4 colors. So did all the cards using +1/+1 counters (Doubling Season, Twilight Drover, Shambling Shell) for their synergy with Graft.

A few more practice drafts to go and the PT will start. Follow the online coverage and see you next week for my PT report.
