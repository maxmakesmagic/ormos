### First Was Stop, Now It's Yield

* * *

![](https://i.ormos.uk/05/7f/057f864a922f76ef5d49d4c793218c12cce7c040ffc804cb9892f21932b68239.jpg)

While you can find out about the yield options in the ***Magic** Online* help files, I thought I'd take some time to go over them in depth. In this article, I'll go over the options available on the **Game Play Settings** window and in a following article, I'll talk about setting yields during a game.

**Standard Disclaimer:** I'm not the most experienced player in the world, and I'm not suggesting that these descriptions are the be-all-end-all for ***Magic** Online* strategy. This article is for newer players who may not understand how the **Game Play Settings** can affect their games. As always, you're free to ignore me.

### What Are Yields?

The **Game Play** yields change how ***Magic** Online* reacts when certain things happen during a game. Whether you have a yield selected or cleared tells the game how to handle a specific situation. There are two broad categories of how this changes game play.

First, you can set yields so when common things happen during a game that you don't want to react to, you're not stuck clicking **OK** over and over again. Having yields set properly can save you a lot of time over the course of a match. Second, yields can help you avoid making mistakes during the game by making sure you get prompts at certain times.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b9/8e/b98ebae1daa5f5be45f14a00e5d3700945d37f0a9b8485b68bf80c07cfeee5b2.jpg)

### Your Default Yields

Just like your stops, there are specific yields selected when you first log on to ***Magic** Online.* If you change your yield settings, you can get the original settings back by clicking the Default button. The default yields are:

*Prompt before taking mana burn*  
*Yield to normal card draw*  
*Yield to combat damage that doesn't affect you or your creatures*  
*Skip the rest of the declare attackers step when there are no attacking creatures*  
*You can only announce a spell or activated ability if you have the mana in your mana pool to pay for it*  
*Never choose to assign your creatures' combat damage to you or your creatures*  

### So, What Do These Mean?

*Prompt before taking mana burn*  
 Mana burn happens when you end a phase without using all the available mana in your mana pool. When the end of that phase comes around, you take one point of damage for each point of mana left in your mana pool.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/07/8f/078f26cb947d2af1656a2b00b3f627f1b66f8419ad82237acb781722c9b71b5d.jpg)

For example, say you play Shock during your opponent's declare blockers step and tap two mountains to pay for it. (I know, I know. Just assume you did.) Only one red mana is used to pay for Shock, and the second red mana sits in your mana pool. If you don't use that left over red mana, when you reach the end of the combat phase you'll take a point of damage.

If you have this option selected (it's selected if there's a little green dot in the check box), a window will pop up at the end of the phase to warn you that you're about to take mana burn. You can either select to continue and take the damage, or stop and attempt to use up the mana before the phase ends. If you don't have this option selected, you'll take the damage at the end of the phase with no warning.

*Yield to normal card draw*  
 When a player draws a card, it doesn't show up on the stack. This is because there's usually nothing that another player wants to do in response to a card being drawn. So, by default, the ***Magic** Online* game simply passes over it.

If, for some reason, you want the opportunity to play something in response to another player drawing a card, deselect this check box. Keep in mind that you'll have to click **OK** every time another player draws, which can get tedious in a multiplayer game.

*Yield to all combat damage*  
 Yielding to all combat damage means that, whenever combat damage goes on the stack, you don't get a chance to react to it. You can't use anything to save your creatures, cause more damage to a specific creature, or play other tricks before damage resolves.

Unless you're running out of time and combat isn't likely to affect the outcome of the game, you should leave this option cleared.

*Yield to combat damage that doesn't affect you or your creatures*  
 This is a slight modification to the *Yield to all combat damage* option that doesn't mean a lot in a one-on-one game, but can save a lot of time in multiplayer. Selecting this option means you always pass priority whenever combat damage is on the stack, but doesn't involve you in any way. So, whenever two other players are involved in combat, you don't have to click **OK** to move through the resolution of the combat phase.

Keep in mind that, if you want to do something during a combat that involves two other players, you won't get a chance if you have this option selected. For example, in a Two-Headed Giant game, you may want to play Healing Salve to save one of your teammate's creatures. With this option cleared, you'll have the opportunity to do so.

*Skip the rest of the declare attackers step when there are no attacking creatures*  
 This is helpful if you have a stop set for the declare attackers step. Usually, if there aren't any attacking creatures, you won't want to play any spells or abilities. Otherwise, if you have a stop set for the declare attackers step, you'll have to click **OK** to move through the combat phase even if there are no attackers.

Again, this may make little difference in a single game, but can save many seconds over the course of a match.

*You can only announce a spell or ability if you have the mana in your mana pool to pay for it*  
 This setting affects the order in which you do things during a game and when card names are highlighted. If it's selected, you have to add mana to your mana pool before you can play spells and abilities. As you add mana, spells and abilities that you can play at that time and that you have the mana available for will have highlighted card names.

With this option deselected, cards and abilities that can be played at that time will be highlighted whether or not you have the mana available to pay for them.

While this setting is mostly about play style than anything else, there are two ways it can help you. The first is, if you're a new **Magic** player, having this option cleared means that the names of cards you can play always become highlighted at times you can play them. For example, sorceries and enchantments will become highlighted during your main phases, while lands will only become highlighted during your main phases if you haven't already played a land that turn.

The second is that you might save some time by having this option selected. For example, say you accidentally click a Giant Growth in your hand during your main phase when you really wanted to click the plains next to it. With this option cleared, the game will then ask you to pay ![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) to play the spell, and you'll have to click **Cancel** to back out. With the option selected, nothing will happen since you didn't have the mana in your mana pool to pay for it.

I suggest you play some games with this option selected and with it cleared, then decide which way works best for you.

*Always choose to draw cards when you control a card which makes card drawing optional*  
 This option only affects certain cards in the game. Basically, it lets you save time by automatically choosing to draw a card when given the choice.

For example, say you have Verduran Enchantress in play then play Vernal Bloom. With this option cleared, a prompt will ask you if you want to draw a card or not. You have to make the choice to draw or not before the game will continue. With this option selected, you'll draw a card automatically.

While having this option selected may save you some time, and is generally what you'll want to do, there are times when you may want the option. The most obvious example is if you're running low on cards and are about to be decked--drawing extra cards is not necessarily what you want to do.

*Yield to your own Madness triggered ability*  
 Whenever you discard a card with a madness ability on it, the ability triggers and goes on the stack. You then click **OK**, then your opponent clicks **OK**, and then the game asks if you want to play the spell for its madness cost. When the madness ability goes on the stack, you can play other instants or abilities before you decide to play the discarded spell for its madness cost.

Most of the time, you'll just click **OK** and then decide whether or not to play the spell. If you select this option, you don't have to click **OK**. If you think you'll want to play something before you decide to play the spell with a madness ability, then you need to have this option cleared.

*Yield to your opponent's Madness triggered abilities*  
 This is the same as the above option, only for madness cards other players discard. With this option selected, you don't get a chance to play spells and abilities before your opponent decides whether or not to play the card for its madness cost. It also means that you'll have to click **OK** every time your opponent discards a madness card.

*Always choose to remove Madness cards from the game when discarded*  
 When you discard a card with a madness ability, you're always given the option of playing the card using the madness ability or not. Selecting this option tells the game that every time you discard a card with madness, you're going to use the ability.

This is great if you have a deck with a lot of madness cards in it and usually have the mana available to play them when discarded. It'll save you time in the long run if you have this option selected.

However, if you're not planning on keeping mana available for every madness card in your hand, you'll waste time clicking **Cancel** when the game asks you to pay the cost. Of course, it probably evens out with the seconds spent telling the game whether or not you want to play the spell if you have this option cleared.

*Never choose to assign your creatures' combat damage to you or your creatures*  
 There's only one card that's significantly affected by this setting currently: Butcher Orgg. Butcher Orgg's ability lets you divide its combat damage between the defending player and creatures the defending player controls. If you block with Butcher Orgg, that means *you* are the defending player. Usually, you don't want to damage yourself or your own creatures. So, select this option and you don't get the chance to do so, which saves you some clicks.

If for some reason you might want to distribute the Orgg's damage to yourself and your creatures, you need to have this option cleared. For example, if you have Broodhatch Nantuko in play, you might want to splatter it hard so you can generate extra insect tokens.

### Conclusion

So, there are the **Game Play** yields in a bit more detail. Hopefully, this helps explain why you would or wouldn't want to select each one. Next time, we'll take a look at playing with the in-game yields.
