<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Tuesday, December 30, 2008</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Centaur Archer" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Centaur%20Archer&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            &#10;            <a>Centaur Archer</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Ice Age</i> common. <table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Monday, December 29, 2008</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Dross Scorpion" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Dross%20Scorpion&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            &#10;            <a>Dross Scorpion</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Mirrodin</i> common. Mixed in among <i>Mirrodin</i>'s Affinity cards were a few more traditional combo pieces. Mixing <a>Dross Scorpion</a> with a <a>Cathodion</a>-imprinted <a>Soul Foundry</a> and any way of sacrificing a creature for a useful effect immediately translated into an arbitrarily large life total, mana pool, or damage. This never really translated into big tournament success, because the Affinity cards were so good that even infinite combos couldn't stand up to them.<p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Sunday, December 28, 2008</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Crooked Scales" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Crooked%20Scales&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            &#10;            <a>Crooked Scales</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Mercadian Masques</i> rare. The obvious way that <a>Crooked Scales</a> is rigged is that the controller can flip coins more than once, which made it pretty good in a Limited environment where a player could reliably scare up ten mana. However, it also takes advantage of timing rules; since the coin-flipping is part of the effect (instead of the cost), any regeneration or other responses must be decided on before anyone knows what the results of the coin-flipping will be. And because it has two targets, it's legitimate to activate the Scales and respond by bouncing your own creature, thus removing all of the downside from a bad coinflip.<p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Before she became Untouchable, Phage was <a>Kamahl's</a> sister <a>Jeska</a>. You can see part of the battle if you put Phage and Akroma's cards side-by-side.<p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

The only other <img alt="2 Mana" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif"><img alt="White Mana" src="https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif"> 3/1, <i>Mercadian Masques</i>' <a>Ignoble Soldier</a>, actually has a <i>drawback</i>, and in fact, the only other mono-white creature in <b>Magic</b> that has 3 power and costs less than four mana without any drawbacks or restrictions is <a>Blade of the Sixth Pride</a>.<p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
