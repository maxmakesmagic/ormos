We still thought it was worth revisiting the format prior to the announcement.*

* * *

Combo deck are always asking themselves two questions. One, how can I defeat my opponent even faster? Two, how can I resist my opponent's disruption?

One answer to these questions is to completely ignore the opponent and pursue speed alone.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Grapeshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150121000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grapeshot)

This was the answer Owen and his teammates brought to the table at [the World Championship](/en/events/2014/legacy-daily-2014-11-22/legacy-daily).

If the opponent doesn't interfere, the deck can play [Goblin Electromancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20150121000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Electromancer) and go off next turn. The deck plays mana acceleration (which are [Dark Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20150121000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Ritual)s, thanks to the Electromancer), cheap draw spells, and a combination of the two—[Manamorphose](https://web.archive.org/web/20150121000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Manamorphose)—to thread into [Past in Flames](https://web.archive.org/web/20150121000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Past+in+Flames) and finally round back with 20+ damage from [Grapeshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20150121000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grapeshot).

However, there are absolutely zero ways to disrupt the opponent.

With this cost, the deck has obtained the possibility of a three-turn kill—originally not thought possible in Modern.
