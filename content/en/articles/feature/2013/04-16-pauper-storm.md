Remember when they [banned all the storm cards from Pauper](/en/articles/feature/2013/01-28-january-28-2013-dci-banned-restricted-list-announcement)? Well, they missed one, and it's currently dominating the format. 

![Temporal Fissure](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Temporal+Fissure)

  

There are a variety of [Temporal Fissure](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temporal+Fissure) decks, all of which use [Cloudpost](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudpost) and [Glimmerpost](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glimmerpost) and, surprisingly, [Ghostly Flicker](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ghostly+Flicker). Some of these decks use [Crop Rotation](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crop+Rotation) to find [Cloudpost](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudpost)s and enable large [Kaervek's Torch](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kaervek%27s+Torch)es, and others include Snap and [Cloud of Faeries](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloud+of+Faeries) as a mana engine, sometimes supporting them with [Sunscape Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sunscape+Familiar) and/or [Nightscape Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightscape+Familiar), and other times not. These decks almost universally include one [Mnemonic Wall](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mnemonic+Wall), which they can bounce with [Temporal Fissure](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temporal+Fissure) to let them bounce all of the opponent's permanents every turn or two. 

The build I'm looking at today is the one deck I saw with both Sunscape and [Nightscape Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightscape+Familiar)s, because I appreciate someone actually playing three colors of lands outside of Affinity in Pauper, and I've always liked the [Sunscape Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sunscape+Familiar) engine. This configuration is much less popular than blue with [Chromatic Sphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chromatic+Sphere)s and [Chromatic Star](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chromatic+Star)s (sometimes with [Fangren Marauder](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fangren+Marauder), which is also adorable), but I can imagine it being faster, which seems extremely important when both players are trying to [Temporal Fissure](https://web.archive.org/web/20130416000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temporal+Fissure) each other out of the game.
