This doesn't happen in Asian Grand Prix. What's supposed to happen is a highly armed posse of Japanese Pro's storms the field, takes half the slots in the top 8 and then one of them goes on to win it. Here in Manila the Filipinos were having none of that. It's been over six years since the last Grand Prix here and they were determined to make it count. Half of the top 8 were from the host nation.

Takuya Osawa was the lone Japanese player to make it to the final draft. Also Osawa, along with former Australian Jake Hart, were the only players to have ever made it this far before, making the top 8 probably one of the most inexperienced ever. Experience didn't help either Hart or Osawa as they both crashed out in the quarters. This set up a dream final between two local amateurs, James Porter and Jiro Francisco.

Unfortunately the final didn't live up to the billing. A scruffy mana-screw and mulligan-afflicted affair, it was eventually Porter who emerged triumphant on the back of successive bounce effects and a Leap of Flame to take him over the top.

Congratulations to James Porter, champion of Grand Prix Manila 2006!

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, March 19: 10:22 p.m. - Top 8 Draft</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/05/97/05971cca435202a943a58a5dce01b2d1bf533c17513a339478d43ca063c30ba9.jpg">&#10;            <i>The final table was set</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Manila has produced a strange top 8. As far as I know there are only two players who've top 8'ed a Grand Prix before. Fortunately they happened to sitting right next to each other so I decided to follow their draft.</p>&#10;      <p>Jake Hart is a strong advocate of the blue-black-red draft and will try and force that if he can. The deck he had from the last draft was just plain ridiculous. The Japanese have also said they favor that strategy, and Takuya Osawa did run it in the last draft, meaning we could have a little scrap on our hands.</p>&#10;      <p>Straight out from the blocks both players opted for the removal spell. Osawa took a <a>Galvanic Arc</a> while Hart took <a>Disembowel</a>. Hart's next pick was a move into blue with a <a>Drift of Phantasms</a>. <a>Watchwolf</a> proved too tempting for the Japanese player and he further strengthened a move into green and white with <a>Transluminant</a> and <a>Veteran Armorer</a>. Osawa also pre-empted having to select a third color for Guildpact by sweeping up a few spare black cards: <a>Strands of Undeath</a> and <a>Mausoleum Turnkey</a>. Bathe and a second <a>Transluminant</a> rounded off his first booster.</p>&#10;      <p>Hart's first few picks were largely unexciting as the second pick Drift was followed by <a>Roofstalker Wight</a> (over <a>Tattered Drake</a>) and a second Drift. Fifth pick was better with <a>Peel from Reality</a> - a card that Hart rates very highly. Another Roofstalker (again over <a>Tattered Drake</a>) and Surveiling Sprites left Hart hoping for a good pay-off from Guildpact.</p>&#10;      <p>The second booster saw both players first pick <a>Last Gasp</a>, but as both players major colors were different this couldn't really be classed as a fight. Hart got the <a>Dimir Cutpurse</a> Osawa couldn't really support while a <a>Faith's Fetters</a> made nice addition to the Japanese player's deck. This booster gave Osawa a couple of good Golgari cards as <a>Drooling Groodion</a> and <a>Golgari Rotwurm</a> made his pile. As his deck seemed quite aggressive I was surprised at a third pick Carytid.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/a2/b2/a2b2a09909625f1f9597fb0e7af531e6b5d21d9c55aecec054fb8722cd5dd59c.jpg">&#10;            <i>Hart and Osawa draft</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Other than the Cutpurse the cards again didn't seem to be there for Hart. This booster he mainly picked up filler stuff to round out the deck: <a>Goblin Spelunkers</a>, <a>Clinging Darkness</a>, <a>Terraformer</a>, another Surveiling Sprites. The pay-off usually arrives with Guildpact and the fantastically deep Izzet cards.</p>&#10;      <p>It seemed on the cards with his first few picks. <a>Electrolyze</a>, <a>Steamcore Weird</a>, <a>Ogre Savant</a>. But then the card quality began to dip. <a>Pyromatics</a>, <a>Izzet Signet</a> (over a <a>Torch Drake</a>), Petahydrox. Someone else must have had a similar plan or the boosters weren't the best as the Izzet cards didn't reach their usual depths this time.</p>&#10;      <p>Osawa took a <a>Ghor-Clan Savage</a> as his first pick and then picked <a>Wildsize</a>, Pillory, <a>Wildsize</a>. Osawa has a lot of bears and with the two <a>Wildsize</a> and a <a>Gather Courage</a> can probably mount a fairly scary offence. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10; He's managed the quarter-finals twice at two Australian Grand Prix. His opponent, Cynic Kim of Korea, is guaranteed a good pot of amateur cash but has to compete with two other amateurs in the top 8. He was actually qualified for Pro Tour Honolulu, but had been unable to obtain a visa.</p>&#10;      <p>Hart had gone for the usual blue-black-red concoction that had served him so well for most of the day. This time his deck was just solid rather than the monstrosity he'd picked up last time out. Kim has a pretty zippy Selesnya-Gruul hybrid.</p>&#10;      <p>Hart had to mulligan his first hand (&quot;11-0&quot;, he said referring to the number of his mulligans compared to his opponents) and his start ended up being very shaky.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Last Gasp</a> took out Kim's <a>Boros Guildmage</a> but there wasn't much to smile about after that as Kim followed up with <a>Burning-Tree Shaman</a> while Hart spent the next two turns waiting for his third land to appear. That allowed the former Australian to stick a <a>Drift of Phantasms</a> in the way of beats that now included a <a>Greater Mossdog</a> and <a>Wojek Embermage</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Hart's mana problems reached farcial levels as both a <a>Remand</a> and gunned down Surveiling Sprite also failed to find his fifth land. The <a>Remand</a> at least kept a <a>Flame-Kin Zealot</a> at bay for a turn and buy him time to <a>Disembowel</a> the Shaman. Rather than recast the Zealot Kim went for the mighty <a>Guardian of Vitu-Ghazi</a> and zipped in with the flaming Elemental on the following turn. A second Drift and <a>Peel from Reality</a> kept Hart in the game as he pleaded with his &quot;filthy deck&quot; for a mountain.</p>&#10;      <p>Finally it arrived and an <a>Electrolyze</a> was aimed at the Embermage. Hart was at 8. Could he now go on to pull this around? He brought out <a>Goblin Spelunkers</a> to start attacking back at Kim's life total. Kim kept up the pressure with <a>Skyknight Legionnaire</a>. He needed a pump spell or some kind of burn spell to punch through Hart's walls though.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Ogre Savant</a> bouncing the big tree trunk clawed Hart a little further back into the game. Kim's wasn't about to ease up. If he couldn't go through Hart's defenses he'd go round as <a>Scatter the Seeds</a> gave him some overlap options. He followed those up with <a>Ghor-Clan Savage</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Hart was down to 4 life and one card in hand. Another attack and finally a wall fell. Hart was down to 3 and the Guardian finally returned. Hart had held on for a while but finally the Korean player was able to crunch through.</p>&#10;      <p>Kim 1-0 Hart</p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/12/e5/12e5d73ec19ef0698aa04998150ee32947d4b8f845588db8bb3e0249690152c8.jpg">&#10;      <p>Game 2 and Kim's draw was like a complete re-run. Turn two <a>Boros Guildmage</a> followed by turn three <a>Burning-Tree Shaman</a> followed by <a>Wojek Embermage</a>. And also like a re-run Hart's deck was giving him fits again. This time it was refusing to cough up a swamp. Hart's offence, two <a>Goblin Spelunkers</a>, was actually pretty classy against another red deck. Unfortunately Hart was racing five points of damage. Well I say racing. Left coughing and hacking in the fumes as his opponent sped off might be more appropriate.</p>&#10;      <p>If Hart could somehow stabilize with a Wall or anything, his Spelunkers would quickly bring the game home. <a>Ogre Savant</a> helped, sending <a>Burning-Tree Shaman</a> back to the hand.
