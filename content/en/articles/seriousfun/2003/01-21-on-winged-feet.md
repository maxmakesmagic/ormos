In Roman mythology, Mercury (Hermes to the Greeks) was the god on winged feet, who moved so quickly and played so many tricks that among other things he was known as the Patron of Thieves. In his youth he was washed by nymphs at the mountain Tricrena, which sounds like a decent gig, and then went on to marry Venus (Aphrodite), Goddess of Love.

Apparently unsatisfied with his resume, Mercury also got an element named after him, known informally as "quicksilver." Which brings us to the day's card:

While this fellow may be the Mercury of the Sliver universe, I wouldn't count on the nymph-bath thing, even if you win your next local Pro Tour Qualifier. And finding your future wife washed up to shore inside a giant scallop? Just slightly more likely, if you live in a coastal area. But this speedy Sliver may still be your patron as you steal victories from more than one opponent.

### THE RACE TO THE SWIFT

What I hear often from group players is that "slow and steady wins the race." Defensive creatures and sweeping sorceries are usually the backbone of this strategy. A Fog Bank here, a Wrath of God there, and you'll eventually outlast the first few players, wind up alive when the moment's right, and strike for victory. (This would be your five-piece combo, or fleet of Iridescent Angels, or whatever.) And it's not just blue-white that favors such a strategy – every color seeks powerful creatures and effects that have a chance of shaking up the board.

What Quick Sliver teaches us is that not every race goes to the slow. I often receive decks from readers (and I'm generally happy to look at multiplayer decks and provide private suggestions, *if* you can give me a clear sense of what sorts of cards your group plays and where specifically you're having trouble). Many of these decks have no instants. Even fewer have instants beyond Naturalize or Terminate. I don't think it's a conscious decision – like I said, we casual players naturally gravitate toward slam-dunk effects, and it's hard to find those at instant speed. Add the fact that instants have the reputation for being a bit fleeting, and they don't seem like automatic choices in the grinding metagame most of us face. But when I give out advice on these decks I try to suggest an instant or two, where it makes sense.

That's because instants, permanents with instant abilities, and creatures with haste change the game's math too fast for many opponents to handle.

Think about what you like in a group game. (And I'm going to assume here that in addition to having fun, you want to win your fair percentage of games.) You like to have a sense of what's going to happen next. It doesn't have to be perfect clairvoyance – it's often enough to know that there's (1) an opponent over there who keeps attacking with three of his six creatures, (2) another who's holding back her three defensive groundpounders, and (3) a freak to your left who's got four pieces of what must be an eight-card combo and you're not exactly sure what it'll look like when it's done but it's likely to be messy.

If you count up the cards in hand, and check for graveyard recursion possibilities, you've pretty much got a map for how the game's likely to turn for the next round or two. You need to have at least two blockers back for the aggressor, maybe put something up in the air that can threaten the wallflower, and try to find time to poke enough damage at Mr. Combo so that he's easier pickings as the game goes on.

But what happens if the aggressor drops a Skizzik? Unmorphs a Skinthinner? Plays Starstorm at the end of your turn? There's a drastic change in the math, and if you didn't account for it during your turn, you're often lost.

It's easy enough to say that instants are good because you can play them on other people's turns – but more useful is to say that *creatures that you can play as instants* are even better, since they have all the advantages of coming out when you need them as well as all the benefits of sticking around to help out as your struggle continues.

![Quick Sliver vs. Aluren](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/0e/d90eed11960ad8a1a4dcbf217993b6c84582a1b598f1e91256ff42f6a4e44dc4.jpg)

Let's look at Quick Sliver again. Is this just Aluren rewarmed? The answer is not that simple. Even if we focus entirely on slivers, Aluren and Quick Sliver work in two different ways and require two different kinds of multiplayer decks.

In an Aluren (Sliver) deck, the enchantment comes down at sorcery speed. You will likely wait until the end of your last opponent's turn before playing out additional Slivers. Of course, in return for your patience, you can play them without paying for their cost. Crystalline Sliver is the natural leader of an Aluren-based sliver deck.

In a Quick Sliver deck, the creature comes down at instant speed. You will likely throw this sucker down in mid-combat, and play out additional slivers just before or after damage goes on the stack. Of course, in return for your impatience, you have to pay for each one. Muscle Sliver and Horned Sliver are the natural leaders of a Quick Sliver-based deck. (Or at least they are until the [*Legions* prerelease](https://web.archive.org/web/20050403211035/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=events/magic/prereleases).)

That doesn't mean you won't ever play Quick Sliver and Crystalline Sliver in the same deck – they get along almost as well as Mercury and Venus did. But you'll have to run three colors (because if you play Aluren to get the Crystalline, why are you bothering with the Quick?), and that sends you in a perfectly fine – but new – direction.

Let's see what happens when we stick to green and blue, and build a deck that's geared toward Quick Sliver's strengths – speed and surprise combat effects. We'll allow for some protection, replacing Crystalline Sliver with Steely Resolve.

What's not to like? You swing, you slip by the defenses, you replenish your hand, you recruit more to your cause, you swing again. Even the most traditional control-through-sorcery multiplayer deck will have trouble facing a concentrated effort from this monstrosity. In an emergency, Evacuation gets your Sliver cards back in your hand – where you can recast them at instant speed during the next battle.

I can't believe I just suggested a deck with Lifeline and Brass Herald in it. More mythology, anyone?

Getting back to the Roman god, Mercury's reputation for theft was so daring that he once stole a cow, gutted it, made a lyre with the intestines, and then passed it off to Apollo for a gift, who we can all suppose didn't know any better. The point I'm trying to make here is, when you play Quick Sliver, it doesn't really matter whether your opponent is Apollo or the cow – he'll end up disappointed.
