The trouble with the matchup above is that Bob’s deck is chock full of removal while my deck is, you guessed it, chock full of creatures! If, as the supporting component, my deck had a card advantage mechanism or some ingredient of disruption, maybe the zombies would have a chance. Ever have a Festering Goblin and Shepherd of Rot as your only guys and had to play them one at a time? Not pleasant. However, with my usual subpar performance in the World Championships out of the way, I’m back to relying on my booming social life for amusement. In other words, I’ve got time for such trendy and rewarding pursuits as ***Magic** Online* in a format that is, for me, largely irrelevant.

Had I not expected a metagame full of Lightning Rifts, then aggressive zombies is what I would’ve run. It’s quite explosive, strong against goblins, and generally enjoyable. Should you decide on this deck, make sure that the Festering Goblins are “slow-rolled” against gobs so that they don’t trade with a Shock or Gempalm Incinerator. Fester is there to trade with Sparksmith, or stall the opening goblin assault by threatening to do so. This gives you time to set up stuff like Rotlung Reanimator, Noxious Ghoul and Soulless One which by that time, especially if you have boarded in Infests, will be plenty to win. Note that Goblin Goon is rarely able to attack against zombies, because there are so darn many of them. Against roguish control decks like green-black or blue-white, the versatility of the deck’s attack is deadly, meaning that they must not only stop your early attackers, but keep removing your zombies so that Gempalm Polluter and Shepherd of Rot don’t burn them out.

The first important thing to understand is that this is NOT an aggressive deck, it is not to be played or sideboarded like one, either. Unless you have decided against the Bidding plan for some very strange reason, your goal ought to be to stall the game long enough to get off a Patriarch’s Bidding for so many zombies, with the right abilities, that your opponent is hopeless. Remember, Rotlung Reanimator and Twisted Abomination make a significant portion of your team last through the various Wrath of God effects, meaning that control decks generally need to kill you before your Bidding, or prevent you from resolving it. In turn, this generally sets your goals with this deck: Defend yourself from lethal damage pre-Bidding, and set up a Bidding big enough that it gives you the game.

So the first big question in assessing Dave’s build is, what about all the anti-goblin cards? Infests and Festering Goblins are hardly good in other matchups, but it’s quite tough to beat goblins with outthem. In my opinion, it isn’t worth it- even if goblins are running rampant, say comprising 25% of the field, you’d be better-suited to just accept that game 1 is tough, and try to win in earnest after sideboard, all the while having stronger chances against the rest of the field. If there were other decks out there that Festering Goblin was good against, that’d be fine, but as far as I know, goblins is the only widely played deck that your Festers are happy to see. On the other hand, there’s the Smother card. Good at disposing of Silver Knight, face-down Exalted Angels, smaller beasts, Withered Wretches, Rotlung Reanimators, Wirewood Elves and many other frequently-encountered creatures, Smother is hardly narrow, though it plays an excellent role in the two-slot against goblins, killing the ever-plaguing Goblin Warchief, Piledriver and Sparksmith.

Next there’s the anti-Wrath plan. Rotlung Reanimator and Twisted Abomination are naturals, giving you a significant team after the destroy/damage effects of Starstorm and Akroma’s Vengeance; but these don’t solve some other problems. One is Decree of Pain. The sad truth is that with Temple of the False God (not to mention Wirewood Elf and/or Explosive Vegetation out of some decks), black control decks can sometimes hard cast the Decree earlier than you’d like. One solution is to Gemplam Polluter them in response, then swing in with the remaining Rotlung Reanimator tokens, hoping that that, plus earlier beats, adds up to twenty damage. The sad truth is that it often doesn’t. What’s the answer? Vengeful Dead. Another common problem for this deck is Form of the Dragon, particularly if you have cast Stabilizer and therefore can’t use Gemplam Polluter (though this can sometimes be remedied by sacrificing the troublesome artifact to Read the Runes) to do the last 5 damage. The answer? Yep, Vengeful Dead. What, other than Polluter, is relevant in the mirror? Vengeful um… yeah…

So, he’s worth playing - ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) for a 3/2, and all. Don’t let me pose as the inventor of this guy, perhaps he’s even a mainstay by now, but he’s certainly a way to improve on Williams’ build. Sadly, the Dead can sometimes be slow and disobedient, especially when your opponent isn’t (for good reason!) removing your creatures. Enter another card - Carrion Feeder. Don’t get me wrong, he’s not the powerhouse he was in the aggro zombies, really more of a team player, but I think you want a couple. 

Another mildly troubling aspect of zombie Bidding is consistency. When you get blue mana, and lots of zombies, Read the Runes, Patriarch’s Bidding, and five lands, it’s a gem - but what about when you don’t? The sad truth is that, as someone wise (I think it was me) once said, “sometimes, you just don’t win ”. But this fatalistic outlook can be excepted (as opposed to being *a*ccepted), if you draw my favorite addition to this deck: Graveborn Muse. Now let me apologize if I haven’t already - these cards aren’t radical innovations on my part. The sideboard is much trickier to build. There are a lot of cards that you want to take out against goblins: Carrion Feeders, Polluters, Vengeful Dead and Graveborn Muses, but can you really spare eight sideboard slots? It really depends on your metagame. I’ve found the best anti-goblin cards to be Smother and Infest, so I go up to four of each and leave it at that. Once those come in, barring some tech from the goblin deck, that should make the matchup pretty favorable. I remove the Feeders, Muses, and a Vengeful Dead.

Against Bad Form, a peculiarly difficult matchup because they can do so much damage so quickly and have Form the Dragon, be ready for the creature sideboard. This often includes Menacing Ogre and Thoughtbound Primoc, which mean that you can’t wait around on your Biddings. Although they do have Starstorm, one Twisted Abomination will be enough to do them in if they don’t get Form of the Dragon. So go for it! Don’t get complacent and lose to something like Searing Flesh or Rorix. Chain of Vapor happens to be a nice, flexible way to race them and solve Form of the Dragon. Stabilizer is also a pretty good card (though I don’t suggest rushing it into play, use your cyclers first and prefer to play it only they have committed the mana to casting Lightning Rift), I suggest two of each. Going second, cut the Graveborn Muses and Gemplam Polluters. Going first, yank two Smothers and keep the Graveborn Muses.

Against Maher red-white (decks are named for who wins with them, not who builds them, after all), swap Polluters for Stabilizers and realize that your deck is nearly perfect against them. Still, be weary of Astral Slides and Exalted Angels, which some people are either starting, or bringing in.

So far, the board is 4 Infest, 1 Smother, 2 Chain of Vapor, 2 Stabilizer. The last six cards are pretty much up to your local metagame. Call to the Grave solves a lot of problems against monowhite and beasts, both of which really can beat you down with creatures before you get off a decent Bidding. Cabal Interrogator is a neat card against decks that don’t have a lot of removal and are slow enough that you have time to use him. And if the mirror is going to be relevant, the third and fourth Gemplam Polluters seem necessary; along with the die roll, they are the most important factors zombie-on-zombie encounters. My current sideboard, therefore, is 4 Infest, 1 Smother, 2 Chain of Vapor, 2 Stabilizer, 3 Call to the Grave, 2 Gemplam Polluters, 1 Cabal Interrogator.

Against zombies, get the Chains, the Smother, the Polluters and the Interrogator in and leave out the Noxious Ghouls and two of the Biddings. Remember that it’s a race, although in some games you will trade creatures and the Muses can be decent. Against beasts, bring in the Chains, Smother, and Calls, removing Feeders, Vengeful Dead and Polluters. Even with all that gas, you’ve got to look out for Decree of Annihilation which can be pretty brutal if you get lazy with your board position. Against monowhite, remove Polluters, Feeders, Vengeful Dead, a Smother, a Withered Wretch and both Putrid Raptors for 3 Call to the Grave, 1 Cabal Interrogator, 4 Infest (they will probably have morphs, Silver Knights and White Knights after sideboard) and 2 Chain of Vapor (can save your Call, return Muse when he is getting out of hand, delay Akroma, stop a Dragon Scaled creature, bounce a morphed Angel, among other things). Be careful to avoid losing to Akroma’s hasty, flying 6/6ness, but usually you will get a big Bidding before that’s a problem.

And that should cover most of the popular decks. If you are interested in playing this deck, I suggest practicing about ten games per expected matchup. The deck isn’t too hard to play, and the games are actually pretty short. Still, it’s important to know how big a Bidding you need, and what your opponent’s most dangerous weapons are. In some matchups, you need to put on some pressure so that cards like Decree of Pain and Akroma don’t kill you. In others, Bidding with a Noxious Ghoul will wipe out their team, solve all of your problems, and signal the end of the game. Get comfortable with how each matchup works, when to cast Graveborn Muse, when to be aggressive (rarely) and when to play for a longer game where your Biddings will give you control. In particular, think ahead about the individual problem cards I have mentioned, and how you can prepare for them. Dig further for Vengeful Dead if your opponent has the mana for Decree of Pain, save your Gempalm Polluter if you are playing against a deck potentially supporting Form of the Dragon (these are usually monored decks), and don’t let your life total drop too low against Decree of Justice and Lightning Rift. And uh… have fun, I guess.
