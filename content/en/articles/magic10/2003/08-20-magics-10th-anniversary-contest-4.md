### Top 5 - Most Memorable Places You've Played Magic

**1st prize**  
*Jon Wells, Malsch, Germany*

Our scuba diving club, based in Germany, is called Magic Octopus. Of the 8 founding members, 4 are Magic players and we now have a number of other players in the club. One of the aims of the club is to promote Magic and introduce new people to the game. On Dec. 17th, 2000, we decided to combine our two favorite hobbies. Jan Olsen (club president) and Jon Wells (Vice President) played their first game of Underwater Magic in the indoor swimming pool in A-stringen, Germany. Unfortunately, only one poor-quality photo exists of a practice run for this event, but we are planning to repeat the event sometime soon! The photo (http://www.jonwells.de/Graphics/temp/UW\_Magic.JPG) shows Jan trying to decide what to do next! In the match, Jon played a blue deck based on creatures found underwater, though not necessarily ones that divers would want to meet! The deck included several crabs (Giant Crab, Horseshoe Crab), Sharks (Giant & Hammerhead) as well as several other nasty creatures (School of Piranha, Sea Serpent, Stinging Barrier). A barrage of counterspells was included to keep the opponent under control! The highlight of the deck, however, was 4 Clam Sessions from Unglued. Unfortunately, when one managed to come into play and stay there until the next upkeep, the attempt to sing underwater failed miserably (and required a short break to stop laughing and regain our composure!) and the creature died before it could attack! The deck also, rather ominously, included Drowned from The Dark. Not great without black mana, but a definite danger for divers! Jan played a fast red/black direct damage/creature removal deck. Pretty basic stuff, plenty of Lightning Bolts, Shocks, Chain Lightnings, Earthquakes, etc as well as Coercion, Dark Banishing, Terror, Diabolic Edict to keep the number of creatures under control. Unfortunately, common knowledge tells us that fire and water just don't mix. Jan proved this and failed to play any red mana onto the table. After being hammered by Hammerhead Sharks, bitten by Schools of Piranhas and Drowned, he was finished off by a single "oeping" from a Horseshoe Crab bearing a Hermetic Study. It was a good thing that the match ended when it did, neither player had more than 10 minutes of air left in there. Thanks!

**2nd prize**  
*John Walker, New York, US*

We, my wife and seven children, arrived in Korea in the autumn of 1993. I was a soldier stationed at Camp Casey in Dongducheon, South Korea. The children, ranging in age from sixteen to five, were experiencing their first trip outside the United States. For a year we lived downtown in a large room, formerly the back room of a restaurant. We kept track of time by the sound of trains coming and leaving the train station next-door. The kids spent mornings being home-schooled and afternoons visiting places around the community. One favorite place to visit was the post exchange.

As the holiday season drew near that year, we were introduced to the wonderful world of Magic. Magic cards were a hot commodity and popular with most everyone. As soon as they arrived, they quickly sold-out. When possible, young soldiers from the camp would come to our home and play Magic with the family. Our Korean friends enjoyed reading the English on the cards. It was a great time. Our furnace broke down nightly, but we stayed warm, huddled together, targeting frozen shades, walls of ice, and "shiver" dragons with fireballs and elemental blasts.

When I left for work in the morning, there might be a dozen people sleeping peacefully on the floor, contented from an evening of playing Magic in our humble abode. Our oldest son left early the following summer to return to the U.S. and attend college. He went on to be a Magic state champion and played in the Magic U.S. Nationals. He sold his Black Lotus and Moxes to go on a honeymoon when he got married. The final weekend of the honeymoon was spent supporting his younger brother at Junior Super Series in New York City. From the Big Apple to the City of Angels and in between our family members have played Magic. There are only two children left at home now, but the entire family continues to play Magic, especially when we get together for the holidays. Though we now naturalize and break asunder each other's enchantments, the most enchanted memory for me is a large one room apartment in the Land of the Morning Calm where everyone played Magic and slept on the floor.

**3rd prize**  
*Rick Silva, New Jersey, US*

In the nine years that I've been playing Magic, I've been fortunate enough to travel to conventions and tournaments in many cities and to play at hundreds of locally run events. I've played in venues dusty card shop back rooms to rented function halls to full scale convention facilities hosting Pro Tour events and other major tournaments. In spite of all of these different experiences, I've found my favorite place to play Magic is in a classroom on the third floor of the school building where I work in Jersey City, NJ.

I teach biology at CREATE Charter High School, a newly opened school in Jersey City's Greenville area, and I'm lucky enough to have the support of my principal in running an after-school game club for my students. The club is an official sanctioned school activity, and one of the most successful in the school. We've met once a week since the early Spring of 2002, and have had over 25 students participate (a decent number of the approximately 150 students in the school). The club raises money through student contributions and by selling cards. I help out by contributing unused commons (after 9 years of playing I have plenty) for our sealed deck leagues and by organizing tournaments and other club events.

The idea wasn't mine originally. One of my students had brought his cards into school and when I mentioned that I played, he came up with the idea for the club. For the last year and a half, the game club has held tournaments and sealed deck leagues. We even organized club trips to the Prerelease tournaments for Onslaught and Legions.

CREATE is a new school located in a typical inner-city neighborhood, with all of the challenges that such a setting brings to mind. Crime rates, drug use, and unemployment are high, while the public schools in the area rank among the worst in the state. Safe after-school activities, especially ones that involve thinking, are an important service to the community, and I'm glad to be able to provide a few hours a week to students who might not have many good alternatives for spending their time.

Only a few of our students had even heard of Magic when we started the club, but several of them proved to be very fast learners. I teach them the basics of the game and give out cards as prizes and as part of our sealed deck league, but I try not to tell them how to play. I want the students to learn from their own ideas and mistakes. They'll be better players in the end and they'll have more fun getting there. It's great to see their reactions as their level of play improves, or the looks on their faces when they discover a new combo or see the potential in a card they've previously overlooked. It's these moments that make my classroom the best place I know of for playing Magic.

**4th prize**  
*William Reynolds, California, US*

On February 24, 2000, Robert, a member of the Magic group I play with, defied gaming convention and got married to a wonderful woman, Kimberly, who didn't mind the occasional Searing Wind he unleashed. Poor judgment caused him to invite us to the wedding. Being a gamer, he decided to hold the whole thing at Dave and Buster's to offset the mood of the event with the presence of an arcade.

Still, fearing boredom, Carlos, Andrew, Travis, and myself, the other members of the group, weren't taking chances. Having no obligation to act proper at a bar, we each brought a Magic deck along. We arrived at Dave and Buster's early to get seats in the back. It took awhile for all the guests to trickle in, so we were forced to start a game. Part of the peculiarities of my Magic group is that the multi-player decks we run cause games to take forever and we have a rule that all games must be finished. This particular contest was drawn out because of Carlos's Planar Rebirth/Constant Mists deck.

Huddled in the corner, no one paid us any heed, and we returned the favor, as we didn't notice the ceremony had started shortly after we began playing. During the match, Travis, using a ridiculous Wrath of God/Purify/Armaggedon deck, announced, "I Allay your Fear," to Andrew. This sounded nicer than it was as doing so allowed Travis to block and kill the Verdant Force Andrew had just brought out that was attacking Travis. This move provoked a nasty remark about Travis's mother from Andrew, which was a tad bit louder than wedding etiquette permits.

Looking up, we saw all eyes had shifted to us. Kimberly had been interrupted in the middle of reciting her vows by Andrew's cursing and was now among the multitude staring at our game. About to pick up our cards and be really embarrassed, Rob, understanding our obsession, shocked the crowd and his bride by declaring, "Why don't you guys finish what you're doing? That way we can avoid more swearing from Andrew. Besides, we all want to know who wins."

Whether he was serious or not, we continued since this moment to cringe in the spotlight was too good to pass up. Luckily, I soon drew into the last piece of the Natural Affinity/Wave of Reckoning/Spidersilk Armor combo I had been waiting on and finished off my friends within a matter of minutes. Realizing that I had won, the crowd heaped sarcasm-infused applause upon our table. Putting away our decks, we watched the rest of the wedding in amused shame.

After the ceremony was over, we were going to aplogize to Rob, but he was busy with guests. Everybody else was standing in a growing line waiting for the free food that was almost available. So the four of us did the only thing we could think of: we took out our cards and started another game. Who wouldn't want a rematch after all that?

**5th prize**  
*Christopher Tsai, Texas, US*

As my flashlight beam glanced upon a dusty but flat and level wooden crate in the corner, I knew that the game would be possible. When lightning flashed outside a distant window, the sight of two intact chairs further confirmed possibility. With a place to sit and a surface on which to play, Jared and I would be able to make the journey to Dominaria without much problem, or so we thought.

Jared turned to me and grinned, excited at the opportunity to enter battle surrounded by darkness. I returned his grin, motioning to the crate to begin our game. Jared scanned the room, content on his choice of location for his first game with mono-black. Degenerate wooden walls, a high ceiling shrouded in darkness, and a creaky floor, all gray with dust, enclosed three rooms and a spiraling staircase. The two rooms to our immediate left and right, bare and blanketed with dust, suggested that inhabitants had forsaken the house long ago, but the third room extended an invitation for entry with the two surprisingly steadfast chairs, the dusty crate, and even a looming bookshelf, which exuded a refusal to surrender; it stood, tall and proud, unwilling to submit to age despite the loss of the books that probably once lined its dilapidated shelves.

The sturdy chairs, too, boasted endurance, their seats strangely free of the dust that devoured every other object in the house. As Jared unrolled the blanket that he brought over the dusty surface of the crate, I could not help but wonder what other beings had entered the house to relieve the chair of inevitable dust. While Jared laid out the life-keeping abacuses and began taunting me about his newly constructed "Death deck"? I swept aside the weak drapes to expose a hopelessly spotted window.

Another bolt of lightning struck, reminding me that our families would probably begin searching for us soon; two hours ago, Jared and I had left our parents as they were browsing the Disney World Store, a pastime that neither challenged our minds nor satisfied our thirst for adventure. With only a promise that we would return before midnight, we had escaped the confines of cheery Disney World (Orlando, Florida) to seek out both a mind-challenging game of Magic "Jared was eager to trounce my Blue Control with his new Corrupt combo" and a heart-pounding adventure.

Apparently, we "or, rather, Jared" found both (he had taken the crucial right turn that led us to this empty mansion).

Settling down into the firm chairs with little but a creak, we opened our deck boxes and silently began to shuffle, the flashlight strategically positioned to illuminate the entire plane of play. When Jared slammed his sleeved deck onto the crate with a confident gesture of dominance, the shelf uttered a loud squeak from the corner, and the floor groaned as a cloud of dust erupted near the crate. Ignoring these noises, I rolled the dice "a 6" and declared to move first. I drew a decent opening hand, but my relief was short-lived, as the sound of an opening door "or a squeaky stairstep" emanated from one of the other rooms, beckoning for examination. I handed Jared the flashlight, ordering him to check it out, but as he left me alone in the room, all the haunted house television shows and horror movies I had ever watched came back to haunt me. Lightning struck, and a long shadow "not Jared" danced across the wall before me. I quickly shouted for Jared to return, and he did, with a grin on his face, seemingly gleeful at my obvious fear. He had not found anything.

I told him about the shadow, but he nervously suggested that it was only a tree, and we commenced our game. With Jared's Attrition in play from turn one with a Dark Ritual, all our early creatures littered the graveyards. As his Carrion Ants entered play with the board wiped clean, Jared grinned, but, simultaneously, a swarm of scuttling invaded our ears, as if someone had dropped an entire bag of marbles on the ground above us. A dry wheezing laugh followed. We both looked up, then at each other, wondering if this house was truly abandoned. We jumped as we turned back to the game; an intimidating black spider had crawled onto the Carrion Ants card! Although Jared unhesitatingly brushed it aside with his fanned-out hand of cards (Thank God for sleeves!), I could sense a shiver from him as well. An exchange of pale-faced glances told us that we agreed on the fright of this locale.

Two Counterspelled Corrupts, an unsuspecting Bribery, and a successful Treachery later, I had my Palinchron, a Bribed Sengir Vampire, and Jared's Carrion Ants poised to kill. Even though I reduced Jared to negative 12 life in a matter of seconds, I did not care to stay and gloat. We tacitly packed up our playing gear and hastily exited the house, mutually certain that we had braved the most frightening atmosphere in which one can possibly play a game of Magic. Best of all is the fact that we live to tell the tale.
