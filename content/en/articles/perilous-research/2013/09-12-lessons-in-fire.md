Welcome back to Perilous Research. Today, I'll be taking a look at another preview card from *Theros*. *Theros* is already shaping up to be one of the most exciting sets in years. Don't miss your opportunity to be among the first to battle with *Theros* at your local Prerelease. [Locations and event times can be found here!](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/tcg/events.aspx?x=mtgcom/events/prereleases) The *Theros* Prerelease will be an excellent chance to meet new players and get involved in sanctioned play, and it will be your only chance to get your hands on one of five exclusive promotional Prerelease premiums. You can hang out and battle all day, or just play a single Sealed Deck event. This Prerelease is sure to be one for the ages! 

I started playing **Magic** a long time ago—I was nine or ten years old when I first started getting involved in the game. I played casually among friends for the most part, but I was hurled into the fiery chaos of competitive **Magic** when my brothers and I first started scouring the Internet for high-level deck concepts. Through the years, I've learned many lessons from specific cards. [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum) taught me patience; [Cabal Therapy](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cabal+Therapy) taught me to read my opponents; [Lin Sivvi, Defiant Hero](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lin+Sivvi%2C+Defiant+Hero) taught me about card advantage; and [Mirari's Wake](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirari%27s+Wake) taught me how to transition between attacking and blocking roles within a particular game.

Everyone has cards that taught them interesting lessons over the years. Perhaps the most obvious lessons are those in card evaluation. There are often powerful cards that seem less than stellar when a newer player first reads them. These cards help us adjust our focus on what is good and what is bad, they teach us how certain decks are trying to win, they allow us to break the chains of defining single cards as good or bad in a vacuum, and they help us understand that each deck we build will require a fresh analysis of cards that we might have deemed "bad" when we previously looked at them.

For me, no card better taught me this lesson than [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup). I had just celebrated my twelfth birthday, and my mom bought me a *Tempest* Starter Deck and a few boosters to reward me for keeping my cool after getting ten injections at the allergist. I sifted through the cards and pulled three to the front. Two copies of [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup) and a [Cursed Scroll](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cursed+Scroll). At first glance, [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup) seemed awful to me; sure, it was like a [Savannah Lions](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savannah+Lions), a card I knew was very good, but dealing myself damage did not seem like something I wanted to be doing. What if I played against another red deck? Still, 2 power on the front end of a one-mana creature was something special and I knew it. Within a few days I had mustered up the courage to try my [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup)s. I found myself crushing people in games where I was on the play with a turn-one Pup. Blockers got [Incinerate](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Incinerate)d and I even won a few games as early as the fifth turn, which was pretty unbelievable to me at the time. 

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Jackal+Pup)](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup)

  

That day, I learned that [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup)'s drawback is only a disadvantage when I was playing defensively. I almost never won games with a red deck when I was being defensive. Excluding red mirrors, I wanted to draw [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup) in my opening hand more than any other card in my deck. I learned that a card might be less than stellar in most decks, but the perfect fit in a deck with a specific goal. Cards like [Glen Elendra Archmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glen+Elendra+Archmage) and [Pyromancer Ascension](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyromancer+Ascension) may have remained overlooked pieces of history in **Magic** if I had never opened those [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup)s. 

Today's preview card will have the opportunity to teach that same lesson to a whole new generation of **Magic** players. Red strategies will be happy to play an aggressively costed and super-aggressive one-mana creature even if it means a detriment to their life totals. Creatures are a lot better than they were back in the *Tempest* days, and Firedrinker Satyr doesn't disappoint. Let's take a look at the latest one-drop for red decks.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/71/83/718327f3e84d7cd4b2c1be468a7e4b1fa336a51d807581f6a570d7881bc9e98f.jpg)  

Yes, it's a [Jackal Pup](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jackal+Pup) you can pump. It's a one-mana creature that attacks for 2 with no help and has the chance to attack for much more if you're running out of things to do with your mana. This will become a staple in aggressive decks and I expect it to be followed up by [Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary) to make a lot of scary boards very early in the game. 

Mono-Red decks are the most obvious home for Firedrinker Satyr. These strategies want to get the opponent from 20 to 0 life as quickly as possible. Let's take a look at what's available in the new Standard for Mono-Red.

Firedrinker Satyr and [Rakdos Cackler](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos+Cackler) are the best one-drops. I want to play at least twelve one-mana creatures, though. [Foundry Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foundry+Street+Denizen) and [Legion Loyalist](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Legion+Loyalist) are the two options I have remaining. [Foundry Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foundry+Street+Denizen) does more damage, but [Legion Loyalist](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Legion+Loyalist) combos nicely with Firedrinker Satyr and [Rubblebelt Maaka](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rubblebelt+Maaka). I'd like to give [Legion Loyalist](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Legion+Loyalist) a try to see how it does in the deck. However, I'm definitely open to changing it to [Foundry Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foundry+Street+Denizen) if testing dictates it.

[Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary) is incredible here. Sometimes you chain a few [Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary)s and have no other two-drop to follow them up with. Firedrinker Satyr provides a mana sink where you can grab an extra point of damage with that excess mana. 

<table><tbody><tr><td><div><div><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary"><img alt="" src="https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Burning-Tree+Emissary"></a></div></div></td><td><div><div><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gore-House+Chainwalker"><img alt="" src="https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Gore-House+Chainwalker"></a></div></div></td></tr></tbody></table>

  

[Gore-House Chainwalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gore-House+Chainwalker) and [Firefist Striker](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firefist+Striker) are the obvious two-mana creatures for a Mono-Red [Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary) deck. 

[Rubblebelt Maaka](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rubblebelt+Maaka) is a fine uncounterable [Giant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Growth) that has the potential to make a board presence if it comes to that. 

Hammer of Purphoros is a great card for any aggressive red strategy. You probably don't want to play more than two; the second copy usually has no value unless your opponent destroys the first copy. One copy is a great addition here, though.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/18/1b/181b251d25ce456b6bbd1f2fde8eff22e40e6d6bd0d35ceb986073788c47c1b9.jpg)  

Red decks always want at least a little bit of reach. [Firefist Striker](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firefist+Striker) fills this role nicely, but Lightning Strike and [Dynacharge](https://web.archive.org/web/20130912000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dynacharge) should be enough to punch through for some nice damage.

*Theros* is shaping up quickly and aggressive decks are well-poised to make big waves at Pro Tour *Theros* in Dublin.
