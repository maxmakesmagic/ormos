The Daily *Magic* Update is a roundup of everything *Magic* you should know on February 8, 2017. Today's Update is brought to you by [The *Magic* Story Podcast](/en/articles/feature/2017/02-08-the-magic-story-podcast-doug-beyer), which kicks off today.

# Top 25

1.  Everything Changes

Oh boy, here we go.

[Pro Tour *Aether Revolt*](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/pro-tour-aether-revolt) changed everything. And I do mean everything.

First of all, we have a new No. 1-ranked player in the world, and it's Portugal's Márcio Carvalho, who not only just finished second in Dublin but is also coming off a second-place finish at the *Magic* World Championship just a few months back. That knocks Shota Yasooka all the way down to . . . No. 2. Not too precipitous a drop for our previous No. 1.

Carvalho isn't even the biggest gainer. Lee Shi Tian and Jiachen Tao both jumped up eight slots, which is impressive, but still doesn't represent the biggest leap. That belongs to Brad Nelson and Ivan Floch, who went from unrated to Nos. 11 and 12, respectively. Oliver Tiu, Eduard Sajgalik, Pascal Maynard, and Craig Wescoe also entered the rankings from off the list. Sajgalik—who made the Top 8 at Pro Tour *Aether Revolt*—is making the list for the first time ever despite a long time spent on the Pro Tour.

Also notable is that Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa continues to climb the list, now entering the Top 5 after *his* Top 8 performance. The way he's been playing, don't be surprised to see him keep rising.

# Deck of the Day

1.  Hualin Bai's [Paradoxical Outcome](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Paradoxical+Outcome)

Let's get this out of the way: this deck did not do well at the Pro Tour. Sorry, everyone who was calling for it to be on stream at Pro Tour *Aether Revolt*.

That said, the deck is a blast to play, does something wild, and is clearly a popular idea. So maybe you—yes you!—can make it better.

Because I have magical Pro Tour decklist powers, I can assure you that this is the exact list Hualin Bai played at Pro Tour *Aether Revolt*. The deck uses a combination of [Sram, Senior Edificer](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sram%2C+Senior+Edificer) and [Paradoxical Outcome](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Paradoxical+Outcome) to turn a bunch of zero-cost artifacts into a ton of cards and, eventually, a win with [Aetherflux Reservoir](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aetherflux+Reservoir).

There are a bunch of different ways to build the deck, but Sram, Outcome, [Aetherflux Reservoir](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aetherflux+Reservoir), and [Bone Saw](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bone+Saw)/[Cathar's Shield](https://web.archive.org/web/20170208000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cathar%27s+Shield) make up the core of the deck. From there, Bai went with a number of cards with improvise and some other artifact-centric cards to synergize up and down the chain.

While it didn't work out for Bai, the idea is exciting, and the [Sram/Puresteel Paladin deck in Modern](http://www.mtgmintcard.com/articles/writers/hapless-researcher/wild-research-sram-cheerios) shows the deck is just a few cards off from being truly good. Do the tools to make it work exist? Maybe, but you won't know until you try.
