This is the third installment of my series analyzing the cards of *Legions* for the purposes of Limited play. The second installment can be found [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/legions-limited-review-blue).

### Green

While not as deep as black, green is my favorite color in *Legions* because of two nasty little words: *Timberwatch Elf*. The most powerful common in the set by a long shot, Timberwatch Elf is worth drafting green for all by its lonesome, as the low number of first-pick caliber common green cards in *Onslaught* means many drafters will go in other directions. It’s worth the slow start to your draft to get this game-breaker third, or fourth.

Of course, the Elf isn’t the only reason to draft green. The creatures are big and they run deep, with provoke proving a real nuisance for whomever your opponent may be and whatever they may be playing. Krosan Vorine is one of the better commons in the format on its own and a real powerhouse, while Needleshot Gourna and Stonewood Invoker offer a lot of power from the common slots also.

In *Onslaught-Legions Limited*, while green can be played with any of the other four colors, it’s best suited to a partnership with either red or black, with the removal those colors provide clearing the path for your usually superior creatures. Oh, and one other thing: when the player passing to you sends a Timberwatch Elf your way because they took the red card over green one pack one, be sure to thank them in your first place tournament report.

**Timberwatch Elf**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Randy, Mark, Brian, Mike and the rest of the R&D gang, on behalf of the rest of the drafting community: thanks for nothing. Sparksmith was obviously a ridiculous card that dominated the environment, but at least you could force its controller into mistakes, going too low on life and the like. Timberwatch Elf brings with it no similar drawback, though it does essentially pacify every one of your opponent’s creatures until they’re ready to line up to win. Absolutely ridiculous.

**Feral Throwback**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Just as game-breaking and harder to kill, the Throwback loses out to the Elf in that it’s just so much more to cast. That said, while most six-casting cost amplify creatures aren’t great because of the tendency to play creatures as they come in Limited, the amplify: 2 aspect of the Throwback’s being makes it more than worthwhile and once it’s in play as a 7/7 or 9/9 Abyss, simply speaking, you’re not too likely to lose.

**Canopy Crawler**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Don’t be fooled by this ranking: come pack three, the Crawler could well rank #1 if you have enough beasts in your pile to truly harness its power. Of course, you need a fair number of beasts in your deck to make it good and anything less than, say, six makes it fair-to-poor, depending on the situation in which you draw it. Do keep in mind: even as a 2/2, the Crawler still possesses its tap ability, so if the 2/2 version is targeted with provoke, it can be saved with its activation.

**Caller of the Claw**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While the Caller definitely falls below the top three, that’s not to say it isn’t a fine first pick in the scheme of the Legions power struggle. If nothing else, the Caller exceeds the morph model with its ability to come into play as an instant, but obviously, it’s designed to do much more. If nothing else, if your opponent chooses to trade morphs on your fourth turn attack, you could always just cast the Caller after combat. That kind of tempo advantage can be pretty tough to overcome.

**Seedborn Muse**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When the Timmy, Power Gamer in me rears its ugly head, I can’t help but giggle about the possibilities the Muse offers. On Prerelease day, I was fortunate enough to get the Muse in play with Krosan Tusker and Contested Cliffs, so you can’t really blame me. Simple enough: your creatures essentially don’t tap to attack and you get to activate creature abilities twice, not to mention doubling up your mana. Those are all very good things without much to complain about.

**Krosan Vorine**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Used on its own, the Vorine is good, a 3/2 creature that can be traded with any creature without a tap ability, first strike or a toughness greater than four. It’s only when used in conjunction with other cards that boost its power and toughness that the Vorine really shines, with the possibility of survival making far more possible. Snarling Undorak, Timberwatch Elf and Berserk Murlodont are all examples of cards that can pump the Vorine out of fatality range on a regular basis. 

**Gempalm Strider**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Ignoring the cycling aspect for a second, this is a 2/2 creature for two mana, a valuable commodity in a Limited environment, but especially this one. Additionally, it’s an elf, so it lends itself well to tribal effects, meaning that if the Strider didn’t cycle, it would still be a very good card. Throw in the fact it can act as a Giant Growth effect for all your elves while drawing you a card and you have a card that goes beyond, providing both power and versatility. A pretty good target for Wirewood Herald too.

**Elvish Soultiller**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Generally speaking, a player doesn’t need large elves as they happen to lie in the same color as the core beasts, but once in a while, one comes along as a bonus. The Soultiller is big. Very big, in fact, and its ability upon dying serves to fatten your deck’s spell content, meaning each of your ensuing draw steps is likely to be better. Unspectacular, but very, very solid.

**Tribal Forcemage**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It’s a morph and that alone makes it playable. If your deck is tribally aligned though, the Forcemage can turn your game around, helping your creatures survive an Infest or Bane or mass creature combat. If nothing else, it can always boost itself to take out a pivotal blocker, but be careful: your opponent could have creatures of the type you’re intending to name in play. They get the bonuses too.

**Stonewood Invoker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Like Gempalm Strider, if nothing else, Stonewood Invoker is a 2/2 for two mana, which is more than “good enough”. Where it pales compared to the Strider is in the fact that until you have eight mana, it’s “only” a 2/2 for two. Of course, when you get to that point, the Invoker is very capable of taking complete control of the table given the right set of circumstances. All in all, a very solid card.

**Branchsnap Lorian**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I love this card. The morph cost is ridiculous and often unaccounted for when your opponent chooses their attackers and blockers, meaning it will often trade for a larger and/or higher picked creature. If you’re fortunate, your opponent will block this early morph with two of their own, which you can take out with the additional payment of one mana. If they only block with one, so be it: you’re trading card for card and getting in a couple points of damage as a bonus.

**Wirewood Channeler**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 We all saw in Venice how strong Explosive Vegetation is and the Channeler is better. With a greater potential mana output, none of the choices associated with Veggies and the extra body the Channeler gives you, it’s a very good card, especially when you consider the extra elf it gives your tribal deck.

**Needleshot Gourna**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I like… big… BUTTS! Ok, ok, I’m old and have little rhythm, but you really have to admire the behind of this beastie. The Gourna is slow and requires double green to cast and doesn’t provide that much offense, but it is fully capable of stopping an opposing offense all by its lonesome and that is a very unusual knack. Not as good against black with its fear and Banish effects as it is against other decks, the Gourna is still pretty good in most situations. Just be careful you don’t have too many spells in the stratosphere.

**Primal Whisperer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Truth be told, I’m not too sure about the Whisperer as I haven’t had it too often, so I may be underestimating it. That said, every morph acts as a minor Monstrous Growth of sorts, throwing off opposing math, and of course, there is potential for this thing to get very, very large. The thing is, for five mana, it has to be very large to be worthwhile, so I’d call it solid to good.

**Patron of the Wild**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)

**Berserk Murlodont**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I like the Murlodont’s aggressive nature. Get it out with Snarling Undorak and the game is practically yours. It’s not without its drawbacks though. **Totem Speaker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)

**Wirewood Hivemaster**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)
