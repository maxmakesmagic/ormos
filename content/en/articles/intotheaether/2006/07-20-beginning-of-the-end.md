### In My (Snowy?) Tribe

> *The weather outside is spiteful  
>  And global warming is downright frightful  
>  But with Yeti and Angels on the go  
>  Let it snow, let it snow, let it snow…*

When I was tapped to join in the Snow Week festivities, I figured it would be pretty easy to tie it into ***Magic** Online* by building a Tribal Wars deck. Snow is a type after all… but then I realized “snow” is a supertype and, after confirming with Rules Guru and archvillain Mark Gottlieb that supertypes can't be used as a “tribe,” had to give up my dreams of Snowy tribal decks.

Or did I?

There are some good quality Snow creatures scattered throughout the colors, and I could utilize another prominent MTGO format – [Vanguard](/en/articles/faq/2005/04-28-magic-online-vanguard) – and the Birds of Paradise avatar to pick the best of them and throw them together in one deck. Birds of Paradise enables all my lands to tap for any color mana, so I could even use a full boat of Mouth of Ronom and Scrying Sheets and not get mana hosed. Here's my “Snow Tribe” deck:

It's hard to argue with the power of Ohran Viper, Stalking Yeti, Heidar, and Adarkar Valkyrie. I was a little torn between Karplusan Wolverine and Rimebound Dead. I liked the defense the Dead could provide, but I ended up going with the Wolverine as early removal against things like Birds of Paradise or the ubiquitous Dark Confidant. Skred just seems like an insanely good removal spell in a heavy snow deck. Rimefeather Owl is obviously the big finisher, and I'm hoping between the Viper and Scrying Sheets to be able to draw into him by the time I'd be able to cast him.

Why Snow-Covered Plains? I thought about just running two of each Snow-Covered basics just for kicks, but then I figured I might be randomly hosed by someone with Mountainwalk, Forestwalk, or Swampwalk, and I think I'd be more prone to see a random Boiling Seas than Flashfires.

Sadly, I won't get to test this deck out before I have to give up my god account, but if any of you Snow fans end up having the cards and the time, let me know how it performs.

### Once More With Feeling: Ken Krouner

> “I love *Ravnica* block, it's the arguably the best Constructed set of all time, but for sealed and draft it's been a tough nut to crack. I think perhaps the most frustrating part of the format is how hard it is to draft and build a Limited deck while dealing with the subtle ways of mana screw, random draft cutting, or packs that don't go your way. I remember another Limited format that was a lot like this… *Invasion* Block Limited! So for my last Team-Up with Bennie Smith here on Into The Aether, I am going to give some comments on two of the sealed decks that did well in this weekend's big events. One in each multi-colored format.
> 
> “I'm going to start with the *Ravnica* deck. While the deck seems incredibly powerful, it had a big hole. There was no good way to run a healthy amount of creatures. It's somewhat ironic that with all the other ways a pool of cards could go wrong, this age old problem would be the one to rear its ugly head. When I first glanced at the deck, I tried to figure out ways to perhaps weaken the power level of the deck to squeeze more creatures in, but it isn't all that viable. The only changes I think I would make are cutting Carom and Fists of Ironwood for some combination of Bathe in Light, Greater Mossdog, Droning Bureaucrats, or Ivy Dancer. I thought for sure that the Bathe would be an auto-include, but with the low number of creatures, it's not all that easy to justify it. Ivy Dancer is an incredible sealed card as we are green with big creatures and that's the default sealed deck in this format. Ivy Dancer can give us a huge edge in those matches.
> 
> “This deck is extremely close to what I would have built. Kudos to Alexander Rathcke for a great build.

> “Now a little foray into the past. You kids today don't know how good you actually have it. Signets and Karoo lands have you spoiled, playing whatever cards you want. Any 3 or 4 colors is just dandy, but it wasn't always that way. There was a time when if you wanted to fix your mana, you pretty much had to play Green. That time was called *Invasion* Block. They put a good number of Blue fixers in that block, but not nearly enough to compensate.
> 
> “This particular deck couldn't play Green, and the 2 color fixing lands don't exactly go hand in hand. In this block, I feel you are a much bigger slave to your mana, since it so rarely will just be there for you. To this end, I would cut the white out of the IPA deck. I cut Vindicate, Shackles, and Manacles of Decay for Shoreline Raider, Rogue Kavu, and Maniacal Rage. I also would cut the 2 Plains and the Treva's Ruins for 2 Islands and a Mountain. This will shore up the mana and make your deck more streamlined, capable of winning with an aggressive early game or a potent late game. Trust me, I would want to play my Vindicate as much as the next guy, but I don't think it's worth the risk to go full three colors in this deck. If you want to play the 1 Vindicate and the 1 Treva's Ruins, I wouldn't object too much, but I love me some good mana in this format.
