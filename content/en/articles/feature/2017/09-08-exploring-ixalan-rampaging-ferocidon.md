As part of our preview season for *Ixalan*, we are going to take a deeper look at some of the cards previewed on our own homepage. Today, we'll be peering behind the scenes of the process that went into creating the art of Rampaging Ferocidon, which was previewed in [Melissa DeTora's article](/en/articles/card-preview/2017/09-08-why-we-make-hate-cards) today. If you're interested in the design efforts that went into the card, Melissa has offered a very thorough look at the process that goes into designing this and similar "hate bears," so be sure to give her article a read!

# Art

You may recall from the article on Regisaur Alpha that the artist for this piece, Jonathan Kuo, was contacted by the art team because of his outstanding Dinosaur art. Here is what he was sent for this card:

> **Setting**: Ixalan
> 
> **Color**: Red creature
> 
> **Location**: On a plain, with terraced farmland visible in the background
> 
> **Action**: Show a **Dinosaur** similar to a velociraptor or deinonychus. It stands **on the body** of a fallen triceratops, which is about twice its size. The raptor is **reared up** on its back legs in triumph.
> 
> **Focus**: The Dinosaur
> 
> **Mood**: Behold the mighty predator!

This is Jonathan's final piece:

![Rampaging Ferocidon](https://media.wizards.com/2017/images/daily/c4rd4r7_ZDVPXepl3q.jpg)

Rampaging Ferocidon | Art by Jonathan Kuo

The key to this art is the manner in which the Dinosaur's ferocity is highlighted. If one were to hear the name of this creature in a vacuum, they would likely envision a massive incarnation of primal bloodthirst, but this creature is meant to represent something perhaps even more terrifying: a small, nimble predator full of unrelenting savagery. This is captured in the art by both the stark contrast in size between the predator and its prey and the shocking amount of carnage inflicted on the fallen triceratops.

The message is clear: don't mess with a Ferocidon if you want your insides to stay in place.
