Earlier in the day I posted a series of basic primers on some of the decks we expected to see at the Grand Prix today.

Unsurprisingly, I've seen a lot of Islands on my wanders through the hall. So many that some players have tried to take advantage by playing main deck hate cards such as [Pyroblast](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyroblast) and [Choke](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Choke). This strategy has not worked out for some. I overheard one player bemoaning a sequence of opponents not featuring a single Island in either its basic or dual-land guise.

"Goblins, Artifacts, Elves ..."

That's the thing with a format as diverse as Legacy. It might look like wall-to-wall [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm), [Force of Will](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+of+Will), and various flavors of Island plus other land type, but it's not actually wall-to-wall [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm), [Force of Will](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+of+Will), and various flavors of Island. That main deck [Choke](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Choke) can end up looking pretty silly when you're being squished by a massive [Wurmcoil Engine](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wurmcoil+Engine).

As I walked around, I grabbed a few snapshots of some of the more unusual decks.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/events/gplil15/lil15_wonders-1.jpg)

Remember [Time Spiral](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Spiral)? Not the set—the card.

At various times in *Magic*'s history [High Tide](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=High+Tide), a deck based around an innocuous Fallen Empires instant, was a much-feared deck. It still lives on in Legacy. [High Tide](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=High+Tide) to boost the mana production of Islands plus ways to untap and retap those islands (for example [Candelabra of Tawnos](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Candelabra+of+Tawnos), [Time Spiral](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Spiral)) is an old way to generate tons of mana.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/events/gplil15/lil15_wonders-2.jpg)

I'm not sure what the Legacy Enchantress deck looks like, but a board of [Argothian Enchantress](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Argothian+Enchantress) followed by [Enchantress's Presence](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enchantress%27s+Presence) is usually an omen of bad things to come.

In an early round I saw a deck that looked like Lands, except it was merrily attacking away with [Goblin Rabblemaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rabblemaster). Not many Islands to [Choke](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Choke) there either.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/events/gplil15/lil15_wonders-3.jpg)

Later on I saw a more conventional interpretation of Goblins. I'm assuming the player had a fun game. [Warren Instigator](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warren+Instigator) putting [Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kiki-Jiki%2C+Mirror+Breaker) onto the battlefield with [Goblin Ringleader](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Ringleader) and [Goblin Matron](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Matron) is all fun and games until the opponent gets buried in Goblins. (It's still fun and games for the Goblin player ...)

(Main deck [Choke](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Choke) isn't too good here either.)

All these old cards, especially in languages players may not be familiar with, can be difficult to keep track of. I saw one player helpfully show his opponent a binder full of [Cavern Harpy](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cavern+Harpy) in various languages after being asked what this strange card did. Your definition of helpful might vary considering the same player had an [Aluren](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aluren) in play at the time ...

Everyone has to start somewhere but when I saw someone pick up a [Stoneforge Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoneforge+Mystic) to read in round two my first thought was: Oof, you're going to have a long day.

While walking round I did pass a game at the perfect time to see an amusing Oops! moment. Both players had blue dual lands in play. One cast [Show and Tell](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Show+and+Tell) and put the last remaining card from his hand card face down on the table. His opponent did the same with a card from his hand. The first player turned up [Omniscience](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Omniscience). His opponent flipped up [Emrakul, the Aeons Torn](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Emrakul%2C+the+Aeons+Torn).

Oops indeed. Sometimes [Show and Tell](https://web.archive.org/web/20150704000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Show+and+Tell) is a perfectly fair card, after all.

That would have been a perfect moment to take a picture ... if I'd had the camera on me.

So I guess that oops goes for me too.
