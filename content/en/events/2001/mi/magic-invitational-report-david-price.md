Long gone are the days when the sixteen Invitationalists were flown to exotic locales like Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia or South Africa. Instead of experiencing the night market, a Malaysian street bustling with sidewalk vendors selling pirated movies and fake watches, or taking a boat ride in supposedly shark infested waters off the coast of South Africa, the Invitationalists, this year, found themselves in Seattle, Washington.

Don’t get me wrong. I like Seattle as much as the next guy. I just miss going to someplace new.

### Tour Day

Admittedly, the rainy bus ride through Sydney, Australia left a lot to be desired. Tour day in Seattle may have topped it, however, as the sixteen competitors were dropped off in the wharf area in Seattle. And I mean dropped off. The only person who stuck around was Thomas Pannell, because I don’t think he had anything better to do. When we asked Worth Wollpert if he wanted to hang out with us, he said no. He had to go to some meetings or something. When we asked Aaron Forsythe if he wanted to hang out with us, he said that he would have more fun at work.

When left to our own devices, however, we **Magic** players know how to get the most out of a city. After a nice lunch, a number of us found ourselves at an Irish pub near the waterfront. What better way to while away the five hours or so until dinner? Brian Kibler, David Humpherys, Jon Finkel and I sampled some Guinness beer, as well as some Red Bull and Vodka. Countless drinks later, we found ourselves at a fancy restaurant eating dinner courtesy of Wizards of the Coast.

One might think that after starting drinking at 2 PM, we were going to get a good night’s sleep in preparation for the tournament the next day. Not so, not so. Instead Eivind Nitter, or simply “Rockstar”, was introduced to a new rating system for members of the opposite sex, or even members of the same sex, if that is your thing. Now some might think that a rating system of any kind is sexist and disgusting. Those people might be right, but I would suggest that they listen to the rating system with an open mind before passing judgment. As you may or may not know, most rating systems work on a scale of 1 to 10, with often outrageous claims of “she’s a 15" or “she’s a -1", which completely compromise the integrity of the rating scale. I prefer a far simpler scale, which was introduced to me by Jon Finkel. A person is either a 1 or a 0. It is a binary system. In polite language, the scale breaks down to this: a 1 is a person that you find attractive; a 0 is a person that you find unattractive. It is the only true rating scale, as far as I’m concerned, and it also gives insight into how drunk and/or easy a person is. For instance, a conversation might go as follows:

 Jon Finkel: “No, a 1 and two 0's.”

At some point, I became frustrated with Eivind’s incredible pickiness. He wasn’t drinking enough, he wasn’t quite getting the rating system, or he truly was some sort of rock star who could get any woman that he wanted and thus could stand to have impossibly high standards. Finally, I spotted two drop dead gorgeous women walking into the bar. I looked at Eivind and asked, “what do you think?” Eivind hung his head and said, “I am not invincible. They are 1s.”

At the point, when we were far too drunk to make a single good decision, Scott Larabee went home. Did I mention he was keeping us company at the Irish pub? No one can deny that Scott was a good man to have a few beers with. Once the only remaining responsible person was gone, we decided to take a cab to the University district. I wish I could tell you about our adventures there, but I don’t remember all that much about them, except that Brian Kibler was ripping up the dance floor and I was barely functional as a human being.

### The Tournament

I don’t want to talk about the matches in detail, as you can see them all on **Magic Online**.

For the record, though, yes I screwed up against Jon Finkel. I should have smashed his face with my goblin deck, but I did not. I’m over it now.

Despite the humdrumness of Seattle as far as an Invitational spot goes, playing on **Magic Online** was a blast. Whereas in other countries, you might find 10 or 20 spectators at a time, with who knows how many people reading the updates on the website, the sheer number of people on **Magic Online** who were watching the replays and cheering us on was astounding. It was such a thrill! People were rooting for their favorites and talking about the Invitational cards. The energy was absolutely contagious. And I have never played so many matches in a row in my life as I did while gunslinging. I played this black-red Magnivore deck that I put together for fun and it turned out to be pretty good. Not only that, but I found that I enjoyed attacking with 11/11 hasty guys. Who would’ve figured?

I’m getting ahead of myself, however. When it came time to play the new Standard with *Onslaught*, an environment that we knew little about, I was 6-6. I had no shot at being one of the top two competitors, dueling it out to see whose card would be printed. I could’ve played a u-g madness deck or a Psychatog deck or something that I knew was at least ok, but I decided that the Invitational was not about playing the same boring deck that you played in the old Standard environment. So I played a goblin deck. From the start, I was in love with Goblin Piledriver. He races past Psychatog and is great with other goblins, especially with Raging Goblin and Reckless Charge. He can do an obscene amount of damage if left unchecked. So I ran it. I ended up going 0-3 with the goblin deck, although as I mentioned before, I should’ve beaten Jon Finkel. And if I had it to do over again, I wouldn’t run a goblin deck again. But it sure beats the boring crap that the other players played, with a few exceptions.

When the dust settled, I failed to win another Invitational, ending up 6-9. I had a night out on the town and otherwise spent some relatively quiet nights playing **Magic** with the other competitors. There were no bad guys at the tournament, something that I enjoyed immensely. It was a good group of people and I liked spending time with them. Besides Chris Pikula and Jon Finkel, who are two of my good friends and who I generally have a good time with, I was especially pleased that Tomi Walamies and Eivind Nitter were there. They have to be two of my new favorite Pro Tour players, joining the likes of David Humpherys, one of my oldest favorites.

The weekend ended with a great dinner at Mark Rosewater’s house, where we got to enjoy the company of many of the Wizards folk who make **Magic** and the Pro Tour what it is.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/mi02/873.jpg)

I suspect this might be my last Invitational and it’s been quite a fun ride. I’ve been playing professional **Magic** for seven years now and, as much as I’d like to go out with a bang, I think I’ll be going out with a whimper. I’m qualified for Pro Tour-Chicago, which is *Onslaught* Rochester Draft and maybe if I do okay there, I’ll end up qualifying for Venice. But, I’ve run out of steam. Many new players have a lot more heart than I do. They eat, drink, and breathe **Magic**. It’s just a snack for me now, a tasty one which probably isn’t very healthy for me. Early on, after I won Pro Tour-Los Angeles in 1998, I thought that maybe I could do this for a living. Four years and a few dollars later, I realize that I was mistaken. I stuck with it for so long because of love of the game, the companionship of certain **Magic** players, and eventually because I forgot how NOT to play professional **Magic**. Many of my good friends are leaving the game, like Chris Pikula, and others have already gone. Recently, I’ve remembered what to do with myself when I’m not playing **Magic** and I like that.

I have no idea if this is goodbye or not, but it is getting awfully close. I would really like to thank those people that voted for me; I hope that I didn’t let you down. I want to thank everyone that watched and cheered us on, as it meant a lot to an old **Magic** player like me.
