    *by Brian David-Marshall*
  

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, April 1: 11:18 a.m. - The secret to his success…</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/secret.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Kenji Tsumura &amp; Richie Hoaen</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Have you ever wondered what it is that separates the Kenji Tsumura and Richie Hoaen's of the world from the rest of us PTQ stiffs? As I was walking through the deckbuilding area I stopped to check in on Rich and Kenji. Rich's deck looked solid but Kenji looked like the cat that ate the canary. Actually he looked more like the canary that ate the cat. I flipped through his deck until I hit the cards that put the smile on his beak…</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;So lucky,&quot; laughed Kenji pointing at himself while I admired the <a>Pyrohemia</a> and Akroma. &quot;They were both in the same pack.&quot;</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, April 1: 1:10 p.m. - Round 8: Undefeated Showdown</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/siu_dyke2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Brian Siu &amp; Jim Dyke</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Steven O'Mahoney-Schwartz is easily one of the most underrated players in the history of the game. In terms of dollars he was the third winningest player in the history of the game when he retired. He had a win at Pro Tour Los Angeles where he had to defeat both Mike Long and Jon Finkel in order to hoist the trophy. He started playing at the very first Pro Tour in the junior division and it is fair to say that the East Coast <b>Magic</b> scene coalesced around him. I know I would never have been able to open Neutral Ground without the counsel and support of the young OMS brothers. </p>&#10;      <p>His teammate is Top8<b>Magic</b> partner and in-game podcaster Matt Wang who has been playing just as long as Steve albeit with not as much success. To be fair to Matt, he did have two early Top 64 finishes before he went off to business school to make a vaguely honest man of himself. Both players are regulars at Finkel's apartment which has become the draft Mecca of the New York for veteran players. </p>&#10;      <p>The showdown of undefeated teams was almost an early nineties reunion as Mark Le Pine - last seen making the Top 8 of Pro Tour Rome - was at their draft table. Instead they drew the pairing of Brian Siu and Jim Dyke the reigning New Hampshire State Champs of 2HG. Given slightly different circumstances this match could have been a PT 1 old-timers game. </p>&#10;      <p>I asked Jim Dyke which NH store he and Brian played at regularly and he shrugged: &quot;You can say we play at Hammer's Comics.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I thought Hammer had to close the store,&quot; was my reply. We were discussing Pro Tour one finalist Shawn &quot;Hammer&quot; Regnier. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I mean…we play in his basement,&quot; laughed Jim. &quot;I actually wanted him to be my partner but he said no so I took Brian instead.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Steve and Matt came out of the gates fast with a <a>Gemhide Sliver</a> and suspended Duskrider. Eric Phillips and Bern Farkas, crusty veterans in their own right, were watching the match and they pumped the fist when there was no kill spell for the sliver. &quot;No one has ever untapped with <a>Gemhide Sliver</a> and lost the game,&quot; declared Farkas. </p>&#10;      <p>Matt and Steve went to work on turn three by accelerating out Reflex and <a>Poultice Sliver</a>s and beating in on turn three. Brian and Jim held them back with <a>Aven Riftwatcher</a> and Matt was content to just attack with a hasty <a>Vampiric Sliver</a>. Steve added a <a>Saltfield Recluse</a> and suspended <a>Shade of Trokair</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>They looked to be in a good position to win the game on the ground but their opponents both had fistfuls of cards. Gabe Walls was talking about the format earlier and was complaining that he never felt like he was winning when he played 2HG. &quot;My opponents always have so many cards and are talking about all these things they can do.&quot;</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/oms_wang2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Steven O'Mahoney-Schwartz &amp; Matt Wang</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Case in point… Steve and Matt attacked with everyone but the Recluse and the Gemhide counting on the rebel to leave the NH team no tenable blocks. Dyke used <a>Sudden Death</a> on the rebel and Siu flashed out Crookclaw to make the <a>Aven Riftwatcher</a> a lethal weapon against the Peregrine. Steve OMS used <a>Fortify</a> to save his flier. Matt Wang asked Siu to dump 80% of his cards with <a>Haunting Hymn</a>. He pitched two lands, Bogarden Rager, and <a>Frozen Aether</a> but kept a <a>Snapback</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Siu and Dyke began to stabilize. <a>Primal Plasma</a> came down as a 1/6 barrier and <a>Brute Force</a> saved their Crookclaw from a pre-blockers <a>Shaper Parasite</a>, allowing it to trade with the Peregrine. Suddenly the path on the ground became rocky and Matt and Steve went to plan B - <a>Screeching Sliver</a>. Matt cast <a>Scryb Ranger</a> and used <a>Evolution Charm</a> to get back the <a>Reflex Sliver</a>. Steve played <a>Poultice Sliver</a> and <a>Screeching Sliver</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>The game was over soon thereafter as they were able to take out seven cards a turn by aiming all their slivers at the top of Dyke's deck. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, April 1: 2:13 p.m. - Round 9: Towel of Power</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/ziegler_sonne.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Eric Ziegler &amp; Gerard Fabiano</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The theme for this weekend's coverage has been focused on Pro Tour Champions and round nine was no different with Gadiel Szleifer stepping into the Feature Match area along with John Pelcak. The duo made up two-thirds of multiple successful three-person teams. With Tim Aten - playing this weekend with Ben Lundquist - they won Grand Prix Chicago. With Chris &quot;Star Wars Kid&quot; McDaniel they got as far as the semifinals at Pro Tour Charleston. </p>&#10;      <p>That semifinal match ignited the firestorm between Gadiel and Wily Edel but there would be no such friction in this match as Gerard Fabiano gets along well with both players. So does his teammate Eric Ziegler - even if Eric didn't exactly realize it…</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Hey BDM Eric and I went to dinner with these guys last night,&quot; laughed Gerard, pointing at their opponents for round nine. &quot;This morning we were looking to see who was in our pod and we saw Szleifer and Pelcak. Ziegler was like, 'Who are those guys?'&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Gadiel's squad won the die roll and Gerard's team did not make any pretense of asking if they would play or draw. While there was some variation from team to team under the old rules, 'play' has been the universal choice this weekend by all the Pros. </p>&#10;      <p>I asked Pelcak if anyone had won a die roll and chosen to gone second. &quot;No. Never. Having one player start with eight cards is like cheating, basically.&quot;</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Phthisis" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Phthisis&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Gerard had <a>Screeching Sliver</a> on the draw while Gadiel's team suspended Ephemeron and Duskwasp. Gerard and Eric cam back with their own Ephemeron and a <a>Phthisis</a> for Ziegler. Pelcak: &quot;Wow!&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Prodigal Pyromancer</a> showed up for Pelcak. Gerard decided to get max usage out of his sliver and used it EOT and again during his main phase hitting a <a>Forest</a> and <a>Empty the Warrens</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>A morph from Gerard was <a>Erratic Mutation</a>ed EOT. Gerard made a show of turning to Ziegler and they discussing whether he should be returning two <a>Island</a>s if he hit a two with the Mutation. He kept selling but Szleifer and Pelcak were not buying. Fittingly it was revealed to be a Trickster when it went binward. </p>&#10;      <p>Pelcak played a <a>Coal Stoker</a> and burned for three, which did not speak well of his hand. Gadiel <a>Cancel</a>ed <a>Bonesplitter Sliver</a> after Gerard resolved <a>Telekinetic Sliver</a>. Then Pelcak <a>Stingscourger</a>ed the Telekinetic and attacked for three. </p>&#10;      <p>Gerard replayed the Telekinetic hoping to contain the impending airforce that was waiting to be mobilized. The fliers took off and Gadiel's team sent in everything but their pinger. Gerard and Eric conferred behind Gerard's trademark towel which has been upgraded to multi-tasker for this event. Normally their for Gerard's notoriously sweaty hands the towel now serves as an ersatz infielder's mitt for obscuring conversation. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/szleifer_pelcak.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Gadiel Szleifer &amp; John Pelcak</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The New Jersey team activated Eric's red totem and ate the <a>Stingscourger</a> taking ten. Gadiel <a>Enslave</a>d <a>Telekinetic Sliver</a>. Gerard's flier came into play on their turn and got in for four - not a good sign for the team that appeared to have the better board position. </p>&#10;      <p>Eric blew up the world with <a>Damnation</a>. Gerard made Ziegler draw four cards with <a>Careful Consideration</a>. There was no play from the Gadiel team with an impending <a>Phthisis</a> which was till effective as a Time Walk and allowed the NJ guys to get in for four with the totem.</p>&#10;      <p>Gadiel and John put up some token resistance - literally when they used a Gerard Fabiano player card to represent a spider token - but Gerard had a hand full of answers and he had helped Eric dig to all his removal and they quickly dispatched the Charleston semifinalists. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, April 1: 3:09 p.m. - Round 10: Twice the Champs</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/yasooka_oosawa.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Shouta Yasooka &amp; Takuya Oosawa</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>As we reported yesterday there were 15 different Pro Tour champions playing on Day One. I had not checked on the count of Champs that made the cut to Day Two but there had been at least one available for the Feature Match area every round on Sunday to this point. I say 'at least' because the team of Shouta Yasooka and Takuya Oosawa each have a Pro Tour trophy to throw their house keys into when the walk in the door. Shouta is also the reigning Player of the Year. Oosawa nearly achieved the very rare feat of two trophies but fell short in the finals of Pro Tour Geneva. </p>&#10;      <p>The American team of Tim Aten and Ben Lundquist had their work cut out for them. Not that we were talking any slouches here. Ben and Tim both made the Top 8 of US Nationals this past year and Ben advanced to make the team. If you read about <b>Magic</b> on the internet - as I suspect you are wont to do - then you are well aware of Tim's prowess as a writer. Tim has also had his fair share of success playing the game as well. He won Grand Prix Chicago with Pelcak and Szleifer and recently finished third at Grand Prix New Jersey all by his lonesome. </p>&#10;      <p>Still a little help never hurts. Aten and Lundquist had not been getting much help when it came to randomization with a die and they groaned when the Japanese players offered the six-sided object to start the process. They claim to have not won any die rolls on the weekend. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Not a one,&quot; sighed Tim. &quot;We have gone first when we have used other methods but that was like three times.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I am not good at die rolls,&quot; deadpanned Lundquist. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;This is the first time that has happened,&quot; observed Aten as Shouta dipped down to six cards. It happened a second time as well when Shouta sent those back and kicked things off with five cards. Oosawa started with eight and suspended Baloth to start the game. </p>&#10;      <p>Suspend was the buzzword for the first few turns as there was a Deepwalker for Ben and a Veiled Oddity for Shouta synched up with the Baloth - a <a>Thallid</a> Shell Dweller for Oosawa. The American team did nothing of interest on turn two besides a <a>Prismatic Lens</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>There was a pair of regenerating Trolls for the Japanese - <a>Hedge Troll</a> and Uthden - on turn three but that was stymied by Aten's <a>Smallpox</a>. Ben floated a blue and emerged from the turn with a 3/3 <a>Primal Plasma</a> thanks to the Lens he had played a turn earlier. </p>&#10;      <p>The five card hand was more or less out of gas for Shouta and the burden was on the Prague champion who played <a>Poultice Sliver</a> after getting in with the Troll. Ben made a <a>Serendib Sorcerer</a> and Tim <a>Assassinate</a>d the headstrong Troll. </p>&#10;      <p>Despite the two card handicap the Japanese team threatened to dominate the ground. There was a <a>Primal Forcemage</a> from Oosawa and a <a>Bonesplitter Sliver</a> from Shouta. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmas07/lundquist_aten.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Ben Lundquist &amp; Tim Aten</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Tim played <a>Rathi Trapper</a> and they fell to nine on the next unblockable attack as the Oddity and Baloth came into play. It would have been one shy of lethal if not for the Sorcerer that worked its <b>Magic</b> on the otherwise 8/8 Baloth. They added <a>Herd Gnarr</a> and <a>Basalt Gargoyle</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>The Americans sent over their Deepwalker and the <a>Primal Plasma</a> to drop the Japanese team down to 12. <a>Stingscourger</a> sent the Baloth back to hand and a <a>Vampiric Sliver</a> appeared on Tim's half of the board. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Sunlance</a> took out the Sorcerer but the tapper was online at this point. <a>Viscid Lemures</a> was just a midrange fatty against the <a>Swamp</a>less Japanese team but that was more than good enough.
