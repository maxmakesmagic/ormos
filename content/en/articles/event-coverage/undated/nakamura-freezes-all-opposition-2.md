Grand Prix Hiroshima is over! After facing typhoon scares and 17 rounds of fierce opposition, Shuuhei Nakamura is the champion. His snow strategy was a success for him all weekend, and it carried him to the finals against Portuguese student Andre Coimbra. Some poor draws and unusual decision from Andre gave Shuuhei the openings he needed, and he exploited those opportunities to the fullest. In two straight games, Shuuhei dispatched his final opponent and claimed the winner's trophy. Congratulations to Shuuhei Nakamura, the 2006 Grand Prix Hiroshima winner!

## Sunday, Aug. 20: 11:05 p.m. - Semifinals Basam Tabet vs. Andre Coimbra

Watching Andre work I'm struck by how often he had [Garza Zol, Plague Queen](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garza+Zol%2C+Plague+Queen) in his opening hand. Do they have a thing? Opposing Andre's removal heavy deck is British pub master Basam Tabet, he of the aggro G/W variety.

Game 1: Basam was stuck on land for too long this game, while Andre leisurely killed Basam's inexpensive creatures, trying to get up to Garza Zol mana. A [Jester's Scepter](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jester%27s+Scepter) from Tabet broke Andre's reverie.

Coimbra: "Everyone plays that card against me!"

![](https://i.ormos.uk/61/3b/613bf18ee75c73e7b870294b17de244efb5b680bd6108d61ecfcae838f8897f3.jpg)*Andre Coimbra*

For all the milling Basam did, it wasn't enough to overcome Andre's hand of [Garza's Assassin](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garza%27s+Assassin), [Surging Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Surging+Flame), Garza Zol, and [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti). Playing just half of those cards was enough to earn Andre game 1.

Tabet: "That was harsh man"  
 Coimbra: "I have…a very good deck.

As the competitors shuffled up for the game 2, Basam revealed an insecurity:

Tabet: "I'd wish you good luck, but you have too many good cards"  
 Coimbra: "Well I wish you good luck anyway."  
 Basam Tabet mulliganing, exclaiming, "You wished too late!"

Game 2: Tabet started with two [Martyr of Sands](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Sands), promising swift beatdown and/or lifegain. Andre languidly replied with his own Martyr, [Martyr of Ashes](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Ashes) AKA The Good One. An upkeep Ashes activation caused serious card disadvantage to Basam. Still, Basam wasn't out of the match yet. [Simian Brawler](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Simian+Brawler)s required an answer, and a late game [Arctic Nishoba](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arctic+Nishoba) was a very relevant entry. Basam decided to take the initiative and attacked with [Arctic Nishoba](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arctic+Nishoba). Andre, tapped out, double blocked with [Greater Stone Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Greater+Stone+Spirit) and an active [Orcish Bloodpainter](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orcish+Bloodpainter). Here Basam made his critical error. Before damage he cast a [Kjeldoran War Cry](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kjeldoran+War+Cry) for one, merely forcing Andre to throw the Bloodpainter at the Nishoba after damage was on the stack. Had Basam waited, Andre *might* have sacrificed a dying guy to deal a point to Basam, allowing him to cast War Cry to save his 6/6 cat. If Andre didn't sacrifice anything, wary of War Cry, then Basam was up a point of life with War Cry still in hand. Sadly for Tabet, that mistake hurt his chances considerably. Garza Zol hastely attacked soon after and it was all over for the British player. Easing the loss was his own bar waiting for him at home, and the fact his amateur status actually earned him more money than the final Grand Prix winner. Not a bad weekend's work. Andre Coimbra moved on to the finals, to face off against Shuuhei Nakamura.

## Sunday, Aug. 20: 11:30 p.m. - Finals: Shuuhei Nakamura vs. Andre Coimbra

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2c/38/2c38720dfb58ffd70694b102e3f28af330a4a8c1e8ab6fbb63ea68da1a992b1a.jpg)*Shuuhei Nakumura*

The crowd grew hushed as the finalists were introduced. Representing the peoples' choice was Japan's own Shuuhei Nakamura. Opposing this Japanese superstar was Andre Coimbra, 20-year old Portuguese student who was more than experienced on the international stage. One of these players would take home a large trophy and a nice check for $2,400. The other would have to settle for a mere plaque, and the still significant $1,700 second place money. The crowd was quiet and respectful of both players; Japan had come a long way since foreigners were executed on sight. Yet the locals still had a favorite, the man who could retain the Japanese championship for one more year. Could Shuuhei keep their love?

Game 1: Andre started off with a very nice hand of four lands, [Garza's Assassin](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garza%27s+Assassin), [Krovikan Rot](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Krovikan+Rot) and [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti). His mana-hungry deck enjoyed having as much land, and time to find land, as possible. Unfortunately for him, Shuuhei's deck was designed to be removal-resistant. Multiple regenerators and counterspells were at Shuuhei's beck and call. This was a trait Andre soon realized as he watched [Rimebound Dead](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rimebound+Dead) strike him over and over, helpless against the 1/1s activated ability. [Frost Raptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frost+Raptor) died to a [Krovikan Rot](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Krovikan+Rot), and a [Greater Stone Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Greater+Stone+Spirit) was tagged with [Rune Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rune+Snag). Still the Dead kept plodding over, until Andre was forced to playing a [Stalking Yeti](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stalking+Yeti) as a simple Hill Giant. A [Zombie Musher](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zombie+Musher) from Shuuhei gave him more resilient offense, although Andre had no snow-covered lands to walk on. Short on snow mana himself, Shuuhei allowed an attacking Yeti to bluff through an attack. [Gutless Ghoul](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gutless+Ghoul) and [Garza's Assassin](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garza%27s+Assassin) came by later, preferably to set up some recursion tricks. That plan was slightly derailed when the Assassin ended up wearing [Gelid Shackles](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gelid+Shackles).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/99/5b/995b2181bd225d9540972573555a90965cd11c4b2d7f33ac788ba872f1aa735c.jpg)*Grand Prix finalist Andre Coimbra*

Andre's hand at this point was near-worthless. His double [Chill to the Bone](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chill+to+the+Bone) had no targets, and a quickly drawn third almost seemed to mock him. [Phyrexian Ironfoot](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Phyrexian+Ironfoot) didn't improve the situation either. A [Greater Stone Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Greater+Stone+Spirit) off the top did resolve for Andre and he was back in the game, actually able to make some damage happen. Shuuhei tried to find the right combination of attacks to win without dying himself; always a worthy puzzle. He only attacked with [Chilling Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chilling+Shade), leaving a [Rimebound Dead](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rimebound+Dead) and [Rimewind Taskmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rimewind+Taskmage) back, along with four lands. Andre had spent his last couple turns drawing double [Martyr of Ashes](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Ashes). If that plan fell though, Andre was a goner. He started off with the Martyr half and Shuuhei suspicious let the creature die, maintaining his four untapped mana. This threw Andre for a loop as he spent a good five minutes re-evaluating his plans. He ended up choosing not to attack with anything, giving Shuuhei free reign to execute his own agenda. That plan was pretty simple: attack with unblockable creatures until Andre died. That's exactly what happened as Shuuhei took the game 1. Andre's dead cards were simply too much of a burden

 Coimbra-0  
 Game 2: The only sound at the beginning of the game was the players shuffling, up until the head judge decided to award Andre Coimbra a warning for slow play. Not the most relaxing announcement when playing in the finals of a Grand Prix. Perhaps due to that incident, Andre kept a sketchy hand of [Martyr of Ashes](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Ashes), [Grim Harvest](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Harvest), [Chill to the Bone](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chill+to+the+Bone) (the only one not sided out) and four lands. Shuuhei was perfectly happy with his initial seven and started off with a [Chilling Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chilling+Shade) and a bunch of snow lands. Andre did draw out of his initial poorness with a Disciple and [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core). Between Disciple, [Magmatic Core](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Core) and a [Feast of Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feast+of+Flesh), Andre was all set to eliminate the [Chilling Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chilling+Shade). Unfortunately, in his haste to avoid another slow play label (the judge made it clear what *that* would mean), Andre forgot about his cumulative upkeep. The Core went away and Andre sacrificed more mental energy kicking himself, right before receiving another warning for misrepresentation. A full-blown Disciple activation did kill [Chilling Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chilling+Shade), but Shuuhei merely replaced it with another one. That Shade attacked Andre for four points, while Nakumura kept one snow-land open for miscellaneous. At the end of Shuuhei's turn, Andre looked at the Shade and the single untapped snow land and aimed [Surging Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20201025202716/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Surging+Flame)s. Shuuhei placed the Shade in the graveyard and was doing recover tricks as the crowd looked on. This reporter had to stop the match and point out the Shade was actually still a 4/4. Andre literally hid his face in his hands over this error; the cumulative effects of zealous judges and a very long day. It was over soon after. Andre couldn't contain the second Shade and conceded the game, match, and tournament to jubilant Shuuhei Nakamura.

Nakumura-2  
 Coimbra-0

Shuuhei Nakumura wins Grand Prix Hiroshima!
