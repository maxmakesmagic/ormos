For those amateurs that just sneaked in it must have been a little overwhelming to find Raphael Levy, Olivier Ruel, Rogier Maaten and Quentin Martin all cracking packs at the same table.</li>&#10;      <p></p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        </p><li>The brothers Ruel are both officially addicted to dragons. Antoine took a <a>Vorosh, the Hunter</a> early in <i>Planar Chaos</i>, and was soon shipped two copies of <a>Oros, the Avenger</a>. Not to be outdone, Olivier has a Vorosh of his own, along with Numot, The Devastator. With a <a>Body Double</a> to effectively bring his count to three too, he seemed pretty happy with his deck. There was even Jhoira in there to allow him to suspend his massive flyers and avoid their awkward mana costs.</li>&#10;      <p></p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        </p><li>Kenji Tsumura is still running around the venue letting everyone know that Blue is the worst colour. Perhaps Rich Hoaen should have dyed his hair Green?</li>&#10;      <p></p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, May 6: 12:31 p.m. - Round 10: Raphael Levy vs. Quentin Martin</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Tim Willoughby</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Yesterday when everyone else was raving about <a>Sprout Swarm</a> in Future Sight, current leader of the Player of the Year race, and hall of famer Raphael Levy was musing about the value of <a>Reality Acid</a> now that Future Sight is in the mix. He felt that there was enough bounce now that it had got that bit more consistent to push it over the top, and now that you see it in pack 2 rather than 3, you could pick up bounce after it that little more easily. </p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="giant dustwasp" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=giant%20dustwasp&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>The common was far from the power card in Levy's deck though. Raph had somehow ended up with <i>four</i> <a>Giant Dustwasp</a>s, and would be looking to make an air assault with his green monsters. His opponent for the first round of draft was Pro Tour Prague top eighter, and <b>magicthegathering.com</b> limited guru Quentin Martin of England. Martin's blue/white concoction wasn't as clearly powerful as Levy's, but it could apply the beats where needed, and, thanks to the wackiness that is <i>Time Spiral</i> block, even featured legitimate removal.</p>&#10;      <p>The game started slowly enough, with Quentin having early <a>Benalish Cavalry</a> and Outrider <i>en</i>-Kor action, while Raph suspended a turn two Dustwasp, and played a morph. Each player raced for a little, and Raph had <a>Jhoira's Timebug</a> to shorten the clock on Quentin a little, bringing his wasp out a turn early. </p>&#10;      <p>Stalled on 3 lands, Quentin played a <a>Sinew Sliver</a>, and lost his Outrider <i>en-</i>Kor to <a>Erratic Mutation</a>. The following turn he played his second <a>Plains</a> and had a little think.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;If I'd drawn that last turn, I don't think I'd have lost this one.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Quentin pondered his position, and declared his attacks, taking Raph to 10, facing down a morph, wasp and a bug next turn. He had a <a>Sunlance</a> for the big flyer, and a <a>Whitemane Lion</a> to block and kill <a>Jhoira's Timebug</a>. Post combat, Raph played <a>Baru, Fist of Krosa</a>, and passed. </p>&#10;      <p>This was a legend that Quentin seemed concerned about racing. He played <a>Lucent Liminid</a> and passed. Raph didn't have a <a>Forest</a> to make his attacks really scary, but he did have <a>Venser's Diffusion</a> to bounce <a>Benalish Cavalry</a> when it double blocked alongside <a>Whitemane Lion</a>. Quentin rebuilt with <a>Jedit's Dragoons</a>, taking him back up to 15, and then was forced to chump block with it when Baru got big thanks to a <a>Forest</a> from Raph. Quentin was back on the air assault with his Liminid, and it was soon joined by a second, though <a>Leaden Fists</a> meant only one would be on the attack. Quentin played a <a>Primal Plasma</a> as a defender to stall the ground, and still had his flying beats unimpeded by Dustwasps. Shortly enough, Levy succumbed.</p>&#10;      <p>Raphael Levy 0 - 1 Quentin Martin</p>&#10;      <p>Levy had the unsurprising turn 2 suspend of <a>Giant Dustwasp</a> to start, while Quentin was all for some suspending himself, with Knight of Sirsi. Raph's draw left him without a play for turn five, having played a <a>Nessian Courser</a> on turn three, but not being able to effectively swing into the face of <a>Oriss, Samite Guardian</a>, and later <a>Poultice Sliver</a>. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpsto07/r10levy.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Levy wonders, 'So where are my Dustwasps?'</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>When <a>Lucent Liminid</a> came along, Raph looked in a bit of a spot. Oriss would make for some very sticky combats for him, and Quentin had enough creatures to both keep back blockers and come in for a little each turn. Raph used <a>Reality Acid</a>, then <a>Dream Stalker</a> to bounce his <a>Reality Acid</a> to take down the 3/3 Enchantment Creature, and passed. </p>&#10;      <p>Quentin attacked, potentially allowing a swing for Raph. This door was quickly closed off though when <a>Judge Unworthy</a> revealed an <a>Infiltrator il-Kor</a> to kill off Raph's best threat.</p>&#10;      <p>Raph started playing the <a>Reality Acid</a> game. He played on <a>Knight of Sursi</a>, and a second on the Infiltrator when it came in. Without a bounce effect, they seemed a little slow, and time was not something that Raph had a great deal of, with an ever growing force being marshalled by the Englishman. </p>&#10;      <p>Raph played a <a>Quiet Disrepair</a> on his own <a>Reality Acid</a> (enchanting Infiltrator <i>il-</i>Kor), and took his beats for the turn, before both his <a>Reality Acid</a>s killed off Quentin's main threats. Levy was heavy on lands, but light on creatures, and in spite of having shrunk Quentin's board a little, it was just not enough.</p>&#10;      <p>Quentin Martin wins 2-0!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, May 6: 2:02 p.m. - Round 11: Kenji Tsumura vs. Mattias Kettel</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Tim Willoughby</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpsto07/r11Mattias.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Mattias Kettil</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>While Mattias is a native Swede, his opponent came a long way to battle this weekend, and has no intention of losing now that he is here. Now, there are a lot of good intentions around, but Kenji Tsumura has all the tools to back them up with action. Widely regarded as one of the best players on tour, Kenji shows no signs of slowing down</p>&#10;      <p>Both players had mulligans, and by the time that the shuffling up was done, Richard Hoaen, across in the other feature match, had knocked Amiel Tenenbaum down to 8 life with <a>Calciderm</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>When things kicked off for Kenji, it was with a <a>Deepcavern Imp</a>, who beat in for two before sacrificing himself to keep <a>Brain Gorgers</a> off the board. An <a>Urborg Syphon-Mage</a> from Kenji met <a>Strangling Soot</a>, and soon Kettil was putting on the pressure with a <a>Reckless Wurm</a>, and then a <a>Haunting Hymn</a> to clear out Tsumura's hand of nothing but lands. Tsumura even used an <a>Evolution Charm</a> to thin his deck in response to the Hymn, but was suddenly in a lot of trouble. </p>&#10;      <p>Kettil had a <a>Char-Rumbler</a>, but before it could do much rumbling Kenji hit it with <a>Death Rattle</a>. He was still knocked down to 9 by <a>Reckless Wurm</a> though before he could play <a>Sporesower Thallid</a> as a blocker. </p>&#10;      <p>When Kettil showed <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a> to clear the way, that was enough for Tsumura to scoop it up. </p>&#10;      <p>Kenji Tsumura 0 - 1 Mattias Kettil</p>&#10;      <p>Kenji immediately went for his sideboard - what Frank Karsten referred to as the best part of the match. Tsumura has frequently pointed out the value of good sideboarding in draft, where it is sometimes forgotten about, and for game 2 he switched out <a>Grave Peril</a> and <a>Midnight Charm</a> for <a>Bog Serpent</a> and <a>Grave Scrabbler</a>. Apparently in Holland, if you don't sideboard at least a couple of cards for game 2, then you've missed your 'Kenji Quota'. The little incremental advantages count.</p>&#10;      <p>For game 2 Tsumura got away without a mulligan, but his opponent wasn't so lucky, going down to six.</p>&#10;      <p>Kenji led off with a <a>Rathi Trapper</a>, who in the absence of creatures got to swing for a turn before the threat of Negator Totem put him back on defence. Kenji had an <a>Evolution Charm</a> for a <a>Plains</a> at the end of one of Mattias' turns, then a <a>Sporesower Thallid</a>. Suddenly the Trapper was in again. This would prove to be its last action of the game, as <a>Arc Blade</a> cut down the 1/1 before going on suspension. </p>&#10;      <p>Kenji played a <a>Pallid Mycoderm</a> and swung. With the Germinator's help he'd soon be able to start making Overruns with his fungus. Mattias played a <a>Bog Serpent</a>, but Kenji attacked in unafraid. He had an <a>Ichor Slick</a> to cycle and play via it's madness cost, drawing him a card, and making the combat good for him. </p>&#10;      <p>Mattias refilled with <a>Brain Gorgers</a> and a morph, and was able to double block <a>Sporesower Thallid</a>, but was stopped by <a>Pallid Mycoderm</a>'s ability and a <a>Feebleness</a>. Sensing the kill was near, Kenji dropped a second <a>Pallid Mycoderm</a> into play. Even without the much talked about <a>Sprout Swarm</a>, he was playing dirty pool with his Saproling generators. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpsto07/r11tsumura.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Kenji Tsumura</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Kettil wanted in on this action, and used <a>Enslave</a> to steal <a>Sporesower Thallid</a>, who remained tapped, and as such couldn't stop Kenji's next swing dropping Kettil to 2. When Kenji played another creature, there was no way the lone stolen fungus would be enough.</p>&#10;      <p>Kenji Tsumura 1 - 1 Mattias Kettil</p>&#10;      <p>For game 3, having seen <a>Enslave</a>, Kenji again looked to his sideboard. <a>Seal of Primordium</a> made the cut, and suddenly <a>Grave Scrabbler</a> just wasn't good enough. </p>&#10;      <p>For Game 3, neither player took a mulligan, but Kettil did stall on just two mountains, having to discard <a>Haunting Hymn</a>, while Kenji had turn 2 <a>Rathi Trapper</a>, turn 3 <a>Deepcavern Imp</a>, and even the <a>Gorgon Recluse</a> to power out with the Imp's echo as a madness enabler. </p>&#10;      <p>Kettil chuckled at his misfortune in the face of such a start. It wasn't even close. He scooped them up</p>&#10;      <p>Kenji Tsumura wins 2-1!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, May 6: 2:17 p.m. - 'It's Red Bull. Honest'</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Tim Willoughby</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpsto07/redbull.jpg">&#10;      <p>Rich Hoaen is pretty lucky to be here today.
