It is Thursday, the day before the Pro Tour, and 411 players have signed up for the Last Chance Qualifier for the remaining four opportunities to qualify for this weekend's tournament – which very likely is under way by the time you read this. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/de/eb/deeb4edf8b1df5e4f31d9353eb7cb71a7f49e34d0810ea8962c19f88734a5fd4.jpg)

There are more than a few notable names on the LCQ sign-up sheet. Steve O'Mahoney-Schwartz and Nicolai Herzog were two names that I knew were going to be playing when I wrote my [preview article](/en/events/2006/ptpra06/cant-hardly-wait-a-prague-playlist) but a few more Pro Tour veterans have shown up to give this format a try. Both Steve and Nico are both former Limited Pro Tour winners. Steve OMS won in Los Angeles during the 1998-99 season after falling in the finals of Pro Tour-Mainz to Matt Place the season previous. His opposition in the finals of L.A. was nothing to sneeze at either, as he had to get past none other than Jon Finkel to hoist his only Pro Tour trophy. 

Nicolai's wins are of a somewhat more recent vintage and against some of the toughest Top 8 fields of all time. During the 2003 season, Herzog finished second a Pro Tour Chicago sandwiched in the final standings between Finkel and a triumphant Kai Budde. The following season Nicolai closed the deal twice. The first time was in San Diego with a Top 8 that included Antoine Ruel, Anton Jonsson, Mike Turian, Masashi Oiso, and a then-unknown Mark Herberholz. He won following Limited Pro Tour in Amsterdam again thwarting Turian and Jonsson as well as Olivier Ruel in their quests for a title. 

On the strength of those two victories, Nicolai headed into Worlds with a narrow lead in the Player of Year Race. After a solid Day One leading into the Limited portion of the weekend, he seemed like a lock for the title barring a meltdown from the best Limited player on the planet.

Herzog has had the fever of late and tried frantically to earn an invite on the Qualifier circuit. He fell short of the mark and as of this writing was registering a deck for the largest LCQ in the history of the Pro Tour. 

“It is a really tough deck,” sighed Herzog as he used up every single minute of the deck registration period to hone the final construction. He could easily have been pulled in three different directions and finally settled on a red-green-blue build that splashed white for Lightning Helix and Faith's Fetters. He had two bombs for Sealed Deck in Master Warcraft and Savage Twister. The latter card, along with the crucial Civic Wayfinder, could not allow him to shift away from green into any other builds.

### Looking ahead to next week…

Fifteen of next week's 16 Invitationalists will be flying straight to Los Angeles on Monday to take part in the annual all-star game. The first format they will be tackling – partially anyway – is the bidding war in the Auction of the Geniuses. Eighteen deck designers from all aspects of the game created Online Classic decks to be used in conjunction with Vanguard Avatars of their choosing.

The creators come from a wide cross section of the game that includes old-schooler Jamie Wakefield, Friggorid spawner John “Friggin” Rizzo, Vintage master Steven Menendian, Standard-setting Resident Genius Mike Flores, House of Cards tenants past and present Chris Millar, JMS and Mark Gottlieb, as well as last year's Resident Genius Tsuyoshi Fujita, and Itaru Ishida (the man behind Kenji Tsumura) – just to name a few. 

The players will bid on each deck with a series of bids based on life and hand size. The default starting bid for each deck is 8 cards and 25 life. Players may bid in lower increments with hand size trumping life total. For example, a bid of 8 cards and 1 life would be outdone by a bid of 7 cards and 25 life. Each players final hand size and life total will be further modified by the stats of the Avatar associate with the deck they end up with. 

Without further ado, I welcome you to a [preview of the decks](/en/events/2006/mi06/2006-magic-invitational-auction-of-the-geniuses) in question. You can play around with them by clicking on the sample hand generator in the upper right hand corner of each deck listing. Keep in mind the modifications and abilities of each avatar.
