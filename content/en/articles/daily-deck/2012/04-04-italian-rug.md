This [past weekend at Grand Prix Turin](/en/events/2012/gptur12/antonino-delver-de-rosa-wins-turin), Marco Cammiluzzi and 2005 US National Champion Antonino De Rosa ran identical seventy-five-card blue-red-green deck (RUG for short) and both players ended up in the elimination rounds! These Top-8-bound teammates took Gerard Fabiano's original decklist and tweaked it for the tournament slightly. And it paid off in spades. The only loss the deck took in the Top 8 was the mirror match, as Antonino defeated Marco on his way to winning the whole thing!

The secret to this deck's success is nestled in its versatility. It ideally aims to play a tempo game, using Delver of Secrets and Tarmogoyf to serve in for plenty of damage while cheap countermagic like Mana Leak and Spell Pierce stop your opponent from stopping your assault.

However, Italian RUG is much more than just a tempo deck. If you don't get the ideal speedy start, that's okay—the deck is set up to play a control game as well! With ten counterspells, Vedalken Shackles, Snapcaster Mage, and some pinpoint removal, you can knock a beatdown or combo deck off of its game plan while you slowly whittle away your opponent's life total with a Tarmogoyf or Vendilion Clique. Even Garruk Relentless joins the fray, removing creatures when you're on the defensive or being a one-planeswalker army when it's time to go on the attack.

Italian RUG's massive success in Turin just highlights how diverse and ripe for exploration the new Modern format is. What will the next major Modern innovation be? Stay tuned to Daily Decks to find out!

Antonino De Rosa's Italian RUG 

Modern – Winner, Grand Prix Turin
