### Feedback

I want to start this article by thanking everyone who wrote to me. A little over a week ago, I posted an invitation to open testing of *Odyssey* Block Constructed. You can view that article [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/02-19-odyssey-block-constructed-week-1). Since then, I received well over 300 emails. A number of people also approached me on IRC, **Magic** Online and in person challenging me to OBC games (offers I never declined) and simply chatting about the format. Even many of the Pro players whose responsibilities to their test groups prevented them from showing their decks would at least make comments and suggestions, ultimately nudging me in the right direction.

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I have even built a 243-card Battle of Wits deck on Magic Online...</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I've received help and advice from a number of respected pros. Among them Adrian Sullivan, Brian Davis, Benedikt Klauser, Alexander Blumke, Andrew Mitchell, Nick Wong, Xavier Petit, Scott Seville, Dan McNeill. I apologize in advance if I did not list your name, for there were many more.

I was unable to reply to more than about 30% of the emails (though I've tried to answer as many as I could). However, I did read all of them and often heeded the advice from pro and amateur alike. It was your emails that showed me the power of Careful Study and Shambling Swarm, and presented me with hundreds of ideas - viable or not - that inspired me to try out more decks and cards I would not have considered otherwise.

So here is what I've got so far:

### Blue-green

There are a lot of ways to build blue-green. Take a look at the deck list I posted a week ago. Since then, I modified it a number of times. A few days ago I entered a 50-person OBC tournament on **Magic** Online with the following version of the deck:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e6/63/e6632160b19524e012f85bdedfa2e88a5cdaf93852c1aec52d72bcf04b4210b2.jpg)4 Basking Rootwalla  
 4 Aquamoeba  
 4 Wild Mongrel  
 3 Werebear  
 3 Breakthrough  
 4 Aether Burst  
 3 Arrogant Wurm  
 4 Obsessive Search  
 3 Circular Logic  
 4 Roar of the Wurm  
 2 Rites of Refusal  
 12 Forest  
 10 Island

I went undefeated through the Swiss rounds, losing in the Top 4 to a black aggro deck that featured Shambling Swarm, Innocent Blood, Chainer's Edict, Nantuko Shade, Shadowmage Infiltrator (no Psychatogs) , Mesmeric Fiend, Mortivore, and Faceless Butcher. This deck, played by Gabriel Krause and designed by Matt Ranks, did not impress me at all at first, but it performed reasonably well and had quite a bit more synergy than I first gave it credit for. I intend to come back to this archetype and test it sometime.

The other finalist - Jon Holt - played a u-g-w threshold deck splashing for Mystic Enforcer. Other Top 8 decks included g-b aggro, my monoblack deck posted last week (with two extra Laquatus's Champion instead of 2 Innocent Blood as the only change), r-g aggro, monoblack Gravestorm creature deck, and another u-g. I was unable to get deck lists, but I watched the matches carefully and have incorporated the ideas I saw in the decks I present here. 

Further testing of this archetype gleaned the following questions and revelations:

*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/60/81/608126616a8d5705f1c1005d5b5fe95283d63e28379bd7bf75806e0835611080.jpg)Careful Study is insanely good. It is practically never a loss of card economy, and it allows the deck to run a very tight mana ratio.
*   Compulsion is very good despite the fact that it is a little slow for a tempo deck. I love Aquamoeba dearly, but Compulsion has replaced it in my current test version.
*   Breakthrough SEEMS really good, but is it necessary with Study/Compulsion? More testing is needed to answer that question. I am currently trying the deck without it.
*   Roar of the Wurm seems like a must to me, but a couple of extra fatties are wanted. I am going to try Krosan Beast. Another option is to splash white for Mystic Enforcer as Jon Holt has done, but I am very reluctant to add another color.

Here is how my current version of the deck looks like:

4 Obsessive Search  
 4 Careful Study  
 4 Compulsion  
 4 Basking Rootwalla  
 4 Werebear  
 4 Wild Mongrel  
 4 Roar of the Wurm  
 2 Krosan Beast  
 4 Circular Logic  
 4 Aether Burst  
 2 Arrogant Wurm  
 11 Island  
 9 Forest

Cards that seem really good but have not made the cut in the current version:

*   Call of the Herd - Is this better than Arrogant Wurm? It may be, but I do not want to give up on the Wurm quite yet.
*   Nimble Mongoose/Seton's Scout - Should the deck's creatures be made faster instead of bigger?
*   Breakthrough - Does this card belong if you already got Compulsion and Study?
*   Deep Analysis - Marc Paschover, who came out of retirement to help me test this format, swears by this card. Tech or garbage?
*   Turbulent Dreams - Since this is pretty much a tempo deck, is more bounce needed?

### Monogreen

A number of players, including Klauser, pointed out that Spellbane Centaur is superior to Seton's Scout in this deck. It seems they are right. The deck is still under-performing but I did not give up on it completely. There aren't enough changes to warrant posting the current list, I will come back to it next week if it shows promise again. In the meantime, Adam Monita pointed me toward the deck lists of another online tournament held by mtgonline.org (see lists [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20050113015335/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.mtgonline.org/tourneys/index.php?fuseaction=viewdecks&authnumber=Mart02220297&&origin=sideboard_strategy_sb20020228a)).

### Monoblack

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/8e/52/8e52d014a831eb808eedea12f9b4dc64e43bdc9e516db01eb6d0d78ccb8b9951.jpg"><br><i>For slower monoblack decks, this card is nuts.</i>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

This deck has gone through the most changes from last week. Gravestorms, although very attractive in theory, just did not work well enough. Several people suggested splashing white for Morningtide in order to make both Gravestorm and Carrion Wurm better, but I doubt it would help significantly enough. I did find a card that was better than Gravestorm in the deck and gave it a lot of flexibility. This card is Diabolic Tutor. The deck now has a feel of Flores Black from back a year and a half ago, with a lot of powerful one-of spells. Although I hated Cabal Coffers at first, Adrian Sullivan convinced me of their quality - and he was right. As it stands, the deck does not do ANYTHING at three mana, so Coffers do not hurt it much. At four mana they break even - and later in the game they just go nuts. So here is how the deck stands now:

The greatest problem with this deck to me, is its inability to deal well with huge amount of untargeted removal, such as the monoblack deck above it sports. A number of others arrived at the same conclusion and are suggesting the Upheaval/Zombie Infestation combo in the deck. It makes sense to me and I will be trying it over the course of next week. If you have a good list for that, go ahead and send it in.

### Other Decks

*   Monored as I have it now beats black and Psychatog decks but loses to any and all decks with green monsters in them. I am going to try a version with far fewer lands, more cheap critters, and Rites of Initiation. I am not all that optimistic, but it may become a metagame call if control is rampant.
*   I have tried several versions of red-green without much success. Still trying to find the one that might work - there is no way that a combination of cheap, fast creatures and burn cannot produce a viable deck.
