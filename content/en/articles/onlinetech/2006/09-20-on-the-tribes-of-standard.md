Solar Flare takes this week's number one slot by a wide margin and last week's top deck, Satanic Sligh, has now fallen to a still respectable 5th place. But these changes appear to be more of a random fluctuation than a real trend. Solar Flare and Satanic Sligh have been defining the Standard metagame in the last couple weeks, along with Structure & Force, Sea Stompy, and Snakes. These decks are here to stay. One week they lose a few percentage points, and the other week they gain a few, but these decks have not left the top half of the popularity table lately and I don't think they will do so anytime soon.

Other decks haven't been that steady. For instance, three weeks ago we saw a [new G/B/R Aggro deck](https://web.archive.org/web/20060920000000/http://boards1.wizards.com/showpost.php?p=9984929&postcount=22) in the Top 8 of the Japanese Nationals. It penetrated the online scene quickly and took first place two weeks ago. Last week it had already fallen out of grace somewhat, down to a ninth place. This week, the deck failed to post even a single Premier Event Top 8 finish! A similar effect could've been observed for the [8 Stone Rain deck](https://web.archive.org/web/20060920000000/http://boards1.wizards.com/showpost.php?p=9987080&postcount=34). These massive swings happen blazingly fast. The online players see a new deck, try it out en masse, and decide two weeks later that the deck wasn't so good after all and abandon it.

Last week we saw a big surge in the popularity of [Counterbalance Ideal](https://web.archive.org/web/20060920000000/http://boards1.wizards.com/showpost.php?p=9986666&postcount=31). It surged to an impressive 3rd place. Searching the combo of Dovescape and Meishin, the Mind Cage out of your deck, followed by Form of the Dragon, has been a winning strategy. The latest innovation, which may be one of the reasons why the deck did so well last week, is the inclusion of Wall of Shards in the sideboard. You can't wish for a better blocker against Satanic Sligh and the drawback is irrelevant for this deck.

We also had three brand new decks this week. One is Dark Boros, which is a crossover between Satanic Sligh and Boros Deck Wins. Its main colors are red and white, splashing black for Dark Confidant and sometimes Hit // Run. Bob Maher's Invitational card is apparently so good that it draws the Boros legion to the dark side. The other two new decks were directly taken out of the top 8 of Dutch Nationals.

If you want to play a control deck, I recommend Structure & Force over Solar Flare. Structure & Force is an amazing deck with a lot of synergy and it is implicitly powerful. While I'm at the topic of Counterbalance Ideal, I strongly advise against picking up the deck and playing it without practicing first. The Elf tribe attempts to put out a semi-unblockable creature (Skarrgan Pit-Skulk or Silhana Ledgewalker) and then the elves boost, they pump, and enhance their unblockable kin to an unmanageable size. Might of Oaks and Blanchwood Armor allow a tiny elf to deal a lot of damage in a short period of time. Elvish Champion is the lord of the tribe. The main weakness of the deck is that it rolls over to Wrath of God and spot removal, mainly because of the card disadvantage the creature enchantments and pump spells impose when your creature is destroyed. Scrying Sheets helps to get some kind of card advantage in the late game, since it can help you get past a land glut.

### Choosing a deck for the tournament

Once I knew how the most popular Standard Tribal Wars decks looked like, I went on to the difficult step: selecting a deck for myself. My first idea was to take Blue/Green Snakes. This is one of the top decks in “normal' Standard. Since it already includes over 20 snakes, it's very easy to carry it over to the Tribal format. I figured that if I transformed a very good Standard deck to the more restrictive Standard Tribal format, and lost almost nothing in the conversion, then I just had to end up with an amazing tribal deck! It's like taking an Extended deck to a Standard tournament after making a couple tiny changes. However, I quickly discovered this reasoning was flawed. The Tribal metagame is different than the Standard metagame; it is less hospitable for snakes. The first reason: the Standard version heavily leans on Umezawa's Jitte and Coat of Arms to turn the 1/1 snakes into real threats. In Tribal, Jitte is banned, and your opponent's creatures will benefit just as much from Coat. The second reason: the most popular tribal decks have amazing bullets against snakes: Humans has Orzhov Pontiff as a one-sided Wrath of God, Elves has Elvish Champion to walk past your blockers, and most control decks play Night of Souls' Betrayal. For those reasons, I decided against snakes and looked further.

For a while, I considered playing the consensus best deck, Black/White humans, until it struck me. Why did I always have to enter a tournament with the goal of winning? I don't have to play the best deck all the time. Why not loosen up a bit, play a creative original deck, and have fun? I talked to my friend Jelger Wiegersma, who gave me a Red/Blue Wizards deck. I liked it, it seemed like a blast to play, and still somewhat competitive. I tweaked Jelger's list somewhat and took this to battle:

The idea of the deck is to hold off early beats with countermagic, bounce, and burn, until you can put down an Azami, Lady of Scrolls - a perfect fit for a tribal deck. You draw some cards, until you find Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind. Once you get both *Legends* in place, your Wizards army turns into a card-drawing, fire-spitting team and winning the game is trivial at that point. A smart part of this deck is that it “cheats” on the Wizards count. As a control deck, you actually don't want to play 20 creatures, rather you want more reactive spells. Martyr of Frost and Sparkmage Apprentice solve this problem. Their main function is as a counterspell and a removal card, while they carry the wizards count up to 20 at the same time! My tournament went like this:

**Round 1: aws32, Mono-green Elves (win 2-1)**

Game 1, he starts off with Silhana Ledgewalker and powers it up with Blanchwood Armor. This puts me on a fast clock and I cannot find Meloku the Clouded Mirror in time to answer it. In game 2, I furiously try to counter all his threats in the early turns, but he manages to resolve Allosaurus Rider – pitching two green cards - when I'm tapped out. Fortunately, I have a Boomerang at the ready, “not Allosaurus Rider's best friend”, in aws32's words. He doesn't find his 7th land for a while and just plays some tiny Elves instead, which I can manage via Electrolyze. At the time he finds his 7th land, I already have Azami, Lady of Scrolls and Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind in place and use Meloku, the Clouded Mirror to make chump blockers. Two huge Allosaurus Riders cannot compete with a Wizards deck that has found all the pieces. Game 3 was easy. He had a weak draw and I can counter most of his early spells. I quickly drop Azami, Lady of Scrolls, then find Meloku the Clouded Mirror to stabilize, and finally play Niv-Mizzet to dominate the board.

**Round 2: sandydogmtg, Mono-Green Elves (lose 1-2)**

He likes to do damage quickly. Turn 1 he plays Llanowar Elves and on turn 2 he casts not one, but two Giant Growths, attacking for seven. That puts me on a low life total, but he's out of cards right away. When he tries to beef up his Elf with a Might of Oaks, I foil it with Boomerang, and then play an Azami for the win. Game 2, he makes a huge Skarrgan Pit-Skulk. I was able to counter his first Blanchwood Armor, but don't have a counter left for his second copy. I never draw a Boomerang and quickly succumb to his unblockable monster. Game 3, he doesn't have any of his pesky Auras, just a couple puny 1/1 Elves. I thought I was winning when Azami, Lady of Scrolls started drawing a ton of cards, until he unloaded his hand. Giant Growth, Giant Growth, Might of Oaks, all on Silhana Ledgewalker. My Boomerangs and Electrolyzes couldn't target his Silhana Ledgewalker, so I lose.

**Round 3: garvin, Red/White/Black Angels (win 2-0)**

He plays a control deck, which shows in game 1. I try an Azami, Lady of Scrolls, he plays Wrath of God. I try it again, he plays Final Judgment. Boring. I play Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind, he plays Wrath of G… …no, wait, he's out of mass removal! Instead, he puts down Night of Souls' Betrayal. Well that doesn't really scare Niv-Mizzet. In fact, his Night of Souls' Betrayal was beneficial for me, since it allowed me to shoot down his Adarkar Valkyrie with my Mouth of Ronom. My big Dragon draws a handful of cards, I counter some of his spells, and after a while he has nothing in hand or in play, while I still have Niv-Mizzet and full grip. Needless to say, I won that one. In game 2, I counter every Dragon he plays, and then he cast Cranial Extraction on Niv-Mizzet. I rip Azami, Lady of Scrolls off the top, and start drawing some cards. He gets out Blinding Angel, but it loses in a fight to Meloku the Clouded Mirror. A bunch of Illusion tokens fly over for the win.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_fk6_picMain_en.jpg)

**Round 4: Yawgatog, Blue/Green Snakes (win 2-1)**

In the early turns of the first game, we are both doing a little dance, slinging creatures, being weary of opposing countermagic, while at the same time trying to use our own countermagic optimally. It was quite gracious, really, but when the dust settles I am in a better position. I have resolved Azami, Lady of Scrolls and Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind and use their synergy to take the game. Game 2, he gets out a couple snakes along with Seshiro, the Anointed, while I have Meloku the Clouded Mirror. I do some math, and then I go all-in on Meloku, returning all my lands to my hand. I have just enough power worth of creatures to win the game in two turns and I have just enough chump blockers to survive his attack. A great plan, except … I forgot that Chord of Calling existed. At end of turn, suddenly more snakes came into play, completely destroying my combat calculations and as a result, I lost the game. In game 3, I can Boomerang a Simic Growth Chamber twice, generating massive tempo advantage. I resolve Meloku the Clouded Mirror, counter some of his spells, and win.

**Round 5: Brettzky, Black/White Sprits (lose 1-2)**

Game 1 is a card advantage race between my Jushi Apprentice and his Dark Confidant. We both draw extra cards, but he gets to play spells in the meantime. He gets out Tallowisp and searches multiple Pillory of the Sleepless. Those shut down my blockers, and a couple tiny spirits do me in. Game 2, I counter his early threats, take down a Celestial Kirin with Mouth of Ronom, and then start drawing cards like a madman. My Wizard army grew exponentially, and Brettzky's only hope was that I'd deck myself. I pointed out that I'd draw Meloku the Clouded Mirror or Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind before I'd deck myself, so he just scooped it up. Game 3 was really close. He gets out Dark Confidant and I don't have a removal spell, so my only hope is to race him with Meloku, the Clouded Mirror. Illusion tokens and life loss from his Confidant get him down to a low life total, but eventually the sheer amount of card advantage generated by Dark Confidant kicks in. He finds Ghost Council of Orzhova and Shining Shoal to stay at exactly one life and wins the damage race by a hair.

**Round 6: m0x, Mono-Green Elves (win 2-0)**

Game 1, he keeps a one-land hand with a couple mana Elves. I have Sparkmage Apprentice and Electrolyze to take down his mana supply. This gives me enough time to easily play Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind, which wins the game shortly after. In game 2, I allow a couple small Elves to resolve, keeping Hinders back for bigger threats, such as Elvish Champion. The small Elves take me down to a low life total, but I just take my time to gain control. Eventually I have established complete control of the game and have basically won it, even though he is at 20 life and I am at 2. That game illustrated well how control decks can use their life total as a resource.

**Round 7: Starvin\_Marvin, Blue/Green Snakes (win 2-0)**

In game 1, he builds an army of Ohran Vipers, Patagia Vipers, and various other 1/1 snakes. I just shrug and play out Azami, Lady of Scrolls along with Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind and shoot down all those slithering creatures. I mean, in a fight, who do you think wins; a measly snake or a mighty wizard? 

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_fk6_pic1_en.jpg)

Game 2 showed that my creatures are indeed better than his in the long term. He plays Ohran Viper, I play Meloku the Clouded Mirror. He cast Patagia Viper, I cast Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind. Guess who won? Wizards simply wield better magic than a bunch of crawling reptiles. 

### Concluding…

So I posted a 5-2 record overall, just missing the Top 8 on tiebreaks. I had a lot of fun with my deck, and it proved to be quite good too. Tribal Wars was a nice break from the tense and complex competitive Standard scene. The format is more casual, it's simpler and straightforward and I definitely enjoyed it.
