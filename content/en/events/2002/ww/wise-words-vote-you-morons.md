### 

“What’s the point?”

How sad. The above is the answer I’ve gotten from most pros when chatting about who they’d voted for in casting their Invitational votes. Finkel, Mowshowitz, Kibler, Wise. Finkel, Mowshowitz, Kibler, Wise. Finkel, Mowshowitz, Kibler, Wise. Blah blah blah blah blah blah blah blah. How boring. The voters know who the winners are before the voting starts and therefore assume they needn’t bother voting and that one extra ballot obviously won’t be making any kind of difference.

Ladies and Gentlemen, the President of the United States, George W. Bush.

Anyone remember Al Gore? He was that mild mannered respectable Democrat sort (the one who never smoked, not the one that didn’t inhale) who everyone knew would win the U.S. election back in ’00, until 57% of registered voters decided there was no real point in voting as the result was a foregone conclusion anyways and frankly, they had more important things to do that day, like debating the merits of fork vs. spoon in the eating of Campbell’s chunky soup, measuring how the comfort provided by two-ply toilet paper might warrant the additional cost, or insisting Millstone is card advantage because it gets two opposing cards out of library on each and every turn.

In case I’m not being subtle enough, let me make this painfully clear:

**Vote, you morons!**

I hope you single-ply types got the message. Every vote counts, and what’s more, every vote is important, because in placing your votes, you’re not only deciding upon which four un-invitees will get free trips and good times, but also making your own declaration on who you think is most deserving, and whose progress you most want to follow on ***Magic** Online* once the tournament gets underway. That’s right folks, all that can be yours with sixteen checkmarks followed by a click of the submit button.

The thing is, I can understand players being driven to skip the process. After all, sixteen votes for four slots seems a little silly in that a lot of the time, in a field of competitors who don’t get the benefit of television or otherwise constant exposure, often those votes will be reduced to name recognition. ‘Finkel, Mowshowitz, Kibler, Wise’ make a little more sense now?

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/a822.jpg)Jonny Magic is only the last Greatest Player Ever before our present Greatest Player Ever, and his name is synonymous with the game. Zvi provides strategic advice and insight unmatched in the Constructed arena. Kibs is playing better than ever before while constantly contributing in communal ways, at the same time holding a place in the minds and hearts of all children thanks to one Rith, the Awakener, while I, well, I guess I write sometimes and got carried to a Top 4 finish in Boston…crashed and burned at Yokohama and Berlin…um…well, the others are all certainly solid citizens.

Be that as it may though, while I do believe the contributions the ‘automatics’ have made combined with consistency over the years makes each viable for your vote in their own way, there are other names that must be considered before you cast that final tally, which, assuming you aren’t from Florida, will most certainly count (sorry, couldn’t resist… Florida vote jokes… I’ve been reduced to late night talk show banter… someone shoot me? Anyone? Next thing you know, Wise Words will feature stupid **Magic** tricks and me throwing pencils at a television camera). Here’s a look at some of names who do or don’t deserve your votes, at least from this man’s point of view:

**Jens Thoren, Daniel Zink, Jordan Berkowitz, Kai Budde, Justin Gary, Mattias Jorstedt, Dirk Baberowski, Bob Maher, Osyp Lebedowicz, Carlos Ramao, Nicolai Herzog, Masashi Ooiso**

What do these guys have in common? Simple: they’re already invited. Be it due to regional superiority, overall pro point flux, singular successes or an affinity for Latin dance (hey Osyp!), these players have already gotten their invites, meaning a vote for any one of them is pointless. To clarify, voting for Kai would be like voting for Abraham Lincoln: a great president, arguably the greatest of all time, but the man already served in office and died some 140 years ago. Voting for him now won’t do much. 

Congrats to the twelve of you. 

Sincerely,  
 The Bubble

### Tomi Walamies

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/a737.jpg)Tomi’s was one of the first boxes I checked when filling out my Invitational ballot, voting for players who I felt had combined strong years with communal contributions and/or been teammates of mine at some point over the millennia. Tomi finished second in last year’s Invitational in Seattle, after which he managed to finish second in Venice and win his third National Championship in a row before advancing to the finals of the team competition at Worlds. Beyond his playing accomplishments, Tomi also writes some of the funnier material on the web as far as **Magic** goes over at Star City, which usually means there’ll be some pretty good post-Invitational storytelling going on should get the invite.

### Jon Finkel

For the first time since 2000, Jon brings to the table vote-worthy merits aside from carrying the poster boy title for a few years in the late nineties. Finkel made it to the Semifinals in Chicago after surviving “the greatest draft table ever”, then repeated in Yokohama, prompting future President Schwarzenegger to quip “he’s back” (by the way, I think the only ‘back’ reference missing from T3 was “Baby got back”. Could you imagine a beaten up Arnie quipping thus as he cast an awkward yet enamored glance at his female adversary as she walked away from him in a desperate search for something to hurl? That my friends, would have been brilliant). Jon is not unlike Michael Corleone in the third Godfather installment, complaining that every time he thinks he’s gotten out, WotC paychecks keep pulling him back in, but let’s face it: despite his protestations, Finkel loves the game, and he’s done a lot to further it. I certainly don’t have my name entangled with Richard Garfield’s on promotional products. Do you?

### Brian Kibler

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/a845.jpg)Recently graduated from University, Kibler is playing better **Magic** now than he ever has, as evidenced by his victory in Boston and a number of near-misses. That said, he still hasn’t found that one big jewel for his Top 8 crown this year, and his writings have slowed to a crawl. If you’re looking to vote purely on play accomplishment, I think next year is Brian’s year, but for now, solid play, deck building and communal contributions make him worthy of your check mark. Then again, I don’t think I needed to tell you that.

### Gabriel Nassif

There’s no other player in the game I can think of that has so many “experts” expecting a breakthrough in the near future that could push their name into Top 5 contention. Gab made the finals in the Yokohama Masters, playing Enchantress, which is pretty cool in and of itself, and he also pioneered the expression “that’s for sure, that’s for sure”. For the moment, his Top 8 in Venice, and Yoko success will have to suffice as far as his resume goes.

### Dave Price and Chris Pikula

Okay, I’ve known these two for about seven years, have always gotten along well with both and thought their presence was a good thing for Magic. With all that noted, I can say with all certainty that you should ABSOLUTELY NOT VOTE PRICE OR PIKULA FOR THE INVITATIONAL. Sorry to yell, but these guys have a combined 10 pro points this year. Chris in all likeliness got voted in last year thanks to Meddling Mage, while Dave got voted in based on his writing, and the true competitor’s spirit that saw him qualify for PT after PT despite poor results thanks to his love of the game. Well, here’s your reality check folks: Chris is married, working and has two pro points this year, and won the Invitational so long ago that The Meddler has rotated out of Standard. Price, on the other hand, never writes anymore save the odd request for Invitational votes and/or Invitational card submission ideas, and at the same time has lost that competitor’s spirit, q’ing for Venice then not bothering to show, and then not making the effort or trip to Yokohama or Berlin. The man announced his retirement. Please, if you want to reward communal contribution and long standing, I’ll completely applaud your wish to do so, but at least cast your votes on players who are still around. Diatribe over, you may start hurling your stones.

### Alex Shvartsman

Alex and I aren’t getting along too well right now, but I can stay objective in this assessment. Alex does make communal contributions and has been around for a long time, giving his heart and soul to the game, a fact that in some years has forced me to acknowledge his being deserving of one’s vote. ’02-’03, however, has seen a reduction in writing thanks to his opening his own store, while at the same time, as I’m sure Alex would admit, has not been one of his more stellar campaigns. If you were casting four votes, I’d tell you Alex isn’t deserving. With sixteen votes however, even I couldn’t fault you for finding a place for him on your ballot. 

### Jin Okamoto

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/a454.jpg)Well, you know his name now thanks to his coming second at Worlds, but what you may not know is that most Japanese players were calling Okamoto their country’s best before Berlin. The winner of the last APAC Championships in 2001, Jin has since been posting strong finishes, earning enough pro points to qualify him repeatedly for the Masters despite not having that one Top 8 finish that usually is instrumental in qualifying players for those payout tourneys. Okamoto, whose writing for *Sideboard Japan* goes unrecognized on this side of the ocean thanks to translation issues, had more pro points in 2002-03 than any other player not already qualified. Probably among the Top 4 in terms of most deserving, his was the second name I checked off in casting my ballot (you’ll never guess who number one was).

PS: If Okamoto doesn’t make the Invitational, it seems far less likely that Okamoto Djinn will be one of the submissions.

### Rob Dougherty

Rob may be the single most deserving player to never get an invite. He owns multiple Magic stores, he writes often and well, he keeps picking up Top 8s too fast for me to keep track of how many he has, is the heart and soul of the Pro Tour’s second best team and may well be the best Constructed player in the world. As a matter of fact, Rob’s teammate Dave Humpherys just finished Top 4 at Worlds, and yet he’s not getting his own paragraph here because Rob just seems the better argument in just about every way. I’m not quite sure how his monumental pro point tallies don’t ever get Rob qualified for the tournament…I guess he just always finishes in the 10-20 range for Player of the Year. Regardless, one has to assume he’ll find his way in eventually. 

### Bill Jensen

Is Jensen the best player in the world not named Budde or Finkel (this based on a quick question asked at Worlds. With no offense intended towards Jon, I’m not sure he’s number 2)? I personally rank him behind Baberowski and Maher, but both of them say Bill is better. His natural gifts are obvious, and no one is better at picking up a new set and understanding how it plays, but despite his two Top 8 finishes this year, it seems like there’s just a missing spark or something…I can’t really put my finger on it. Does he deserve your vote? He had a strong year, is one of the best players in the world and will likely be sitting at six online poker tables while playing his Invitational matches. Sounds like a solid choice to me.

### Jeroen Remie

Okay, I’m fully aware that Jeroen’s chances of making the Invitational, to coin a phrase, are un-good. Be that as it may, he’s been on a pretty remarkable Constructed streak of late, with Top 8 finishes at Chicago and Berlin adding to his 6-0 run in Extended at Worlds a year ago. Likely the best Rock player in the world, Jeroen has also devoted time to writing for Brainburst Premium, and with both tournament excellence and communal contributions fulfilled; he’d be a worthy candidate. 

### Zvi Mowshowitz

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/72/7472bb3c7eea55c0eec299ffde45f7275dc181183306c4c15b0bf6411b00ef3c.jpg)Zvi’s a strange guy. Most everyone who meets him can agree that the term “status quo” doesn’t really apply, but you know what? **Magic** is the field where strange comes to play. Whether you’re accepting of this fact or one of the morons who searches for self-fulfillment in the making fun of others, you have to admit that as both a player and a writer, Zvi has been remarkably consistent making unequaled contributions to the community in sharing his understanding of Constructed and deck building, all the while serving as a skilled teacher who bestows knowledge both through spoken word and action. I have closer friends who I voted for over Zvi, but if I were filling out my ballot without knowing any of the players in question (a reflection on my feelings towards others, not a slam on Zvi), he’s have to be one of the first choices I’d make. 

### Peter Szigeti

The other night, sitting in my apartment, a Seinfeld repeat came on in which there was constant discussion of Superman’s old foe Bizarro, a failed cloning experiment whose result was a ‘Superman’ composed on inorganic material, who spoke backwards and did everything wrong, thinking it was right. The joke was eventually taken too far by DC comic publishers, eventually leading to an entire Bizarro world, where other bizarre heroes like Yellow Ring (An allusion to Green Lantern whose ring was of course always without power as he was too dumb to recharge it) always fought valiantly to ruin the day, only to have the original Superman save it.

I voted for Peter Szigeti.

Does the Bizarro departure make more sense now?

Peter’s been hanging around the PT long enough to have established himself as a player, but what’s really struck me about him this year is his writing. Peter’s always made “contributions” to the online community, but this year they’ve taken on a more positive attitude than in the past, and the man really does want to do everything he can to help the game. One of the Pro Tour’s true personalities, his writing on Togit.com has been awesome of late, but even more importantly, his presence at the Invitational would definitely make for a more dynamic event. This is after all the man who was seen running around GP-LA with a prosthetic leg that didn’t belong to him, and the only player issued a match loss for wiping his butt with a Searing Flesh. This vote went totally against my grain, but sometimes you’ve got to go for it. 

There are other names that should probably be discussed in a debate like this one. Eivind Nitter, Olivier Ruel, Darwin Kastle, John Larkin, Marco Blume, Eugene Harvey, Tsuyoshi Ikeda, Jeff Cunningham, Anton Jonsson…. the list goes on, but this has gotten too long already, so its time to call it quits. Do me a favor: when you go to vote for the Invitational, don’t just mark off the sixteen names you recognize. Instead, either vote for the four you really want to make it, or do a little research and figure out who most deserves it, or even figure out who you like, not who you recognize for good or bad. We don’t want things getting to stale, do we? That said, even if you just want to close your eyes and click on 16 names, its better than not voting at all. You know where to find the ballot; you’re already on the site. Take a few minutes and vote, you morons. Unless, of course, you want George Bush playing in this year’s Invitational, which I must admit, could be kind of amusing.

Have a good week.
