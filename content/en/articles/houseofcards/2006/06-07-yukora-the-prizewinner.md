To borrow a phrase, I'm the third best player in my playgroup. The results don't lie. Well, sometimes they do - like the time my results told my (ex-)girlfriend how she looked in those jeans. But most of the time they're honest. I recently finished third in a draft. With four other people. I guess that isn't really that great, but it's [better than 499th](https://web.archive.org/web/20060607000000/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgevent/gptoro06/stand11). Some of the things I liked best about Mark Gottlieb's House of Cards were the Reject Rare Drafts (which can be found [here](/en/articles/houseofcards/2005/06-28-reject-rare-draft), [here](/en/articles/houseofcards/2004/03-18-reject-rare-draft-2), and [here](/en/articles/houseofcards/2005/03-17-reject-rare-draft-3)). That's where you and your friends rustle up forty-five of your most unloved Rare cards, make them into makeshift booster packs and then draft them. I did one of these drafts recently, and I'd like to file this report.

### Dramatis Personae:

**Justin “Aura Shards” O'Neil**  
 A fencer, but not like Zorro. He builds fences. I'm pretty sure that every time I'm relying on keeping an artifact or enchantment in play for my deck to work, Justin is playing Aura Shards. I don't blame Justin, I blame the card.

**Greg “Teroh's Faithful” Fickes**  
 My arch-nemesis. Greg once tried to build a Bear tribal deck, but he didn't have enough Bears. His solution was to use Boars, as well, since, hey, they're just one letter off. “Bears and Boars” was born. 

**“Yawgmoth's” Will Johnston**  
 Will's a competitor and as such likes casting stuff like Psychatog, Yawgmoth's Bargain, and Umezawa's Jitte. Luckily for us, he's just as likely to cast Confusion in the Ranks and Radiate.

**Rob “Searing Wind” Barkley**  
 Rob can usually be found burning something (with **Magic** spells) or casting seven-mana creatures. What he is most known for are the names he gives his decks. “Wake and Blitz,” “Destruction Absolute,” and “Let Me Think About That … No!” are my personal favourites, maybe because they remind me so much of my college years.

**Chris “No Nickname” Millar**  
 When I'm not missing deadlines, I'm working the Grand Prix circuit where, so far, I have one Top 500 finish to my credit.

**Round 3 Vs. Greg**

Greg decided to play a Constructed deck. As the only player drafting White as his primary colour, he ended up with enough “playables” to make a two-colour, sixty-card deck. The fact that his deck was incredibly slow, and almost every card in it stalled the game somehow, made decking his opponent a near-viable strategy.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_cm22_picMain_en.jpg)**Game 1**  
 I put on the pressure early with Vampiric Spirit and Troublesome Spirit, but Greg had Solar Tide to clear the board. Greg's Caribou Range wouldn't normally have been a big deal, since I have so many evasive guys, but all I had was Yukora. According to the flavour text, “it took ninety-nine monks to weave the spell that trapped Yukora.” I guess they don't have Caribou in Kamigawa, because apparently a Range of them does the trick just as well. Other habitats of non-threatening or herbivorous Northern Ontario woodland critters that are hostile to arch-Demon Yukora include:

Squirrel Nest  
Bearscape  
Snake Pit  
 Elk's Lodge.

I attack with Yukora, just to whittle down his Caribou forces, and naturally he has Righteousness. Greg plays Pegasus Refuge. Gee, it's a good thing Yukora is long gone, because this safe haven for flying ponies would put a real cramp in his infernal style. I answer with Sengir Vampire, and clone it with Dance of Many. Next turn, I attack with both, you know, to whittle down his Pegasus forces, and naturally he has Hold the Line. I should've quit right there. 

He keeps making Caribou tokens, and I'm forced to Last Word his Hair-Strung Koto. I'm decked anyway. So, in a single game, I managed to walk right into Solar Tide, then Righteousness, and then Hold the Line. Then I hit my head on a low-hanging tree-branch, sat on a Whoopee Cushion, and pulled Rob's finger. Not a good day for me.

**Game 2**  
 I bring in Sway of the Stars and Dystopia from the sideboard. Troublesome Spirit, Sengir Vampire get Aurified. Greg adds to the Wall-fest by dropping Opal-Eye and Ageless Sentinels. Hero's Demise and Dance of Many kills Opal-Eye and Chainer. For some reason, I decide to try to mill him with Dreamborn Muse. He's a bit manascrewed, and Dystopia is keeping his castable permanents off the table, but Caribou Range is allowing Greg to keep pumping out White creatures to sacrifice. Eventually, I Reweave the Swamp with Caribou Range on it, and Dystopia finally eats the Aurification, freeing up my guys. I feel comfortably in control, since he is almost out of cards and life, and either the Muse or my flyers will be take him down. If they couldn't, I had Sway of the Stars in hand as a back-up. Unfortunately, that was the last card I wanted to have in my hand when Greg played Soulscour! With no permanents, and a very short library thanks to my Dreamborn Muse, I scooped up my cards.

*Match Record 1-2, 2-5*

### Yukora, the Pile-Maker Returns

**Losers' Bracket Vs. Justin** 

**Game 1**  
 I have another aggressive start, but Justin has Collective Restraint and Skeleton Ship to blunt my offense. I'm still able to squeeze through some damage with Troublesome Spirit and a 2/2 Thought Devourer, and when Skeleton Ship suffers a Hero's Demise, that's game.

**Game 2**  
 Justin's Fleeting Image held off my Vampiric Spirit until I took a gamble and made a second one with Dance of Many, leaving me at ten life. Unfortunately for me, he plays Do or Die and I lose one of the Spirits. We spend the next few turns trading creatures and getting in a little damage here and there. He finds a Planeswalker's Fury, which will end the game on his next turn, but I play Heightened Awareness, dumping my hand. I dodged one bullet, but he had another, killing me with a sideboarded Mindblaze. 

**Game 3**  
 I start things off with the Raven Guild Master, and get super, super greedy and clone it with Dance of Many. Rathi Assassin and Gangrenous Goliath clog up the ground, but I draw and play a Troublesome Spirit. So we race. He's way ahead, and Planeswalker's Fury puts him even further ahead. Luckily, Heightened Awareness gave me Wave of Reckoning off the top and clears his side of the board. He spends the next few turns burning me with the Fury, revealing my freshly drawn Dominaria's Judgment and then Last Word, knocking me to two. During my turn, I play Dominaria's Judgment on my Troublesome Spirit and counter it with Last Word. I attack and drop him to 11.

I play Yukora because I don't want to risk him pulling it with Planeswalker's Fury. Once again, he plays Do or Die and I bin the Troublesome Spirit. I attack with Yukora and make a Lumengrid Augur. Justin keeps using the Fury, but since I'm stockpiling excess land, he's been whiffing. He makes Greel, Mind Raker. I attack again with Yukora, and he lets it through, dropping to one. Here I get paranoid, and decide to clear the board with Forced March just in case Yukora decides to make an unexpected exit.
