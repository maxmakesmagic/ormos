This past weekend showed us the first glimpses of *Gatecrash* in Standard play. And *Gatecrash* has not disappointed thus far! Many notable decks at the most recent StarCityGames Standard Open in Atlanta, GA, featured new cards, both strategically and "just" as mana-base components. Yes—and this probably has nothing to do with beloved Boros Week—there were some Sacred Foundries (and company) kicking butt and burning down opposing defenses. For those who love a 5/5 for five mana, [Obzedat, Ghost Council](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Obzedat%2C+Ghost+Council) made its first—presumably of very many—Top 8s. But in Atlanta and abroad, the biggest impact, at least thus far, seems to be from [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground)... [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground) in a variety of not just creature decks, but combo ones too!

*[Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm), [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner), [Sacred Foundry](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sacred+Foundry), [Skullcrack](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skullcrack)*

  

Andrew went with the typical pre-*Gatecrash* red beatdown shell and took a "Boros" angle to it rather than the pre-existing straight red or Rakdos angles. [Rakdos Cackler](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos+Cackler) doesn't need-need black mana at the one... red mana is just fine. Andrew ran up the curve from one-drops to [Ash Zealot](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ash+Zealot), [Hellrider](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellrider), and huge [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite) at the five. 

  

The big switch here is [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) at three instead of [Pyreheart Wolf](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyreheart+Wolf) or [Hellhole Flailer](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellhole+Flailer)... from this writer's perspective, [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) is a huge upgrade relative to the previous options. In a deck—of however many colors—that can hit ![](http://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/red.gif)![](http://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/red.gif)![](http://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/red.gif), it is just fantastic. A 3/3 creature for three mana is almost "green" in playability... and this one has multiple crazy abilities! 

Taking down [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk) in combat *and* living to tell the tale? First strike is quite the big game. 

[Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) certainly taxes the opponent's resources—up to and primarily life total—if and when it is answered. This is a creature that will change how Standard will be played. No longer is blocking and trading with value an easy answer to red aggro. 

[Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) rewards the deck higher up on the curve. The "really" red decks—rather than the black-based Rakdos beatdown decks—are much more vulnerable to mass removal than the ones with [Gravecrawler](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gravecrawler) and [Geralf's Messenger](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geralf%27s+Messenger). In addition to its damage-based abilities, [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) is a counter to [Supreme Verdict](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Supreme+Verdict) and the like. 

  

...but what about those damage abilities?

Ka-pow!

[Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) is like a Char to the face for one less mana... and no collateral damage. 

And combined with [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite)? 

*10.*

...and it might not even be the best two-mana burn card available in this deck.

  

Two mana, 3 damage, ho-hum... *except* when the opponent is banking on [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk) or [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation). Yes, the opponent will still be getting that big body or drawing a bunch of cards... but a big reason those cards are so popular—and so powerful—is the life swing they offer. No more. 

With its new *Gatecrash* tools, Boros might be the deck that most redefines the rules of what is viable and how to play Standard. Attack decks were already great in Standard, but [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) and [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) really change the rules and shift the math of the format.

Last year, Hall of Famers Jon Finkel and Jelger Wiegersma crashed into the Top 8 of [Pro Tour *Dark Ascension*](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/magazine/article.aspx?x=mtg/daily/eventcoverage/ptdka12/welcome) with a [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) build featuring [Drogskol Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drogskol+Captain), [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists), and [Lingering Souls](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lingering+Souls). While [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) is still in Standard, the disappearance of [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder) has severely restricted that cross-format All-Star's efficacy... 

But if you can't go little, how about big?

  

[Obzedat, Ghost Council](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Obzedat%2C+Ghost+Council) is one of the most exciting new threats to hit the scene via *Gatecrash*. Exceedingly mana efficient on its face, Obzedat *also* has a stack of abilities that allows it to crush all different opponents from all different angles. Control? Try stopping this Spirit with sorcery-speed removal. Beatdown? Point after point, life totals going both ways (neither of which is good for the other guy). Obzedat is hard enough to deal with normally... but add [Drogskol Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drogskol+Captain)? *Not exactly a fun time for the opponent.*

  

[Blind Obedience](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blind+Obedience) helps to cut off the opponent's angles. Like Obzedat, [Blind Obedience](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blind+Obedience) grinds life points over and over—giving you a solid buffer against beatdown decks, as well as a way to kill an opponent putting you in lockdown without having to use the red zone. But, in addition, [Blind Obedience](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blind+Obedience) can turn off [Restoration Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Restoration+Angel) shenanigans (no mid-combat surprise blocks for you!) and takes the oomph out of [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite), [Hellrider](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellrider), and [Falkenrath Aristocrat](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Falkenrath+Aristocrat). 

  

And with [Blind Obedience](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blind+Obedience) and Obzedat giving you such a life-point buffer? 

What are a couple of points between friends?

[Devour Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Devour+Flesh) isn't *quite* [Diabolic Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Diabolic+Edict)... but it's the [Diabolic Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Diabolic+Edict) we have. Once in a blue moon you will target yourself, and [Devour Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Devour+Flesh)—a card designed to end creature's lives—might just keep *you* alive!

One of the exciting new decks enabled by *Gatecrash* is Humans Combo Reanimator. 

[Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise) was always a big game with cards like [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells), but now you have a legitimate look at an infinite combo deck in Standard. 

The early setup is similar to other Reanimator decks... [Grisly Salvage](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grisly+Salvage), [Mulch](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulch), and so on. The goal is to dump cards into the graveyard in various combinations. Lots of Humans in the graveyard is fine... you can either [Unburial Rites](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unburial+Rites) or hard-cast [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise) and they will all come back. 

<table><tbody><tr><td>[card]Unburial Rites[/card]</td><td>[card]Angel of Glory's Rise[/card]</td></tr></tbody></table>

  

The unique setup of this deck is to assemble [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise), [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter), [Cartel Aristocrat](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cartel+Aristocrat), and some way to take advantage of an infinite loop—[Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) or [Cathedral Sanctifier](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cathedral+Sanctifier). 

How you get the catalyst cards online is up to you. [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) and/or [Cartel Aristocrat](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cartel+Aristocrat) can be in play or they can be in the graveyard. You might have most of the tools already on the battlefield when you draw [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter). The most common setup is probably just to dump a ton of cards down and target [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise) with [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter) in the graveyard. 

<table><tbody><tr><td></td><td><div>[card]Fiend Hunter[/card]</div></td></tr></tbody></table>

  

This is what will happen:

*   [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise) will enter the battlefield.
*   In our scenario, it will quickly be joined by [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter), [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells), and [Cartel Aristocrat](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cartel+Aristocrat) (all of which are Human).
*   [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter) will target [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise).
*   [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) will give you 2 life and a 2/2 Wolf.
*   [Cartel Aristocrat](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cartel+Aristocrat) will gobble up [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) (putting it back into the graveyard), and then [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter).
*   [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise) will return to the battlefield, returning [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) (and its 2 life and 2/2) as well as [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter) (which will re-target [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise)).

You will do this over and over as many times as you want, assembling nigh-infinite Wolves and nigh-infinite life points. Even if your opponent has the sweeper... you know, *1,000,000 life and all that!*

Note [Appetite for Brains](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Appetite+for+Brains) in the sideboard. This deck can go truly infinite given setup time, but it really, *really* has to pre-empt [Slaughter Games](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Slaughter+Games) in order to avoid having its strategy slaughtered.

Naya Humans has been more or less the darling deck of the Star City Open Series in recent months. Originally a bit counterintuitive— *where are the [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk)s?* —Naya Humans has put up great results on the back of the [Champion of the Parish](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Champion+of+the+Parish) opening, solid follow-up, and redundant offense. 

[Mayor of Avabruck](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mayor+of+Avabruck) might be at its best in this deck. I have seen players run out main-phase [Restoration Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Restoration+Angel)s to prevent their own Mayors from flipping into Wolf-machines. *Then my Humans would all get smaller!*

[Restoration Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Restoration+Angel) lacks the Achievement Unlock with [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk) in this build, but can accomplish a similar anti-beatdown enters-the-battlefield pairing with [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells). 

Speaking of pairing, the offensive ace in Naya Humans is [Silverblade Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverblade+Paladin). That card is a one-turn elbow drop and can paint an opponent into a corner even if he or she manages to survive a big attack... [Silverblade Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverblade+Paladin) obviously gets on well with [Kessig Wolf Run](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kessig+Wolf+Run) in longer game situations.

Naya Humans has quite a bit of card crossover with Combo Reanimator—[Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) and [Fiend Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiend+Hunter) here, while older decks have sideboarded [Angel of Glory's Rise](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Glory%27s+Rise) as anti-sweep and anti-Zombies—but it is clearly a swarming offensive deck, rather than an infinite combo deck. As we've said, Naya Humans has also been a good performer for some time, happily putting up Top 8s without [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk). So why add [Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic) and company? 

  

[Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic) is a good curve card in this deck; it can be played on the second turn via [Avacyn's Pilgrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Avacyn%27s+Pilgrim) and does two things. First, [Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic) allows Naya Humans to get even more aggressive against other aggressive decks. An inherent risk to attacking is that the opponent gets to choose how he or she blocks... with [Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic) leading the charge, *there are no good blocks*. You might be able to stop some damage, but you shouldn't be able to block and kill the Naya deck's creatures via conventional creature combat. 

And of course, [Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic) helps shut off important X-spells. These would include the format-defining [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) as well as cards like [Bonfire of the Damned](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bonfire+of+the+Damned) that are great against Naya Humans itself. Lots of contextual value, all attached to an efficient body. 

[Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) is surprising in one way: It's in the sideboard! 

It seems like [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) offers everything that the Naya Humans deck already wants. Its double strike clause gives you "more [Silverblade Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverblade+Paladin)s" and can make for tricky turns given the quality and speed of these creatures. Like [Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic), [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) can keep your guys alive. When getting into a huge brawl with another creature deck, [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) might be even better because the opponent doesn't necessarily see it coming. You might not *want* to block into a [Frontline Medic](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontline+Medic) waving the "I have battalion" banner, but you might have to. [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) doesn't give you the courtesy of a calling card on the other end. You might just lose all your guys. As far as combat tricks translating to Constructed go? This one is a blowout. 

And, of course, "deal 4 to you" is a solid ability at two mana... Patrick Sullivan has had repeated success in Legacy—a huge format—with [Flame Rift](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Rift)... which does four to both players for two mana! In a deck like Naya Humans, whose victories are so predicated on creature attack, adding a little bit of reach—especially for this amount of damage at a reasonable cost—is a nice extra option to help put the deck over the top and end games that might have been closed down otherwise due to [Terminus](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terminus), [Fog](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fog), or just big blockers.

New Cards: 

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/td/td233_newmonoredgreen.jpg)

*[Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner), [Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary), [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar), [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground)*

  

This decklist—with exactly twenty lands—is obviously quite tight and, from my perspective, looks very exciting. It abuses the curve, and although it is still creature-based (like most Standard Red Decks), it is put over the top by its use of [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor). 

  

[Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor)—typically the tool of "green" decks—can make for some big problems here. [Ash Zealot](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ash+Zealot) was not meant to be given a [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) hat. First strike and 4 power? Saito has the [Ash Zealot](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ash+Zealot)s that can not only kill a [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk), but walk away and deal a damage through as well. [Rakdos Cackler](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos+Cackler)—a sometimes-dropped one-drop—is suddenly trading with everybody. 

Of course, he has the general setup of decks like these... [Hellrider](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellrider) near the top end, [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) we have talked about already (all twenty of Saito's lands tap for red)... but what about other new cards? 

[Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary) is extremely exciting here! Saito can chain two-drops almost like a Suicide Black deck of old. In addition to just providing bulk mana, Burning-Tree Emissary can filter out green... so with two or three Mountains in play, Saito could cast a [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar) (3/3) or get the necessary color for his [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor). 

<table><tbody><tr><td>[card]Burning-Tree Emissary[/card]</td><td>[card]Flinthoof Boar[/card]</td></tr></tbody></table>

  

Of all of these, [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar) might be the most exciting new card of all. It's not exactly new ([Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar) is from ***Magic** 2013* , not *Gatecrash*), but it has just received the keys to the kingdom via—you guessed it—[Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground). A deck like this—with sixteen Mountains of twenty lands as its mana base—is the perfect home for [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar). Although it is technically green, [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar) doesn't care how you got the green, whether it was from a basic Forest, a [Rootbound Crag](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rootbound+Crag), or filtered off a Burning Tree Shaman; but it really *really* cares about having a Mountain of some kind in play. [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground) pulls this one from the "never gets played" pile to challenge for "best two-drop in Standard." It is essentially a [Watchwolf](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Watchwolf)/[Boggart Ram-Gang](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boggart+Ram-Gang) split card... huge on turn two, savagely challenging on turn three, deadly anytime thereafter.

*[Experiment One](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Experiment+One), [Ghor-Clan Rampager](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ghor-Clan+Rampager), [Skullcrack](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skullcrack), [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground) (and [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar))* 

  

Rounding out this week's discussion of successful [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground)s is Tyler Lindsey's Experiment Jund, a Jund beatdown (rather than midrange) deck featuring numerous *Gatecrash* specials, starting on [Experiment One](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Experiment+One). 

[Experiment One](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Experiment+One) is [Champion of the Parish](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Champion+of+the+Parish)'s opposite number. In a green beatdown deck, it functions not just like [Champion of the Parish](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Champion+of+the+Parish) (a quickly growing offensive threat that takes great advantage of early game mana) but like a [Gravecrawler](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gravecrawler) or [Geralf's Messenger](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geralf%27s+Messenger)... [Experiment One](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Experiment+One) is resilient against removal and can survive a good tussle.

It is not nearly the most interesting *Gatecrash* card in this deck. 

  

[Ghor-Clan Rampager](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ghor-Clan+Rampager) has already been called the card with no downside, and it is easy to see why. A 4/4 for four mana is reasonable... but this card is also a mini-[Hatred](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hatred) that provides trample and protects its target's toughness!... for relatively little mana. 

This is especially compelling in Experiment Jund, where the name of the game is haste. This deck puts [Dreg Mangler](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreg+Mangler) next to not only [Strangleroot Geist](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Strangleroot+Geist) but [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite) for a whirlwind of entering the battlefield tapped (and attacking). [Flinthoof Boar](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flinthoof+Boar) makes an appearance here as well, although it is a mite less sexy than in Saito's deck, as Lindsey's won't always be 3/3. Still... a great two- (or three-) drop and a nice bit of bonus haste to put on pressure or give you Rampager targets. 

I hope you liked this showcase of [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20130205000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground) and other new cards from this exciting new set. It will be interesting to see how the Standard format continues to shape up as we approach the upcoming Pro Tour *Gatecrash*!
