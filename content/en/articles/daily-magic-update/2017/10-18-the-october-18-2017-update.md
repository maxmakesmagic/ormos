# After That, Read, Watch, or Listen to These

1.  Breaking down the final game of the World Championship | ChannelFireball

In the wake of a tense, difficult, hard-fought final game of the World Championship, there were a ton of people talking about what plays could have made the difference. Now, two pros—Luis Scott-Vargas and Ondřej Stráský—break down that final game in a pair of articles. Check out their analyses [here (Stráský](https://www.channelfireball.com/articles/worlds-finals-game-breakdown/)) and [here (LSV)](https://www.channelfireball.com/articles/luis-scott-vargas/lsvs-play-world-championship-finals/).

1.  [Commander VS: Old Borders](http://www.starcitygames.com/article/36026_Commander-VS-Old-Borders.html) | StarCityGames.com | Justin Parnell and Stephen Green

Commander VS gets in its way-back machine to play *only* with cards printed prior to *Eighth Edition* (the Modern line in the sand).

1.  [Finding Azcanta](https://www.channelfireball.com/articles/finding-azcanta/) | ChannelFireball | Josh Utter-Leyton

The Worlds Top 4 competitor and almost Hall of Famer gives a bulleted master class in [Vizier of Many Faces](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vizier+of+Many+Faces) plus [The Scarab God](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Scarab+God). There's a whole article after that on Blue-Black Control, but be sure to stick around for the Props and Slops section at the end.

1.  [Drafting *Ixalan* with Pros #2](http://www.hareruyamtg.com/article/en/category/detail/195) | Hareruya English | Jeremy Dezani

Dezani grabs four pros—Thiago Saporito, Marc Tobiasch, Grzegorz Kowalski, and Kenta Harane—and has them all chime in on some *Ixalan* picks. It's always most interesting when they disagree, naturally.

1.  [How to Come Back from a Misplay (Modern Week)](http://www.constructedcriticism.com/podcasts/constructed-criticism-205-how-to-come-back-from-a-misplay-modern-week/) | Constructed Criticism | Spencer Howland and Michael Hinderaker

You screwed up. It happens. We all do it. Maybe you didn't cast that [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) when you hit five mana—well, bad example. There's no recovering from that. Pretend you commit some lesser offense. Now what? How do you climb out of that hole? Howland and Hinderaker discuss. Though, might I suggest casting a [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) as a way to catch back up?

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Standard Red-White Approach](https://web.archive.org/web/20220829045512/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/2017natus/us-nationals-9-16-decklists-2017-10-15)

Possibly the most interesting deck to come out of US Nationals was Red-White Approach, a redder take on the White-Blue Approach archetype that has become so popular. Adam Bialkowski just missed the Top 8 with this list, finishing 10th. The key to the deck and the main reason for red? This card:

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sunbird%27s+Invocation)](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sunbird%27s+Invocation)

This red enchantment looks a bit random and wacky, but it can be anything but. First of all, it turns nearly every one of your spells into two spells, assuming you hit. Second, it can actually be an instant-win combo with [Approach of the Second Sun](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Approach+of+the+Second+Sun).

It works like this: With [Sunbird's Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sunbird%27s+Invocation) in play, you cast [Approach of the Second Sun](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Approach+of+the+Second+Sun) and put Invocation's trigger on the stack. The key at this point is that you have not yet fulfilled the stipulation of having cast [Approach of the Second Sun](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Approach+of+the+Second+Sun) from your hand once already. Invocation resolves and, if you find another [Approach of the Second Sun](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Approach+of+the+Second+Sun) in the top seven cards, you can cast it. Since you've already cast [Approach of the Second Sun](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Approach+of+the+Second+Sun) (the one still on the stack!), you win if it resolves!

Of course, it's not always that easy, and sometimes you do need to dig for the second Approach. Without blue cards, the deck relies on [Chandra, Torch of Defiance](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Torch+of+Defiance) and [Vance's Blasting Cannons](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vance%27s+Blasting+Cannons).

The [Huatli, Warrior Poet](https://web.archive.org/web/20171018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huatli%2C+Warrior+Poet) looks a bit more like a "fun-of" than anything, though more planeswalkers are always welcome in a deck like this.
