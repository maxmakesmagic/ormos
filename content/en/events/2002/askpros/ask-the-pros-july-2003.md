<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 31, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/24/ed/24ed5be5578a04d4bd33e38ab1834ea7e497fbcee8f490f096ac18a1ccfd9f54.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: As a whole, do you think blue has been increasing or decreasing in power since <i>Invasion</i>? Do you like the changes to blue?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Andrew Cuneo</b>: It has definitely been decreasing in power. Weakening permission and bounce isn't necessarily bad, but Wizards needs to give it enough stuff to make playable. If <i>Mirrodin</i> doesn't have some good blue cards, the color will probably just be ignored in Constructed. Constructed formats where one or more colors are useless get stale pretty fast.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: Decreasing, thank goodness. Yes, it's a good change. Every dog is supposed to have its day in the sun. Back when <b>Magic</b> first started, I used to think that the Wizards R&amp;D sat around in blue pinstriped shirts with blue underwear and blue socks (who needs pants?), and cackle &quot;blue blue blue, oh I'm so fond of blue de doo!&quot; while they made their cards. But now I know better, because I met some of R&amp;D personally. Now I think they eat donuts and Cheetos while they cackle about blue.<br><b>Gab Tsang</b>: Decreasing. Blue cards were too strong in general, but they might be too dumbed down now. The most important part is to find a balance but I’m not sure Wizards has yet.<br><b>Ed Fear</b>: I think blue has obviously become nearly unplayable in recent expansions. While I don't consider myself a blue player per se, I generally think that removal of counter control as a viable deck type has had a deleterious effect on the game. The creation of more efficient discard, as well as more potent higher casting cost creatures (Spiritmonger, pit fighter legends) appeared to solve many of the balance issues with control; the neutering of blue seems to have been unwarranted.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: With Counterspell leaving after <i>7th Edition</i>, it seems obvious it's been decreasing. I've always been a control player, so I'm not in love with this development, but I like that it opens up all kinds of new possibilities as far as Constructed goes.<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: I remember back in the day blue used to be the coolest guy in the world. All the spells wanted to date him, and all the creatures wanted to be him. Now, blue is that guy who hangs out in the parking lot of your high school, trying to relive the glory days. No one cares about your days at Tolarian Academy, what have you done for me lately, blue? How the mighty have fallen. <br><b>Paul Sottosanti</b>: Definitely increasing. Between Decree of Silence, Hindering Touch, and Temporal Fissure, you can control the game like never before.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 30, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/d5/77/d577174465a6216c4498875eafb3da2b3211ea99f3f114f05b0965ac4275f693.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: Do you think bringing back Savannah Lions will give White Weenie the boost it needs?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Andrew Cuneo</b>: Not a chance. White needs to get some 2-power-for-2-mana evasion creatures for white weenie to make a comeback.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: No. Critters are so good now that just being a 2/1 doesn't seem enough to me.<br><b>Alex Shvartsman</b>: I don't think Savannah Lions alone are enough. The viability of White Weenie over the next year will largely depend on how much help it gets from <i>Mirrodin</i>.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: It makes the archetype seem more powerful, but I don't know if it has the big Armageddon-esque finisher it needs to go all the way.<br><b>Gab Tsang</b>: Yes, they are good beats.<br><b>Ed Fear</b>: My gut opinion would be that White Weenie will not be tier 1 in the near future due to its inherent vulnerability to all kinds of spells and permanents that it has no answers to. White-x probably won't be around either due to mana base problems.<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: As I recall, Savannah Lions were good when backed by a Swords to Plowshares or a Lightning Bolt. I don't think the pride is nearly as fierce these days.<br><b>Paul Sottosanti</b>: Yes, but only because Tundra Wolves is back as well. Those two were the cornerstone of one of my favorite decks from 1995.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 22, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/50/6a/506a81655d6f8e73a751a1459805e125bbc0f9a665920c3a74b8f7a7a16a7928.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: I just started playing <b>Magic</b> and I like the game. What do you think the best place to go from here is to become competitive?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Eric Taylor</b>: I would suggest playing in the next PTQ (Pro Tour Qualifier) in your area (www.wizards.com/protour). You gain two great things from this: 1) You get great competition and 2) you meet very good players that you can barn to (slang for make friends).<br><b>Gab Tsang</b>: The local tournament scene, maybe the local gaming shop that features <b>Magic</b>.<br><b>Nate Heiss</b>: Pittsburgh.<br><b>Ed Fear</b>: Just find other players who are better than you and play with them until you're better than they are, then find another crew.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: I wouldn't rush things. Becoming competitive comes with time, and there are formative steps you need to take to get to that point. Try local stores and Prereleases as a starting point for meeting other players and finding opponents in your community, keep an eye on Sideboard.com, MagicTheGathering.com, StarCityGames.com, Brainburst.com and the like and play a lot of Limited, as doing so will force you to use and eventually understand all of the “lesser” game mechanics (not to mention build your collection). Skipping right into competitive Constructed will deprive you of the fundamentals, meaning your game won't be as complete as you want it to be.<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: I would start practicing in school, preferably with many girls around. It will toughen you up for the Pro Tour.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 23, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c7/c1/c7c1fe5cf49f58117fbd931561f1b60bd3183ad6276cf758a8fd3c7d6702fe27.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question: How important do you consider the non-verbal communication aspect of Team Rochester? If your team has a defined set of gestures, which concepts do these gestures communicate and why are they so important? Have you considered learning sign language to expand your non-verbal vocabulary?</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Victor van den Broek</b>: It's very important. We indicate, &quot;I want this card&quot;, &quot;Do I want this card?&quot;, &quot;It's going to bounce&quot;, &quot;If I don't take it my opponent will and it'll screw me over&quot;, and many more... I haven't considered sign language, as I don't think it would add anything. Too much communication is bad.<br><b>Jeroen Remie</b>: The most important signs are: I want that card, defense-draft that card, and leave that card for me. This way your teammates at least know what you think of the cards... the rest is gravy and you learn in time. Learning sign language would be nice, although it looks kind of slow.<br><b>Adam Horvath</b>: Fluid non-verbal communication is essential to success in Team Rochester Draft, especially when you're being timed. Common gestures my teammates and I use indicate the concepts of playable cards, mana curve, hate draft, and color combinations. Learning sign language has never been a consideration of mine.<br><b>Andy Stokinger</b>: Very important, otherwise you waste a considerable number of picks unless all three members of your team can be aware of everything. My team communicates colors, hate drafts, matchups, and amount of removal spells. No sign language for me, I think it would let us argue too much and waste time. <br><b>Tom van de Logt</b>: I think it's very important. My team currently uses a few very simple gestures to communicate during a draft. It might be useful to learn sign. <br><b>Andrew Cuneo</b>: Signals are much less important to me than having a good overall strategy. During the draft, I try to stay focused on what cards my opponent has, and what I need for the matchup. During the draft, you need to be able to tell your teammates what colors you are playing, what card you want to pick out of a pack, and what card, if any, you want them to hate draft. Anything beyond that is nice, but less important having a good strategy beforehand, and remembering what cards your opponent has. When doing practice drafts, I like to allow talking 80% of the time, because it's a much more conducive to getting a good strategy.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: More important than the non-verbal communication is working together as a team. If you know each other's personalities, you don't need the hand gestures as much.<br><b>Antonino De Rosa</b>: I think silent communication will make the difference between winning or losing certain drafts but I think having a plan is far more important. If you have a plan going into the draft, even if you can't communicate effectively with your teammates, you still be able to draft good decks and mess up your opponents' decks.<br><b>Paul Jordan</b>: I think it is very important to have pre-set signs for things like defensive drafting, enchantments, evasion, number of creatures, and of course colors of each player. I've not considered sign language, but I have considered taking classes in improving my memory to improve my Magic game.<br><b>Ken Krouner</b>: Sadly sign language is illegal as it is considered verbal communication. Signaling isn't that important. I have found if you know how to signal which colors you think people are and which pick you want take, you are fine.<br><b>Gerard Fabiano</b>: Which colors is your opponent? How is your curve? Can you handle that bomb rare we may ship? What do you want for lunch? And I need to borrow some sleeves. Sign language is illegal if you check the rulebook. I thought about it though.<br><b>Aaron Forsythe</b>: It is obviously very important. You can't just sit there like statues until it is your pick; you need some way of communicating which card you want or which card you think your teammate should take. Car Acrobatic Team's signals were pretty simple: an open hand over a card meant &quot;I want this&quot;, crossing fingers or an &quot;X&quot; meant &quot;hate-draft this&quot;, a pushing motion towards the other team meant &quot;let them have all the cards of this particular color&quot;. I don't think you have to get much more involved than that.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 2, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/50/6a/506a81655d6f8e73a751a1459805e125bbc0f9a665920c3a74b8f7a7a16a7928.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: In the <i>Odyssey</i> and <i>Onslaught</i> blocks, which card would you use to describe yourself and why?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>William Jensen</b>: Krosan Cloudscraper, I think it should be obvious.<br><b>Victor van den Broek</b>: Probably Psychatog. I think I know how to use my resources well enough to kill an opponent. That and I played enough Psychatog to pick that over Mongrel...<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: Hypnox, I'm really broken but misunderstood.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: I'm afraid &quot;Forgotten Ancient&quot; best describes me.
