Rob, with impressive effort, *also* recorded his screenshots from the preceding game. I was very surprised to open my inbox a few days later, seeing a letter from Rob stating how he was running the game over in his mind, trying to figure out where/if he screwed up. Included in the letter were screens from every turn of the game, along with his thought processes for each move. 

As I said, this was an impressive effort of self-improvement. I'll say now that I can't take anyone's screens like this in the future, but this one time it's worth discussing. For each major turn there's Rob's commentary, which I'll include verbatim in blue along with my own thoughts. (Your pardon as Rob's screens are a little smaller than ideal.)

### Round 1 vs. limitedinformation

**Turn 0**

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw37_Rob-hand.jpg)

Rob: “Opening hand is fine, so I keep. Both Jaya and Dryad can be cast off reach on turn 3.”

I put this hand at way more borderline than Rob. It's all right, but on the draw it's extremely slow. If the Dryad comes down on the third turn to block something you can catch up, but that's a question mark. Your first play is either a 2/1 or a 2/2 you don't want to block with. I might keep this one, but it's right on the edge. I don't necessarily disagree with Rob hanging on to it, but he's more complacent than this is worth.

**Turn 1**

Rob: Suspended Giant from him, so I go with mountain to get Jaya instead of Dryad, so I can kill off his guys before I get smacked around.

**Turn 2**

Rob: Another suspended Giant means I have two consecutive turns where I'm going to be attacked without any defenses. Assuming he plays nothing else, I'm at 11. My plan is to try and kill him before that happens.

This is a problem. Rob has completely changed his game plan around based on one suspended Ivory Giant leading into a second. Unfortunately, on each turn Rob puts forth the exact opposite sentiment I'd wager. One Ivory Giant, even with a slow hand, is fairly raceable. Two plus whatever else from a U/W deck? I think it's time to start Inferno-fresh. Luckily enough, between the Dryad and Fungal Reaches Rob can do exactly that without taking too much pain. His race plan has some merit, but an awful lot of things have to go right.

**Turn 3**

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw37_Rob-3.jpg)

Rob: Outrider for him changes the math to lower my life to at least 7, and that's if I chump Outrider each turn. I figure I have to race. I play Jaya over Dryad because I want to play Gnarr next turn so the mana isn't a big help, Jaya is better all around, and any blue creatures he may play will die and I'll have enough mana left over for Dryad so I won't miss a drop.

The Pyroblast + Dryad argument is solid, but that's for the control route. It still seems like Rob is splitting his focus.

Rob: He attacks, and suspends Deepwalker. I now have some very fun turns to look forward to. I think about killing Outrider, but I don't really have the luxury of missing a drop, and if he has Temporal Isolation I might as well resign.

The possibility of burning Outrider, hoping for no Isolation, and then building counters for Jaya's Inferno crosses my mind, but the best possible scenario, if he manages to miss drops on all FOUR of his upcoming turns, still will leave me at 3 after the inferno, and him with a full turn after I kill off the Deepwalker. 

This is one of the turns I disagree with. Whether you're running the control route or the racing route, an invincible Outrider puts a real kink in the plan. Without Fungal Reaches it gets a lot closer, but with the storage land I *love* killing the 2/2 and adding a counter. What Rob calls a wasted turn I assert is just re-ordered. Eventually that pumped storage land is going to do something excellent, whether it's allowing Jaya to Incinerate + cast a creature, or setting up a speedier Inferno, or activating Jolrael off a single land. There's a good chance if he burns the Outrider and adds a counter, I don't win this game. 

As for the Temporal Isolation, sure, it's fatal here, but when will it not be? Temporal Isolation will be backbreaking whenever it's cast. Things are far too tight to worry about things like that, so don't. There's nothing to be gained from worrying about a card that will end you whenever it's played.

**Turn 5**

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw37_Rob-5.jpg)

Rob: He plays Zealot, which is wonderful for me as it can't block. The next couple of turns will see me defenseless, so at least it limits my options so I can focus entirely on aggression. I know I need to play a creature.

At the time, my line of reasoning was that Jolrael, unanswered, would kill him next turn. It wasn't completely on the board, as I needed the land in my hand, so there's a chance he wouldn't see it, or, even if he did, take it as an acceptable risk. I figured he was obviously in the aggressive mindset, and from his point of view, I was dead *unless* I drew the land. If his hand was unplayables, he might take that risk. Of course, he might also have nothing in his hand at all, as he was obviously tight on mana, so I could just win right now. The plan was to present a threat and force him to answer it.

In retrospect, I could have played the Dryad, which would also pump my Gnarr, and give me enough mana next turn for Inferno (which would probably only be used to draw), then shoot the Zealot, keeping my Dryad alive. He'd have gone to 14 instead of 12, then swung back for 5 taking me to 11. Assuming the next turn he plays some random white 2/2, I end the Ivory Giant insanity at 1 life, perhaps able to stabilize or get him to 6 for the draw.

The intricacies of two players playing aggression. We want to win the race before the other person's cards become overwhelming. I had no choice, and correct or not, Rob put himself in a similar situation. Interestingly, at different points we were both playing for the draw off Inferno. As for Rob's logic, it again asks for a lot. The fact is, if my hand was so flush with uncastables that I couldn't play anything new, than I'd simply hold something back. Because all the creatures were Incinerate-proof, it would have slowed his aggression as well. It's certainly better for Rob if I leave something back, but in no scenario will I actually die the next turn. That drawn Strength in Numbers does make things more favorable for Rob's race plan, however.

Rob: Even though I lost, I'm still pretty sure I made the right choice. The next three turns he had creatures coming, none of which I could do anything about but chump until I died. Now, the turns coming up are when I know I screwed up.

**Turn 6**

Rob: Giant comes into play, I go down to 10, and my easy kill is negated by the morph. Again his misses his land drop, but it's not quite a cause for rejoicing.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw37_Rob-6.jpg)

I draw the useless Flamecore Elemental. The only white morph is Weathered Bodyguards, which cost 4, so I'm not dead on table at the moment.

Here's where I messed up. I figure:  
 1. I can nuke the morph with Jaya, swing him down to 5, after playing Dryad. I survive at one life. Any U/W pump spell has me dead anyway, Psionic Blast, Celestial Crusader, Griffin Guide, Unstable Mutation, so offering him the chump by attacking before I kill the morph serves no purpose, as killing the Zealot just leaves me at 2, which is just as dead from any pump spell.  
 2. By making sure he takes 5, he's dead next turn even if he doesn't swing. He only has 4 blockers (assuming he plays something) to my 7, so he's dead. I have 8 if I draw a land, so even if he somehow makes two drops I have a chance.  
 3. What pumps for 1 in U/W??

Here's what I SHOULD have done.  
 I did the math for killing him with strength, which takes him exactly to 0. Unfortunately, my mind blanked at this moment, and I somehow imagined the morph as being both ALIVE *and* WHITE at the end of combat, swinging back for the win. I have no idea how I screwed up and jumped to that conclusion.  
 1. Play Dryad for ![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)  
 2. Swing with everything.  
 3. Now, I can't see why he wouldn't block, as the morph is getting tapped anyway, and I could have strength (though 'hidden' in the reaches). Even if he blocks he's taking 10 and going to 2. But on the off chance he doesn't block, I win the game right now. I always tell my friends that if you can give your opponent a chance to throw the game away, do so. Here was mine and I didn't.

The reasoning is exactly the same as my previous plan, except I get the chance to win right away, and my opponent ends at 3 lower life. I had already resigned myself to losing to any pump spell, so this is simply the BETTER choice. It also turns off Psionic Blast.

“If you can give your opponent a chance to throw the game away, do so.” It's succinct, brilliant, and totally true. Now *I* know I would have blocked there – I was *eager* to block there – but Rob didn't. There's no reason not to give yourself all possible opportunity to win. Rob did also forget Crookclaw Transmuter, although oddly he was far better at naming time-shifted outs than common ones.

Rob's analysis, in hindsight, is very insightful, using the game as a springboard to look at situations in a new way. He presented his ideas in a logical manner, admitted errors, and sought out other opinions in a desire to learn. This is the absolute *best* way to get better at **Magic**. While I personally disagree with some of Rob's conclusions, the end result is a lot less important than the capacity to reflect and improve. Plus it's always fun to see both sides of the board. 

Sadly, game two was far less interesting. A turn-one Ancestral Visions was a great start, followed nicely by Errant Ephemeron. While the first Zealot died, the second did fine work against Goblin Skycutter and Wormwood Dryad (inhaling a Strength in Numbers to save the Dryad).
