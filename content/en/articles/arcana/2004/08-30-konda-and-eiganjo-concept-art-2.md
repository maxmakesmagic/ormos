The Kamigawa block is suffused with setting, story, and flavor - and Magic Arcana will be one key way to discover the world behind the cards. Watch this space for behind-the-scenes concept art, rich storyline info, a glossary of Japanese terms relevant to Kamigawa, a look behind the flavor text of Kamigawa block, and more. 

Today we focus on a single man whose destiny was central to the set's main tale. Rei Nakazawa's [feature article this week](https://web.archive.org/web/20040830000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=mtgcom/feature/227) previews **Konda, Lord of Eiganjo**, the card representing the patriarch of a powerful ruling family in the world of Kamigawa. Haven't read Rei's article yet? [Go read it!](https://web.archive.org/web/20040830000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=mtgcom/feature/227) 

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;						<p>Lord Konda was a <i>daimyo</i> - a feudal lord and landowner who commanded a host of samurai soldiers. He ruled from Eiganjo Castle - hence &quot;Konda, Lord of Eiganjo.&quot; </p>&#10;						<p> To the left you can see a concept sketch for Konda by Ittoku, an artist responsible for a lot of the look of the Kamigawa block. Ittoku is the illustrator of <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20040830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rude+Awakening">Rude Awakening</a>, <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20040830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arcbound+Fiend">Arcbound Fiend</a> and a few other cards in <i>Mirrodin</i> block. </p>&#10;					</td></tr></tbody></table>
