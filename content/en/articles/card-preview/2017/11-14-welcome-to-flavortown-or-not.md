It's not a secret that I love fun. Not just the fun of cracking a booster pack for Sealed or seeing a sweet new card preview, but the fun of playing Commander a bit too late into the night, running a Pauper cube that's been copied and remixed by dozens of enthusiasts, writing a column (right here!) literally titled "Serious Fun" for years—the more different and wild and *un*usual the *Magic* fun, the better I like what's happening.

![Hammerfest Boomtacular](https://media.wizards.com/2017/images/daily/c4rd4r7_UEXVOPR8Ij.jpg)

I was around when *Unhinged* dropped back in the day. I have a complete foil collection of the set. The return of *Un*-fun is something I am, to put it mildly, overjoyed to see.

*Un*\- sets aren't like other *Magic* sets. I'm not talking about how the cards have silver borders and aren't legal to play in competitive events. I mean the experience and gameplay they create are different. Wild, weird, and wacky things you cannot expect are baked in. Even if you just use a handful of offbeat cards, the effects cascade.

One trick *Unhinged* had was a "words matter" theme. [Carnivorous Death-Parrot](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carnivorous+Death-Parrot) makes ["gotcha" cards](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&text=+%5bgotcha%5d) more powerful for opponents, but is a flying [Grizzly Bears](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grizzly+Bears) in my cube. [Double Header](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Double+Header) is in my cube as well, as it hits a surprising number of permanents, like [Grizzly Bears](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grizzly+Bears) (and [Star Compass](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Star+Compass) and [Armadillo Cloak](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armadillo+Cloak) and [Azorius Guildgate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Azorius+Guildgate)). [Tainted Monkey](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tainted+Monkey) was one of my favorite cards to draft, since choosing "the" means your opponent will lose life almost every time.

But one of the many ways words could matter was the artist of the card. Suddenly, the basic lands you played should match—or not—for more reasons than personal aesthetics. Minimizing the power of an opponent's [Circle of Protection: Art](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Circle+of+Protection%3A+Art) or [Framed!](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Framed%21) was a sideboard and deck construction consideration.

Another words-mattering consideration was flavor text. [Frazzled Editor](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frazzled+Editor) looks overpowered at a glance, but [flavor text doesn't make a card "wordy."](http://markrosewater.tumblr.com/post/112272557693/un-rules-question-does-protection-from-wordy) [My First Tome](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=My+First+Tome) put memories—and Vorthos know-how—to the test. And we already covered [Carnivorous Death-Parrot](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carnivorous+Death-Parrot).

I'm delighted to share that *Unstable* is bringing flavor back. Presenting Ineffable Blessing:

![](https://media.wizards.com/2017/ust/en_5daa633Pbv.png)

# Let's Get Wordy

Flavor text was a minor moment in *Unhinged*. A few cards cared specifically about flavor text, but it wasn't a full-blown archetype unto itself. Ineffable Blessing is on another level. We get a pretty simple choice: draw cards either when creatures with flavor text enter the battlefield, or when creatures *without* do.

First things first, two facts:

1.  Token creatures don't have flavor text—unless there's some Mark Rosewater *Un-*ruling I'm not aware of. That makes bland token decks draw cards like there's no tomorrow, if you so choose.
2.  You may not have a tomorrow if you're not careful, as the draw trigger is mandatory—not a "may." If you make tokens, or put a bunch of creatures with flavor text onto the battlefield, you'd better have enough cards left in your library.

With the obvious disclaimers and token shenanigans out of the way, let's put on our best Frank Karsten impression and look at a few numbers. Across all of *Magic*, there are more than 6,250 creatures with flavor text, and more than 3,080 creatures without. On the surface, that's two-to-one odds on a random creature having flavor text, so the choice to go for "with flavor text" on Ineffable Blessing is a no-brainer, right?

Maybe. We need more numbers.

In *Unstable* Draft, you'll find seven green creature cards with flavor text and eleven without. Across the entire set, it's 48 with flavor text and 56 without. If you're drafting Ineffable Blessing early and can plan accordingly, it isn't tough to swing one way or the other. This is pretty typical of a build-around kind of Draft card, though finding it in the third pack will definitely feel disappointing if you wanted to visit Flavortown.

![](https://media.wizards.com/2017/images/daily/ceTXcoj6pF.png)

But the best part of *Un*\- sets is what you do with the cards afterward. In a format like Commander, many powerful and splashy legendary creatures *don't* have flavor text. Your commander, if you plan to play it repeatedly—see [Rhys the Redeemed](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rhys+the+Redeemed) or [Derevi, Empyrial Tactician](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Derevi%2C+Empyrial+Tactician)—can be an excellent source of card draw. That said, looking across a lot of decks on [EDHRec.com](https://edhrec.com/) and pulling out all the top creatures yields a mix that's closer to even.

Getting into the popular green creatures tells one tale:

*   *Every* version of [Eternal Witness](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eternal+Witness) has flavor text. The same is true for [Sakura-Tribe Elder](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sakura-Tribe+Elder) and [Elvish Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elvish+Mystic).
*   Creatures like [Wood Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wood+Elves), [Reclamation Sage](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reclamation+Sage), and [Llanowar Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Llanowar+Elves) have only hard-to-find versions available without flavor text.
*   Commonly played creatures like [Acidic Slime](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Acidic+Slime) and [Oracle of Mul Daya](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oracle+of+Mul+Daya) don't have versions with flavor text. But the vast majority of popular green creatures have flavor text on them.
*   Across all top-played mono-green creatures, it's 27 with to 21 without flavor text—a little closer.

Looking at all creatures reveals a similar pattern:

*   There are no versions of [Solemn Simulacrum](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Solemn+Simulacrum) or [Deathrite Shaman](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathrite+Shaman) with flavor text. In fact, most creatures come *only* in with or without flavor text flavors.
*   For the top-played creatures overall, it was 56 with flavor text to 44 without—in line with green creatures alone and closer than the two-to-one ratio we saw across the game.

Making the most of Ineffable Blessing in a wacky Commander deck will require some trade-offs; you don't want to just jam all of one or the other, since you have to play lands and will want some noncreature spells in the mix too. (And *that's* before adding a color like white or black to be able to find Ineffable Blessing early enough to take advantage of it.)

But going big with flavor-less creatures under Derevi doesn't seem far-fetched.

# A Cube-Shaped Hole

There's one tiny little detail we didn't hit yet: [cubes](http://markrosewater.tumblr.com/post/165690033548/youve-asked-as-at-least-twice-now-what-type-of).

Cubes add a whole new layer of possibility for making *Un*-fun happen. While getting started with a cube is pretty straightforward, thanks to [Melissa DeTora's wonderful introduction last year](/en/articles/feature/2016/12-28-building-your-first-cube), taking it to a new level is entirely up to the designer. While I went all-in on commons as the key differentiator for my cube versus others, *Unstable* is a treasure trove of interesting opportunities.

Ineffable Blessing is exactly the kind of card that can kick off an archetype in a cube. Making specific choices about versions of cards—say, the *Magic 2015* Game Day promo copy of [Reclamation Sage](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reclamation+Sage) versus the *Commander Anthology* version—to twist how good Ineffable Blessing is can craft a refreshing experience. If you go all-in on a bigger theme like a [vanilla creatures](/en/articles/makingmagic/2005/01-24-a-few-words-from-r-d) cube, more creatures will have flavor text, and related cards like [Muraganda Petroglyphs](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muraganda+Petroglyphs) can shine, too.

It's also a way to add tension to cube drafts. If flavor text matters, do you draft the flexible-and-keeps-my-colors-open-with-flavor-text [Burnished Hart](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burnished+Hart) or the obviously-more-powerful [Yavimaya Elder](https://web.archive.org/web/20171114000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yavimaya+Elder)? One of the secrets to good cubes, in my humble opinion, is creating not necessarily more decisions but *more interesting* decisions that matter. Ineffable Blessing, in the right cube designer's hands, will offer some fascinating ways to think about drafting cards.

Like dropping it right into a cube with token-making as a theme. Just saying.

# Bland Band

*Unstable* isn't your typical *Magic* release, but it's full of ways to twist the ordinary to extraordinary. Whether you like your decks flavorful or bland, you'll find something to your taste when the set releases on December 8!
