Welcome to Snow Week! Where I live, we have between twenty and thirty unofficial Snow Weeks, so I figured I'd sit this one out. I was just getting over the fact that winter exists, and this whole Snow theme is just a grim reminder of those troubled times. Luckily, *Coldsnap* is shaping up to be an old-fashioned Johnny-fest, so I can't wait until I can start playing with the cards online. Literally, I can't wait. I'm going into hibernation, just like a bear. When I wake up, I'm going to be very hungry and playing with Zur the Enchanter. Just like a bear.

For anyone curious about how well *Coldsnap* interacts with the other cards in Standard, look no further than Juniper Order Ranger. This Human Knight is not only very protective of his juniper bushes and their surprisingly edible berries, but he also hosts a mean party. Whenever a new guest arrives, he gives them a balloon, a fancy-looking pencil, and a +1/+1 counter. Giving is its own reward, so he gets a +1/+1 counter as well. “One for you, one for me,” he might say, in the Juniper Order's signature sing-song.

Now, if only *Ravnica* had some Guilds that liked putting lots of creatures into play or goofing around with +1/+1 counters. Since none exist, I'm going to pair Juniper Order Ranger with the Selesnya Conclave (token city!) and the Simic Combine (counter town!). The Ranger is like Super Graft-Guy, one of Momir Vig's Mutants on steroids. Instead of losing a counter when he “Grafts,” he *gains* one. Selesnya Guildmage will start to make 2/2 tokens when the Ranger is on the scene. Supply // Demand will do the same, but it can also be used to fetch the Ranger from your library. *Coldsnap* brings with it another way to put a whole bunch of creatures into play at one time, and that's with Surging Sentinels. Since we can only play with four copies of the card, it might seem unlikely that we will be able to take advantage of the Ripple ability in Constructed (where the deck is also much bigger). That's where Congregation at Dawn comes in. Instead of just crossing your fingers when you play Surging Sentinels, you can put three of them on top of library, ready to be Rippled. It is particularly fun to follow up a turn 5 Juniper Order Ranger with a turn 6 Congregation and Sentinel. You will end up with four 3/2 First Strikers and a 6/8 Ranger. 

With all the +1/+1 counters sure to be floating around, I added a set of Cytoplast Root-Kins, Aquastrand Spiders, a Helium Squirter (the Ranger will appreciate the evasion), and a pair of Simic Guildmages. When he's not shuffling counters around, the Guildmage can nullify opposing spot-removal by moving Shielding Plax from creature to creature. To round out the deck, I paid homage to the Ranger's professional colleague, *Alliance's* Juniper Order Advocate, with Tolsimir Wolfblood.

### Me Tarzan, Yukora

[Aaron Forsythe wrote an article](/en/articles/latestdevelopments/2006/06-16-akromas-bracket) a little while ago, in which he outlined five groups that Rare cards fall into. The A-List consisted of boring old tournament staples like Char (no casual player would ever use this card). Category B was for super-cool casual favourites like Circu, Dimir Lobotomist (brain surgery is very popular among casual players). Boring, yet super-cool, casual *and* tournament staples like Darksteel Colossus made up Category C. My favourite category is D, the slightly-less-than-super-cool casual almost-favourites (like, say, Juniper Order Ranger). The last category, cleverly titled Category E, is reserved for cards that nobody likes. Cards like Giant Solifuge.

To help Aaron out, I have devised a little test to determine whether a Rare is a D or an E. Step one: In an article, suggest that the card is bad, terrible, or unplayable. Step two: It's just me and you. Step three: check your inbox for hate-mail. If you find some, the card is a D. If no one pipes up to defend the card's honour, you have yourself an E.

I recently participated in a Reject Rare Draft, which is [conveniently documented here](/en/articles/houseofcards/2006/06-07-yukora-the-prizewinner) . In that article, I was really down on Yukora, the Prisoner. I suggested that various be-antlered mammals and even flying ponies could beat him up. I would just like to get one thing out of the way. I *know* Yukora doesn't suck, either *like* a vacuum or *in* a vacuum. Four mana is cheap for a 5/5 creature (especially in Black), and ol' Yukie has definitely seen play in tournaments, mostly in mono-Black control-style decks (often alongside Kokusho, the Evening Star).

I received a bunch a mail in response to that article, mostly along the lines of “how could you call one of my favourite cards a *Reject*?” People chimed in to defend cards like Fiery Gambit, the enchantment loving duo of Rayne, Academy Chancellor and Hakim, Loreweaver (Just add Galvanic Arc!), and, of course, Yukora himself.

![Spawnbroker](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/cardart/RAV/Spawnbroker.jpg)I was understandably nervous when Frank Pulmanns, otherwise known as Bran\_Dawri, sent me an email on behalf of the Yukora Promotional League. (Warning: Don't get caught impersonating a Y.P.L. officer. That's what got the Demon into hot water in the first place! His self-promotion was criminally shameless. To avoid getting caught, just don't go to places where there are 99 monks, like monasteries and Beastie Boys concerts.) The Y.P.L. is pretty shrewd, because it knows that sometimes the best thing to do with a card that has a brutal drawback is to give it to your opponent. How? Spawnbroker! Usually armed with some very dangerous arm-candy in the form of Bronze Bombshell, Spawnbroker is like the Ravnican version of Santa Claus, except less sinister. Naturally, Bran's deck also featured this combo (which was also sent to me by Ed Black, Daniel Van Pelt, Matt Filbert and Gilbert). Since Mark Gottlieb already covered the 'Broker/Bombshell interaction in [his magicthegathering.combos *Dissension* Edition article](/en/articles/feature/2006/05-29-magicthegathering-combosdissension-edition) (on this very site!), I was hesitant to use it. Adding Yukora to the mix changed all that. The trick is to give Yukora to your opponent with Spawnbroker, taking, say, their Yosei in the process. Then, play one of your bounce spells, like Peel from Reality, and return Yukora to your hand. Since he will have left play, your opponent will have to sacrifice all of his non-Ogre creatures, which will usually be all of them, even if they're indestructible, untargetable, and have Protection from Black.

I made a few modifications to Bran's deck in order to play it online. Sorceress Queen, Spawnbroker's tag-team partner, is not available online, but you can get a similar effect from Oboro Envoy so I made the switch. The only other thing preventing the deck from being Standard legal was a pair of Cavern Harpies, so I snipped them for more Bombshells. One of the key inclusions is Rise // Fall, which is perfectly suited to the Bronze Bombshell and Spawnbroker combo. If you pull off the combo once, the Rise half will allow to set everything back up, returning your Bombshell from your graveyard and your Spawnbroker from play. It's also handy when you want to get back a Transmuted Drift of Phantasms. For those less-than-fanatical card-readers among us, I will point out that in order to play Rise // Fall you'll need two targets: a creature card in a graveyard, and a creature in play. Don't forget that you can use Rise to return an opponent's creature from their graveyard to their hand. This isn't something that you'll want to do too often, but sometimes you really just want to bounce an opposing creature (or your own Spawnbroker). 

Along with Fellwar Stone (on occasion), the Forbidden Orchards make it possible to play the Fall half of Rise // Fall against Red decks, which is something that I strongly advise (Bronze Bombshell doesn't like to get burned, and just about any burn spell will burn her to death). The Orchards also give your opponent creatures that can be traded for Bombshells. Fellwar Stone, meanwhile, allows you to use the activated abilities of creatures you steal, which is nice when you steal a Guildmage, for example. Genju of the Falls will soften the opponent up a bit while you get your combos together, but it's also not the worst thing in the world to trade with Spawnbroker. At the end of the turn, you will have their three-power creature and they will have an Island with an enchantment that they can't use on it. Here's the deck:

In one game, I played against an opponent who wielded a creatureless Blue and Black control deck. His only win condition appeared to be a set of Confiscates. Strategic genius that I am, I eventually figured out that Bronze Bombshell would be good in this situation (Confiscate seven to the dome!) and decided to pitch my entire hand when my opponent tried to Perplex the adorable Construct. After a few turns of Bombshell beatdown (believe it or not, she can actually attack), the unthinkable happened and I glimpsed it. While my Muddle the Mixture (later cut from the deck) was on the stack targeting my opponent's last-ditch Twisted Justice, lightning must have struck his home because he lost the connection. That was really too bad. I had a very elaborately choreographed victory-dance that I was planning to show off, called the Rub-It-In Shuffle, a dance that my opponent really wanted to see. Sure, he didn't say anything during the game that would indicate this (in fact, he didn't say anything at all), but I could read between the lines. Ah well. I'm sure he'll get to see it some other time. Maybe when those crazy people at the cable company figure out how to make a reliable connection. And lightning stops striking just as I'm getting ready to gloat.

In another game, my opponent was playing a Simic deck. There was much “boo”-ing and hissing when I played a Bronze Bombshell, but after all the complaining my opponent played a Simic Sky Swallower. In Plan B mode now, I made like Isiah Thomas 'brokered a deal, sending Yukora to the Knicks, er, to my opponent, for a Vigean Hydropon and future considerations. A Clutch of the Undercity on Yukora sent the Demon Spirit back to my hand, wiped my opponent's board clean, including the big, mean, untargetable Simic Sky Swallower, and to add insult to injury, caused him to lose three life for good measure (Since Clutch returns the permanent to its *owner's* hand, but causes the permanent's *controller* to lose life). If you were playing Bran\_Dawri's original deck (which was Legacy legal), you could be doing the same thing to the definitely-not-an-Ogre Darksteel Colossus.

### Marching Up and Down the Square

After a one week hiatus, I'm back building decks for Standard Tribal Wars. (Seriously, check out the format.) Since I've already built decks featuring some of the other more obvious tribes like Centaurs and Skeletons, I figured that I'd take the time this week to explore one of the wackiest tribes ever conceived: Soldiers! Talk about craziness! Other than a rigid hierarchical order and a strict obedience to senior members, I just don't see how these guys can work together. Despite that, I'd been tinkering with a pre-*Coldsnap* Soldier deck. It was basically a Boros deck, with a suite of sweet weenies backed up by a barrage of burn. It was all very alliterative and very straightforward. Attack, attack, attack, burn, burn, burn. To complement the attacking part of my strategy, I was using Glorious Anthems, but with the arrival of *Coldsnap*, I made a very clever substitution:

Field Marshal is a card I've been hoping to see for ages. Elves had their Champions, Goblins had their Kings, the Undead had their Lord, Blue's guy went into retirement after a Hall of Fame-caliber career, but what did the White tribes have? Soraya the Falconer? It would not Shock or Char me if Field Marshal turned up in *10th Edition*, alongside the Green, Red, and Black tribal lords. In a Soldier deck, Field Marshal is three cards in one. It's a Glory Seeker, a Glorious Anthem, and a Knighthood (of Glory). I originally had some Sunhome Enforcers in the deck, because they work well with Agrus Kos, Field Marshal, and Sunforger (more life-gain!). Apparently, they have very large pianos in *Ravnica*. Did you know that, in development, Sunhome Enforcer was called Sunhome Ivory-Tickler? True (false) story.

As for the other *Coldsnap* Soldiers, Kjeldoran Outrider could be a good fit, but I went with Veteran Armorer instead. Darien, King of Kjeldor could also be very good (“Char you, make two guys.”), although I think the Darien deck wants to be Green and White and use Chord of the Calling. Flopping Kjeldoran royalty on to the table with damage on the stack seems like a very, er, cool thing to do. G/W also lets you use Congregation at Dawn and Surging Sentinels.
