## Grand Prix Calgary 2013

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration//mtg/images/daily/events/gpcal13/trophy.jpg)

Tomorrow might be Alexander Hayne's birthday, but today he's going to celebrate his first Grand Prix victory.

With a Pro Tour victory already under his belt and a runner up finish at Grand Prix Montreal in 2011, Hayne has piled up an impressive resume just short of his nearly-here 25th birthday. Hayne may not be representing Team Canada at this year's World **Magic** Cup, but he's quickly becoming Canada's great Stetson-wearing hope.

Surrounded by a field of Jund Midrange, Gruul Aggro, and Bant Hexproof, Hayne and and his UWR Flash deck—not to mention that Calgary-approved hat—proved to be more than up to the challenge of countering his way through the largely Canadian field. After narrowly dispatching Jacob Wilson—who has three Grand Prix Top 8s himself—in the quarterfinals, Hayne soundly defeated two Jund deck on his way to the trophy. After splitting the first two games, Hayne managed to silence Stephane Gerard with a complete victory punctuated by [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite) and a bevy of [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation)s.

And while Jund paced the field, the Top 8 showed us just how many things were possible. Gerard rode [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie) all the way to the finals—in his first Grand Prix no less—while Adam Lafrost (Mono Red [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth)) and Trent Douglas (Nearly Mono Black Control) put entirely new archetypes on the map.

But the trophy, the white hat, and Canada's love belong to Alexander Hayne, Champion of Grand Prix Calgary and newly-minted 25-year-old. A very Happy Birthday, Alex!

## Quarterfinals - Adam Laforest vs. Stephane Gerard

*by Blake Rasmussen*

 

  

When filling out his Top 8 questionnaire, Stephane Gerard wrote that his worst matchup was Mono Red [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth). Cue the matchup against the one deck in the tournament with 4 maindeck [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth).

But there's a reason we play the games.

Gerard lost the game where [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth) showed up on time, but took the match when it mattered most, taking down Adam Laforest's Mono Red [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth) deck.

Helped by a bit of mana trouble from Laforest and a constant narration from Gerard himself, the Saskatchewan native talked his way to the semifinals.

His play may have had something to do with it as well.

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration//mtg/images/daily/events/gpcal13/qf_gerard.jpg)  
*Stephane Gerard* 

The first game was all about [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth). Laforest had it in his opener, flashing it on turn three to Gerard's [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie) (which did actually nab [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner)). [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix) and [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) put on enough pressure to keep Gerard under the gun the whole game.

Still Gerard nearly raced with [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie) and a [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk), as Laforest chose to throw a [Pillar of Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pillar+of+Flame)s at Gerard rather than the [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie). That left Laforest needing to actually topdeck two points of burn to win.  
  
 He drew [Hellrider](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellrider).

That and [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix) were enough to get the final four points in.

All told, [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth) did 13 damage that game.

The second game was all about [Golgari Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Golgari+Charm), but not the way you think.

[Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth) didn't make an appearance in Game 2, instead, [Golgari Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Golgari+Charm) kept two wolves and a [Ravager of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravager+of+the+Fells) from dying to a Miracled [Bonfire of the Damned](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bonfire+of+the+Damned). The resulting attack was enough to put Laforest too far under the gun to come back. Even a [Blasphemous Act](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blasphemous+Act) that cleared the board wasn't enough to keep him alive.

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration//mtg/images/daily/events/gpcal13/qf_laforest.jpg)  
*Adam Laforest* 

Laforest made an interesting sequence of plays that might have doomed him, however, as he neglected to cast either [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) or [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite) into an open board with no real instant speed plays in order to, theoretically, set up [Blasphemous Act](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blasphemous+Act). But the move ended up backfiring when he ended the game with those same Hellkites still sitting uselessly in his hand. He did cast one, but Gerard had a healthy life total and an [Olivia Voldaren](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Olivia+Voldaren), giving the boisterous Gerard a chance to have a little fun to end the game.

"You're at 4 and you're tapped out? I am going to attack you with your [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite)."

In between games Gerard talked to himself about whether to bring in certain cards...even right to Laforest. It was hard to tell if he was playing games or just being friendly. Laforest couldn't tell, but Gerard was telling the truth about all of his sideboard moves.

![Rakdos's Return](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Rakdos%27s+Return)

"I left [Rakdos's Return](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos%27s+Return) in on the play, but I took them out on the draw. Seems right," Gerard offered in between narrating the procedures of cutting, shuffling and presenting.

The third game, after two incredibly compelling games, was a bit anticlimactic. Laforest kept a strong hand with a few three-drops and didn't find his third land till much too late, even as Gerard mused about what Laforest could possibly have.

In the end, what he didn't have was land, and Gerard was onto the semifinals.

"I just survived a harrowing quarterfinals," Gerard. "I hope I've done everyone a favor by knocking out the Mono Red [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth) deck."

He forced discards, pitching [Dreadbore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreadbore) while Hayne gave up [Steam Vents](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steam+Vents). Hayne passed another blank turn, and things began to look dire. Gerard forced more discards, his [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie) for Hayne's [Clifftop Retreat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Clifftop+Retreat), then played [Scavenging Ooze](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scavenging+Ooze), starting to clear out Hayne's graveyard. All the while, his Zombie hitting for three.

Still nothing for Hayne, and now the writing was on the wall. He stuck it out to the bitter end, through a too-late [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite) as Gerard trampled over for the win with [Kessig Wolf Run](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kessig+Wolf+Run).

### Hayne 1 - Gerard 1

The deciding game started slow. Four lands and no play on either side of the board. Despite this, Gerard entertained the crowd with a seemingly endless monologue, delivered in his best thespian voice. Hayne broke first with an end-of-turn [Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice). He untapped, paid two life to get his [Steam Vents](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steam+Vents) untapped, and passed the turn. Gerard played his fifth land and went for [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk). [Syncopate](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Syncopate) for one left Hayne enough mana to flash back his [Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice).

His next [Steam Vents](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steam+Vents) came into played, and he met another [Thragtusk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thragtusk) with a [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage), flashing back [Syncopate](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Syncopate) for two. Hayne hit for two and passed. Gerard's draw had failed to develop. In the face of seven mana from Hayne, all he could muster was [Dreadbore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreadbore) on the [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage). [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) for four put Hayne way ahead. He untapped and summoned [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite), leaving three mana up. Gerard had [Putrefy](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Putrefy), and Hayne was happy to let it go at that.

Again, Gerard had nothing to add to his board but a land. Hayne played an [Augur of Bolas](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Augur+of+Bolas), and missed, but still had seven in the grip. Gerard got a look at them with [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie), and it was all bad news: Two [Supreme Verdict](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Supreme+Verdict), [Counterflux](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterflux), [Turn & Burn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Turn%2F%2FBurn), [Oblivion Ring](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oblivion+Ring), [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate), and another [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation). [Burn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Turn%2F%2FBurn) took care of the [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie), and a turn later Hayne was casting a [Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) for six, and turning to those cheering for Gerard in the crowd.

"Sorry, fans of his."

The game went on a handful of turns, but it was already over. Gerard drew nothing but lands, adding injury to injury. His last hurrah came when he played his final card, another [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie).

"And you have no cards in hand?" Hayne asked. Gerard nodded. "Okay, well, I have a [Restoration Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Restoration+Angel), and I'm going to let you take it." Gerard let out a triumphant "YES!!!!" and then the two players shared what some would describe as an epic high-five across the battlefield. Gerard even did Hayne the favor of using his [Kessig Wolf Run](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kessig+Wolf+Run) on one of Hayne's [Moorland Haunt](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Moorland+Haunt) tokens (featuring ManaDeprived's own KYT) and allowing it to take the match singlehandedly.

### Congratulations to Alexander Hayne, Grand Prix Calgary Champion!

## Top 5 Cards

*by Blake Rasmussen*

 

 

![Sphinx's Revelation](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation)

 

### 5\. [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation)

Remember this spell? It's still a viable card, guys. Will all of the talk about [Scavenging Ooze](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scavenging+Ooze) and Jund and [Burning Earth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Earth), people seem to be overlooking what has long held the title as the best spell in Standard. It may not still hold that lofty perch, but a number of players showed it was still one of, if not the, most important, enabling control cards in the format. Not only did Alexander Hayne's UWR deck lean heavily on the card (at least pre-board), but Jacob Wilson and Matt Nass (who finished 9th on tiebreakers) took straight UW control decks to the top of the standings largely on the promise that [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) could save them all.

  
  

 

![Lifebane Zombie](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Lifebane+Zombie)

 

### 4\. [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie)

A key component of Stephane Gerard's Jund deck and Douglas Trent's Nearly Mono Black list, more than a few players were willing to play the anti-Green/White beater in their main decks. With Jund, Gruul Aggro, and UWx control decks sitting at the top of the standings all weekend, there was always something to target. Gerard even managed to nab [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) against his Mono Red quarterfinals opponent, and the Zombies single-handedly won a key match against Melissa DeTora for Trent. Expect to see more of this M14 Standout for quite some time.

  
  

 

![Xathrid Necromancer](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Xathrid+Necromancer)

 

### 3\. [Xathrid Necromancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Xathrid+Necromancer)

Speaking of Black cards from M14, [Xathrid Necromancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Xathrid+Necromancer) is the single reason the BW Humans deck even exists. DeTora actually said the deck was virtually unbeatable with Necromancer on the board (though, to be fair, she thought the deck was pretty bad without it), and Trent Towns proved that for most of the weekend before falling off just short of the finish line. In one memorable game, Towns beat Top 8er Brady Boychuk by spamming the battlefield with 1/1 [Gather the Townsfolk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gather+the+Townsfolk) tokens...only to turn them into a veritable [Army of the Damned](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Army+of+the+Damned) to attack for the win. How powerful the Necromancer will be once *Innistrad* takes [Doomed Traveler](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doomed+Traveler) and its fellow Humans with it remains to be seen, but until that time, you better be ready for zombies.

  
  

 

![Scavenging Ooze](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Scavenging+Ooze)

 

### 2\. [Scavenging Ooze](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scavenging+Ooze)

While quizzing players on the most impactful M14 cards all weekend, I kept getting the same response: "I want to say [Scavenging Ooze](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scavenging+Ooze), but I be everyone is saying that." And they were, but with good reason. The card has already had its mettle tested in Legacy and proved itself time and time again. Now transplanted to Standard, the hungriest of oozes hasn't seen any noticeable drop off in power. It almost single-handedly put the damper on previously powerful Junk Reanimator, virtually shutting it out of this tournament. The life gain and size increase made Jund even more powerful, and the card was so good Douglas Trent deemed it worth splashing in his Nearly Mono Black deck.

  
  

 

![Thundermaw Hellkite](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Thundermaw+Hellkite)

 

### 1\. [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite)

Now on its final lap through the format, [Thundermaw Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thundermaw+Hellkite) is making its presence known even more than before. With Gruul Aggro topping their curve with the monstrous dragon and UWR using them in both the main and the sideboard, depending on player preference, the [Lingering Souls](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lingering+Souls)-killer has come to define the format. Alexander Hayne employed them to great effect all weekend, not least of all in the semifinals. One after the other turned the board around for Hayne against Ryan Zawalsky, ending the game in a flurry of [Lava Axe](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808065716/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lava+Axe)s rumbling across the red zone. It's big, it's bad, and it might just be Red's biggest loss in the rotation. The Hasty Hellkite will be sorely missed by many, but in the meantime, it was probably the most important card at Grand Prix Calgary.
