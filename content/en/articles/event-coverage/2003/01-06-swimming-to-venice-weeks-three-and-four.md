I'm not sure that this will see the light of cathode ray until after Grand Prix: New Orleans, but I wanted to update you on the last two major Extended events of 2002 in the Tri-State area. 

The first of those events was a Grand Prix Trial for New Orleans at Neutral Ground on the Saturday before Christmas. I had been playing u-g for the first two weekends and it was increasing in popularity, taking multiple Top 8 spots in almost every tournament. I did not want to go back to the endless mirror matches of the previous Constructed season and set out to find something that beat u-g.

I was talking to Mike Flores and he suggested I give his new decklist a try…

At this point, I imagine there is collective groan going up from the northeastern United States portion of the *Sideboard's* readership. While Mike is held in high esteem as both a writer and **Magic** historian, his Constructed decks are generally held at arm's length, downwind. I think Flores builds decks like Stephen King writes novels — incessantly. They can't all be good. In fact, quite a few of them are real stinkers, but every now and again there is a gem. It's just a matter of being able to tell which decks owe more to “Christine” and “Cujo” than “The Stand” and “The Shining”.

…I looked at the deck and liked it very much. It seemed like a lot of fun to play and I decided to run it at the Trial. Maybe I would try it again at the PTQ the following weekend.

Mike and I went back and forth about the Choke in the maindeck for quite a few conversations but, based on the past few weeks of decklists, we both agreed the field was going to be predominantly u-g and Psychatog. Ultimately, I gave in and played it, but I wasn't comfortable with it.

### Round one: Jimmy Xu playing u-g madness

Jimmy was yet another of the seemingly endless parade of Team Hong Kong Supermarket players that show up at high-level events. We talked a little before the match and I got the feeling he was playing with my decklist from this article series.

**Game 1**  
 Jimmy came out of the gate with an Aqaumoeba and a Wild Mongrel. He Dazed my first Pernicious Deed and used Circular Logic on the next two. There's not too much more to tell then that.

**Game 2**  
 I got a little hand disruption this game and I was able to play out a Cursed Totem with no fear of countermagic. Suddenly, his powerful deck was filled with Grizzly Bears and Durkwood Boars. I followed up with a Quirion Dryad that I grew to a 13/13 while killing his guys and stripping his hand.

**Game 3**  
 This was my favorite game of the day. I was holding Jimmy at bay with a Silklash Spider, an 8/8 Dryad and a Faceless Butcher which was sitting on an Arrogant Wurm. Most of Jimmy's islands were stuck under Choke, but I was out of gas and he was climbing back into the game. He had another Arrogant Wurm, a Wild Mongrel, a Roar token, and a freshly-cast Waterfront Bouncer that was going to make life rough on me if I didn't find something quick. I drew a Duress and looked at the one card in his hand: a Wonder. I looked into my graveyard and found a Cabal Therapy. I sacrificed my Butcher to flash it back and was able to wipe out his entire team with the Spider's ability.

Brian - 2 Jimmy - 1

That was the highlight of my day. I should have just dropped from the tournament and gone home then and there.

**Game 1**  
 Teej conducted a clinic in why Rock is good this game. The life totals were deceptively close, but I was down something like 20 cards when the game ended thanks to recurring Elders and Walls.

**Game 2**  
 I thought I was going to win this game, as I used early hand disruption, a Pernicious Deed and a Haunting Echoes to leave Teej only one way to kill me — he still had all of his Treetop Villages. He peeled three of them in a row and I never found the Edicts or another Deed to deal with them.

Brian - 0 TJ - 2

### Round three: Andrew Levine playing Draco-Explosion

Andrew is a solid player from TOGIT who recently posted a high finish at NJ States with his Tax Man deck, which has been a local Standard staple ever since.

**Game 1**  
 Andrew double mulliganed and I tried desperately to stay above 17 life. From the look on Andrew's face at the end of the game, I'm sure he could have killed me, had I just tapped my Llanowar Wastes one more time.

**Game 2**  
 Andrew Brainstormed and Scroll Racked around my discard and got me to 4 with his combo. He finished me with two Fires.

**Game 3**  
 I was faced with a difficult decision this game. Andrew had out a Scroll Rack and had just Accumulated Knowledged for three cards. I was at five mana and played a Pernicious Deed. Andrew Racked in response and ultimately let it resolve. Holding a Cabal Therapy, I had to choose between doing a cold Therapy and Deeding for two to destroy the Scroll Rack. I cast Therapy naming Erratic Explosion and saw two Dracos and a bunch of land. He was able to kill me next turn.

Brian - 1 Andrew - 2

I should have dropped at 1-2, but I stayed in and lost the next round to a u-g reanimator deck. 

Overall, the 

Choke

was terrible in the maindeck and I did not care for the Naturalize either. The deck was not nearly as bad as my performance, but it definitely needed some work. I was prepared to give it another go the following week in Edison at the PTQ.
