Wake didn't change a lot since the Masters Series. When Jelger Wiegersma and several other Dutch pros included Exalted Angels in the main decks at their Nationals, I copied that for some time. But after playing with them a bit, I don't think they deserve to be in the main deck. They are pretty worthless against Psychatog, monoblack control and in the mirror. They are somewhat good against blue-green, but they aren't exciting. Most versions have three or four Unsummons main deck and spending seven mana to morph and then unmorph an Angel just to get it bounced for one blue mana is rough. And then they can just block it with Phantom Centaur or Roar of the Wurm with a Wonder in the graveyard. It is just not very good. They shine against Sligh and green-red if you manage to hard-cast them, but if you survive until you can do that, you are winning the game anyway. I'd like to say that they belong in the sideboard, but I don't even think they are good enough for that. The problem is that everyone expects you to have them either in the main or in the board, so people sideboard accordingly. I've seen Wonder, Unsummon, Ghastly Demise and Chainer's Edict in my opponent's deck after sideboard and if you don't have the Angels to give them targets, it means that they have useless cards, which is a rather good thing.

Matchups:

*   Against blue-green madness, it depends what their board looks like. If they don't get the Wild Mongrel, double Circular Logic draw in game 1, it is quite favorable for you. If they board Upheavals, Counterspells and enchantment removal, you shouldn't win. If they don't have Upheaval and Counterspell, it remains a good matchup for Wake.

*   Psychatog is similar. If they have a lot of creature removal in the main, you win game 1. If they have several Cunning Wishes or Duress, it becomes quite difficult. After boarding, you are in for a tough time. Four Duress is pretty much standard and the card is more than just good against Wake. They also don't have useless cards any more. If you can get a Compulsion on the table, you still have a good shot to win.
*   Monoblack control is a really good matchup. Game 1 is almost unwinnable for them. After board, they usually have Nantuko Shades, Mesmeric Fiends and Cabal Therapies, but if you leave in the Wraths of God, it is still favorable for you. Certain Finns should read that sentence really carefully and probably write it and paint their walls with it as well, just to help the learning process.
*   Green-red really needs Elephant Guide or a bad draw on your side if it wants to beat you. Four Moment's Peace and four Renewed Faiths in the main along with Chastises in the board are usually too much. Flaring Pain can be annoying, but you should win more often then not even if they board and draw those.
*   Sligh is very similar, only that they don't have Elephant Guide and their burn spells are a lot less dangerous. The only problems are Goblin Piledriver and Reckless Charge. Both can deal massive amounts of damage before you even get enough lands in play to do something against them. If they don't draw a Piledriver, it's looking pretty bad for them.
*   Zombies are easily the worst matchup you can get. Duress, Mesmeric Fiend and Cabal Therapy are all very annoying. Withered Wretch makes Moment's Peace ineffecitve and Rotlung Reanimator protects them against Wrath of God. Just avoid this if possible.
*   Cycling: Congratulations, you have been awarded a bye.
*   Elves are another bye in game 1, you just need to realize that Wrath usually won't get you anywhere. You need to set up Wake, Mirari, Cunning Wish and infinite Moment's Peace. The deck is pretty slow and with Wrath and Peace to make them even slower, it is usually no problem. Flaring Pain is a serious threat after board, as Peace is essential here. Chastise and Renewed Faith always bought me enough time in the four matches I played against elves, but I can see the matchup going down to about 45-50% after board.

### Green-Red Beatdown

Canavesi's deck is a fine example. Most people dropped the non-Wild Mongrel two casting cost creatures in favor of the slow-but-powerful Phantom Centaur. That makes the deck slightly worse against Wake as it is a bit slower, but against Psychatog, the change doesn't really matter. You are a bit slower, but if a Centaur hits, you are most likely winning. And the deck still runs 12 creatures in the one mana slot, so things should be fine.

Matchups:

*   Blue-green madness got a bit worse. When Dirk Baberowski and Marco Blume ran g-r in the masters, they claimed to win at least 75% of their games against madness. These days most versions are packing main deck Unsummons, which are very bad news as Elephant Guide is pretty important in this matchup. Back then, Ensnaring Bridge was also secret tech and people didn't know that it was coming in, losing games they would win otherwise, but without a solution to the Bridge in their whole deck, it just wasn't possible. Right now, I'd expect every madness player to at least board Turbulent Dreams and maybe even 2-3 Naturalizes to make sure that Bridge can be dealt with immediately. Knowing that Daniele dropped the more powerful Bridge in favor of Threaten is a big indicator. The green-red player has the faster deck anyway and should be able to get rid of mostly all early creatures the blue-green deck can put into play, until the madness deck makes a Roar of the Wurm token. If you have a Threaten when that happens, it is pretty much game over. Even if the madness player has another creature in play, he will be most likely forced to chump block to stay out of burn range. The plan gets a lot worse if they don't draw either Roar or Arrogant Wurm but in that case the green-red player is probably winning anyway.

    Psychatog: If they don't have Persuasion in the board, Phantom Centaur tears them apart. Compost is good as well if they have lots of black removal but I probably wouldn't board in all four. Three should be enough. If they show Smother, Chainer's Edict and Innocent Blood or Ghastly Demise, you want all four, but usually they don't have that much black removal. Roughly 65% in your favor.
*   Cunning Wake: You need Flaring Pain badly. But if you have it, you have a good shot. But most of the wins you get in this matchup are due to the Wake deck just not drawing the right cards (Wrath of God, Moment's Peace) while you have a fast start. If the game takes more then eight or so turns, you most likely won't be the winner. Not a good matchup.
*   Monoblack Control: Game 1 is rough. Elephant Guide helps a lot if you can get it safely into play. But their deck has a lot removal and the Corrupts mean that you can't really win with burn. After sideboarding, the matchup is a lot better. Compost wrecks them and Daniele has some technology in the form of Anger as well. Overall a good matchup, but only because the games after board are in your favor.
*   Sligh: Your burn is superior, your creatures are superior. You can lose to weird mana draws and too many painlands, but other than that, the matchup is very, very favorable.
*   Reanimator: Phantom Nishoba is somewhat annoying. You need a good mana curve and some burn to finish them before they can get going. If they get an early removal and turn 3 Buried Alive followed by a reanimaton-card, you don't really have a chance though. Elephant Guide really shines here as it gives you the possibility to race the Nishoba. Compost doesn't do anything and that means that your board isn't very exciting. Threaten and Bridge are both good, so board in whatever you are running.
*   Zombies: Ah, little black men. There are few things as enjoyable as killing something with a Firebolt and drawing a card for the trouble with Compost. If you think they have a problem against Phantom Centaur, well, you're right. You are definitely their nightmare-matchup.
*   Cycling: Avoid this matchup. If you can't find some clever way to avoid it, you'll have to play it out. And if you don't manage to mana screw them, well, you lose.
*   Elves: Violent Eruption is great here. The problem is that you need to kill a LOT of the little green men. ### Cycling

To be honest, I haven't seen anyone doing well in a bigger tournament with this in the last month. There are so many awful matchups like Cunning Wake, Psychatog, Reanimator and monoblack control. The only really awful matchup this deck has is the zombie deck. You just can't win this one. Wretch, Edict and discard are too much. Sligh and green-red are both pretty favorable and monoblack control is also good. If you have the Wurm in the main deck, they can hardly win game 1, but Arcanis is probably enough as well. If they have Withered Wretch after board, it gets close, but most people don't and then they need a really good draw to beat you. Madness is troublesome as the main deck Unsummons are obviously great against you. Thomas wrote in his report that the matchup is still pretty good as with Smother and Innocent Blood, they usually can't put up enough pressure and every reanimaton spell trades at least 2-1. But that sentence describes the problem in my opinion: you have to resolve Buried Alive. That is even more problematic against Psychatog. If you don't manage to resolve it, the whole deck doesn't do all that much and they have enough time to abuse their card drawing. If you manage to go Duress, Therapy, Buried Alive, you are most likely the winner. Even if you don't have something to reanimate right away, you should draw into it sooner or later and the deck runs so many reanimation-cards that the control deck can hardly counter all of them. Arcanis is the perfect creature against Cunning Wake and Cycling. Even if they Wrath it away, chances are that you can put it into play again after drawing three cards. Astral Slide itself is also pretty useless if you have Anger in the graveyard. They can remove Visara as often as they want, it will still kill an Angel every time it hits the playing field.

To sum that up:

Bad matchups: Zombies, Psychatog if they have bounce or Coffin Purge or other stuff that helps in this matchup.

Good matchups: Everything else if the deck gets going.
