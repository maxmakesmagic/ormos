 ![Ravenous Baloth](https://i.ormos.uk/a4/79/a479edc203a57b78fae0cecefd05e8e52ea75c0a513e92d9e0fd062d7834b363.jpg) 

Looking at Ravenous Baloth, it should be fairly obvious that this will be one of the most heavily played creature cards to come out of *Onslaught*. On its face, a 4/4 for four mana is generally considered efficient. In Limited, creatures of this size will almost always be high draft picks, sure to dominate the board, and in Constructed, we will often overlook even significant drawbacks for the opportunity to play creatures built like the Baloth (just look at the longtime success of Erhnam Djinn); In the current case, Ravenous Baloth in fact has not a drawback, but a fairly significant special ability that will affect existing matchups and should have significant interaction with spells both old and from *Onslaught*.

### Kicking Red Mages in the Teeth

If there is one thing that has plagued red, even during the days of Dave Price's dominance with Lava Hounds, Fireblast, and Cursed Scroll, it has been an efficient green creature, especially one with 4 or more toughness, on the other side of the table. Historically, decks like Secret Force or The Rock, those able to churn out efficient green creatures relatively early in the game, have been able to dominate red players, both by locking their beatdown creatures out of the Red Zone, and by being able to crack back for a significant chunk of the opposing life total with little likelihood of themselves being taken out... at least without large sacrifices made by the opponent.

Most of the games where burn decks *do* come out on top of green creature decks develop in one of two ways. In one case, the red deck will draw enough burn to kill every mana accelerator on the green side (generally 1-toughness Birds of Paradise and an assortment of Elves) for the first several turns, stranding the green mage's more expensive threats in his hand while simultaneously establishing its own creature beatdown. By the time the green mage can slam down that 4/4 or 5/5 for 4 mana, hoping to stabilize, the red mage can send an alpha strike of generally unimpressive creatures into the Red Zone, knowing that some or most will die, but that those that survive will be able to deal just enough damage to make way for the heat in his hand to finish the game. In the less confident games, the red deck will send its unimpressive creatures into battle during the early turns, while his green opponent will be unwilling to trade those all-important Elves in combat, as they are often necessary to bring out the heavy hitters a turn or two later. These early creatures, and each and every point they can eke out, tend to be crucial to the red mage, allowing him to set up desperation blocks a turn or two before the end, as he hopes to draw enough land and burn to finish his opponent without having to get through in actual combat... before he is overwhelmed by a rush of Centaurs, Elementals, Elephants, Saprolings, Squirrels, Wurms, or, yes, Beasts.

The presence of the Ravenous Baloth in green decks will change the way both sorts of games develop in the future.

By itself, Ravenous Baloth does wonders for the race against a red mage. The moment that it hits play, this Beast represents at least 2-for-1 card economy on its face, as most red players will be more than willing to trade one small creature and one spot burn spell to get the thing off the board. But at the same time it is removed - if that moment even comes - Ravenous Baloth will gain for its controller 4 life at no mana cost! Just think about how annoying cards like Bottle Gnomes and Spike Feeder have historically proved for red players over the years... for a one-time investment of just one additional mana, Ravenous Baloth represents more life gain than Bottle Gnomes with a more significant board presence than either.

Where in the past, the last ditch flurry of red spells would close games, Ravenous Baloth will oftentimes buy green decks additional turns to play out more impressive creatures (blockers), and draw additional life-generating Baloths.

### Keeping Those Sneaky Blue Mages Honest

 ![Nantuko Shade](https://i.ormos.uk/ba/71/ba71d140bd2ff1b8e63e2ce5c40f104688efae4179b778581514ea04548c9951.jpg) 

When Nantuko Shade was printed in *Torment*, R&D's Lead Developer, and former Pro Tour Champion, Randy Buehler explained that blue had gotten a little out of hand with cards like Fact or Fiction, and that one of the best ways to keep permission decks in check was to print highly efficient beatdown creatures. A signficant threat for just 2 mana would be one that could both slip underneath early countermagic and end the game all by itself; Nantuko Shade, as we all know, is probably the single most dominant creature in *Odyssey* Block Constructed, and as far as two-power creatures for two mana go, is one of the few currently legal cards that can give Wild Mongrel a run for his money.

So what does this have to do with Ravenous Baloth? Blue mages have traditionally been happy to see even very efficient four mana green creatures on the other side of the table. At least without mana control like Rishadan Port or Tangle Wire, these threats are generally too slow to face off against Constructed-quality permission spells. Should they resolve, the wily blue opponent often has something nastier in mind, such as drawing a card from Repulse or untapping all of his lands post-Treachery.

In the case of Ravenous Baloth, our 4/4 for four has a special ability that you might just want to read again. While, as a Beast, the Baloth is quite capable of sacrificing itself, any Beast you control is up for sacrifice! While this ability will almost certainly be more relevant in Limited, where drafting decks with synergistic creature types seems to be where successful players will go, in Constructed, there is an interaction or two with existing Beasts that may also be significant.

 ![Wretched Anurid](https://i.ormos.uk/63/a8/63a80c7bbe43c7970d14b155cffd948a7e9e512c1d3fadaf3d504a1be99b8e43.jpg) 

Just look at just one of Ravenous Baloth's brother Beasts from *Onslaught*:

Wretched Anurid is a great beatdown creature for Constructed. A 3/3 for only two mana is a significant threat that can slip under many a permission wall, and a 3/3 is generally big enough to brawl with most Constructed creatures (at least the non-green ones). If you recall how successful cards like Carnophage and Sarcomancy - Zombies that potentially damaged their controllers every turn - were during Rath Cycle, just think about how good Wretched Anurid might be in a b-g creature deck. He is there to get in early damage, a Hill Giant-sized threat at half the cost. Against creature-poor decks, he has no drawback at all. As the midgame approaches, and Wretched Anurid's value to the board goes down, it can make way for big brother Baloth, and against damage-based decks (which are generally not permission-based), Ravenous Baloth can bail out the collateral damage his predecessor did, and ensure that he isn't responsible for any more as the turns go by.

At the same time, Ravenous Baloth can guard the midgame from blue's card advantage-generating tricks. While losing your best Beast may not be the ideal solution to blue board control, it is certainly better than handing him over to Persuasion and getting your own teeth kicked in... glue that ability onto an efficient 4/4 and gain 4 life while you are at it, and sacrificing a creature in response doesn't seem half bad at all.

### Putting Beasts Together

Traditionally, Limited **Magic** has been a test of the classic elements of the game: mana consistency, tempo, and of course, card advantage... only with a less broad set of resources with which to work than in Constructed **Magic**. Over the last few sets, R&D has decided to challenge players with another zone of play: the graveyard. Just as counting towards threshold while keeping track of its effect on the board has been a test of player skill in *Odyssey* Block Limited, just as weighing various color combinations' ability to fill the graveyard, and measuring the power of card advantage against threshold and madness have forced players to look at card interactions with new eyes, *Onslaught* Limited gives us something different to count as well: creature type.

In much the same way that dumping cards from our hands to our graveyards seemed very oddly the right thing to do in *Odyssey* block, and as cards like the *Judgment* Advocates challenged our accepted notions of card economy, I believe *Onslaught* Limited will reward players for managing certain numbers of specific types of creatures in their decks, and in play. In a format such as this, especially a format where victory has almost always come on the back of creature damage, Ravenous Baloth's ability to sacrifice any Beast for 4 life may become even more relevant than it is in Constructed...

But I suppose that is something for you to see for yourself at the local Prerelease.
