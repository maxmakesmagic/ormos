### Grand Prix-Boston

Just over six hundred players attended the third Grand Prix to be held in Boston. With so many of them sporting two or more byes, the organizers chose to run Day 1 with – count them – NINE rounds of Swiss. Up until now, the only Grand Prix I can remember that kicked off Day 1 with nine rounds took place in Japan. It was not much of a problem as the event was run very smoothly. The last round finished before 11 PM, which is definitely a feat considering all the extra time needed in the morning to build and register sealed decks.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpbos03/957.jpg"><br><i>Hammer led a barrage of comeback kids. It was like 1996 all over again.</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

This tournament was notable for the return (and in some cases success) of some very old names. The Top 16 featured such veterans as Jon Becker, Shawn “Hammer” Regnier and Seth Burn. Unfortunately, of the three, only Becker won a Pro Tour invitation, as it only passed down to twelfth place this time. Other old-school players found at this event included Mark Le Pine, Dave Bachmann and Mike Bregoli. It was also Ryan Fuller’s first major tournament since the end of his DCI suspension. Fuller managed a Day 2 finish, but had a poor draft day.

The tournament was marred by allegations of foul play as rising pro star Nick Eisel was ejected from the event for allegedly adding cards to his sealed deck. A number of players have come forward since to argue that they saw him open the card(s) in question, but it did not help Eisel who was already ejected from the tournament by then.

After six rounds of drafting, a strong Top 8 emerged, featuring such names as Mowshowitz, Rubin and Kibler. It was Brian Kibler who finally earned his second Grand Prix title defeating Matthew Cory in the finals. He drafted an excellent white-black soldier deck featuring such bombs as Akroma and a pair of Whipcorders. Kibler’s previous GP win was a very long time ago, at GP-Toronto. He since quit the game for several years and then made his return, earning a number of top finishes including a Top 8 at Pro Tour-Chicago. 

### Grand Prix-Sevilla

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpsev03/880.jpg)European players had their own GP on the same weekend as GP-Boston. It was held in the Spanish city of Seville. At least I think Seville is the correct English name for it, as Sideboard coverage lists the name as Sevilla – which it is, in Spanish. As previous GP events in Spain, the turnout did not disappoint, with over 800 people playing. 

The greatest surprise was the number of South American players making the trip. For a long time now, U.S. and European pros have traveled to Asia and South America in search of additional pro points and prize money. South America’s top pro pair – World Champion Carlos Romao and Diego Ostrovich have now joined the ranks of such pros and their performance did not disappoint. Ostrovich placed in the Top 32, while Romao managed a second place finish. He was defeated by Sweden’s Anton Jonsson in the finals. Two-time Pro Tour Top 8 finisher Jonsson earned his fourth GP Top 8 and his first career championship title. 

### Grudge Match Finals

The Grudge Match series between Neutral Ground and Your Move Games has been among the best-known tournament series that are not directly tied in to the Pro Tour. Initiated by Brian David-Marshall and Rob Dougherty several years ago, the Grudge Match allowed each store to host a season of qualifiers, with winners competing for significant cash prizes at each of the two stores. Finally, the two winners would meet to defend the honor of their city and play it out for an additional cash prize. There have been a total of five Grudge Match seasons. New York won all four previous Matches – which no Neutral Ground player would ever let you forget.

Grand Prix-Boston hosted fifth – and final – Grudge Match finals. The format was Unified Standard. That means each player needed to build three decks while not using more than four copies of any non-basic land card between all of them. Each store was represented by relative unknowns this time – New York’s David Chung versus Boston’s Lucas Galvin.

The first matchup was a Psychatog mirror. Galvin swept his opponent in two straight games. The luck turned against him in the second match, as his beast deck faced an overwhelmingly bad matchup against Astral Slide. Chung took full advantage of the favorable matchup to sweep it 2-0. It all came down to the final match. Chung brought a red-green beatdown deck to the table, while Galvin played something totally unexpected – a cleric white weenie deck with maindeck Master Apothecary! This time around, the matchup definitely favored the Bostonian, who won the first game handily. Chung managed to steal the second game with Ensnaring Bridge – as Galvin did not board in any Disenchants. Galvin corrected that oversight for game three and won it easily – finally winning one season for Your Move Games. 

Although the Grudge Match series is now over, Tom Shea of TJ Collectibles has a new tournament program to fill in the void – with even more prize money and involving more stores on the East Coast. I will introduce it in detail in next week’s column.

Judging for the first time in my new hometown of Portland, OR, at the *Legions* Prerelease, two players in a late sealed deck flight were each fighting to stay in prize contention. When Player 1 landed Phage the Untouchable, Player 2 looked to be on a short clock. But a couple turns later, he evened the board position with a Scion of Darkness. On his next turn, he swung into Player 1 with the 6/6 trampler. Player 1 chose to block only with Phage, so Phage died to lethal damage and both creatures triggers went on the stack. Player 2 got very excited and asked if he could take Phage with the Scion's ability into play. My quick answer was "yes" but having been on my feet for 15 hours I decided to double check my ruling with another judge. Player 1 noted that if Phage hit the board for his opponent he'd be in bad shape and would concede. By the time I returned to confirm my initial ruling Player 2 had taken the time to read Phage and had realized that it said ‘if you do that, lose the game’. Disaster was thus narrowly averted.”
