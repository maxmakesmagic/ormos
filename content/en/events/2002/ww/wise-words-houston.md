What a whirlwind. So much went on this weekend, from heroic stories of survival to gritty stories of perseverance to, of course, to cumulative stories of triumph. Enough with the flowery talk: let's get into it.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/357.jpg)Justin Gary's win in the main event almost came as an afterthought to his team's dominance. Dave Humpherys had to be the loneliest man in the world as he stood alone watching his teammates win match after match. The Hump, who was eliminated from Top 8 contention in round five, was the only core member of Your Move Games not playing on Sunday, with Rob Dougherty, Darwin Kastle and the eventual champ himself not only competing, but dominating the Top 8.

Combined, the three YMGers not only didn't lose a match to the other five competitors, but they didn't lose a game. 3-0, 3-0, 3-0 and 3-0 were the scores when the movers played the competition, and like that isn't astounding enough, they did it with three different decks: Darwin with The Rock, Justin with Oath and Rob with Benzo. That three YMG decks were tuned to the point of Top 8 precision suggests this team's understanding of the format far exceeded everyone else's. The three members making the Semifinals is a first, as is three teammates finishing 1-2-3 in the Swiss. YMG may have just had the single most dominant performance for one team in Pro Tour history.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/379.jpg)Not that it was without adversity. Rob's life has been more than a little chaotic of late, with a child arriving just after PT-Boston, which he personally played host to, but at least he was firmly on the Masters. Darwin entered the tournament needing a Top 32 finish to avoid falling off the gravy train, a point that becomes all the more impressive when you realize the man still hasn't missed a tournament in the Tour's seven year history. Darwin started the tournament 10-0, putting to rest any doubts about his qualification status for Chicago before conceding to Justin in an attempt to help his teammate make Top 8, losing two matches and then, finally, beating Italy's Dario Minieri.

Speaking of qualification, another historic figure in PT circles has avoided fate once again. Dave Price needed a Top 64 finish in Houston to qualify for Chicago. Dave failed to make Day 2 and played in Saturday's qualifier, only to lose in the Semifinals, leaving the Sunday PTQ and Grand Prix-LA as his only chances to qualify for the Windy City. To fail would end a streak as long as Darwin's. Fortunately, that fate was averted with a Sunday win, beating Punisher Johan Sadegphour in the Finals of a very strong Top 8.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/596.jpg)Another Deadguy, Jon Finkel, was breathing life into what was looking like a dangerously-close-to-death career. Jonny Magic, who hasn't won a PT dollar since World Championships 2001 in Toronto, finished just 53rd, but before a disappointing finish, he was looking like the Finkel of old, with a 6-1 Day 1. Finkel's long-time partner in crime, Steve O'Mahoney-Schwartz, was qualified for the tournament, but elected to not attend, while another old timer, Kyle Rose, had his first triumphant tourney in almost two years, finishing ninth with a final round win over Kamiel Cornelissen, with whom he'd teamed for 2001 PT-NY.

Back to YMG, it is possible that Rob Dougherty has passed Kai Budde as the best tournament Constructed player in the world. Look at these results:

**Houston** - 2nd place  
**Osaka** - 3rd place  
**New Orleans** - 13th - lost to Humphreys last round  
**Chicago** - 4th

No victories, but that's three Semifinal appearances in the last four Constructed PTs. When your weakest PT over a run is a three-game final round mirror match loss to a friend and teammate who's on the Masters and knows your decklist card for card, you're doing okay. At this point, you have to start mentioning Dougherty's name with the likes of Rade and Budde as far as Constructed play goes.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/595.jpg)Speaking of Kai, can anyone remember the last time he made no money on the PT AND the bare minimum of $2000 in the Masters? He won Boston, came 45th at Worlds, 5th at Nice, and 24th in Osaka, taking him all the way back to last year's PT-San Diego. Budde was 4-2 going into the final round where he was paired against former Sideboard Magazine cover mate Superman (a.k.a. Ben Rubin), whose teammates and friends all rallied him on, suggesting he and his Psychatog deck play rather than drawing into Day 2 with Budde's Reanimator, in part because of the good match up, in part because of the lack of respect Budde is perceived to have of the Americans and their deck building strategies. Rubin won, Budde lost and the German sat on the sidelines scouting for his teammates on Day 2, swearing revenge the next time any Budde affiliate paired against any Rubin affiliate with Day 2 on the line in the future. The storm clouds of war could be on the horizon.

One interesting revelation out of Houston was Dirk Baberowski's confidence in Bob Maher's ability to become the 2002-03 Player of the Year. Budde's closest confidant, Dirk, having seen Bob's Top 8 finishes in Copenhagen, Boston and Houston first hand, sees a determination in Bob that could carry him past the Juggernaut. Says Kai "I really need to win another Pro Tour or it won't even be close. If he keeps going to all the GPs, I won't be able to keep up." Says Bob " I want to be Player of the Year. I'll keep traveling to PTs as long as my schedule permits." Budde's 4-3 finish in Houston leaves him with just 86 PT points for the past year, the first time he's been under a hundred in a looong time. That's right folks, come Houston, you'll have 14 points to play around with if you want Mr. Budde on your Fantasy Pro Tour team.

How'd you like to be Christopher McGuire? You spend years trying to qualify for the PT, finally make it into one, get paired against one of the best players in the world in Maher and lose the die roll? How does it get worse?

The man was facing a Verdant Force in play before his first Pro Tour draw step. Welcome to the big leagues, kid.

Bob, Neil Reeves and Bill Jensen took on Tomi Walamies, Jens Thoren and Anton Jonsson Saturday night in a high profile three-on-three booster draft. The Europeans won 5-4, with Walamies taking out Maher in the final match. It was a good one to win for Walamies who, without the PT points from New Orleans, has been forced back to the PTQ circuit. Tomi went 4-3 in the Sunday PTQ, leaving him unqualified for Chicago, which he likely wouldn't have attended anyways due to school conflicts. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/922.jpg)Thoren isn't far behind. Jens dominated the Masters right up to the end, where the many bombs in his deck were cited as the main reason he'd be unstoppable, but the simple fact is that he was the best player sitting at that Top 8 table, and he proved it with his double mulligan victory over Alex Shvartsman and his bomb-laden deck in the quarters. Jens, enjoying Masters success a little too much, hasn't done much on the PT since his back to back Top 8 finishes last year, and as a result, actually finds himself needing to accumulate seven PT points over the next two PTs in order keep from falling off the train himself. Johnsson, in case you're wondering, is healthy as a horse.

This weekend was one that saw a lot of dreams realized. Jeroen Remie, who went 6-0 on Block Constructed Day at Worlds, finally made his first Top 8, as did Punisher mainstay Mattias Jorstedt. No rookies in this bunch, the Top 8 had over 200 PT appearances between them. Maher made his second Top 8 in a row, while John Larkin, Ireland's lone ambassador to the gravy train, posted his second individual Top 8 in a row and third overall. Larkin, who has more or less decided to full-time the PT for the remainder of the year, is the first player from the British Isles to reach such heights, surpassing John Ormerod's two Top 8s. Warren Marsh and Tony Dobson are the only other players from the area with Top 8 finishes to their credit.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/949.jpg)As great as the long-awaited riches of the Top 8 were for long-timers Jorstedt, Remie and Peter Myrvig, perhaps the best story took place in the Masters, or more specifically in the Gateway tournament, where Allan Shulinder, a university buddy of Scott McCord's and Bryce Currence's who qualified for the Gateway on rating having never played in a PT, managed to push his way through the pros to reach the Masters on Gateway qualification, defeating Ben Rubin in the final match of their quarter-bracket. Shulinder, with his invite in tow was handed the small task of taking on Kai Budde in the first round, but to the shock of all, including Allan himself, he swept the Juggernaut 2-0 before Jareth, Leonine Titan felled him in the round of 16.

While that final round of the Masters Gateway had to be disappointing for the Americans, with both Rubin and Jensen losing their matches, the infamous PTR, a.k.a. Peter Szigeti, managed to keep American hopes alive with a final round win over European Champion David Brucker and his bomb-infested red-green deck. Jensen's loss, while a tough pill to swallow, had to be made easier by the fact it came at the hands of Invitationalist Itaru Ishida, Japan's biggest money winner. Upon his victory, Ishida, who is often characterized as quiet and expressionless, was instead expressionless and quiet. He lost to Dave Humphreys in round one of the Masters, while Szigeti was losing to Eivind Nitter.

The fourth qualifier was Christophe Haim, only halfway through a remarkable trip with roommate and teammate Franck Canu. The two, speaking around 50 words of English between them, made their way to GP-Philadelphia, after which they made their way to Houston. The trip will end with them attending GP-LA. Haim, who made Top 8s at European Championships, French Nationals and PT-Osaka last year, was in fine form in Philly until hitting a 0-5 drop spiral on Day 2. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/891.jpg)One other story from the weekend. I made the Masters Finals! Sorry folks, I couldn't help myself. I got smashed in the Finals by a superior opponent, but one thing you need to know about playing on Sunday is that its more than just a match, it's an event, and who you share that experience with is important. While there are a lot of **Magic** players I'd rather play than Jens Thoren, there aren't a lot of people who I can say the same about. Jens is a good man; I'm glad I got to share the stage with him. Thanks to everyone who helped me get to the Finals this weekend; it was far from a one man job. Have a good week.
