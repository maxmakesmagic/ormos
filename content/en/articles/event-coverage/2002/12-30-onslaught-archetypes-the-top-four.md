*“Blue-black will never beat red-green without a few Dreamwinders, Mirror Walls, and Aquamoebas.”* 

*“Blue-green is really good against a slower control deck like blue-white if you get a couple recurring utility creatures or effects like Looter, Nantuko Disciple or Narcissism.”*

Ahhh, Archetypes. Archetypes have been around forever, in one form or another, beginning with their origins in the realm of Constructed. The main reason for having a sideboard for your Constructed deck is to tune your deck against the other archetypes you are likely to face. Also, lots of times, knowing how a certain matchup goes for you will also enable you to play it in the manner that is most likely to produce a win. What many people don't realize is that archetypes and matchups are just as prevalent in the Limited formats as they are in Constructed. Knowing when to pick certain cards for your deck over cards that are generally superior is the key to winning matchups against the color combinations against which your deck is weak. This also can help you draft sideboard cards higher when you know your deck is bad against a certain archetype.

The above quotes were from some discussions earlier this year about *Invasion* Block Limited before Pro Tour-Boston. However, not much has been written about the archetypes of Onslaught Limited. This is odd, considering that it is pretty important stuff to know before PT-Chicago rolls around in January. So, let's have a look, shall we?

After weeks and weeks of drafting and testing out theories and specific cards, I feel that I have a pretty good understanding of the format as a whole. I have outlined what I believe to be the top four color combinations in the triple *Onslaught* booster and Rochester draft formats. Below these are the archetypes that just missed the cut and some explanation of why they didn't make it. 

### Blue-Red

We might as well start with my personal favorite, as well as what I believe to be the most powerful archetype in the format. This deck draws its allure from what is arguably the best combo in the format: Mistform Wall and Lavamancer's Skill. Obviously, the Skill is the main reason to draft blue-red, but there are many others. The first thing most people ask when you tell them you're in u-r is “how many Skills”. Sometimes, the answer to that is zero, to which they offer you good luck because they believe your chances of winning will be slim. Certainly Skills, Sparksmiths (best in these colors because of the Mistforms) and the like are excellent tools in a blue-red deck, but you're also not out in the cold if you couldn't get some. Blue, in general, is very underdrafted, despite having solid commons in the form of Ascending Aven, Mistform Dreamer, Mistform Wall, Sage Aven, and Imagecrafter. However, it still can't realistically support more than two drafters per table, which makes it a good deal if you're one of those two. It is still very possible and likely to win a draft with a u-r deck, even if you have no Skills or ‘Smiths. The deck is very good at quick flying assaults backed by burn, Mistform Walls and Choking Tethers. There are a number of cards that I would like to cover whose values fluctuate largely when they are incorporated into a u-r deck:

*Aphetto Alchemist* – This guy is the gatling gun when incorporated into most blue-red builds. He acts as a second round of fire for your ‘Smiths, Skills, or even something more trivial like Disruptive Pitmage. He also turns the normally mediocre Embermage Goblin into a firing squad. When he's not doing all of this or allowing one of your attackers to pull defensive duty, he's acting as Grey Ogre for three mana. 

*Erratic Explosion* – This card, while excellent in many other archetypes, is very mediocre in the blue-red deck. The reason for this is the enormous amount of good one-mana commons the deck has access to. There are Imagecrafters, Goblin Taskmasters, Shocks and Goblin Sledders. You usually end up playing 2-4 one-drops in your deck. Explosion usually targets morph creatures, which is why it is bad with all the 1 mana cards. I still like to have one in the deck. It is particularly good with an effect like Sage Aven or Aven Fateshaper. I don't like to draft more than one Explosion unless my deck is especially suited to it. Often times, I will take something like Mistform Dreamer over the extra Explosions, if my deck has a decent removal base.

*Charging Slateback* – This guy is huge in the u-r deck, adding a quick clock to the board once your pingers have cleared the way. The Slateback is very good in blue-red because you don't really have any hard hitters besides Ascending Avens, and when he isn't going aggro, he will just morph like any good creature should. Also note: the Valesk is even better for this duty, despite being uncommon.

*Battering Craghorn* – I don't know what it is, but I really don't like this guy in these colors because he doesn't really do enough. Sure, you can argue that people are more likely to block the morphs in this archetype and then he is great, but even when he does his job, I don't find him nearly as exciting as I do the Slatebac. This is because of his 1 point of toughness and the extreme vulnerability that goes with it. We all know what any old cycled Solar Blast does to this guy. He also gives Crown of Suspicion a juicy target. It could be a personal problem, but I'm really not that impressed with this guy.

*Skirk Commando* – Though I don't usually have many of these, because most people take them far higher than they should, this guy definitely has a place in the deck and is especially good if you get multiple copies of him along with multiple copies of Choking Tethers.

*Riptide Shapeshifter* – An insane card in general, the Shapeshifter provides a trade with another guy, or better yet, a bomb creature. This guy is great even without bombs, as you can just search up a big red beast or a good flier like Ascending Aven.

*Lightning Rift* – This card goes without saying, though it is even more ridiculous in these colors because of the huge amount of cycling blue commons like Backslide, Mage's Guile and Choking Tethers. Every time I've had this in u-r, I've had at least 10 cycling cards to go with it. When it is cast on turn two, it is a position from which it is very hard to lose.

*Screaming Seahawk* – This guy can save the day for a draft gone badly if you can get about three or four of them. They provide a nice stream of card advantage and damage if your deck is lacking in other areas, though I wouldn't go looking to draft them. I've found three to be the best number to reduce the chances of drawing multiples. They are usually present in the decks that are lacking, from my experience.

Basically, the strength of this color combo lies in the tricks it can produce with the pinger creatures and enchantments, as well as its ability to stall the ground with Mistform Wall and drive through in the air with cost-efficient fliers. It is solid without the tricks and near unbeatable with them, a definite contender for best archetype in the format.

### Black-Green

Beasts you say? Well you've come to the right place. This archetype is all about the beast tricks and fat green monsters combined with black removal, Nantuko Husk and Dirge of Dread. Green's top two commons, Snarling Undorak and Wirewood Savage, are best exploited in this color combination simply due to the enormous number of common beasts in the colors combined. Anything from Wretched Anurid to Spined Basher to Barkhide Mauler will do the trick, as they can all become pumpable cantrip creatures. The zombies are also important for the deck's signature removal spell, Cruel Revival, as well as other things like Shepherd of Rot and Frightshroud Courier. However, the appeal of drafting black-green is again drawn from a particularly annoying two-card combo: Nantuko Husk and Symbiotic Elf. That curve, which often starts with a two-drop like Shepherd of Rot, Elvish Warrior, Wirewood Elf, Wirewood Herald, Wretched Anurid or Wellwisher is very hard to overcome due to the enormous power of the Husk. The Husk is extremely optimal in this deck because of the Symbiotic creatures and the high quality of previously listed early creatures as well as the powerful Festering Goblin. These factors make the Husk a higher pick than any of the green commons.

It should also be noted that Patriarch's Bidding is not only good, but rather insane in these colors due to the presence of Barkhide Mauler and Krosan Tusker. You can get the Bidding late and, if you have enough cycling beasts, it will usually end the game on turn five. The other tech that Gary Wise already unveiled in a previous article is Death Match, which is another black-green specific rare. The card is very hard to deal with when it hits the board, simply due to the number of “double use” creatures these colors have, as well as the fatties that live through it.

*Wirewood Herald* – This guy is an excellent addition to most b-g decks, assuming you have some guys to fetch. Wonderful with the Husk, as well as for searching for important creatures like Savage, Wellwisher, Symbiotic Elf or something like Elven Riders or Voice of the Woods.

*Dirge of Dread* – As already mentioned, this card is a necessary evil for most b-g decks simply because of the amount of firepower that the deck puts out. A lot of games end with the black-green deck casting Dirge.

*Wellwisher* – Very good in decks that need a way to offset the life loss from Wretched Anurids. It also provides a good combo with the Shepherd of Rot, basically draining the life out of your opponent.

*Crown of Suspicion* – Good Sparksmith control as well as a suitable end for other problematic utility creatures. Can also act as a mini-Tribal Unity in a more tribal-oriented deck.

*Aphetto Dredging* – Particularly powerful because of the concentration of good black and green cards into two main types: zombies and beasts. Most of the time, this will return two to three guys, which makes it very worthwhile.

*Screeching Buzzard and Severed Legion* – Necessary evasion guys that get around the defenses of Mistform Walls and large white creatures.

*Vitality Charm* – Great pump spell, trample for the big boys or +2/+2 for the Husk.

Clearly, this deck is the other contender for best color combination in the format, with more people on its side than that of blue-red. The deck boasts a quick and effective assault of efficient men backed by pump and evasion spells, bolstered by good black removal. Let's not forget the elbow drop of a mana curve in Festering Goblin, Wretched Anurid, Nantuko Husk, Symbiotic Elf, which you really can't appreciate until you've been bashed by it. 

### Green-Red

The aggressive deck with burn always seems to be a contender in any Limited format and this one is no different. Like black-green, you have the beast tricks, although the red beasts are not nearly numerous as in black, and not nearly as synergistic with the deck either. However, that is a moot point, seeing how Contested Cliffs actually costs green and red to activate and not green and black. It's really not important to go any deeper than the surface regarding that card and how amazing it is in a deck with enough beasts to feed it. These colors have the usual compliment of fast green and red men, combined with burn and Wave of Indifference. Erratic Explosion is also at it best in the deck, due to the high quality of the more expensive spells. The question of Battering Craghorn or Skirk Commando is also more easily posed in this deck than in blue-red, due to the probability of the morph guy turning out to be a Disruptive Pitmage, Riptide Biologist, or something like that in the blue deck. The deck doesn't really have any specific combo that it seeks like the two archetypes before. This puts the color combination a little lower on the charts than the above, while still remaining extremely potent.

*Wave of Indifference* – This is the only color combo that I feel can really afford to play the Wave, though it is arguable for white-red. This card is very important in the deck because there will be a lot of situations where you could do a ton of damage, but not be able to break through for the final points. This card allows for easy access to that.

*Goblin Sledder* – Your creatures were already hard enough to block due to their original size, but throw in the Sledder and you will be pushing damage through for quite some time. 

*Wirewood Elf* – Very underrated for what he does, the Elf actually does a pretty good job. If you've ever had to play against a turn two Elf, turn three Undorak opening, you'll probably understand what I'm talking about. However, it's not just for that curve, but really because you often have a ton of high casting cost beasts that can be deployed much earlier than normal when you have the Elf on your side. Speed is a very important part of the r-g deck's game plan.

*Elvish Warrior* – Though I already wrote an article for Star City regarding this guy and my overall opinion of him, he is very good in these colors due to their aggressive nature. The same goes for Shock, which allows for a huge morph advantage when going first, by playing two morphs and Shocking their first one by turn 4. The one thing I really don't like is that I am often playing a Sledder when I'm green-red. I don't like that I can't cast him and the Warrior by turn two. This gives my deck awkward mana and turns the Sledder into a bad Vitality Charm in most cases.

*Lay Waste* – While not particularly amazing by any means, Lay Waste can cripple an opponent who's already stumbling off the blocks and bury any chance he ever had of stopping your aggressive team of creatures.

*Searing Flesh* – A game-ender like Wave, except that it doesn't require the creatures in play to do so. A very good card to add to the g-r deck's arsenal.

This is probably the most common draft deck in *Onslaught* block, just because of the usual cohesion of the colors green and red and their similar motives, which are reflected in the cards. It's fast, it has big creatures, and it has burn - what's not to like?

### White-Red

The last spot of my top four is not really set in stone, but I figured I would get yelled at if I didn't include a white deck of some variety. Red is my favorite color to combo with white, despite not being a white fan to begin with. The reason for this is the quick, aggressive fliers like Gustcloak Harrier and Dive Bomber and the second best two-drop in the set (behind Sparksmith, of course): Glory Seeker. This deck is fast, even more so than red-green when you have the appropriate distribution of offensive soldiers. Glory Seeker starts the game off on the right pace to be followed by your fliers, Daru Lancers, Pacifisms and burn. Notice a theme here (Burn in 75% of the top decks)? This is the best deck for the Skirk Commando, due to the three common creatures present that you don't want to block: the Craghorn, Lancer, and Gravel Slinger. Most of the time, in this deck, the Skirk will get through if your opponent doesn't have extensive knowledge of your morphs from earlier games or a Rochester draft.

*Piety Charm* – This deck is all about the soldiers and this Giant Growth is simply amazing. Doubling as an answer to opposing Pacifisms, as well as the ever-potent Lavamancer's Skill, the Charm is a common addition to any deck sporting these colors.

*Commando Raid* – Though also playable in some versions of blue-red with more fliers than normal, this card is great in w-r because of the fliers, along with the Pacifisms and the fact that Daru Lancer will usually get through a few times once face up. It's also good because of the morph situation and the 75% chance of getting wrecked by blocking.

*Sandskin* – Underrated, and AS good as Pacifism in a lot of situations, such as when you are beating through the air with fliers, or as an answer to a bomb creature like Rorix or Silvos, not to mention the combo with Ironfist Crusher.

*Daru Cavalier* – Start the chain, as it's called. These guys are absolutely amazing in white-red if you get three-four and still pretty good if you only have two. They create a near impenetrable wall of first strike as well as a great offensive force, once they get going.

*Grassland Crusader* – Despite how he looks like at first, this guy is actually pretty good in any deck with a decent amount of soldiers. He's usually at the top of your curve and, once online, has a pretty good impact on your side of the game.

Unfortunately, I don't know that much about the white decks beyond the surface, due to my general dislike for the color, but I have drafted white-red a few times to decent enough success to call it one of the top four contenders in the format.

There are some other archetypes I believe to be worth mentioning as well, as I'm sure there are plenty of black-white lovers out there, as well as a handful of black-blue fans.

Black-white missed the cut for a single reason: the only times I have ever had success with the deck were when I had 12+ clerics. It is pretty hard to get a very solid base of clerics when enough of them are good enough that other black and white drafters are taking them over non-cleric cards. Any soldier/zombie deck that I've ever put together has always lost in the face of superior cards and superior card synergies. 

I will draft black-blue on the rare occasions where I keep opening Cruel Revivals and get passed things like Cabal Archon that are very good with Imagecrafter and Mistforms. The problem I have with this is that I end up splashing or passing Lavamancer's Skills, which is something that is suboptimal ether way. I'd rather just be blue-red and take them, but that's just me.
