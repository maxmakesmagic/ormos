Tribal decks have been a popular part of *Magic* dating all the way back to *Alpha* with its [Lord of Atlantis](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lord+of+Atlantis) and [Goblin King](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+King). \[Editor's note: And the eminently forgettable [Zombie Master](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zombie+Master).\] *Onslaught* successfully expanded this theme in the early 2000s, but it was only really the Goblin tribe that made it into Constructed during that era of Standard. *Lorwyn* broke the floodgates open in 2007, making creature swarms play a big part in defining Standard for the next two years.

# Doran

Technically, I guess [Murmuring Bosk](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Murmuring+Bosk) makes this a tribal Treefolk deck, but the real reason I'm listing it first is chronological. Uri Peleg won Worlds 2007 with a version of this deck. The [Abzan Treefolk legend](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=244674) ushered in a comeback for grindy black-green "Rock" decks with a mix of discard, removal, and efficient creatures that have been with us ever since. Okay, fine, [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) probably deserves a lot more of the credit in retrospect, but at the time it was turn-two [Doran](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=140201) that scared most opponents. (The list in the Gauntlet is Nico Bohny's deck from PT Hollywood 2008.)

# Skred Red

By the end of the season, the combination of a bunch of powerful hybrid cards and a very efficient removal spell from *Coldsnap* turned a different kind of mono-red deck into a metagame definer. Michael Jacob used it to win US Nationals in early August, then a couple weeks later the finals of Grand Prix Copenhagen was a Skred Red mirror match!

# Teachings

Even with an entire year's worth of cards devoted mostly to powerful creatures, blue mages would still find a way. Guillaume Wafo-Tapa used the [Vivid lands](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&name=+%5bVivid%5d&set=+%5b%22Lorwyn%22%5d) from *Lorwyn* alongside [Reflecting Pool](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reflecting+Pool) to give himself access to all the colors of answers he could want and then used [Mystical Teachings](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mystical+Teachings) to go get them out of his deck exactly when he needed them.

# Faeries

The truly dominant tribe of this era of *Magic*, of course, was Faeries. While Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa (PVDDR) was the only player to make Top 8 of PT Hollywood with them, that was partially because it had a target on its head. It had won the Standard GP in Shizuoka less than two months earlier. It then helped Paul Cheon claim a US National team slot. By December, PVDR and four other Faerie pilots made the top 8 of Worlds. One of them, Antti Malin, even took home the trophy.



# Cruel Control

This tournament was probably the peak of Gabriel Nassif's amazing career. Not only did he beat LSV in the finals, not only did he help design the most powerful and famous control deck of his era (too much power to fit into 60 cards!), but he also gave us one of the most iconic moments in Pro Tour Sunday history by calling his shot in the quarterfinals. Faced with one crucial draw step before succumbing to Matteo Orsini-Jones, Gabe drew his card face down, slow-rolling all of us (himself included), and arranged his lands into the form of the card he wanted to draw: [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum). There may never have been a better named card or a better named deck.



# Swans

This deck came completely out of left field at Grand Prix Barcelona in the spring of 2009 and crushed an unsuspecting field. Joel Calafell won, and PVDR also made Top 8 with the deck, which uses a bunch of cascade spells that all put [Seismic Assault](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seismic+Assault) into play (since it is the only card in the deck that costs three or less). The idea is that you can then point lands at your own [Swans of Bryn Argoll](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swans+of+Bryn+Argoll) to turn one land into two cards. Since the deck runs 41 lands, this eventually gives you enough lands to throw at the opponent and deal 20! (There was a little proto-Jund running around at this time as well, but we'll save that more traditional use of the cascade mechanic for the next Gauntlet.)
