Our friend <a>Pyknite</a> here was the first creature printed as a cantrip, so to this day R&amp;D members refer to cantrip creatures as &quot;Pyknites&quot;. <a>Pyknite</a> is also a perfect example of the standard formula for cantrip mana costs. Though there are exceptions, being a cantrip typically adds two generic mana to what a given card would otherwise have cost. </td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

With <i>Coldsnap</i> the designers wanted to explore completely new possibilities for cumulative upkeep. The untapping mechanic of cards like this actually began as a twist on cantrips. At the time, cantrips were seen as something that should only be done in sets from time to time, and since cantrips had appeared in <i>Tempest</i>, it was felt the Urza block needed something else.
