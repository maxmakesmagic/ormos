* * *

Wizards of the Coast tries to make ***Magic** Online* a safe, friendly, and fun environment for all of our players. However, there are many things that happen both inside and outside of the ***Magic** Online* game that we can't control. So when you're online, keep in mind some simple rules to protect yourself and your account.

### Don't Give Out Your Password

Protecting your password is essential to keeping your account safe. The only times you're ever asked for you password in the ***Magic** Online* environment (game, store, message boards, etc.) is when you're logging onto our systems. No employee of Wizards of the Coast will *ever* ask you for your password. If anyone posing as a Wizards of the Coast employee asks you for your password, don't give it out and report the incident to magicconduct@wizards.com immediately.

### You're Not Required To Make Your Personal Information Public

You don't have to enter any personal information about yourself in your **Profile** screen. You have the option of entering more information if you want, but don't give out information about yourself (like your real name, your phone number, where you live, or your email address) that you don't want other players to have access to.

### Don't Patronize Websites That Offer *Magic Online* "Cheats"

The only websites associated with the ***Magic** Online* game are the official Wizards of the Coast sites, including the subsites of the [main Wizards of the Coast website](https://web.archive.org/web/20030423000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.wizards.com&origin=magic_magiconline_safetyrules) and the [***Magic** Online* Store](https://web.archive.org/web/20030423000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://magiconlinestore.wizards.com&origin=magic_magiconline_safetyrules). Any site other than those that says it's "official" is probably a scam.

There are no sanctioned cheats, hacks, mods, or any other kind of attachments for the ***Magic** Online* game. Most of the time, when someone advertises a site that offers these things, the site will attempt to "mine" your password from your computer. And talking about, advertising, or patronizing such sites is a violation of the [Code of Conduct](https://web.archive.org/web/20030423000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.wizards.com/coc&origin=magic_magiconline_safetyrules), which means you may be suspended for doing so.

### Don't Accept "Official" Emails At Face Value

If you receive an email that is supposedly from Wizards of the Coast, take the time to check who sent it. Does it come from an "@wizards.com" address? If not, then it's definitely not an offical email.

And even if it looks official, if it asks for your password, offers you free cards, or otherwise says something suspicious, don't fall for it. Report any such emails to magicconduct@wizards.com immediately.

### Anything Done Outside The Game Is Unenforceable

Only purchases made at the [***Magic** Online* Store](https://web.archive.org/web/20030423000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://magiconlinestore.wizards.com&origin=magic_magiconline_safetyrules) are valid external transactions. Any other agreements you make with players outside of the ***Magic** Online* game interface (for example, transactions agreed to via Ebay or using PayPal) can't be enforced by Wizards of the Coast. So, if you agree to purchase cards from someone outside the game, make sure you trust that player.

### Be Careful When Trading

The current system allows for trades of no more than 32 items at one time. Please be aware that any trades of a larger nature should be broken up into smaller fair trades.

Please understand we can't be responsible for "promises" made by your trading partners. Treat each trade as one individual trade. If you suspect your trading partner might be up to something fishy, simply decline to trade with him or her. Be sure you understand what you are trading for and take a good look at what you are getting prior to pushing the confirmation button. Once you commit to the second confirmation, the trade is validated and can't be reversed.

### Wizards Doesn't Replace Digital Objects Lost To Scams

If someone steals your password or pulls a fast one on you during a trade and you lose digital objects because of it, Wizards will not replace them--it's your responsibility to safeguard your information and avoid scams. If Wizards had this type of replacement policy, we'd have the same people who steal digital objects from other players attempting to steal them from us, also. This is why you need to be careful about fishy situations and avoid trading outside the game. Remember, if it looks too good to be true, it probably is!

### You Don't Have To Deal With Troublemakers

While we try to maintain a pleasant and fun environment, in any online community there will always be troublemakers. If you are being harassed by another player, you can simply block them by right-clicking their name, and selecting **Block user**. This will prevent further harassment by that player.

If you have screenshots of harassing behavior, either towards you or other players, send them to magicconduct@wizards.com. If a player is disrupting a room or game, report it to the Adepts and they will try to handle the issue. You can always find an Adept in the **Moderators & Support Staff** room.
