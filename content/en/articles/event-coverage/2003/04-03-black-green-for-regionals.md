![](http://www.wizards.com/regionals/images/regionals.gif)It’s that time of year again. With most of the Pro Tour-Yokohama qualifiers behind us, players are turning their eyes to one of the more important tournaments of the year: Nationals. Some people won’t have to worry about qualifying for it, as the rating cutoff is extremely low in some countries. Qualifying for U.S. Nationals might be as hard as qualifying for a Pro Tour, though. It has a rather high rating cutoff and only one set of qualifier tournaments for it: Regionals.

In the Netherlands, Nationals is early in the season this year. April 18-20 is when the best of Holland will battle it out. This also means that we had our Dutch Nationals Qualifiers already. Even though everyone who is qualified for Dutch Nationals in another way couldn’t play, there still are some decent players left to try and earn a slot there. Those tournaments saw a rogue deck perform remarkably well.

This rogue deck was first brought to those tournaments by Maarten Ferguson. He told me he had seen a black-green Caller of the Claw/Nantuko Husk deck on StarCityGames.com’s forums and tuned it some. He won some local tournaments with it and it made sure he qualified on rating for Nationals. He gave the deck to some of his friends and they finished first in two of the Nationals Qualifiers. The deck turned out to be very versatile and could be tuned to beat any deck in the metagame. This flexibility is what gave it the power to be consistent over a couple of tournaments.

This version was built when the local metagame mostly consisted of r-g and u-g decks. The 4 Braids in the sideboard were maindeck most of the time. I took a version with Braids, Cabal Minion maindeck and played it on ***Magic** Online*, posting reasonably good results. I started out with more Wish targets in the sideboard and a bit more consistent starting configuration, running 4 Ravenous Baloths, 3 Braids and 2 Callers instead of the 2 Phantom Centaurs and 4 Smothers.

The general idea of the deck is to find an answer to every threat your opponent has, or to have so many diversified threats that your opponent doesn’t have an answer. Depending on which deck you’re playing against, you are either the control deck, or the beatdown deck. Due to the lack of strategic dedication this deck is somewhat weaker in it's aggressive role and defensive role than U/G and Psychatog are. It makes up for this slight weakness by adapting the strategy that is needed to beat the opponent.

### Matchups

*U-G Madness*

Here, you are obviously the control deck. Keep in mind that u-g only has Circular Logic before boarding and you should be able to push through the true threats. Stronghold Assassin is insane against it, turning every Wirewood Herald and Llanowar Elf into a Dark Banishing they can’t counter. It takes out Arrogant Wurms and Roars of the Wurm. When Wishing, you will want to fetch Withered Wretch first, taking out their Wonders, Roars of the Wurm and Deep Analyses before they can flash them back. If they discard a Roar of the Wurm to a Wild Mongrel, you have time to remove it from the graveyard before they can cast it; if they discard it through a Careful Study, you won’t. In the late game, you should get out an Oversold Cemetery and win with whatever you feel like. Just make sure you don’t lose early on and you’re winning this matchup.

  

*R-G Beats*

In this matchup, it matters a lot how r-g’s maindeck is configured and how you have configured yours. If you run maindeck Smother, this matchup is a lot easier than if you don’t and the matchup gets harder if they have maindeck Phantom Centaur. Just like against u-g, if you survive past the first few turns, you should eventually win. Grim Lavamancer can give you some trouble, but if you can get recurring Ravenous Baloths, that shouldn’t be a problem anymore. Eventually, you can Living Wish for Visara and deal with the little pests.

Slide is one of the bad matchups for this deck. It has mass removal, threats you can hardly deal with (Exalted Angel) and it controls the board with Astral Slide and Lightning Rift. Fortunately, the deck loses to Wake and Psychatog, making it a mediocre choice for Regionals and you shouldn’t be too scared of running across it. If you do face down the cycling deck, you should focus on putting the pressure on early on. If you get out a Wirewood Herald, keep ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) open as long as you can. If they Wrath of God or Slice and Dice you immediately get out an army of bears to keep the pressure up. You generally Wish for Nantuko Vigilante or Visara the Dreadful but even then, the matchup doesn’t look good.

  

*Wake*

Wake is a mediocre matchup, but much better for you than Slide. If you don’t have Braids, Cabal Minion maindeck, it gets a lot tougher. If you do have her in the starting 60 cards, the matchup is okay. If you get her out, your opponent will have to find an answer very quickly or succumb to your pressure. Cabal Therapy can buy you precious time, but this matchup still isn’t in your favor.

Of course, the deck does have its weak points. Right now, people like to run Callous Oppressor against u-g. Unfortunately, that guy is rather good against this deck as well, as you only have Smother and Stronghold Assassin to deal with it. Also, Phantom Centaur is slightly troublesome, just like Compost. As you are 50% black, these cards are a nuisance, but not impossible to deal with.

If you decide to play this deck, do be aware that there are a lot of little tricks and opportunities you might miss. For example, Caller of the Claw triggers when it comes into play and doesn’t count the number of creatures that went to the graveyard until the trigger resolves. This means that you can sacrifice the Caller of the Claw to a Nantuko Husk to get an extra bear or an extra +2/+2. A trick that doesn’t work is if you have 3 creatures in your graveyard and a Braids, Cabal Minion on the table is to sacrifice a creature and return it with Oversold Cemetery. For Oversold Cemetery, you need to have both four creatures in your graveyard at the beginning of your upkeep and during resolution of the effect.

The most important thing during a game is to realize whether you are the control deck or the beatdown deck. Play out your hand accordingly and make sure that you force through the true threats against the control decks. They will have to deal with your measly creatures at first, and you can make sure that you get out that Braids afterwards, messing up their plans.

There are also some ideas I haven’t had the time to test yet but are worth at least a couple of games. You could add fetch lands to the deck and one mountain, so you can Living Wish for Contested Cliffs. Adding Phantom Centaurs to the deck should improve both the ‘Tog and r-g matchup, but weaken yourself against Wake and Slide. Furthermore, adding Duress should highly improve the Slide and Wake matchup but weaken yourself against u-g and r-g again.

The deck is still in development mostly, but it could turn out to be a consistent and versatile deck. In that case, it would be a very good choice for Regionals. Regardless, I wish you good luck at them.
