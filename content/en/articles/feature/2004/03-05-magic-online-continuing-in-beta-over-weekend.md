Some folks evidently missed [the announcement](/en/articles/feature/2004/03-04-please-read-emergency-changes-to-magic-online) that all accounts are being directed to our beta server, so I'll repeat that here. Until you hear otherwise, when you log on to ***Magic** Online* , you're going to our beta server. On this server, your account doesn't have its normal collection. That collection is in the live server database, where it won't be affected by anything happening on the beta server. As soon as we point accounts back to the live server, you'll see your normal collection.

The programmers have been making steady progress on the code all day. What is progress? At 11:00 this morning, once we hit about 500 players the master server would crash. By 5:00 this afternoon, that number had reached 1650 players. Given the progress that has been made, here's what the plan is for the coming weekend.

The programmers working on the server code will be in until 12 midnight tonight (that's about six hours from the time I post this announcement). They'll also be working from 8 am until 12 midnight (0800 to 0000 hours) Pacific Standard Time on Saturday and Sunday. During this time, expect ***Magic** Online* to be bouncing a lot and occasionally going down for extended periods of time as the programmers make code fixes.

During the hours programmers aren't working on the beta server code, we'll leave the servers running. You can expect ***Magic** Online* to continue to be unstable during this time. As long as the servers are able to restart themselves, the game will come back up. However, there is a chance that the game will go down and won't be restored until the programmers come in the next day.

On Monday morning at 9:00 am (0900 hours) PST, the team will be meeting to discuss the weekend's progress. My next update will most likely be after that meeting is finished and I know what has happened on the development side.

Keep in mind that, if you log on to ***Magic** Online* over the weekend, you'll be on the beta server. This means developers and other team members will be online and actively working on the game. They have administrator privileges and can kick players from the system with no warning. We want you playing on the system to help us test, but we don't need players who harass folks trying to fix the game. If someone on the ***Magic** Online* Team has to kick you from the beta server, don't expect to get back on until the live server is up.

What is the goal of continuing with the beta code up? Since the live server became unstable on Thursday, we decided to push ahead with code that could both improve stability and also might restore the **Premier Events** room. Our current goal is just that: to have a normal load of players on ***Magic** Online* and also have tournaments completing without crashing the game.

Why are we pushing ahead right now? Because system stability was completely unacceptable by Thursday of this past week. The programmers have been working with the beta code for months and had already made substantial progress cleaning it up. Given their recent hands-on experience with that code, it seems better to go ahead and get the beta code ready for live then to attempt to bandage the live server code.

I apologize for the inconvenience. We know you want to get back to playing with *Darksteel* cards on your normal accounts as soon as possible. Hopefully, you'll get a chance to enjoy some free play on the ***Magic** Online* beta servers for a bit while we put things back to rights.

In the meantime, I'm going home and trying to kick the cold I seemed to have picked up. See you all on Monday, when I tell you how things went this weekend.
