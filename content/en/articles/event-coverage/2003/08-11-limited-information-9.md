Our last edition's *Scourge* pack contained some of the set's best green cards, as well as the ubiquitous "free" morph, Zombie Cutthroat. Gains in synergy for the archetype, specific deck, and tribes therein will all be considered in this close pick between power cards. Only three cards received a relevant (more than 5% of the total) number of votes, and rightfully so; no other card comes close to rivaling their power, in this deck or any other red-green build for that matter. Krosan Warchief, offering the beast tribe two welcome abilities, reigned supreme with over half of the votes. Zombie Cutthroat is worth considering in any deck, and the public felt this was no exception as it came in second with about a fifth of the votes. Ambush Commander, which many tout as a must-pick bomb rare, followed close behind the Cutthroat in third. As to be expected, the professionals are one step ahead of the crowd.

**Jeff Cunningham**: What this week's pick boils down to is the knowledge that in life there are good guys, like firefighters, and that there are heroes, like Batman. Krosan Warchief is a good guy, but Ambush Commander is a hero.

Immediately, it's obvious that the call is between those two cards (with Zombie Cutthroat being a clear third.) However, the contents of your deck do not make deciding between these two easy. On one hand, you have some great beasts for the Chief (Vorine, Branchsnap) but on the other, you have a large green component (including five elves), which qualifies the Ambush Commander as a bomb. 

Either choice is going to be good in the deck, but Ambush Commander is simply a more powerful card. Once it's in play, your opponent will be in a very difficult position to make decent blocks. Often, a single unblocked creature can threaten absurd amounts of damage, totally affecting your opponent's game plan. Warchief, even while providing several awesome effects for its mana cost, is halted by many of the same cards that plague conventional green strategies. Sparksmiths and Skills, walls or high toughness creatures, and defensive white-based strategies can all get in the way. Don't get me wrong-- Warchief is great, and great in this deck, but (barring occasional insanity with Krosan Vorine,) it will not contribute to winning games as much as Ambush Commander will, and that's the bottom line.

Ambush Commander, get in my pile. Let's bounce. 

**Antonino de Rosa**: Wow, and I thought the Limited Information a couple of weeks ago was hard. I think the three cards to consider here are Krosan Warchief, Ambush Commander, and Zombie Cutthroat. I believe that Krosan Warchief is the most powerful card in the pack, but I don't believe it is the right pick. You only have six beasts, which I don't think is enough to warrant the pick since Scourge does not have many beasts that you want to draft. Cutthroat, like all the pros mention every week, is very powerful and gains you so much tempo if ever unmorphed to kill an opposing creature, but I don't think it's as powerful as Ambush Commander. Ambush Commander is a bomb, makes your lands creatures which is very good in tight races, and allows you to Giant Growth at will. The Giant Growth ability is just amazing because once the Commander hits the board and it sticks around, it will allow you to win every creature combat war, which is very good. All three picks are very good and sometimes you will pick one over the other on preference alone, but if you want the most powerful spell, Ambush Commander is the way to go.

**Ed Fear**: I pick Krosan Warchief out of this pack. The only three reasonable choices I see are the Warchief, the Zombie Cutthroat and the Ambush Commander. Alpha Status, while sometimes providing an impressive effect, seems particularly ill suited to this deck due to its dichotomy of creature types, and suffers all the inherent weaknesses of creature enchantments. Accelerated Mutation is also good in r-g but we have an Undorak and Patron for pump already and will probably have a chance at a couple of Mutations later in the draft. The Ambush Commander is a tempting pick, and will doubtless win the people’s vote due to its gold expansion symbol, but it is simply inferior to the Warchief in this situation. You are not playing more than perhaps nine forests, needing to support double red spells. Your Goblin Clearcutter may occasionally be eating forests. You have no Alpha Status, no Wellwisher, no Timberwatch Elf, actually no real effects offering any synergy with the Commander. Then of course there are the land-short draws or the times your world is wrecked by a cycled Slice and Dice. There are certainly times the card is a bomb, and every time it hits the table it is an issue to be dealt with, but it is simply not the powerhouse many think it to be. The Zombie Cutthroat will be the powerful card in this deck it always is; you have no Skirk Commandos to really spice up the package, and can not ever hard cast it if your life falls too low, but it is the second best card in this pack. The Warchief, however, remains the best card. It serves as an excellent accelerator to more aggressive draws. Combined with a few large beasts, especially an Undorak, it will usually provide enough power to win a game. As a beast itself, it powers your Wirewood Savage, and encourages the picking of more beasts to do so. It has especially powerful interactions with several beasts in your deck, including Krosan Vorine and Branchsnap Lorian. Combined with your Gournas, you will have constructed a deck that will be quite well-suited against any color but black, which you must simply try and outrace. Leave the rares for the kiddies.

**Eugene Harvey**: There are four cards that stand out in this pack. Zombie Cutthroat, Rush of Knowledge, Krosan Warchief, and Ambush Commander are all first picks. First I would rule out the Rush, because the deck as it stands is pretty solid and has no way or need to splash another color. The Cutthroat is next to go because it just isn't nearly as powerful as the other three cards in contention. 

The current cards we have are all playable-to-very-solid in level. The mana curve is fine, and with the exception of Timberwatch Elf, the deck has all the good green commons. Because the deck has no real holes currently, the best option is to go for power. Ambush Commander is the more powerful of the two cards, as it can win the game within turns of being cast. Maybe if the deck had many more beasts than elves, the pick would change, but the way it is set up the Commander is definitely correct.

**Bryan Hubble**: The best pick here is Ambush Commander without a doubt. First off, it will hands down win you the most games of any other card you could pick. The Commander dominates the attack and provides you with a mid to late game finisher. While the Krosan Warchief, the second best pick in this pack, combos well with the selection of beasts in your deck (especially Krosan Vorine), the Commander is a one-card combo. It provides much the same effect as the Warchief except instead of simply regenerating only beasts, it allows any creature you control to survive combat while dealing more damage and eliminating nearly any blocker. Played on turn five, or even turn four with the Wirewood Elf in your deck, the Commander threatens a potential nine extra points of damage next turn. At this point in the draft, you also have only four red cards so the potential to play as many as eleven forests makes the Commander even better.

A cursory glance at the last few installments of Limited Information would have one believe I have something personal against Zombie Cutthroat. Most proclaimed it the best common in the set, and in a historic first, the best common for any color or color combination. Yet first I attempted to demonstrate its weakness compared to an innocuous creature enchantment, and now I immediately rule it out yet again. The reason for disseminating my anti-Cuthroat doctrine is simple: the card is overrated. While one should consider it as a potential pick in any color combination, and its surprise value can win a game on its own, one should not automatically select it. Recognize that its effectiveness is determined through a cost-benefit ratio. The costs are ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) or ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif) and five life for the return of a 3/4 zombie, or a 3/4 zombie instant-speed trick. In this deck, one can never select the ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) cost, and the zombie creature type is only relevant against an opposing Cruel Revival. Also detrimental to the Cutthroat cause is the consistency of the surprise that its five-life option generates. In a green-red deck, your opponent will expect large creatures more than anything else (unless as Ed Fear points out, you have already made him fear Skirk Commando). This is not to say that Zombie Cutthroat can not still be a tempo-gaining surprise. If you play a turn 3 morph on the draw and your opponent attacks with a face-down Snarling Undorak, Wingbeat Warrior, or Gravel Slinger, Zombie Cutthroat is still the only trump card. The point is that the probability of your opponent playing into your Cutthroat varies based on your color combination, and that green-red is a very poor archetype for it. The chance of yielding the return one demands from it goes down, and the rigidness of its cost goes up. While it is still leagues better than the actual green and red Scourge commons, we are fortunate enough to have superior options.

In direct opposition of each of the confident professionals and the lopsided data reflected in the public poll, I feel that Krosan Warchief and Ambush Commander are of nearly equivalent value in this deck. Ambush Commander is in fact the bomb rare many would have you believe. It provides several different functions that a green deck often craves, and does its job well. Turning all of your forests into 1/1 elves renders not only an exploitable tribal effect but also a threat of swarming your opponent out of the game. Sacrificing any elf for a Giant Growth makes this newfound agglomeration even harder to contain, as any blocked land or low-casting cost elf can add to the unblocked damage. Its activated ability makes combat unattractive to your adversary, resulting in a total lock with the flying protection of Gournas. Trading with any of your larger beasts is inadvisable, particularly if it requires a gang-block. Not only is Ambush Commander powerful, but it is versatile, and the good does not stop there. The threat of its activation is often as good as the activation itself, meaning that the opponent must have a great understanding of what your game plan is and what resources are most important within that plan, just to play against it appropriately. 

Ambush Commander can win a game that almost no card is capable of winning, but its value is conditional. A price of five mana is not without its risks. One might stumble on mana and be unable to cast it. Without two mana open to protect it from the majority of burn spells, it is quite fragile as a five casting cost 2/2. In an even mana base or a deck without many elves, its value goes down proportionally. Perhaps it is drawn along with several other expensive spells while playing the role of the control player against a quick red-black or blue-white flying beatdown. Note that since its power is based on the availability of excess resources (particularly land), it is by far at its best either in the very late game or in the hands of the aggressor. Green will rarely be capitalizing on an opponent's weak early game in modern-day Limited Magic, where it is more often the control deck. On the other side of the coin, if the green deck's goal is to reach the late game, an effect that consistently dominates that portion of the game has great value. The Commander only adds to the tempo problem (a constant thorn in the side of a slow green mage), but it takes all the luck out of the game when the time is right.

Krosan Warchief might fight fires in his spare time, but he's in the Justice League as well. This Green Lantern-worthy three-drop is not only reasonable on its own, but accelerates and protects all other beasts. Due to green's slow but combat-dominating creatures, its weakness is early aggression. This makes mana acceleration such as Wirewood Elf and Explosive Vegetation very valuable, but these cards have the weakness of hurting your action: mana ratio, and thus your late-game draws. This is not a problem with the Warchief, which provides a reasonable body, a great ability, and mana acceleration. Its ability spells creature elimination with provoke, and either an Abyss or quick clock with a low-casting cost, high-power beast. Late game, a Krosan Warchief will ensure that victory is only a giant man away. While its weaknesses are not as numerous as Ambush Commander (at the price of its decreased power), it has the much-discussed turn three face up weakness, and obviously requires the tribal benefit to be online moreso than the Commander. Jeff Cunningham correctly points out that a worst-case scenario for green such as active Lavamancer's Skill/Sparksmith or a toughness-based defensive fortress will trump Krosan Warchief. I would contend that active Lavamancer's Skill/Sparksmith means that Ambush Commander will never be given the chance to win you the game either, as you will be too far behind. Commander could very likely win you a game against a white defense that Warchief will do nothing to help against, though. 

Once one understands the two in the abstract, the deck in question must be examined as well. Where tribe is concerned, the deck has six beasts and a Wirewood Savage for the Krosan Warchief, and five elves for the Ambush Commander. Krosan Vorine and Branchsnap Lorian create an especially potent combination with the Warchief's regeneration ability, while the rest of the beasts benefit from its casting cost reduction. The Savage and the Warchief work very nicely together as well, as more cards mean more of a need for resources and vice versa. Seven beast-friendly cards are enough to make the Warchief good but not great, though Ben Rubin argues that many more will be coming. While there are tons of large guys in Scourge, the only beasts are Krosan Warchief, Kurgadon, Hunting Pack, Titanic Bulvox, Bonethorn Valesk, and Chartooth Cougar. While that only totals three commons and three uncommons, that's a relatively high percentage of how many vaguely playable red and green cards there are. Patron of the Wild equals two Giant Growths with an Ambush Commander, and thus a notch in its column. As Bryan Hubble points out, there are only four red cards and therefore the deck will play enough forests to ensure a very effective Commander, an eventuality even more likely given red's Scourge quality. Eugene Harvey asserts that the deck has no real holes and should go for power, and I agree that this is a great reason to choose Commander. Without a game-breaker, whether it be the relatively weak Wave of Indifference or a bomb card, Ambush Commander's late-game supremacy is even more valuable.

Unfortunately, neither card breaks away from the other when analyzing its value to the deck. The decision comes down to whether it is more important to aid a green deck's early game tempo and combinations, or supply it with a consistent late-game winner. Though it is close, I choose Krosan Warchief. My instinct tells me that the deck has a greater need to get its beasts on the table on time than to outright win the late game, where it has a solid chance already. Bear in mind that my perspective on draft errs on the side of the early game, as demonstrated by the first Limited Information where I chose Ascending Aven over Centaur Glade.
