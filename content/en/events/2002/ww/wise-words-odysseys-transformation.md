![](https://i.ormos.uk/e8/24/e82456fffbf885f5471aa4d04837f3598c369a253b3098fa9c0fb29319d99f4e.jpg)Over the past few months, our community has become accustomed to the way Constructed environments can change. In observing the machinations within the Extended format, the only thing we knew for sure was that next week, nothing was for sure, and that we could each individually be wrong about what was going to be good whenever we showed up for any given tournament.

One difference that became apparent during that season between Limited and Constructed play was the way an environment could change in Constructed. With such a broad card pool, each week, people were finding new ways of beating the decks that had surprised everyone the week before, meaning the quest for new decks was constant and needed vigilance if it was to be successful. In Limited, while the hope for archetypical discoveries is there, the emphasis must be put on environmental knowledge: how to build the archetypes, what each one is strong or weak against, and most importantly, how each card is valued.

With the introduction of *Torment* into *Odyssey* Block Limited, the old environment wasn't so much added onto as eliminated and begun anew. With the addition of the cards of *Torment* to the environment, the card values in *Odyssey* change, meaning that if you want to be truly prepared to draft at Friday Night **Magic**, with a couple of friends in your basement or at the Pro Tour, in order to be best prepared, you must come to an understanding of how those values have fluctuated. This week's Wise Words is being written in order to help you do just that.

In any environment, Limited or Constructed, when a new set is added, we must first account for the contents of that set in attempting to ascertain how it might affect the strength of other cards. In looking at *Torment* as a whole, as we must when looking at a format that uses cards at random, here's what we find:

1.  Black is now the dominant color. This shifts the emphasis from the pattern that had been established of alternating blue and green players.
2.  Madness is the newest five-color ability. This means that when you're drafting *Odyssey*, you must take the desire to abuse this ability into account.
3.  There aren't a lot of large common creatures. 3/2 and 2/3 are as big as it gets in black, 3/3 the biggest in green. This means that extreme size may have an added value.
4.  White is pretty awful in *Torment*. There will seldom be more than two white players at a table as a result.
5.  There are a lot of one-toughness creatures in the environment.
6.  There are no artifacts in *Torment*

While that's a lot of information to assimilate in making these distinctions, there are some pretty easy nuggets we can dig up from these:

1.  Anti-black cards increase in strength, while green and blue could be a lot of fun to draft now they aren't being fought over.
2.  Creatures that allow you to discard a card of your choice are a little better now than they once were.
3.  4/4 creatures are very powerful in this environment and should be respected in this regard when being considered for drafting.
4.  Anti-white cards are weakened considerably, while if you're the white player, you may want to draft your other color a little more heavily under the assumption you'll get the white stuff late.
5.  You'll want to avoid having too many one-toughness creatures yourself while cards that deal with one-toughness creatures effectively find their value enhanced.
6.  Enchantment kill is just as effective as enchantment/artifact kill.

So with this information as a starting point, here's a look at the cards in *Odyssey* whose values have changed with the addition of *Torment*:

### Junk Golem, Patchwork Gnomes, Dirty Wererat, Fledgling Imp, Rites, Krosan Archer, Acceptable Losses, Patrol Hounds, blue Atogs, and any other card that allows you to discard a card from your hand

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6f/da/6fdaa2797226e7be11337879c16235f11e73e688dcb738e70469e5af7ec0491f.jpg)A year ago it would have seemed ludicrous to suggest that discarding could be a good thing, but here we are in the age of threshold and madness. All of the cards listed above share one attribute: they allow you to discard a card from your hand for a reasonable price, and with both madness and threshold relying upon your ability to discard, that becomes important. When drafting *Odyssey*, keep in mind that despite madness, there aren't thousands of ways to discard in *Torment*. That means you'll want two or three coming out of the first two packs.

### Frightcrawler, Shadowmage Infiltrator, Ghastly Demise, Zombie Assassin

With the heightened amount of black in the format, those black cards that don't want your opponents playing black are going to be devalued. *Torment* means more untargetable creatures for banish effects and that Fear is not as strong an ability as it once was. 

### Whispering Shade

What once was little more than a sideboard card is now main deck playable. If you're siding a card in more than half the time, you should probably play that card main, and that would be the case with the Shade. I still wouldn't draft it too highly, as you'll want to board it out vs. non-black decks, but more often than not, when you cut down to twenty-three cards, this one will make the grade.

### Chainflinger, Painbringer, Psionic Gift, Magma Vein, Engulfing Flames, Volcanic Spray

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c7/c4/c7c4f846b73f8c265718001cc569b29d74ab10ffb9b7348e64a3c5bbb974baec.jpg)Each of these spells, grouped together by virtue of their ability to deal one point of damage to one or more creatures more than once, should find their value heightened by the number of small creatures in *Torment*, the importance of killing the Torturer for some decks and the ability to kill larger creatures when combined with other effects. 

### Aether Burst, Flame Burst, Muscle Burst

These cards, which improve in multiples, suffer in that there is now one less pack in which to find the Burst in question, Pardic Firecat or Diligent Farmhand. Each is strong enough on its own to warrant play regardless of numbers, but the possibility of true exploitation has definitely diminished with the introduction of *Torment*.

### Phantom Whelp, Dematerialize

Unfortunately for Eric Lauer's blue dog, it's been rendered obsolete by a newer model. Aquamoeba is a turn two play that stays on the table, with a greater toughness and potential greater power that offers you an outlet for madness. Sorry puppy, you just can't compete.

Dematerialize has the same problem. White the flashback is a nice bonus, at seven mana it isn't practical, which means that Churning Eddy is the preferable choice.

### Treetop Sentinel

Green is less plentiful. Who cares, I'm still picking it high.

### Pilgrim of Virtue, Sphere of Grace

![](https://i.ormos.uk/40/69/4069e7181d0087672807b9cb38572997323f4ce4375f4c36fde22ea0185d1369.jpg)Simple math here: increased black means anti-black cards increase in value. The Pilgrim is main deck playable now, though shouldn't be drafted too highly.

### Pilgrim of Justice

The value of protection from red hasn't diminished in the face of the unusual numbers in *Torment*, but with a heavy increase in non-damage related creature kill, as well as the increased importance of anti-black materials, there just isn't room for this Pilgrim in main decks any more.

### All white cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f2/97/f297e95678e39ca17445c494667d43c97ec137c462ab80c815ea384d340e78bf.jpg)Some players are going to be unwilling to draft white in this format, but for those of you who aren't so intent on limiting yourselves, there are two approaches you can take: either you can force the color on the assumption that no one else wants it, or you can wait to see where the draft takes you, with a fourth pick Mystic Zealot or Shelter providing the signal you need to let you know you'll head into white. 

If you take the former approach, white cards will have their values increased. If you take the later approach though, the white cards will actually be devalued, as you're only drafting the color because it's being neglected. The result of this is that you'll have to fight for cards in your other color, while the hope that early white cards might come back to you isn't an unrealistic one. In other words, using this strategy, white cards will be devalued.

### Dogged Hunter

Fewer Elephants and Beasts to kill for this sideboard card devalues it.

### Mystic Crusader, Resilient Wanderer

These creatures are both capable of single-handedly taking out the black mage. Give them a little more credit when drafting them.

### Eggs

For those white and green players who are going to find themselves a little low on cards. The Eggs provide an option that will help cycle you through your deck in the constant search for bombs.

### Mindslicer

The quality of instant-speed bounce and creature kill has diminished to the point where it will be much more difficult to take great advantage of the Slicer's 'drawback'. Of course, it could be interesting with Madness.

### Repentant Vampire

![](https://i.ormos.uk/28/f3/28f3fa6a3e2366f8cd33823aa7bdaa283d4d5b3a7bfc3207a8f445e9dfadd64f.jpg)A few weeks ago, in one of my first *Odyssey-Torment* drafts, I used the Vampire's tap ability for the first time. It used to be little more than an Aven Windreader. Now, it's definitely a more important card. 

### Execute

The best sideboard card in the format is still very powerful, but there just aren't as many worthwhile targets to go around. Draft it a little lower.

### Mystic Enforcer, Thaumatog

White-green is so bad now that you virtually have to splash either of these cards if they're to be played. The Enforcer is still worth it: the 'Tog probably won't be.
