If we're talking about players at [Grand Prix Los Angeles](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-los-angeles-2014-coverage-events), we can't overlook Brian Kibler. He missed out on the Top 8 by a single win, ending up in the Top 16 with this Temur Aggro build. [His deck](/en/events/2014/gpla14/deck-tech-temur-with-kibler) is notable for its handle on tempo.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ashcloud%20Phoenix)](https://web.archive.org/web/20141031000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashcloud%20Phoenix)

It contains cards we haven't seen much in the new Standard, like [Heir of the Wilds](https://web.archive.org/web/20141031000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Heir+of+the+Wilds) and [Ashcloud Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20141031000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashcloud+Phoenix), both of which can force a one-to-one trade with cards that have higher mana costs, and [Crater's Claws](https://web.archive.org/web/20141031000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crater%27s+Claws), which can deal more damage than their mana would normally permit (assuming you can meet the ferocious condition).

Another feature of this deck is its willingness to treat anything as a disposable resource—a philosophy represented well with the inclusion of [Boon Satyr](https://web.archive.org/web/20141031000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boon+Satyr). Kibler's focus on tempo is so honed, he's even electing to play without his trademark Dragons, leaving [Stormbreath Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20141031000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stormbreath+Dragon) on the bench.

Sometimes, the best thing to do is not to add, but to subtract. Sounds simple, but it's easier said than done.
