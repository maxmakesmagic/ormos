### Green

It's tough when a good color experiences a drop off. Green, the class of straight *Odyssey* Limited, takes a hit in *Torment*, as expected. Where green's great common creatures went ten deep in *Odyssey*, they're few and far between where *Torment* is involved. That leaves the color short of cards if people draft green as they once did.

Best in tandem with red for the most aggressive deck in the format, or with blue, the color most able to exploit green's great threshold creatures, green is still solid overall, but the problem now lies in there not being enough green in *Torment* to satisfy all those lured in by Wild Mongrel, Beast Attack and the other green goodies of the first two packs.

Not that green is without its high points in *Torment*. The madness creatures are extraordinary, Gurzigost is a powerhouse and Acorn Harvest provides a newer, better way to produce massive numbers of squirrels, but the good threshold stuff is gone, as are the good enhancement spells, and that should leave the green mage longing for the end of 2002. 

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/81/18/811837f9406ba9e2c0f71e61b29293c2ffcba92a6a87967db10254ad26f4ccef.jpg)**Acorn Harvest**  
 This card is a lot better than I originally thought. For six mana and three life, you get four squirrels, providing you with plenty of opportunities for all manner of swarming, chump blocking and the like. Especially good in green-red where Rites of Initiation and the Harvest can win a game as the only spells cast throughout, the Harvest will give blue-black mages fits regardless. A 4th - 6th pick 

**Anurid Scavenger**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Scavenger provides a high power and toughness for the mana involved and as a bonus you get its protection from black, but the problem with this card can be found in its drawback. With green sporting so many strong threshold creatures, it's seldom that you want to be removing non-threshold cards from your graveyard, and the Scavenger's survival basically ensures you'll never get threshold again. Don't leave it in your sideboard if you get it, but before you pick it over some other playable card, consider the effect it could have on your deck. A 4th - 6th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e6/63/e6632160b19524e012f85bdedfa2e88a5cdaf93852c1aec52d72bcf04b4210b2.jpg)**Arrogant Wurm**  
 This guy is insane. If this were a mere 4/4 trampler for five mana, it would be very solid in this environment, but its madness aspect makes it so much more. Whether it's just an undercosted creature or one that comes out as an instant to kill off an opposing attacker, the Wurm is going to dominate as long as you have ways of discarding it in your deck. Make sure you do, and pick this high. A 1st - 2nd pick. 

**Basking Rootwalla**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Like the Wurm, it's the Rootwalla's madness cost that makes it so good. Granted, one casting cost creatures aren't usually able to trade with Elephant tokens, and the 'Walla's pumpability would make it the best one casting cost green creature in the format by a long shot anyways, but when a card whose effect is so strong as it effectively replaces itself, you have the makings of greatness. A 2nd - 4th pick 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e8/db/e8dbb7b4c058a758f21a06459e338096486209ad15c3dce9f2d0bc4c8b042213.jpg)**Centaur Chieftain**  
 This is a great card. Four mana for a 3/3 is a very solid deal in this format, and that this has haste is a great bonus that makes it a high pick caliber card. The real bonus though is what the Chief does with threshold, creating an Overrun style effect that throws in an additional attacker as a bonus. There's very little the green mage should be taking over this particular Centaur. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Centaur Veteran**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Of course, then you have your not-so-great centaur varieties. I think I'd prefer the Chieftain's abilities if the two cost the same, and yet the Veteran costs two more. Granted, the Veteran gives you a madness outlet, but that just isn't enough to warrant the playing for a 3/3 creature for six mana in a green deck. An 8th - 10th pick. 

**Dwell on the Past**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Dwell on this: This card is not good in Limited. A 15th pick.

**Far Wanderings**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Much like Deep Reconnaissance, the problem with this card is that it costs three mana instead of the two we've come to hope our mana fetching cards will cost. Turn three is too important a turn to be using to put more land into play more often than not, so you should reserve your use of this card for those times you need to ensure your ability to draw your third mana type. A 9th - 11th pick, its main deck playable in most sealed decks.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/44/4e/444e1d62db2228bb63e194de8baeff96a07fe98216daf0eebd58c54e5fe8f3f4.jpg)**Gurzigost**  
 Now this is a dominant card. Play Gurzigost, attack four times, game ends. The bad news with this card is that you draw one card a turn while needing to get rid of two from your graveyard, meaning eventually you'll run out. The good news is that by the time you do, your opponent will be at -4 life. A 1st pick. 

**Insist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Like Overmaster, Insist is main deck playable in that it cycles for one mana, but on top of the benefits of being a cantrip, Insist can prove to be pretty good against blue players. Simply, making sure your creatures can't be countered (This should be especially good with Arrogant Wurm) adds a degree of security

**Invigorating Falls**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A four mana sorcery that on turn four gains you zero life. When you're playing green, focus on killing your opponent, not on stopping your opponent from killing you. A 13th - 15th pick.

**Krosan Constrictor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Because the environment is what it is, the Constrictor is main-deck playable in a pinch. 2/2 creatures for four mana that don't do anything against non-black decks except attack and block shouldn't command much respect when you're drafting, and if you're playing black, the odds are that you'll face more non-black players, so I'd recommend leaving it in the sideboard at that point. 8th - 10th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/56/87/56871347fb67bcdf68659063e78cff2b51fcf0386eee35f5286f43f6985bc57a.jpg)**Krosan Restorer**  
 While the Restorer is hardly optimal, there are situations where you'll find your decks do want or need it in order to maintain some sort of balance in the face of a poor mana base. If you're desperate to straighten out those mana problems before they start, you could do worse. Much better in sealed deck. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Nantuko Blightcutter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While the upside is very situational, there isn't a lot of bad to the Blightcutter. It's passable (though not brilliant) as a 2/2 for three, while the protection from black and threshold abilities are both able to factor in heavily during the course of the draft, so all in all it's a pretty good card. I'd prefer something that's more consistently solid, but the green mage can't be too picky in *Torment*. A 4th - 6th pick.

**Nantuko Calmer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If green's problems in *Torment* are exemplified by any one card, this is probably it. The Calmer would hardly have been playable in straight *Odyssey* draft, with so many good green creatures in that set, but the selection in *Torment* comes up lacking by comparison, which makes this a lot more playable. Yes, the abilities provided by tapping and threshold are both nice as far as versatility is concerned, but I prefer strength to versatility in my green creatures. A 5th - 7th pick. 

**Nantuko Cultivator**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A very solid card, the less important your excess land is, the better the Cultivator is. That means you want your deck to be as quick as possible with this guy. If it is, you'll be doing well when you draw three cards for playing your 5/5. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Narcissism**  
 The best green non-creature spell in the set, Narcissism does it all. Making every one of your creatures a behemoth, it also provides a medium for madness, allows you to get to threshold easily and makes your otherwise weak cards strong again, turning each one into a Giant Growth of sorts. The moment this hits the table, your opponent is assured of a fight for their life. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Nostalgic Dreams**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Most trumpeters of this card point to its ability to return your bombs to your hand, but more often than not, casting those bombs the first time should leave you in good shape to win regardless. This isn't to say the Dreams is unplayable, but for it to be active you have to have reasonable targets in your graveyard and disposable cards in your hand. That's a lot that has to happen for a card that probably isn't playable in the first seven turns of the game. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Parallel Evolution**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Five mana is just too much for a card that will give opposing green mages duplicate squirrels and elephants, even with the flashback. A nice concept, but I think more often than not, if you make the quirky decision to main deck Parallel Evolution, you'll wish you'd left it in the board. A 12th - 14th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ca/ac/caac9617cf3d3aebc507c05a3bbbc65449af3da260f8adb0c5e5f8d1521e26a6.jpg)**Possessed Centaur**  
 Of the four possessed creatures, this is the weakest, but that doesn't mean it isn't good. If this were a mere 3/3 trampler for ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif), the Centaur would be very solid in the format, but with its threshold ability being what it is, this becomes a viable first pick, especially for the black-green mage. Trample is the least impressive of the four abilities, and 3/3s for four are less exceptional in green then elsewhere, but this is still a pretty good card, no matter how you paint it. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Seton's Scout**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While the Scout is no replacement for Wild Mongrels lost in the third pack conversion of *Odyssey* to *Torment*, this card definitely provides its own merits. Best in blue-green decks where you're going to get threshold faster than other color combinations, the Scout is an offense-capable two drop that doesn't lose consequence in the late game. The Scout won't do too much for you on defense, though having the option to trade it for a flyer is nice. A 4th - 6th pick.
