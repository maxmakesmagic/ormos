A complex political format such as Rochester Draft can only complicate this for a beginner. Not only do you need to make the right picks, but there is additional pressure regarding which picks not to make -- knowing when to/not to counterdraft is almost as difficult as understanding pick orders, print runs, and archetypes by color combination.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnag05/b1.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>If you're gonna hurl, hurl into this.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>It's enough to make you sick. In fact . . . one player (who will remain nameless) sat down for his first ever Pro Tour event and looked around and saw at least six Pro Tour Top 8 appearances between the players on either side of him. He suddenly looked very green and glanced around nervously as his hand went to his mouth. He jumped up from his seat and looked around frantically. He spotted a trash can and proceeded to get sick in it. With that out of the way he returned to his seat and prepared to draft.</p>&#10;      <p>If anyone else at the draft table noticed it, they said nothing. After all, everybody's a Pro Tour newbie once in their lives. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Friday, January 28: 10:43 am - Plan B</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Brian David-Marshall</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <p>I settled in behind Anton Jonsson to watch his first draft on the weekend. Anton was the beneficiary of a fourth-pick <a>Kitsune Blademaster</a> and was well-positioned for white despite a second pick <a>Kabuto Moth</a> by England's Ben Twitchen. He looked to be blue-white by the third pack when he added <a>Cage of Hands</a> and <a>Consuming Vortex</a> by the time the first-pick marker wound its way around the table to him.</p>&#10;      <p>He opened nothing useful and could scarcely believe it himself as he put the 6-mana <a>Kami of the Palace Fields</a> on top of his three other cards. This started something of a trend for Anton as his next couple of picks were pricey white fliers. By now, Anton's countryman Simon Carlsson was also in blue-white and Anton watched as the fifth pack was cherry-picked for <a>Teller of Tales</a> and <a>Kami of Ancient Law</a> while he had to take a wormy <a>Kami of the Painted Road</a>. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnag05/b2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Anton Jonsson was stymied in his attempt to solidify his deck.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>It seemed like Anton was destined to never see another tier-one common creature in blue or white. He did manage to pick up a second <a>Cage of Hands</a> along with <a>Mystic Restraints</a>. His next creature was the decidedly unglamorous <a>Samurai Enforcers</a> followed up by his second <a>Consuming Vortex</a> before his draft came to its crossroads. </p>&#10;      <p>Anton had a choice between his second <a>Mystic Restraints</a> or a <a>Dampen Thought</a> and he divided his cards into two distinct piles. The first was his arcane spells with a couple of <a>Ethereal Haze</a>s, <a>Consuming Vortex</a>es, Eye to Nowheres, a <a>Sift Through Sands</a>, and the like. The other pile offered the potential to beatdown with five and six-drop fliers backed up by double Cage and Restraints. He took the maximum amount of time to make his decision and finally resigned himself by taking the splice-based path. </p>&#10;      <p>Anton let out a bitter chuckle as the next pack offered him a choice between <a>Teller of Tales</a> and <a>Eerie Procession</a>. He thought long and hard about taking the ersatz second <a>Dampen Thought</a> but took the Teller instead, hoping that the rest of the table might let the tutor wind its way back to him. It almost made it all the way around by Chih-Hsiang Chang jumped on that grenade before it happened. </p>&#10;      <p>The following pack was Anton's to open and he was greeted with <a>Hikari, Twilight Guardian</a>. Hindsight is 20/20 and he looked at the <a>Eerie Procession</a> he passed on last pack and shook his head sadly. The draft wrapped up with Anton -- in no particular order -- taking a late <a>Reverse the Sands</a>, blue Zubera, and a second <a>Mystic Restraints</a>. </p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Eerie Procession" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Eerie%20Procession&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>The two players between Anton and Simon continued to peck at cards that might get to Anton late such as an Eye to Nowhere and a <a>Soratami Cloudskater</a>. Anton was still shaking his head when the draft ended.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I don't know what happened,&quot; Anton said after the draft. &quot;I don't want to be green -- I don't see how people win with green. I don't want to be blue-black either. That's about the only two things I don't want to be,&quot; he explained when I asked him what his intentions were when he sat down to draft. </p>&#10;      <p>Anton ended up with a deck that was halfway between a blue-white evasion deck and a blue-white Dampen Deck. &quot;This is a bad version of the Dampen Deck. I am missing the key cards. I need the draw-two cards (<a>Counsel of the Soratami</a>) and I need more <a>Peer through Depths</a>.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>He struggled to figure out the correct build for the deck and laughed as he went through his sideboard and held up a Reiko Lantern. &quot;Did you notice how every pack had one of these? Although it as not as good as people think it is against this deck.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Anton laid out 21 cards and tried to figure out his last card. His two choices were <a>Reverse the Sands</a> and one of his two <a>Mystic Restraints</a>. He had the opportunity to take an <a>Eye of Nowhere</a> over the second one but attempted to bounce it around the table -- it ended up in Hsiang's sideboard along with the Procession. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I probably should have taken the Eye. It would have made the deck right now over the Restraints. 8-mana spell it is…&quot; shrugged Anton as he moved the <a>Reverse the Sands</a> into the played pile. &quot;I have never played with this card before.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>When asked about the opportunity to pick <a>Eerie Procession</a> over <a>Teller of Tales</a>, Anton was still unsure about the pick. &quot;It ended up being wrong when I got Hikari…and it might just be the wrong pick anyway.          <i>by Brian David-Marshall</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <p>While Anton Jonsson went into the archetype somewhat reluctantly Quentin Martin -- who recently came to the <b>Magic</b> community's attention by drafting the archetype at Grand Prix-Paris -- is always ready to go in that direction. </p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Dampen Thought" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Dampen%20Thought&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>&quot;I often force the archetype,&quot; Quentin said. &quot;Today I picked up an early Peer and Reach but I always take those anyway -- just in case. Then with the seventh pack of the draft I got to wheel a <a>Dampen Thought</a> and a <a>Sift through Sands</a>.&quot; </p>&#10;      <p>Quentin started out black-blue but eventually was able to pick up a couple of white cards to support the blue. He was very happy with how his deck turned out and even happier about it in the context of his draft table. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;There was no hate in the entire pod. No <a>Distress</a>, No Hisuke's Defiance, no <a>He Who Hungers</a> -- that card is insane against my deck,&quot; he said. </p>&#10;      <p>There was only a <a>Cranial Extraction</a> in the very last pack and he suspected it was a rare draft.</p>&#10;      <p>The big buzz of the first draft was Columbus winner Pierre Canali's deck with four copies of <a>Dampen Thought</a>.
