Winning in cool ways can make it more fun, but generally I want to win. I do, however, have tons of fun regardless of winning in drafts and casual formats. <br><b>Gary Wise</b>: I have a different kind of fun than I used to. A long time ago, Magic was a hobby and trivial pursuit, in which I could afford to be creative and not care about competition. Now, it's a challenge, a locking of wills, both in the playing of the game and the attempt to figure out formats. It's not the same, wholesome, unadulterated fun, but at the same time I get to have good times with good people because of my competitive attachment to it.<br><b>Victor van den Broek</b> : I have fun when I'm winning.... and I try to make sure I can play casually with friends as well. Usually, if I play a GP or PT, I am not having all that much fun playing, but I enjoy the mental battle.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: Only if I win. So, rarely.<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: Yes and no. In the beginning, the fun came from simply playing the game. Now, most of the enjoyment I get from the game comes from the people I meet and the places I get to travel.<br><b>Nathan Heiss</b>: Yes, otherwise I probably wouldn't play. I find much more enjoyment in DC-10 and other silly formats than regular sanctioned play most of the time - I can't wait to try out Two-Headed Giant Draft!<br><b>Jeroen Remie</b>: I really don't know. If I play Magic every day for a long time, I really get sick of it fast. I guess I don't really like the game that much, but I play it for the people. Seeing your friends from around the world every PT makes it all worth it. After I went on hiatus before Worlds, I started liking the game again though, and at the moment I love it. Making Top 8 does that to a man.<br><b>Kai Budde</b>: Yes, otherwise I would've stopped. I usually play once a week a Two-Headed Giant Team Sealed kind of thing. Other than that, I play only in tournaments though.<br><b>Dave Humpherys</b>: Playing at the top competitive level is a lot of fun, although I don't play much for &quot;fun&quot; in a casual sense.<br><b>Brian Kibler</b>: Absolutely. I'm not playing <b>Magic</b> for a living because it's a great way to make money - I'm doing it because it's something challenging and enjoyable that also happens to make me some money while I'm at it.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>August 27, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f0/9f/f09f4d2a172231ed02bcfadb60e0264389ebd0a5d2bbaaf40ad6a927154a8f0e.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: What is your favorite creature type?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Gab Tsang</b>: Apes; they are the most fun.<br><b>Ken Krouner</b>: Spellshapers definitely do the coolest things. <table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>August 21, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/24/ed/24ed5be5578a04d4bd33e38ab1834ea7e497fbcee8f490f096ac18a1ccfd9f54.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: What mechanic would you want to see more of in future sets, and why?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Andrew Cuneo</b>: It's not really a mechanic, but I'd like to see more cards like Nevinyrral's Disk and Powder Keg.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: Cycling land is awesome. It helps solve the problems of manascrew and manaflood.<br><b>Nate Heiss</b>: Cycling – it’s awesome!<br><b>Alex Shvartsman</b>: I really love cycling and echo as mechanics for Limited play. And since we just got to play with cycling for a bit, it would be great if they brought back echo in one of the upcoming sets. My least favorite Limited mechanic is probably the whole rebel/mercenary-summon-creatures-straight-out-of-your-deck thing.<br><b>Paul Sottosanti</b>: I liked kicker. It gave cards a lot of flexibility and allowed players to stretch their mana bases without penalizing them too much for not drawing all their colors. Morph does the same thing, but in a less exciting fashion.<br><b>Ed Fear</b>: Dancing. There's no better fun than large gamers doing the &quot;hokey pokey&quot; because after all is said and done, that's what it's all about.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: Spellshaping. It finds alternate uses for otherwise useless cards, making the game less draw-dependant, and by default, more skill-based.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I'd stick to triple <i>Torment</i> for now.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: <i>Beta</i>, <i>Beta</i>, <i>Beta</i>. If I got some Black Lotuses, I could sell them. My Falcons and Armor would own.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: Wow, awesome question. I guess I'd go with a set like <i>Urza's Destiny</i> that had a lot of bomb rares spread out among a relatively small set, most of which have easy casting costs to meet. If you go that direction, you could do worse than opening a Masticore.<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: <i>Odyssey</i>, <i>Beta</i>, and <i>Judgment</i>. I want Wild Mongrel, Ancestral Recall, and Wonder.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>August 1, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/24/ed/24ed5be5578a04d4bd33e38ab1834ea7e497fbcee8f490f096ac18a1ccfd9f54.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: Is it harder to build decks for smaller (Block, Standard) or larger (Extended, Type I) card pools? Which do you find more interesting?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Andrew Cuneo</b>: Although Type I isn't really played much anymore, it's probably the hardest format to build a deck for. Your card choices matter a lot more because each game, you can have access to almost any card in your deck. In Block and Standard, individual choices just aren't as important.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: The more cards, the more difficult (and interesting) it is to find the right deck as long as the format isn't degenerate. Block Party is probably my favorite format because compartmentalization solves the degeneracy while keeping the complexity of the large card pool.<br><b>Gab Tsang</b>: It’s the same old stuff, but I guess smaller sets are easier. It really depends on how hard you want to work and the size and dedication of your playgroup.<br><b>Nate Heiss</b>: It is harder to break open smaller formats, and easier to play rogue decks in larger formats, but building decks is about the same.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: Definitely harder for a larger card pool because there are more options and more opposing strategies you have to factor in.<br><b>Paul Sottosanti</b>: Both present their own challenges, but I definitely enjoy the smaller card pools more. Fewer powerful tier one decks mean that there is more room for innovation and new decks don't have to be absolutely devastating in order to have a chance.<br><b>Alex Shvartsman</b>: It is certainly easier to build decks in a format that features fewer cards. There is much less of a chance to miss a crucial strategy, and you are forced to play with somewhat inferior cards and that makes it a little more like playing Limited - which I prefer anyway. I guess I find the Block Constructed more interesting because it forces you to play with weaker cards. That usually makes for longer and more interesting games - no turn 2-3 kills there.<br><b>Osyp Lebedowicz</b>: It's harder to build decks for Extended because of the massive card pool to work with, but I think Block is more fun and skill intensive, since the difference between a good deck and a bad deck in Block is usually very subtle and only reveals itself through extensive testing.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
