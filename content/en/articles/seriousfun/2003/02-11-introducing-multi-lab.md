![Anthony Alongi, Bureaucrat](https://i.ormos.uk/50/b1/50b1b9a9334dcbb5320fe701ce7020e5eec0c307b28e6de20efc03fdb1bb2e34.jpg)

It should surprise none of you that I am a man devoted to public service.

Yes, public service. What other value would compel me to bring you the latest in kitchen table technology? And to find the latest combos with Jangling Automaton? (HINT: provoke.) And to warm up Jay Mutton-Spiceloaf's audience, 48 hours early? That's right—public service.

In that eternal spirit, let me introduce you to your newest group **Magic** resource—the Multi-Lab.

We'll shoot right to Q&A...

***Q:** What is Multi-Lab?*  
**A:** It's a free, easy service to this column's readers. In short, you write to me at seriousfun@wizards.com with a group-oriented deck, and I make suggestions privately. Every month or so, I'll pull together a few of the most interesting cases and put them (along with my responses) in a Serious Fun column.

***Q:** Why are you doing this?*  
**A:** Like I said, it's my undying devotion to the masses. That, and I get tons of these requests already, so I figured I ought to do something productive rather than just keep them to myself.

***Q:** Will every request get a response in your column?*  
**A:** A response, yes. In the column, not necessarily. The most interesting decks will have the best chance of public treatment. I get to choose what's most interesting.

***Q:** So I just send you my coolest deck, and you fix it, and I get my name in lights?*  
**A:** Slow down, tiny dancer. I'm not exactly sure how much e-traffic I'm about to bring down upon my head. So let's set out some guidelines:

1.  I will still respond to every honest email. By "honest" I mean non-spam, non-virus. Everything else gets a reply. This is what I've always done. People who've contacted me in the past know that it takes me anywhere from two minutes to two months to answer. This, I cannot help.
2.  Emails that want "Multi-Lab treatment" should say so. An easy way is putting "multilab" in the subject header, but don't let me stifle your creativity. (When being creative, please recall that my wife also uses the account that seriousfun@wizards.com points to, and she can delete your email in a heartbeat. She does not suffer fools gladly, yours truly excepted.)
3.  I don't build decks for people. If you ask, I'll still respond—but the response will be no. I don't have the time to build everybody's decks.
4.  To be as helpful to you as I possibly can, I need information. As briefly as your personal style allows, I need to hear from you:
    *   a decklist
    *   how you expect to win
    *   what cards/strategies you struggle against in your group
    *   what cards seem least helpful in your deck, or get left in your dying hand.

If rares are not a problem for you, let me know. Otherwise, I'll hold back on too many expensive choices.

***Q:** Will you help me with my Vintage, Extended, Standard, or Block tournament deck?*  
**A:** My DCI constructed rating is just barely above my IQ. Shop smart, people. Would you go to a hog farm looking for tofu?

***Q:** All right, I'll stay casual. Is there a deadline?*  
**A:** No. This is ongoing, at least for the remainder of 2003. 

***Q:** Will you still do [Break This Card](/en/articles/seriousfun/2002/11-12-breaking-cards-again)?*  
**A:** Yes. (Expect the next one soon.) I'm just adding another regular feature. It's important for our columns to change and try new things.

***Q:** Why didn't you consult with the **Magic** player community before making these changes? DON'T YOU CARE ABOUT WHAT WE THINK?!?!*  
**A:** I can assure you that in time, you will get used to this change, and will continue to find Serious Fun a rewarding and challenging experience.

Here's an example of what Multi-Lab would look like. This email is from Brian Bart, edited slightly for space concerns:

> Anthony,
> 
> I could use some expert advice. I use flanking and burn decks decks with a few auto-kills, and all my friends are using Elf decks that rely on the Wirewood Herald and Wirewood Pride to win, not to mention trying to get as many elves out as possible then kicking my ass. I was just wondering if there were any modifications or advice you could give me to win once again.
> 
> Thanks for your time,

Brian,

Thanks for writing in! I think many casual players build decks in Brian's style—lots of different kinds of cards, pulling in many diverse ideas at once to try out. On one hand, that's great for experimentation, and this column will never say that's a bad thing. On the other hand, that's a bad thing. (Damn. I stink at promises.) In all seriousness, I think group decks have as much reason—if not more—to be focused as tournament decks. It's not about winning, though I believe deck focus will increase your win percentage. It's about choosing a trick for fun, committing to it, and seeing your creativity bear fruit as many times as possible. Just because you're using statistical principles doesn't mean you're not having a good time. (This is what my econometrics professor at Harvard taught me.)

So, focus. What does that mean? Let's take the flanking deck first.

**Is the deck as consistent as it could be?** Lots of 1x and 2x in there! That often happens if the player has a young collection, but I can't tell if that's the case for you. (You're using *Mirage* block cards, which is a signal you may be a mature player and are making a conscious choice here.) I would advise finding those cards that best advance your path to victory, and going to 4x. The flankers seem most important—nothing wrong with 4x Fallen Askari, right? 

Often, I imagine you're facing down a ton of Elves, want nothing more than to draw a Dark Banishing or Pestilence... and you pull a Jabari's Banner. *D'oh!* That's where inconsistency nails you. You have plenty of creatures with flanking in there. What, exactly, does the Banner add?

Good for you for sticking to 60 cards. This will help you get more consistent, more quickly. 100 to 200 (and larger) decks have their place, but I think many players make this tradeoff without really considering the consequences.

**Is your creature count high enough?** You have a deck built on flankers, but you have more non-creatures than creatures! Find creatures that emulate flanking—first strikers, or double strike creatures like Ridgetop Raptor. And like any deck with red, you've at least got to glance at Flametongue Kavu. Ditch the *Invasion* Master; you're not using him to full potential. If you've got your flankers all at 4x, and you add 4-6 more creatures, you'll find your deck attacking more often, which is where flanking shines. But you can't make use of flanking if you're constantly holding stuff back because you don't have enough creatures, right?

Find room by pulling out some of the 1x and 2x in "other spells"—I'd take a hard look at the Banner, War Cadence, Binding Agony, Death's Duet, Furious Assault, and others. A lot of these cards pull you away from your deck's theme (and that's not fun), as well as your path to victory (which isn't efficient). 

If you still have room within 60 cards, ramp up Dark Banishing, and consider more Pestilence. (Though that's tricky in this deck, isn't it? And Death's Duet isn't effective enough to compensate.) 

**So what about those elves you're facing?** Find a Tsabo's Decree or two. If you can't acquire rares easily, consider Commando Raid (since your flankers are likely to go unblocked) and/or lots of cheap instant speed removal that you can use in response to pump spells. 4x Shock, 4x Seal of Doom, 4x a Laccolith creature would all send a message that you're prepared for combat.

Let's move on to the burn deck—a staple of many casual players' arsenals.

**Is the deck consistent enough?** As with the last deck, "1x" is probably what's killing you. You can't get the card you need, when you want it. It's nice to have highlander-style decks that make a point of having only one of each card; but I think unless you're doing an intentional highlander format, you're putting yourself at a disadvantage. Rummage around your local shop's common bin (many have these, selling any common you can find for five or ten cents each) and get up to 4 Seal of Fire, Scorching Lava, etc. Choose the cards that get you to victory the fastest. They don't have to be powerhouses.

**Are you losing to enchantments?** Mono-red crumbles in the face of decent enchantments. What's your plan, there? If you're just willing to take the risk, time is of the essence. You might consider investing in a couple Furnace of Rath. Searing Rays and Mana Cylix just aren't going to get you there.

**How on earth are elves surviving this deck?** Perhaps you have too much player burn (Flame Rift, Sizzle) and not enough creature burn (Pyroclasm)? Also, consider red that doesn't do damage—Flowstone Slide, a *Nemesis* rare, can kill creatures with protection from red.

**What can you remove?** Cards that appear to detract from your deck, and/or don't give you enough punch or consistency: Burning Sands, Lava Axe, Ghostly Flame, Searing Rays, Mana Cylix. I'd also consider dumping any given 1x from the deck, though I understand rares are hard to come by and fun to use. I do that, too.
