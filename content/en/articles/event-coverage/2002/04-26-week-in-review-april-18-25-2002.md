### National Championships

As promised last week, I am going to open this column with results and links to Nationals coverage around the world. If your country's Nationals are this weekend, please be sure to drop me a note at ashv80@hotmail.com so I can include it in next week's article.

*Australia*  
![](http://www.wizards.com/worlds/images/flag_aus.jpg)The country that is soon to be hosting the World Championships was probably the one where players fought the hardest to qualify - as is usually the case. Justin West won the tournament with a Noir variant, defeating Dante Rosati who played a Trenches deck, the same deck played by last year's champion Rob Nadebaum, who finished in fourth place. Daniel Clifton took third with Zevatog. 

[Top 4 deck lists](http://www.mtgparadise.com/cgi-bin/ikonboard/ikonboard.cgi?s=3cc838f964b2ffff;act=ST;f=10;t=7)

*Finland*  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/e2/5f/e25f0ee8b6dc81cae3dfa346854e8d924264f41fe22b7f1a569776b54bd192c7.gif)Tomi Walamies became National Champion for the second year in a row, playing a rogue Goblin Trenches deck that has not been showing up in many other tournaments recently. He defeated a green-black Braids build in the finals, played by David Farkas. The third member of their national team is Tuomas Kotiranta, who navigated a Psychatog variant that splashed red into the Top 4. The Finnish National Team is spearheaded by perhaps the best player to qualify for Team Challenge at Worlds so far, but will his rookie teammates have what it takes to help Walamies post an impressive team finish?

Top 8 featured a crazy b-r-g madness deck (with Static Orb!), b-r-g non-madness deck (without Static Orb), and the champion played a Goblin Trenches variant recently seen at the Australian Nationals.

### Rules Clarification

After reading last week's Play of the Week, a number of you emailed me complaining of an error. "How could Levy's opponent use his Butcher to remove Levy's, since the card says 'target creature other than Faceless Butcher'?" was the question. The fact is, any time a card is worded that way, it is referring to ITSELF rather than any other copy of that card. Thus, Faceless Butcher cannot target itself, but it can certainly target another Butcher that is in play.

### Magic Trivia

Last week's question:

What creature changes its creature type when it's tamed?

Answer from Yoshiya Shindo: Orgg. Orgg is "Creature - Orgg", but Trained Orgg is "Creature - Beast". Maybe it loses its Orgg-ish mind when it's trained.

### Bad Play of the Week

Courtesy of Brett Allen:

"This happened to a friend of mine at the sealed Qualifier tournament during PT-Osaka. First, his opponent cast Innocent Blood when my friend had a Chamber of Manipulation, so my friend stole his opponent's best creature and sacrificed it. Sure, it's a little oversight that could happen to anyone. However, his opponent later repeated the error by casting Chainer's Edict, resulting in another creature being stolen and sacrificed. But the clincher that makes this the Bad Play of the Week is that, some turns later, his opponent FLASHED BACK the Edict, losing yet another of his own creatures to the Chamber!"
