It wasn't long ago that I first heard about the idea for **Magic** Colony. Eight players living together with one goal in mind: breaking the format for Pro Tour-Osaka. I was intrigued, and when I was asked to participate in the first grand experiment, I was upset that my school commitments prevented me from completely uprooting myself for a month to live in some obscure town in Washington and sling cards all day. Since all of the players who ended up being a part of the Colony were my friends, I was involved with the project despite my absence from the house itself, exchanging ideas and playtesting online with the likes of Terry Tsang and Brian Hegstad, and all the while hearing the Colony gossip - who wasn't putting in their share of time testing, who was angry with who, etc.

The first **Magic** Colony was a mixture of success story and horrible failure. On one hand, Ken Ho took the u-g Standstill deck designed in the earliest days of Colony testing and piloted it to the Pro Tour championship. On the other hand, however, with the exception of Ken's performance, the Colony's results left much to be desired. Members were split on what deck to play, and most ended up playing the u-r-b Madness deck designed largely by William "Baby Huey" Jensen, with the lone money finish of this contingent being Ben Rubin - who hadn't actually lived at the Colony house - in 21st place. This disagreement on what to play was symptomatic of more than just the usual team squabbling and personal preferences, but rather revealed a schism that had grown between the members of the Colony in testing, which ultimately led to the frustrated Canadian contingent leaving the Wenatchee house early and doing much of their final testing in their home country - and taking Ken Ho with them. This group produced the only money finishers who'd been a part of the colony itself, with Jeff Cunningham finishing 41st to go along with Ken's victory.

Despite all the strife, however, the Colony did what it intended - win the Pro Tour. It's hard to argue with a result like that, even if the rest of the Colony members failed to post similarly impressive numbers. The experiment had to be considered a success, as it had accomplished what every team testing for the Pro Tour sets out to do: win. In the wake of Osaka, rumors abounded about the possibilities for a second Colony - where it would be, who would be included, etc. I was excited by the prospect, as word was that Colony II would take place over the summer to prepare for Worlds, which would mean that even those of us with school commitments would be able to take part in person. 

The popular rumor for quite some time was that Colony II would be held at Bob Maher's parents' place in Las Vegas, a veritable gamer's paradise. Visions of days filled with drafting and playtesting by the pool followed by nights of blackjack at the Bellagio danced in my head for months, but alas, they were too good to be true, as Bob informed me around the time of U.S. Nationals that Vegas was a no-go. It was back to the proverbial drawing board.

It was then that Eric Froehlich suggested what has apparently become known as "Mini-Colony". I'd tested with EFro for GP-Milwaukee and U.S. Nationals alike, as my usual cohort in such matters - Ben Rubin - wasn't planning on attending either due to school conflicts (which he managed to get out of for Nationals at the last minute), and we'd worked well together and produced impressive results. EFro invited Ben and me to stay at his place right outside Washington D.C. in order to test for Worlds, and with the possibility of a more structured Colony looking bleaker and bleaker, we were quick to accept. We looked for a possible fourth member to make our little group an efficient playtesting machine, and it wasn't long before Brian Hegstad signed on for the job. His commitment to the project has wavered several times since, as he's been unsure of his ability to attend Worlds, but as of this writing he'll be returning with the three of us from Origins to get our testing started in earnest.

Once we had our stateside roster set, we began networking overseas. EFro had worked with Godzilla for PT-Osaka, and regularly talks with Kai and company on IRC. When Godzilla broke up recently, Kai asked Eric who he was planning on testing with for Worlds, and when he learned about the Mini-Colony plans, expressed interest in working with us. After a bit of discussion, the decision was made. Mini-Colony would have two focal points - EFro's home in Springfield, VA, hosting myself, Ben Rubin, and Brian Hegstad, and Christoph Lippert's home in Cologne, Germany, serving as the gathering point for Budde, Patrick Mello, Felix Schneiders and Austrians Ben Klauser and Helmut Summersberger.

Our testing didn't really begin until after Grand Prix-New Jersey, but once that tournament was over, we wasted no time. I babbled on and on about decks for both formats pretty much the entire duration of the car ride from Secaucus to Springfield, or at least that small fraction of it that I was awake. Upon arrival in Virginia, we hastily removed our cards from our backpacks and threw decks together - none would be completed, however, thanks to the sleepless nights accompanying Grand Prix Sundays and the nigh-irresistible allure of **Magic** Online.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e8/24/e82456fffbf885f5471aa4d04837f3598c369a253b3098fa9c0fb29319d99f4e.jpg)

Since recovering from our sleep deprivation, we've managed to put together the beginnings of our OBC gauntlet and sketch out a few decks we plan on adding, as well as get close to a hundred or so playtest games under our belts. And that's just with the three of us in about a day and a half. It's really rather amazing how much **Magic** one can play when living with people for pretty much the express purpose of playing **Magic**. At this rate, we'll have played more games than most small countries by the time Worlds comes around, and with our partners in crime on the other side of the Atlantic doing much the same, our decks should be quite busty. We're off to Origins this weekend for some casual gaming before we truly buckle down and get some real testing down. 

If you curious about anything involving the "Mini-Colony," let us know by posting on the message board. This includes anything from testing strategies or approaches to drafting. Clearly we can't reveal our top decklists for Worlds, as that would undermine the entire purpose of our project, but we can talk about the formats in a more general sense to give you a sense of the metagames you can expect to see in Sydney, or just give you a sneak peek into just what it's like to live the life of a professional gamer. Let us know what you want, and we'll give it to you. Until next time...
