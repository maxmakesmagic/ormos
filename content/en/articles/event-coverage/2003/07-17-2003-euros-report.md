Euros never was a good tourney for me. I usually ended up making money, but nothing serious. This year didn't look to be much different. I have exams in a few weeks and didn't have that much time to test. As Euros isn't much more important then a GP to me, I probably wouldn't put in more time anyway, but that is another story. About a week before the tourney, *Scourge* finally hit ***Magic** Online*. I played a bit in the casual play room and talked to John Larkin a lot. He randomly played against Stan van der Velden and both were trying a version of Tight Sight, a strange blue-green combo deck that wins with Future Sight/Krosan Reclamation/Early Harvest recursion to generate infinite mana. Stan gave John his version and he passed it on to Dirk Baberowski, Marco Blume and me. We didn't want to play one of the big netdecks and a new combo deck seemed just perfect for a tourney we weren't taking all that seriously anyway. When we started to play with the deck, we realized that the new card Scourge introduced to the deck was the card that made the deck good: Mind's Desire. John had only two of those but we quickly increased the number to four. After all, the idea is to cast it and hit the next one.

The Desire turned the deck from a really slow design that had to take total control of the game into a combo-deck that was capable of winning in one single turn early in the game. We had several turn 4 kills when we played at Dirk's place and turn 5-7 seemed to be average. Well, talking about the deck any more without showing you a list seems pointless, so here we go. ### How does the deck win?

The cooler win condition is probably Hunting Pack but in reality the Brain Freeze in the sideboard ends 99% of the games you win. I'll go through a sample game to show you how the deck works in theory:

**Turn 1**: Island, Sleight of Hand  
**Turn 2**: Forest, Rampant Growth  
**Turn 3**: Land, Deep Analysis or Concentrate  
**Turn 4**: Land, Rampant Growth, use Moment's Peace on your opponent's turn  
**Turn 5**: Land, Deep Analysis or Concentrate, Flashback Moment's Peace on your opponent's turn  
**Turn 6**: Land, Wild Growth, Flashback Analysis, Early Harvest, Cunning Wish for Early Harvest, Early Harvest, Mind's Desire  
 At this point you get to reveal six cards with the Desire. The best-case scenario is to just reveal another Mind's Desire. Just cast the other spells you revealed first and then cast the next Desire, which is even bigger now. If you manage to cast the second one for 10 or more, the game should be over. You either hit Early Harvest and some card drawing to get to the next Mind's Desire or just the third Desire right away. If you cast the third, you should be able to reveal your whole library by now with the extra spells from the second Desire. By now, you easily played twenty spells. Use a Cunning Wish to get the Brain Freeze from the sideboard, cast it on your opponent and either pass the turn and win the game when he can't draw a card during his draw step or just use Deep Analysis on him to win on your turn.

Now that you know how the deck is supposed to work, I'll go over the decklist.

    **Wild Growth**
     This is one of the most important cards in the deck. The key is that most of the time you should NOT play it if you have it in your opening hand. The reason for this is that it is basically a free spell. It costs one green mana and if you enchant an untapped land, it gives you a green mana. That makes it a perfect play in the very turn that you want to cast Mind's Desire, as it will give you an extra copy of the spell. You do want to play it on your first turn if you are mana shy or if you need it to accelerate into a turn three Concentrate or Deep Analysis.
*   **Rampant Growth**
     The deck needs quite a few lands in play and it is pretty good to cast the card drawing spells one turn earlier. Rampant Growth helps you with that and strips lands from your library. I've seen a few versions with Far Wanderings but if you want to get to threshold fast enough, you need to run Quiet Speculation, Mental Note and Careful Study and all of those weren't good enough in our testing. If you got the Mental Note, Quiet Speculation, Far Wanderings draw, things were perfect, but in the real world, it just doesn't work like that. If you get threshold by turn three, you don't draw the Wanderings and if you have the Wanderings, you obviously don't have threshold.

Stifle, on the other hand, can deal with the whole storm-effect, making it a devastating card against this deck.

    **Deep Analysis, Concentrate, Future Sight**

Now you know how the deck works and what a perfect game looks like. And this is where the problem comes up: While it is quite consistent on turn 6 or 7, that just isn't fast enough. We expected a lot of green-red hate at Euros, but in fact everyone just realized that green-red is the best deck right now and went with that. I'd think that about 40% of the players used Karplusan Forests and Wooded Foothills. Green-red mirrors were the norm, several players packed Forgotten Ancient or Phantom Nantuko to have an edge. The good news is that green-red is the worst matchup for the one deck that Mind's Desire deck doesn't want to face: zombies. Still, even the huge amount of Composts and Phantom Centaurs didn't mean that people discarded the black creature deck. There were still a lot more copies of that deck then there should have been after U.S. Nationals.

Our problem was that our version wasn't really fast enough to beat green-red consistently and Flaring Pain after boarding doesn't help either. Olivier Ruel and a few other French players ran a different version without the four mana card drawers and with Careful Study and Peek to make sure that they drew the key cards as fast as possible and to boost the first Mind's Desire even more. There were still quite a few Wake decks, Cycling decks and monoblack control around, but we didn't really get to play against enough of those to give us a good overall winning percentage.

My personal record with the Desire deck was a stellar 2-5 in the following matchups:  
**Round 1**: Pavo Sissala, monoblack control, 2-0  
**Round 2**: Norberto Giusti, Zombies, 2-0  
**Round 3**: Giuliano Moretto, green-red, 0-2  
**Round 11**: Farid Meraghni, Goblins, 1-2  
**Round 12**: Jaroslav Sita, green-red, 0-2  
**Round 13**: Eugenius Idzikovsky, Zombies, 1-2  
**Round 14**: Julien Silverstone, green-red, 0-2

Those weren't really the matchups I wanted to see, but it was a good mirror of the field – not much control and mostly fast creature decks.

Pavo in round 1 actually got to double Duress me both games but it didn't help as he couldn't put up any pressure and didn't draw a tutor to find his Haunting Echoes. I got quite lucky against Norberto as I managed to draw into Future Sight both games after an avalanche of discard ripped my opening hands apart. Giuliano just played some creatures while I stalled on lands both games and I didn't cast too many spells in that round.
