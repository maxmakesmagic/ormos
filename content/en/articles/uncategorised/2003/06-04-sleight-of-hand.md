### Avoiding Pit Traps And Looters

* * *

Numerous threads have graced the [***Magic** Online* boards](https://web.archive.org/web/20030604000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://boards.wizards.com/tcg-bin/ultimatebb.cgi?category=14&origin=magic_magiconline_articleak060403) discussing scams, hacks, and a variety of other unscrupulous tactics an immoral few might attempt to part you from your collection. Clearly the topic deserves some attention, so this article focuses on educating players how best to protect themselves from those who use these tactics. (My thanks to all the board posters who have brought to light the various tactics unleashed on the unwary.)

### Infernal Contract

ScamminJoe offers you six boosters for your Birds of Paradise. The best you've managed in all you're wheeling and dealing is about fourteen tickets in trade, so this deal beats them all hands down. One problem though, Joe tells you he needs your Birds as part of another deal he's working, and he'll give you the packs as soon as he closes that deal.

So, you make the deal, only to find out that Joe doesn't contact you in a few minutes to finish up, and worse, he's logged off to boot!

This scenario is easily avoided. Unless you completely trust the individual you are making a deal with, avoid trades that don't neatly fit into the trade windows.

### Illusionary Presence

Shadydealer offers two Wrath of Gods and thirty commons of your choice for twenty tickets. What a deal! You complete the trade, only to find out all you've gotten for your tickets are the commons. You add Shady to your buddy list, track him down and tell him you didn't get the rares. The last thing you remember him doing before he blocks you is laughing in the chat box.

Most of the time, the swindler trying to pull this one off will tell you just how much of a hurry he's in, and urge you to speed up the transaction, as he has to get off the computer super quick.

All you need to do to too sidestep this scam is just take a moment to review the trade window. Is everything you want there? If not, don't complete the trade until it is. Remember, you have two chances to confirm a trade. Once you confirm it the second time, however, you've put your seal of approval on the deal.

### Treasure Trove

Quickchange is offering one hundred tickets for only ninety dollars. That's a healthy ten percent discount off the full retail price you'd pay through the [***Magic** Online* Store](https://web.archive.org/web/20030604000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://magiconlinestore.wizards.com&origin=magic_magiconline_articleak060403). In fact, it's almost enough for an extra draft pack!

A couple of easy clicks later, you're money is wired to his Paypal account and you leisurely await the trade request from Quickchange. Problem is, you never get the trade request. Surely it's a misunderstanding, but for some reason, you find that Quickchange has blocked you, and you can't initiate chat with him any longer.

Steaming mad and not sure what to do, you ask the Adepts to come to your rescue, only to find out they are unable to intervene when outside websites are involved.

This is why we advise players to keep trades inside the ***Magic** Online* game. Certainly, there are reputable sites and folks to deal with outside the friendly confines of the game, but if you are going to make deals offsite, understand you're on your own. Do research and know whom you are dealing with. If the deal looks too good to be true, it probably is.

### Merchant of Secrets

A friendly ear whispers to you that Magikhookyouup.com has a cheat code that allows you to gain access to every card in the game and lets you build and use decks even though you don't even have the cards. Well, heck, who wouldn't want this cheat?

The site is polished and professional and all they need to get you set up is your email address, ***Magic** Online* account name, and password. Once you've typed it in, you're golden.

How amazing is that? All the cards at your fingertips and you didn't have to spend a dime. A day later you log onto your account only to find you're missing most of the rares you've worked so hard to accumulate. You think to yourself, "Must be a glitch or technical problem with the game."

No glitch, you've simply given away access to your account by providing somebody else with your password. Someone logged onto your account and traded off the rares to another account.

Keep your password to yourself. Do not share it with others and don't leave it in plain site near the computer you use. There is no reason that anybody from Wizards will ever ask you for your password.

There are some great non-Wizards sites for deck information, banter, rulings, reports and the like. Feel free to visit them all, just remember there is no such thing as a "cheat" for ***Magic** Online.*

### Stoic Champion

"Look, I really need this win, another five rating points and I break the 1800 mark. I'll tell you what, concede and I'll give you the eight packs I win. I don't care about the boosters, I just want to hit 1800."

Nice, eight packs plus the four you'll get for finishing second, just for throwing a game. You click concede and smile as the four packs pop up into your window. You open up a trade window with Joeprodude, but he closes it and ignores your query when you ask him what's up.

What's up is that Joe has just tricked you into a loss. The 8-4 booster draft queues are set up to allow for a prize split of six packs each, but there is no official system way to ensure any other bargaining for boosters. Furthermore, deals of that type would be considered a bribe as well and thus against both DCI and ***Magic** Online* rules.

### Careful Study

In virtually every online gaming community there will always be a small percentage of folks who seek to gain advantage over others--whether through cheating, scamming, or providing misinformation. Your best defense against this type of person is to simply stay informed and use common sense when dealing with folks.

Never rush into any type of deal. If you have second thoughts about the person you're dealing with, simply don't deal with him or her. There are thousands of other folks online that will deal honestly with you.

While the Adepts can't tell you the going rate for the hot chase rare of the day, they certainly are available to answer questions regarding the trade system, the [***Magic** Online* Store](https://web.archive.org/web/20030604000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://magiconlinestore.wizards.com&origin=magic_magiconline_articleak060403), or give advice to you if you think something shady is going on.

May all your hacks be magical and all your trade routes clear.
