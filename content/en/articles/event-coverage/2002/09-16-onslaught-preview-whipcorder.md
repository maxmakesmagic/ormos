If you weren't playing two years ago, you probably don't know just how good rebels were. Dwarves, goblins, elves, merfolk... there are creature types that go far deeper than these Luke Skywalker wannabes, but none has dominated in both Constructed and Limited the way these obnoxious white dorks did.

In Limited, they were a joke. You drafted white if you wanted to go 3-0 because white was the rebel color, and if you lucked into the good rebel engine, you just couldn't lose very easily. If you missed out on the rebel train, your deck invariably sucked, because without Ramosian Lieutenant or Defiant Falcon, white just couldn't stack up to its adversaries. That's how important they were.

As good as they were in Limited though, that couldn't hold a candle to the effectiveness of the rebel chain in Block Constructed. 2000 Pro Tour-NY was a ridiculous affair because of these things. While Waters won at the end of the day, rebels constituted 57% of the field, and playing forests or mountains was so futile an effort that more Rishadan Ports were played than either basic land type. That's because red and green just had no hope of beating the rebel deck unless they compromised every other match up.

In Standard, the rebel decks showed up in two forms: the classic monowhite model and a blue-white variant sporting Unnatural Selection for the mirror and Meddling Mage plus countermagic for everything else. At the last Standard Pro Tour, 2000 Chicago, despite everyone admittedly feeling that Fires was the number one deck in the format, the two rebel decks, in the hands of Kai Budde and Kamiel Cornelissen, met up in the Finals, with the classic taking the honors. 

In Extended, it was 2001 World Championships, when Ramosian Sergeant and Steadfast Guard made their presence known in the white weenie deck played by semifinalist Antoine Ruel among a host of others. After that tournament, the obvious power of Trix started redefining the environment and the rebels disappeared off the map. It's about time for a re-emergence

Simply put, of all the rebels ever printed without search abilities, none has come close to matching up with Whipcorder. Steadfast Guard has always received the nod over Fresh Volunteers because... well... they're the better creature, but really, does not tapping to attack even compare with the same-sized Whipcorder's tap ability? Unlikely. For years, tappers have been taken first in draft, and some have even made it into Constructed decks, but as with its context in the rebel hierarchy, Whipcorder outshines them all, attacking as an efficient weenie against creatureless decks, acting as removal of crucial blockers and a stopgap for the otherwise unstoppable fatty, and offering itself free of charge whenever friends like Ramosian Sergeant and Lin Sivvi make themselves known. That's a lot of good for a two mana package that comes with the added bonus of morph that can protect this little guy's identity until you pay the ridiculously low one mana at end of turn, tap your surprised opponent's best blocker and charge in for the win.

Whipcorder is going to see more than just a smattering of play over the next two years. In Limited, it's an amazing card, a first pick more often than not, even in bomb-laden *Onslaught*. '![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif), ![Tap](https://i.ormos.uk/bb/31/bb31ab0400c6bc497a5dfa0b4f8a1ccfb035016915dd04c1d5289fbc423245e5.gif): tap target bomb. You paid seven mana for that? What were you thinking? I paid two for this, attacked twice before you even had a blocker and it's still kicking butt on turn twenty.' 

In case you're wondering, no, there aren't any new rebel searchers that make this card even more ridiculous in forty card formats, and that will keep Whipcorder's first pick viability over the monster cards we all want to open in check, but there will be times when you'll open the 2/2 and a six casting cost rare that makes everything go boom, bam and boff and think 'I have no two drops' and see Whipcorder making its way to your pile.

Whether Whipcorder will see play in Block Constructed or Standard is really up in the air (Of course it will see play, but the real question is how much), dependant on the strength of white weenie decks. In block, its being a rebel could actually hurt the 2/2 when every other creature in the deck will likely share a creature type and rely on that kind of thing, and it's being a white card will hurt in a Standard field full of Wild Mongrels, Upheavals, Roars of the Wurm and Mutilates. Extended though, that's a different story.

Most of Ruel's Worlds deck is still intact, while most of the other decks in that environment have fallen by the wayside in light in the recent announcement about the Extended cycle, and that means white weenie has gotten stronger. If you're the kind of player who likes to attack with little white guys, I recommend heading to your local T.O., getting a stack of deck sheets and writing '4 Whipcorder' on each one. Its induction into the deck will be about that simple. If I'm right, we could be looking at another rebel winter. If not, you'll just have to content yourself with drafting it and winning because of it. Neither option seems all that bad to me.
