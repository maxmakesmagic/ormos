It's safe to say that *Theros* has shaken the Standard metagame. Thanks to the powerful devotion mechanic, the field has changed a bit more than was predicted by most of the pros. However, those same pros are the ones bringing the big, splashy new decks. It almost seems like a bait-and-switch by the community. Each team thought *they* were the team that had come up with the best new deck and tried to convince others into thinking there would be no changes.

Esper and Selesnya Aggro decks of varying stripes still rank atop the actual breakdown, above newcomers like Gruul Midrange (most sporting big new creatures featuring the monstrosity mechanic), and Mono-Blue and Mono-Red Devotion. But those decks are gaining fast.

The big three strategies are Control, Midrange, and Devotion. Other Aggro decks are still around, and Selesnya Aggro (featuring [Soldier of the Pantheon](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soldier+of+the+Pantheon) among other aggressively costed creatures) and Naya Midrange (basically Naya decks without [Soldier of the Pantheon](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soldier+of+the+Pantheon)) are still top contenders. But devotion-based aggressive strategies with either [Fanatic of Mogis](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fanatic+of+Mogis) or [Master of Waves](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Master+of+Waves) are the top choices for the field.

Within each broader archetype and strategy, there is plenty of variation within. Esper may play a range of creatures, like [Blood Baron of Vizkopa](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Baron+of+Vizkopa) and [Prognostic Sphinx](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Prognostic+Sphinx), or a lone [Ætherling](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86therling), or completely creatureless featuring up to twelve Planeswalkers. The addition of [Ashiok, Nightmare Weaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashiok%2C+Nightmare+Weaver) has proven to be an even more consistent way to win than the deck had before, and each color combination has representation and variety as well.

  
 Every color but white has some devotion decks represented and often with different varieties. Some even use splashes to cast large basically off-color spells – harnessing [Nykthos, Shrine to Nyx](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nykthos%2C+Shrine+to+Nyx) to provide large amounts of colorless mana needed to cast the splashy spells. Mono-Green uses [Karametra's Acolyte](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Karametra%27s+Acolyte) to power out an obscene [Mistcutter Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistcutter+Hydra) and [Polukranos, World Eater](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Polukranos%2C+World+Eater), while Mono-Black does exactly what you think it would with tons of black mana symbols: make [Gray Merchant of Asphodel](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gray+Merchant+of+Asphodel) and [Abhorrent Overlord](https://web.archive.org/web/20141210000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abhorrent+Overlord), again, and again, and again.

So far it seems there's no consensus on where the metagame truly is, but we'll have more convincing evidence tomorrow, when we see which decks actually made it through to the next day.
