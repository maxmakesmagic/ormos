This week, we’re taking a little time off from the normal run of things to talk about the column in general. I receive many emails each week – and it is apparent that I need to lay out exactly what my criteria for All-Time MVPs are. 

So, what goes into making an All-Time MVP? What qualifications must the card have? 

### What’s an All-Time MVP?

If it had to be put into a single sentence, the best explanation for what makes an All-Time MVP would probably be this: An All-Time MVP is a card that has had a significant, historical impact on tournament-level **Magic: the Gathering**. Now, that is a very general statement. Lots of questions would still arise. How do you weigh the formats? How do you judge historical impact? What do you consider significant? 

Let’s go one at a time. 

*How do you weigh the formats?*

It’s difficult to find cards that have had an equally dominating presence in every format. Necropotence and Tolarian Academy spring to mind as cards that would fit that definition, with both having been dominant decks in all Constructed formats (though Necropotence struggled in the very rough Block Constructed of its day). These sorts of cards are easy fits into the All-Time MVP pantheon. But most cards that will become All-Time MVPs do not fit this bill. Which formats does a card need to perform in to be named an All-Time MVP? Well, performing well in Type I (Vintage) is a pretty good indicator that a card will be named an All-Time MVP. Of all the Constructed formats, Type I receives the least focus for two reasons. First, there are very few serious Type I tournaments, as compared to the other formats. Second, for cards printed after Legends, most anything that would make the Type I list would certainly make the Extended, Block or Standard (Type II) lists. Special consideration will be given to cards like Mana Drain, the Moxes and other pre-Dark cards that never got a crack at the more modern formats, but most of the other cards in Type I will earn their MVP status through higher-level exploits.

For the most part, MVPs will be drawn from Constructed formats, rather than Limited. There are two main reasons for this. First, it doesn’t make a good story. Very few people want to sit down and read a whole article on *Mirage*’s Stalking Tiger, though it would clearly be a Limited All-Time MVP, as one of the cards that taught us that 3/3s for four mana are good. Second is that cards that are only good in Limited have a much shorter shelf-life than Constructed cards. Where the most successful Constructed cards see play in Block for a year, Standard for two years, Extended for several years and Type I forever, good Limited cards are there and back again in a single year. Even the best Limited cards rotate quickly and are only strong in a single metagame (or up to three very short ones, if you consider each additional set in a block). As such, players have many fewer fond memories about Limited cards than they do about Constructed cards. 

Cards that do well in Block Constructed also receive less weight if that’s the only format that they were good in. This is because these cards essentially have the same shelf life as Limited cards. However, if a card here can make an impact and then move on to bigger, better things in Standard or Extended, the Block Constructed experience will definitely factor in.

Standard is a huge proving ground for cards to earn All-Time MVP status. Nestled right between Block Constructed and Extended in terms of card pools and power levels, success in Standard is generally a good sign of things to come. Many environment-defining cards in Standard go on to perform well in Extended and Vintage, even while carving a place for themselves out in Standard. Cards that are introduced in Standard and go their whole two years being played in high-level decks are definite candidates for All-Time MVPs. Additionally, cards that come into the metagame late, often from being printed in the second or third expansion of a block or by becoming relevant because of the next block are considered, but they need to be stronger, since they spend less time in the limelight. However, with the exception of cards in the core set (and possibly reprints in the expansions), no Standard legal card will be chosen as an All-Time MVPs. Think of this as its early career. When a card is Standard legal, it is generally in its heyday – and it’s hard to judge how powerful the card will be in context at this time.

In Extended, cards have their biggest chance to make a splash, as they are there for awhile, swimming with some serious sharks. If a card can be successful in Extended, it has a great chance to become an All-Time MVP. They are also around for a long time, giving them a chance to star in several different metagames. Cards that dominate Extended are often the most powerful and most influential cards in the game. 

*How do you judge historical impact?*

Historical impact works on a number of factors. A card can qualify on historical impact several different ways. First, there are superior cards. Many of these cards did not see tons of play outside of Type I, as they were so dominant, they were quickly banned out of other formats. Ancestral Recall never saw a game in Standard or Extended, but it’s still an All-Time MVP. It would simply dominate any format it was placed in.

Other cards do not have the broad, sweeping historical impact of cards like Black Lotus and Ancestral, but they are still MVP-worthy. Some cards punched through in a couple of metagames, years ago, and have even been overshadowed by better cards recently. A great example of a card like this is Erhnam Djinn. While Erhnam Djinn is very, very outclassed these days (over a half dozen superior creatures spring to mind), it wasn’t always that way. No, it wasn’t that way at all. In 1995-1996 Standard, Erhnam Djinn was the most-feared green creature in the game. It found its way into red-green beatdown decks, as well as green-white Erhnam-Geddon strategies. Despite the fact that Erhnam Djinn wouldn’t make a deck in ANY format these days, that doesn’t mean it isn’t an All-Time MVP. The fact that it was a powerhouse years ago more than makes up for its relative obscurity these days. 

*What qualifications must a card have?*

There are no hard and fast rules as to what a card has to be. **Magic** is as much of an art as a science – and this is one of those art areas. Some cards, like Tolarian Academy, are scientific choices. The Academy dominated every format, requiring restrictions and bannings. There is no way in any universe that the Academy is not an All-Time MVP. 

Think of it like the baseball Hall of Fame. Any player who breaks huge records is going to be included, obviously. But most of the inductees are not Hank Aarons or Babe Ruths. Most of them are great players who are recognized for being great, not for breaking records. Most cards are not Academy. Cards like Wild Mongrel, which will almost assuredly be a future All-Time MVP, did not demolish other decks. Rather than being a brutally obvious choice, the Mongrel is a subtle enabler of several decks. It is a team player, but a great team player.

Necropotence: A huge card. Necropotence dominated in every format – having to be restricted in Type I. In Extended, Necro-based combo decks fueled the format for over a year, ruling metagame after metagame. There’s no way that Necro isn’t a huge All-Time MVP.

Stormbind: While Stormbind is a powerful card, and it did make a couple of splashes, it would be hard-pressed to make All-Time MVP. While it did well in metagames populated with white weenies and Necro-based black weenies, it never asserted itself as a powerhouse. 

Hammer of Bogardan: The Hammer has never been a format-dominating powerhouse, but it certainly has had its fair share of successes in Standard. Hammer is one of those cards that always looks to break out – and it briefly did in a blue-red environment with Shard Phoenix and Forbid. Though it lacks the format-defining dominance of a card like Necropotence, the Hammer is a likely candidate for All-Time MVP, as it is a powerful card with a powerful mechanic. 

### What Do You Think?

Which three cards would you like to see battle it out for the right to be an All-Time MVP? Remember that the cards should either be similar in function (like Hymn to Tourach, Duress and Hypnotic Specter) or from a similar time period (like Balance, Icy Manipulator and Erhnam Djinn) or have some other thread to tie them together (Lin Sivvi, Crystalline Sliver, Lord of Atlantis – creatures that “lead” their tribal deck).
