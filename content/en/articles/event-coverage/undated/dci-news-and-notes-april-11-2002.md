Hi folks,

I've agreed to do this feature a couple times a month for Sideboard Online. I'll update people what's going on with the DCI, some reasoning behind our decisions or policy changes and a little about the DCI tournament scene.

### Regionals

![](http://www.wizards.com/regionals/images/regionals.gif)The big thing on everyone's mind here at the DCI is Regionals. US and Canadian Regionals take place this Saturday. Many other countries have already had their Regionals, but a few still haven't run theirs. Regionals is the day of the year where the highest number of people play in a Standard tournament on the same day. This phenomenon is similar to Prereleases where the highest number of people participate in limited tournaments on the three Prerelease weekends each year. Regionals are definitely more competitive, of course, since it's one of the best ways to earn an invitation to the National Championships. If you haven't played in Regionals before, I strongly recommend attending. It's a cool feeling to play in a large tournament with a bunch of folks competing for the prize. Even if you don't think you'll win, it's still a fun way to spend a Saturday. Info about Regionals can be found [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20041113070143/http://www.wizards.com/worlds/main.asp?x=Regionals).

### Banning the Cheaters

You've probably noticed that we've handed out [quite a few suspensions](https://web.archive.org/web/20041113070143/http://www.wizards.com/dci/judge/main.asp?x=judge/resources/suspended) lately. There are a few reasons for this, in my humble opinion. 

The first reason is that we have much better algorithms to catch people who fake tournament reports. It's fairly easy to see patterns in fake reports and we monitor the situation very closely. Thankfully, most of the people who fake tournaments don't do a very good job, so they are easy to spot. 

We've also had a decent number of people come forward to report tournament fraud that they know about. Some people are taking advantage of the "whistleblower" clause, others feel the weight of their conscience and others just crack under the pressure when they are questioned.

It's pretty satisfying to remove fake tournaments from the DCI database. When folks decide to fake tournament results, they are essentially stealing from the honest players out there by taking up invitations slots to events or getting byes in tournaments that they didn't earn legitimately. 

We've also caught a fair number of people adding cards to sealed deck lately. They seem to like the idea of "bring your own sealed deck". Unfortunately, most of the other people in the tournament have to stick with the cards they open, so you can see the imbalance that creates. The DCI has a pretty tough stance on bringing your own sealed deck. The suspension length can go up to 4 years, depending on the exact situation and severity of the infraction. Adding cards to your sealed deck is also a way that people "steal" from others in the tournament. If you add cards to your qualifier deck, you are trying to steal a qualifier slot from all the other players in the tournament who are following the rules.

We had a player assault a judge recently. Even though the player only pushed the judge and yelled at him, we have a zero tolerance policy toward that type of behavior. The player we caught recently received a ten-year suspension. His **Magic** tournament career is definitely finished. Keep in mind that judging tournaments is a pretty thankless job - we don't want to also make it a dangerous job. Give your local judge a quick "thanks" next time you see them.

### That Time of Year

We're in the middle of the process of writing next year's DCI rules and regulations. We recently had an excellent meeting here in Seattle where we invited a few people from around the world to discuss changes to next year's rules. Some of the hot topics include the note taking rule, the three-judge system, time limits and so on.

One of the topics that we discussed for a long time was whether a player was responsible for telling their opponent what the current power and toughness of their creatures is. Should a player have to calculate the power and toughness of their creatures whenever the opponent asks? We also talked about whether a player could tell their opponent that they were holding a Counterspell, even if they were not. Top-level judges vehemently disagree on this issue and have different opinions on the subject.

I hope you found this interesting. Feel free to email me if you have any comments. I won't be able to respond to each email, but I will certainly read them all.
