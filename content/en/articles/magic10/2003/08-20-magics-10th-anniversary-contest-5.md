### Top 5 - Best Trip You've Taken To Play Magic

**1st prize**  
*Don Lippincott, Massachusetts, US*

It was as if I had been brought from the depths of a fever dream and been rescued by the cool, icy fingers of a Serra Angel when I arrived at Origins '95. It was my present for turning eleven. My Dad and I came from Boston in his car, whose engine would die unless we kept the heat on full. So with the ninety-degree July heat outside coming in the open windows, and the heaters inside the car blasting, I slept through the exhaust fumes and humidity, arriving in Philadelphia sweaty and nauseous.

Those last few steps along the Philadelphia sidewalks were the culmination of all my silly childhood fantasies. I had a red and black deck. I was playing Sengir Vampire's and Shivan Dragon's. I didn't even know what Type 2 was. 

I followed my Dad up what must have been a hundred steps. He opened the door for me and I walked into a cool, air-conditioned lobby. Up a few more stairs, and… I reached an entire room of people selling cards, dealers positioned behind boxes of Fourth Edition and Homelands. I saw singles binders stacked so high they were barely at eye level. There were packs selling below retail. Some single rares for only a dollar.

I was in heaven.

My Dad hurried me along. "We can come back here later. What time does the tournament start?"

In the gaming room, we were greeted by a whole host of CCGers playing and trading on numbered seats. I saw a man skip playing a land turn one and instead, discard a Polar Kraken and two turns later, play Animate Dead on it. I saw new decks, new possibilities--everything. I signed up, got my own DCI card, and learned about Type 2 and what a format was for the first time.

I only had three unplayables--two Juzam Djinn's and an Ali from Cairo. I put them neatly into hard plastic cases, slipped in adequate substitutions (a couple Dread Wight's and a Soul Burn), and geared up for my first "real" tourney.

The results themselves are a blur. I know I went 1-2 and dropped. But I wasn't disappointed with my record. I was actually very happy with it. I was happy with the chance I had to play Magic in such a large room, with stiff competition. I never expected to be better than anyone else, I just wanted to play Shivan Dragon's and maybe double Dark Ritual out a Sengir Vampire turn one. 

The next day, my Dad took me to the dealer room where I bought about ten different packs, and I traded with a dealer for a General Jarkeld. (Who doesn't love legends?)

We went out for a late lunch before heading back to Boston, and the entire seven hour ride home, I relived the plays, the games, and the trades, oblivious to the heat and the fumes. I had found bliss and a few days after my eleventh birthday.

**2nd prize**  
*Stephen Haley, Texas, US*

My story begins in early February 2002, on a cloudy day outside an Austin, TX hotel. A Pro Tour Qualifier had just finished, and I was calling Christina, my girlfriend, who was waiting to hear how I had done. When she answered the phone, I simply told her,

"Pack your bags, we're going to France!"

It was my first time ever to qualify for the Pro Tour, and I had already promised to take her with me. Even though Pro Tour Nice was three months away, we began planning our vacation immediately.

It was my first trip outside the United States, and it was fabulous. Being in France was one of the most amazing experiences I've ever had. Playing Magic:The Gathering at the Pro Tour level was an experience I'll always remember, especially playing my first matches against some of the top players in the world. Although there was no Cinderella Story, (3-4 record for day 1), I did win a few matches against some top players, helping make the Magic tournament itself was something to remember.

The real story is in the vacation that came after the tournament. We stayed a few extra days to give us plenty of time for sightseeing. A few whirlwind days of touring had us seeing a whole host of sights; on Saturday, we took a bus up to the other end of Nice and saw several museums, including the Matisse museum and the Archaeological museum. On Monday, we took a guided tour to Monaco, which included several stops along the way. A tour of a perfume factory, a visit to the perched village of Eze, and then all the sights we could find in Monaco, from the Cathedral near the Palace and the changing of the guard, to the Oceanographic museum and aquarium and the beautiful park just outside. 

Of course, all this could be had in any vacation, really. What really made the difference was the extra effort we put into turning what could have been just a simple weekend of Magic (although in another country) into a romantic getaway. We picked the fanciest hotel we could find, the Negresco, and we spent every meal at a new and exciting restaurant, each more romantic than the last. It was France, it was spring, and we were in love. And so, that first day, after we settled into our hotel room, I asked Christina to marry me. And when she said yes, it made for the trip of a lifetime.

**3rd prize**  
*Silvia Bortott, Turin, Italy*

Travelling by Magic: Europe

When I first approached Magic, I just saw it as an entertaining hobby to spend some afternoons with my friends and not to stay home while my boyfriend went to all those endless tournaments...

My dad is a travel agent and he passed on to me the passion for discovering new places and cultures; but I have never been 'and most likely never will be' more than a casual player, so I thought strolling around the world for Magic tournaments was possible only for Pro players who committed themselves totally to the game (with the bonus of making some money too!). It was a great discovery when my eyes opened on the world of Grand Prix competitions, which happened when I was working as staff in a GP held in my town, Turin, Italy. The fact than any player can enter these events at a reasonable price and be able to compete with more skilled players, thus often learning strategy and gaining experience, made it easier for me to link the two passions: travel and Magic. So, the opportunity to visit another country motivated me even more to go and play in Magic tournaments and in autumn 2001 I was off with some friends to Wien for my first Grand Prix: a 12-hours ride by train and we could play in an historical building, all mirrors and plasters. Eventually, we also had some sightseeing even if we did't stay more than the weekend of the tournament.

Last spring I, my boyfriend and another friend headed north to Prague, and decided to stay there for a whole week. As a matter of fact, there were lots of places to see so it was a great idea to stay longer! We rented an apartment downtown and sometimes cooked ourselves some good old Italian pasta - though it was real fun entering a traditional brewery and ordering some dish at random from a Czech-only menu! GP was also such a nice experience (the biggest in Europe ever with more than 900 competitors) as we could meet people from different countries and exchange opinions on play.

Next travel…who knows? Maybe I'm heading out to Berlin to watch the World Championship and play in some side events, and almost surely I will go to GP Lyon, France next autumn.

In 3 years, I have visited more European cities than I ever did before, and I'm trying more and more to improve my playing skills so that I can demonstrate all around the world I'm no ordinary tourist!

**4th prize**  
*David Johnson, Ohio, US*

At 35 years old, I never thought I could be so excited about a card game. My wife didn't understand why I was buying cards and asking to go to the mall with her. At first she said it was my 'midlife crisis'.

After watching me enjoy myself playing the game online and with friends, she signed up for her own online account. She swore that she wouldn't get 'addicted to the game' the way that I was. Soon we were buying theme decks and spending every night together playing Magic.

We started playing around March of 2003. I had really gotten into the Onslaught block. I talked to my wife about my going to the Scourge Pre-Release tournament in Columbus. She said she thought it was a great idea, and that \*WE\* would have a great time. She arranged for a babysitter and the next thing I knew my wife of 7 years and I were flying down the highway to play cards in Columbus, a 2 and a half hour drive.

We were both very new so we took all of the onslaught cards we had to read on the way down and tried to learn enough to not look silly. We had no idea what to expect. The people at the tournament were fantastic and very hospitable to us.

Unfortunately, our babysitters time was valuable, and we couldn't stay for the whole thing. We left around 2:30. For the first time since we had children, we spent precious time together laughing and talking about the tournament.

That was the best trip I have taken to play Magic, and one of the best times my wife and I have ever had. We love each other very much and spend a lot of time 'playing cards' the way our parents and grandparents did... only this time it isn't pinochle!

Now we play with our nephew and other friends and have found Magic to be a great way for a family to get together.

**5th prize**  
*Mark Hinkley, Ontario, Canada*

The best trip to play Magic I have ever taken is easy, 2001 Worlds in Toronto, Canada. I had taken a day off work to attend this, looking to get into a sealed deck tournament, which I figured I had a better chance at succeeding than any of the other formats. In my "greenness", this choice would prove to be a huge learning experience on so many levels.

People were playing in this Wednesday sealed deck tournament from all over the world, much to my surprise, and most were very helpful in deck design. A group of gentlemen from the Netherlands were eager to help my friend, Michael Agapay, and myself build our decks, giving us a template in which to start. "24 lands, no more than 7 non-creature spells, and strictly stay to the 40 card deck minimum". When we ended up playing these guys, they were helpful to critique our playing style, suggesting when to attack, why to attack, giving us more of the "tournament mentality". We left Toronto after taking serious beatings (one win between the two of us, which I defeated Mike), and decided to come back on Saturday and get dirty all over again.

We took our new education and put it to some use. I started off to a roaring 1-0 start before taking my beatings right down the line. My appreciation for the appeal of the game grew at this point, as I played a woman who was old enough to be my mother, and a couple of young boys who could have been my kids. I always had this impression that the game appealed to the 14-25 male demographic, and here I'm playing people that in no way fit that mold. I was very impressed.

My greatest humbling ever was playing against a kid who was about 9 or 10 years old who had a Gerrard and two Flagbearers out. Meanwhile, at the other side of the table, I was playing r-g-b, with 4 Forests and 1 Mountain out and a handful of Black and 2-Red-cast cards. I didn't think it was possible to be beaten 55-0, but I was. My "friends" were cheering the kid on, and even the one judge got a chuckle out of the situation. I was playing up my severe beating for the kid, who was absolutely ecstatic by his victory. I take it easy on no one. He beat be cleanly and often. The kid earned it. By the end of the day, Mike had earned his first win, I went 2-5, called it a day.

We were merely casual players, looking to soak up the atmosphere, and play some cards. We were amazed at the caliber and class of play and players in general, and how it felt like being a part of something important, rather than being fleeced for $20 in a tournament, like we have experienced in the past. We walked away far better players, both in sealed, and in Magic in general.
