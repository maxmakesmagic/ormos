### Black

Did you ever think it would be like this? I mean, when I heard *Torment* was the black set, I figured there would be a few more black cards than white and that white would have some interesting anti-black cards to balance things out and that would be that. Instead we have this insanity: it's always night in *Odyssey* Block Limited.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/98/7d/987d3f7c2cf266937e8f623811e6ce1dba6c10a698f58a443f12fcbb0a4764fa.jpg)There are twice as many black cards as white or green ones, there are off-black cards that are more efficient when played with swamps, the dual lands require you to have swamps in play, black fills half of the top ten commons list and those interesting anti-black cards... well, they're hardly worth writing about.

Behind the strength of Faceless Butcher, Crippling Fatigue, Cabal Torturer and the other black commons, this has become the powerhouse of the environment, as much as any color has dominated any set in the history of the game. Probably best with blue, black is draftable with any of the other colors, and as a result, this environment has basically come down to deciding if you're black or not.

### The Cards

**Boneshard Slasher**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 1/1 flyers for two mana won't ever excite me, and even though the Slasher's threshold ability is substantial, it also leaves the thing so fragile that it's just not worth playing. You could conceivably sideboard it against any deck where you want your weenies to trade with Mystic Visionaries, Crypt Creepers and Dusk Imps, but even then you should have better stuff to do with your deck. A 10th - 12th pick. 

**Cabal Ritual**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Sorry guys, but one card for one mana will never be playable in Limited. Heck, I never even liked Dark Ritual in Limited play. A 15th pick.

**Cabal Surgeon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card is so slow that grandmothers lap it. At 2/1, the Surgeon's is obviously not the body your deck is hoping for, but there is an upside. In the late game, once your graveyard is full, this thing becomes a real issue for opponents if they don't kill it quickly. I don't want the Surgeon in my deck, but if I'm able to make things go to the late game, there are worse things to draw. An 8th - 10th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0b/3b/0b3b0e2e5420c5b88d51d2bc6a121eeccb4ccf992dd039845cc220eed470c68a.jpg)**Cabal Torturer**  
 If this guy were just a Prodigal Sorcerer, he'd only be really good. That the Torturer's ability gives -1/-1 means that it isn't susceptible to damage prevention while it kills off one-toughness weenies and proves a real issue in creature combat. Think of the threshold ability as a nice little bonus: even without it, the Torturer is one of the best commons in the set. A 2nd - 4th pick. 

**Carrion Rats**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 These have a made a lot of decks they probably shouldn't have because of the temptation involved with a 2/1 for one mana, but the cost required to dominate it in play is so minimal that to cast it at any time other than turn one is a complete waste of space in your deck, and frankly, it doesn't do enough when cast turn one to make a difference. A 10th - 12th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/00/20/0020d35456b789663afdc33077698864c86dec6e891634580fc94bcbc4c07ff9.jpg)**Carrion Wurm**  
 One trick to remember with the Carrion creatures is that their ability can only be activated when triggered upon attacking or blocking. That means that if you attack, they block and then they try to stop them from removing cards from the graveyard, they'll have to put the cards back into the graveyard and the Carrion will still deal its damage. The Wurm is great. A 1st -2nd pick 

**Chainer, Dementia Master**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While Chainer's ability is obviously very powerful, it does come at a pretty high cost. If your deck is going to be incapable of slowing early opposing aggression, by the time Chainer becomes useful, your life will be low enough to nullify the legend's effectiveness. Obviously, a very powerful card, I'm not saying you shouldn't pick it high, just that you should try to make sure your deck is well balanced with early ways to slow your opponent down in order to make Chainer as effective as possible. A 1st - 3rd pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1a/a0/1aa06258532d7f6f71a996189d6adac7143fefba8ede14d9122c0935fb3cb849.jpg)**Chainer's Edict**  
 Any time one spell kills two creatures it's going to be pretty good, and while the Edict doesn't allow you a lot of options, that card advantage put in the right context (namely, when combined with lots of other creature kill) is very good. Obviously seven mana is a lot, but Morbid Hunger's flashback is nine and it still comes into play. Don't take this over Faceless Butcher or Crippling Fatigue, but the contents of your deck (the more creature kill the better) may make this better than Cabal Torturer. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Crippling Fatigue**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Yet another great back common and we're only at the letter "C". The Fatigue's primary positive attributes are speed and strength, providing a powerful effect for little mana, a description that describes most of the best Limited cards in the game. Even when you take too much early damage to use the flashback effectively, the Fatigue is a solid three mana kill spell, and that's the card's downside. It's usually a lot better than that. A 1st -3rd pick. 

**Dawn of the Dead**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) is a lot to pay for any card. For one mana more, you get the Cabal Patriarch, so for this cost, Dawn of the Dead should be better. Yes, the ability to bring back an additional attacker each turn is a good one, but there will only be so many good attackers available, and the Dawn comes with drawbacks to boot. Maybe if you have a lot of interesting coming into play abilities in your deck the Dawn can become a worthwhile endeavor, but it will take some kind of commitment. A 9th - 11th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/75/a2/75a2ff903a651c4c710c88d49ccf69b2395529e0ba2568ef8be590cc21933f9e.jpg)**Faceless Butcher**  
 I think we have to call this the best common in the set. The Butcher has been called the new Nekrataal, but Nekrataal wasn't common. With a black 2/3 body, the Butcher is hard to kill, and while susceptible to bounce-related tricks, the 'drawback' is not enough to devalue this card any. The Butcher deals with black creatures, non-attackers and creatures with a toughness that's greater than the number of cards in your graveyard. In other words, it has none of the shortcomings of other kill spells in this set: Just take this when you're playing black and be thankful you had the opportunity. A 1st - 3rd pick. 

**Gloomdrifter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Without threshold, the Gloomdrifter is a solid, unexciting creature. With threshold, it kills more than half the creatures in the format, while at the same time preserving some of your own. The gap between the two distinctions is quite large, and as such, you'll have to be the judge of your deck's ability to attain threshold. If it will happen quickly, this is one of the best uncommons available. If not, it's an unexciting card I wouldn't pick before fifth or sixth. A 2nd - 4th pick if you can optimalize it. 

**Gravegouger**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I've been surprised to find out how annoying this little guy's ability can be. Removing cards from the graveyard can rid you of problems with both threshold and flashback, if only for a time, and that advantage can be enough to tip things in your favor. I'd never draft this guy high, but when you're looking for low casting cost filler, the Gouger is a solid choice. A 7th - 9th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4d/0a/4d0a4555ef67e34446b6433369206c3a7f19367dea91d26a6df5f77fa964981d.jpg)**Grotesque Hybrid**  
 Grotesque is the word all right. Through out the last couple of years, we've seen a lot of 3/3 black creatures for five, but most of them haven't had this thing's upside. Let's face it, without the discard ability, the Hybrid would still be very playable, but with that addition, it becomes an incredible anti-white-green card that can also rule the skies. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Hypnox**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Well, this big fellow is a lot of fun, but I don't think I'd play a card for ![8 Mana](http://www.wizards.com/images/Symbols/Symbol_8_mana.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) that read "You win the game". Sorry guys, it just takes too long to cast, though if you have multiple Zombifys.... An 11th - 13th pick

**Ichorid**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This one is a tough call. Ichorid is kind of like a bad 3/1 regenerator that can't block, and that certainly isn't the most enthusiastic endorsement in this article, but at the same time, against your more controllish blue-black decks, this thing is a real pest. If you have a lot of black creatures already, you still shouldn't pick it too high as there will only be one or two others at the table that can take any advantage, but this is a good card in a race, so it should find a place in your deck. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Insidious Dreams**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If yours is a bomb-filled deck whose only issue is finding those bombs, this may be the card for you, but the inherent card disadvantage and high cost means this will seldom be playable in your deck. Try to get Diabolic Tutor instead. It's a lot better. A 9th - 11th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/14/91/1491c23aaed447ac3ef8bd0f9c422816437a8c5a7d41ca9defe3b238391669e8.jpg)**Laquatus's Champion**  
 This guy is insane. Not only is the field of play yours as long as it's out, but it also sucks almost a third of your opponent's initial life total off of the score sheet. I've heard it debated that Faceless Butcher may be better, but for my money, there's no creature as good in *Torment*. A 1st pick. 

**Last Laugh**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A powerful but impractical card, it won't make it into most decks, but it may just find itself a niche in some removal-based deck that is most capable of removing permanents. It seems unlikely though. An 11th - 13th pick.

**Mesmeric Fiend**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In a color that's nearly devoid of quality two mana plays, the Fiend is the card you always want in your opening hand. While the body it provides isn't too impressive, the Fiend takes the card around which your opponent is building their early strategy, and with luck, will keep that card for a good, long time. While the 1/1 likely won't interact in combat too often, it will get in a few points of damage here and there when an opponent has no blockers, and can trade at an important juncture with a one toughness attacker. A 4th - 6th pick. 

**Mind Sludge**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If you manage to draft monoblack (I'm not kidding here), then this card will be great, serving as a fifth turn Mind Twist. If, however, you share the misfortune of many by having to delve into a second color, then the Sludge will work best as a card you won't use in the sideboard. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Mortal Combat**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 You won't have 20 creatures in your deck. If you do, don't play for turn 31. A 15th pick.

**Mortiphobia**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As cool as the name and picture are, this card is outshone by Coffin Purge, which is a fourteenth pick. A 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0a/5c/0a5cc4e9f71125d215553e9391a5f93cd42742abac37539378ae53bd6b017736.jpg)**Mutilate**  
 Among the top 5 cards in the set, Mutilate should almost always be drafted ahead of any of the black uncommons or commons in the set. Yes, even the Butcher. Simply, for four mana, you get a card that will kill 98% of the creatures in the format once you have four swamps in play, and once you have the card, you can draft in such a way that you can afford to run ten or eleven swamps. Basically, if your deck is in any way control-oriented or just happens to have a lot of four toughness creatures, there's no better card in Torment for you to draft. A 1st pick.

**Nantuko Shade**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card is obviously nuts. Arguably the best 'bear' in the game's history, the Shade comes down on turn two and dominates on turn twenty. There's not a lot more to say about this card: it's excellent at any time and should probably always be a first pick if your deck is running at least nine swamps. A 1st - 2nd pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4a/b3/4ab339518baab6e2c2c801482eeb7d697cc967ae2700e995ff0b69574024d50f.jpg)**Organ Grinder**  
 Black's creatures generally share the problem of having a low enough toughness that they all become susceptible to cards like Psionic Gift, Chainflinger and Psychotic Haze. The Grinder falls into this problematic category, but at the same time provides an early game threat and a late game finisher. I think you'll find the special ability is seldom worth using unless the end of the game is near, but it's a nice bonus to have on a three-power, three mana creature. A 4th - 6th pick. 

**Psychotic Haze**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 At four mana, the Haze is an expensive spell that really doesn't produce enough power to justify the mana, while the madness cost, which does make it a more enticing option, will still end up costing three if you're discarding the Haze to Fledgling Imp or Dirty Wererat. The real problem though is that with so many one-toughness black creatures in the environment, it is seldom that the Haze will even break even, let alone net you some kind of card advantage. Get one for your sideboard, it could be very good, but I don't think you'll find it benefits you consistently enough to warrant a slot in your main deck. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Putrid Imp**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Generic 1/1 creatures for one just don't cut it in this day and age. The threshold ability is unexciting and hardly worth the wait. Don't bother. A 12th - 14th pick.

**Rancid Earth**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 You really don't want to run this in your main deck in draft, though in sealed deck it's a decent option. Side this in against bad mana, Squirrel Nest, Chamber of Manipulation and Caustic Tar when you need something. It's good to have in your board for those situations. A 9th - 11th 

**Restless Dreams**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It seems like this card can be really good, but in the end I've found it to be too slow. Simply, it takes a long time to get enough creatures in your graveyard to exploit this card, while cards like Morgue Theft and Gravedigger become active much faster and don't net you card disadvantage. It's unfortunate, but in an environment with a number of strong cards that return creatures to your hand, this is only going to be playable if you have none of the others. A 9th - 11th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7b/de/7bde0e711bee9a3acfdfde2db986b59e7ff567f4f5eba4f24a292b2c142709aa.jpg)**Sengir Vampire**  
 What a monster. The Vampire has no down side, providing a lot for the mana you spend, then being virtually unblockable once it's out. Black four-toughness creatures are pretty hard to kill, and the fact that this one can get bigger should be all the more frustrating towards that endeavor. Really, there's no card I can see myself taking over the Vamp aside from Violent Eruption in a heavy red deck. Even then, it's hard to pass up a classic. A 1st pick. 

**Shade's Form**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If it's utility you want, Shade's Form may be your card. Whether you're using it to make an otherwise unimpressive creature a genuine threat, to allow you to reuse a coming-into-play ability or to take control of an opposing creature by enchanting it, then killing it, the Form will find itself useful. Somewhat mana intensive, the Form isn't an automatic deck-maker, but it'll see it's way into your initial twenty-three more often than not. A 6th - 8th pick

**Shambling Swarm**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Obviously the mana is an issue here, but if you can cast it with some regularity, the Swarm is going to wreck opponents easily and often. Simply, either your opponent has to be ready to trade three cards for the Swarm or take three points of damage each turn. Eventually, they'll have to choose the former option, and then there shouldn't be much coming back. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Sickening Dreams**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I originally thought this card was going to be pretty good, but as I've been finding with most of the 'Dreams' cards, the cost is just too much to make the spell effective. Sickening Dreams has two problems: it forces you to trade cards for effect in a way that's really hard to avoid card disadvantage on, and it provides a universal effect that your creatures will seldom be able to survive. A good way to get yourself threshold or madness, I'd recommend you not have more than one Dreams card in your deck. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Slithery Stalker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A good sideboard card, the Stalker is especially effective when dealing with Elephant tokens or any other creatures that won't come back if the Stalker is removed. If your opponent is half either green or white, bring this in. It'll serve you well. An 8th - 10th pick.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/magic/general/Soul_Scourge.jpg)**Soul Scourge**  
 I've been finding as of late that this isn't quite as good as I'd hoped, mostly because of it's two-toughness for five mana issues. Three-power flyers without drawbacks are pretty good, and the Scourge is no exception, but I'd definitely take Faceless Butcher, Crippling Fatigue and Cabal Torturer over it, and often you'll find your four and five slots getting extensive enough that taking lesser two- and three-casting cost spells over the Scourge will prove worthwhile. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Strength of Lunacy**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In a format with so little white, Strength of Lunacy won't be as good as its white counterpart, but that isn't to say it's without its uses. There are a lot of ways to turn madness into card advantage in this format and the Strength is no exception, bringing with it the added bonus of potentially making your evasion creature a very fast clock. A great sideboard card if nothing else, don't be afraid to main deck this enchantment if you have a lot of ways to discard it. You'll be surprised at how good it can be. A 6th - 8th pick. 

**Unhinge**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There have been cards like this before, and they've never quite made the grade. At three mana, Unhinge provides you card advantage of the worst kind: the kind that only gets you your opponent's least important card in the trade while risking a beating at the hands of madness. Best suited to a control deck that tries to match kill spells with opposing creatures, I'll never celebrate about having this card in my deck, but I will play it if card advantage is the name of my deck's game. A 9th - 11th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2b/27/2b2733a4e05c833464f3e40bf3c5dd6b354ace6cca63767835d67af2278dd7c6.jpg)**Waste Away**  
 The more I've played this card, the more I've come to appreciate it. At first glance, I thought it was just a black Acceptable Losses, but it's actually a lot more than that. Obviously, the fact it's an instant is something of a big deal, allowing you to recoup your losses by demolishing the stack, but it only becomes apparent this is a good card when you throw in everything else: helps with threshold and madness, kills a lot of the creatures in the format that almost nothing else kills and is an easily splashable card for those decks that are desperate for some way to deal with Aboshan or Cabal Patriarch. I wouldn't ever want more than two in any one deck, but up to two I'll be happy with. A 4th - 6th pick. 

**Zombie Trailblazer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Once again, we have triple black, but this time we won't be getting too much for it. Regardless of this card existing in an environment without that many zombies, the cost of a creature to activate each turn is a high one. Without its abilities being active, the Trailblazer is a 2/2 for ![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif), and that's a lot of mana-anxiety to go through for a 2/2. if you can cast it, you may want to play it as there will likely be four other black players at your table, but you shouldn't be happy about playing it. An 9th - 11th pick
