This is the fifth installment of my series evaluating the cards of *Legions* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the fourth installment [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/2003/04-15-legions-limited-review-red).

### White

It's funny. When you look at the cards, white sure looks like one color. They all have the pearly boarders and the sun symbol in the upper right corner, but that's just a part of the charade. The creatures of *Legions* white, which of course comprise of the entire color, might as well split down the middle: clerics and soldiers.

Don't get me wrong, there will be times when Aven Redeemer will make your soldier deck or Deftblade Elite your clerics and cards like Akroma and Windborn Muse are going to be playable in either, but these are the exceptions to what has plainly become a rule: there is no white deck. There are only shades of one.

Aside from green, white can fit in well with any of the colors, generally using soldiers with blue or red and clerics with black, though all three color combinations can conceivably go both ways. Just understand that when you're drafting with the plains in mind, tribal effects are going to be important: That means you should try to pick a tribe and run with it.

### The Cards

**Windborn Muse**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The 2/2 flyer has forever been my bane, but when you tack an extra toughness on the end, you have a pretty good card. That said, when you add an ability like the Muse's that completely ends your opponent's hopes of winning any kind of a race while you have at least one evasion creature, you have a potentially dominant card that's often worth the splash.

**Akroma, Angel of Wrath**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As good as the Muse is, there's still something very appealing about Akroma and no, I'm not talking spandex. At eight mana, this little lady gives new meaning to the term “high maintenance”, but she makes it more than worth your while… and no, I'm not going to continue this metaphor. Simple enough really: if you can manage to get her into play, you're going to win. It's a big “if” though.

**Defender of the Order**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I once referred to this card as a bomb to a couple of guys in Wisconsin whose reply was to laugh in my face, but here we are weeks later and its still rating this high on the list. If the Defender were just a 2/4 that morphed for ![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif), it would be a very strong card, but the additional bonus when it flips over makes it ridiculous. At the least, the +0/+2 is going to get you a little card advantage. At most, it's going to get you a lot.

**Aven Redeemer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Before you even think about saying something, this is not a 2/2 flyer for four. This is a healer, a powerful one whose special ability can dominate a game that happens to come wrapped up in a 2/2 body that happens to fly. The difference is that the 2/2 flyer's for four's primary objective is to perform as an evasion creature, which the Redeemer seldom does except on defense. An excellent card, regardless of tribe.

**White Knight**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In a field where tempo is at a premium and white two drops are scarce, this guy is in-sane. It costs less than morphs yet defeats them in battle, coming out on a turn where little else does. The double-white casting cost is a little annoying, but I'd hardly call it a drawback. The only reason it doesn't go right to the top is because its effect, while profound for a two drop, isn't going to be as widely felt as some of the more expensive cards on the list.

**Essence Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 3/3 for four is great, but it's the triggered ability that makes this guy special. Life gain isn't the most valuable commodity when it comes at the cost of a card, but when its an added bonus on what can otherwise be called a better-than-average creature and additionally can spread its mojo to your other creatures if you plan accordingly, you have one great card.

**Celestial Gatekeeper**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I may be overestimating this one, but it's always had a profound effect on the game when I've played it or against it. Obviously, of its own accord, it does nothing special: you need clerics in your deck to make the Gatekeeper worthwhile, but once you have them, “worthwhile” doesn't come close to defining how strong this thing is. A virtual three-for one unless you're forced to rush it out, the Gatekeeper can be a game winner. No, by the way, you can't return it to play. Its triggered ability can target it, but it's removed from the game before it returns to play.

**Aven Warhawk**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 General rule: 3/3 flyers for five are good and that means you only need to have one amplify payment candidate at the time of casting. The Hawk can be a lot bigger, but I recommend you not get too greedy: Don't hold all of your birds/soldiers if they're your only turn three and four spells: the tempo disadvantage isn't worth it.

**Sunstrike Legionnaire**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While it might look innocent, the Legionnaire can be a game winner. With approximately 19 creatures per deck, this little guy taps two creatures a turn more often than not, doing so with no activation cost and a lower-than-low casting cost. Don't look at the failure to untap during that step as a drawback: it's going to untap twice a turn as a result.

**Stoic Champion**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Okay, truth be told, the Champion should probably be a little lower on the list and this ranking assumes you have at least a few cyclers. The benefits are obvious: every time the Champion interacts with your opponents cards in any way, they have to take the possibility of a cycling card in your hand into account and, at the same time, it provides one of those 2/2 bodies for two mana I keep mentioning. A very good card all around, when you get it, it's up to you to know whether or not it's going to be great.

**Cloudreach Cavalry**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Moving up a lot in the right deck, there are few cards you'll want in your possession more if you have a bunch of birds and illusions with which to pump this little guy up. Think about it: we've been conditioned to believe 3/3 flyers should cost five mana, but the Cavalry offers the opportunity to cut more than half the casting cost off. Yes, its conditional, but the Cavalry can win the game before your opponent gets going.

**Wingbeat Warrior**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 That the Warrior is this far down the list is testimony to white's depth, because this is a very good card. Two-powered flyers for three mana have value in their generic form, but the morph ability the Warrior possesses makes it a lot better than that, offering the opportunity for some card advantage before flying through the air. That's a lot of good for a three casting cost creature.

**Daru Stinger**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 See, it's cards like this one that make a true ranking system impossible, because the Stinger is an easy first pick for the soldier deck. Simply, Heavy Ballista was the best common card in its entire environment and with two soldiers with which to amplify the Stinger, it exceeds is predecessor. Of course, the Stinger has been known to exceed even that standard. Yes, you can draw it later in the game after you've cast all your soldiers, but the effect an early Stinger has on the game is pivotal enough that you have take that chance… there's just too much good not to.

**Deftblade Elite**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The only creatures that come close in the game's history as far as Limited play goes are Mother of Runes and Ramosian Sergeant. That's some pretty exclusive company, but the Elite more than earns the praise, working with Daru Stinger to destroy opposing forces, blocking your opponent's largest attacker or drawing their most crucial blocker's ire. A great card.

**Gempalm Avenger**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Three mana cycling shouldn't occur in the early goings, but in the Avenger's case, that's okay. The thing is, you don't want to cycle it any old time because of the huge advantage it can give you in cycling more strategically, killing opposing attackers or blockers or protecting your soldiers from Swat and burn. Later on, it can even be a monster of a creature for you, with its 3/5 hide sure to prove potent against non-green decks. A very solid card, it's going late these days, so you might be able to get it around the table.

**Akroma's Devoted**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It's not too often you can attack with the Devoted without trading it for a morph, but when that's a card's biggest “drawback”, you don't have much to complain about. If this was a generic 2/4 cleric, I'd be happy to have it in my deck; the simple fact is it can be a whole lot more though. Pick it high if you manage to go tribal.

**Swooping Talon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Low ranking on this list not withstanding, the Talon can be a very powerful card. The six casting cost works against it, but provoke coupled with that kind of toughness can be very powerful. If your deck is the kind that will do a lot of trading and keep the table clear, the Talon moves up in value a bit.

**Liege of the Axe**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 You see? You see how deep white goes? This is a good card, with a built-in trick that will often take out an opposing creature, an ability that leaves behind a solid body and yet it ranks this low. Why? Well, it doesn't evade, doesn't contribute much to deck synergy and the mana requirements are nothing special, but really, none of that matters. The Liege should be a fine addition to your main deck.

**Beacon of Destiny**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 An annoying little sod, the Beacon serves as a 2-drop that slows down opposing flyers, fear creatures and the like, while protecting you from Searing Flesh and other red cards that can go to the head. Too bad Sandskin doesn't improve things any, but its still a good card for decks needing a little early defense.

**Daru Mender**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Always playable, the Mender may not cut it in your soldier decks due to lack of synergy, but you should never be too disappointed if it does. Daru Mender fits the 2/2 for three base around which the set revolves and, for one mana, can leave in its wake a 1/1 creature when trading with another morph in combat. Better yet, that one mana can save your more important creatures, be it from combat or targeted kill spells based on size. A nice, versatile card.

**Daru Sanctifier**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While often not quite good enough for the main deck with its triggered ability being a must, not a may, the Sanctifier is still a card you very plainly want to have, be it in main or sideboard. If you're playing clerics, great, it should warrant a main deck slot for sure, but if you are playing soldiers and worry about synergy, this is the most effective way to take out annoying enchantments like Lightning Rift and Lavamancer's Skill. **Whipgrass Entangler**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Again, if you can get one without mortgaging too much, you definitely want one Entangler in your cleric deck. That said, it's useless unless clerics are your base and if they are, they won't be for many others, so don't try too hard to get one. Insane in the cleric mirror.

**Lowland Tracker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 White has a number of cards - Daru Stinger, Gravel Slinger, Catapult Squad - that allow you to deal damage to attacking or blocking creatures. When you have a couple of those kinds of creatures, the Tracker can suddenly become dominant, allowing you to shoot down the target of its provoke ability, with the first strike often allowing it to live. Not great on its own, try to get those other cards in your deck if you want the Tracker.

**Starlight Invoker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The second worst of the Invokers, this guy just isn't exciting. Yes, it's a 2-drop, yes, it can stop morphs and survive, but it doesn't do anything offensively, and the activation of its ability is usually going to encompass your entire turn. That's not to say it's entirely unplayable, but you should probably try to avoid it unless your mana curve insists.

**Wall of Hope**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Simply put, the Wall doesn't really do enough a lot of the time to warrant main deck inclusion. Yes, you get a lot of bang for your buck: one mana is very reasonable for what you get, but there are some decks that just won't be affected enough. Side it in if your opponent has a lot of two-power creatures.
