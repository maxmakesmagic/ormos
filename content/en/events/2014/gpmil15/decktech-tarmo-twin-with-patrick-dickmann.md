"No one else seems to be innovating on [Splinter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter) Twin anymore," Platinum-level pro Patrick Dickmann complained. "Seems I have to do it all myself."

The German player clearly had made the deck his own. He won a Grand Prix with Twin, he made it to the Top 4 of Pro Tour *Born of the Gods* with Twin, he was widely considered the premier expert on all things [Deceiver Exarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deceiver+Exarch) and [Splinter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter) Twin even then, and just last month he added yet another top finish to the list, piloting an updated version of the deck to a ninth place at Grand Prix Madrid.

One thing that played a big part in all of Dickmann's various Twin designs and re-designs was the minimal focus he put on the actual combo. He usually build and played the deck more like a blue-red tempo deck and used [Splinter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter) Twin plus [Deceiver Exarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deceiver+Exarch)/Pestermite almost like a back-up. "I tend to win a lot of my games without ever assembling the combo," Dickmann explained. "A few creatures and burn spells, supplemented by some counterspells, can do wonders—especially when opponents are constantly on the lookout for the combo. They need to play around it, keep up mana for removal spells at all times, and then they simply lose the fair game."

At several points in time, the fair, noncombo part of Dickmann's Twin decks had included a splash of green for [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf). Dubbed "Tarmo-Twin," this version of the deck was what brought Dickmann all the way into the semifinals of the Pro Tour for example. More recently, however, [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) had fallen out of favor. As of Grand Prix Madrid, Dickmann had considered [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) a bad choice because of all the delving done by the Twin deck's own [Dig Through Time](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dig+Through+Time) as well as opponents' [Treasure Cruise](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasure+Cruise)s. But now, something has changed evidently.

"Yesterday morning I looked through my cards, came across [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), and thought to myself, 'Hey why not give the [Lhurgoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lhurgoyf) another chance.' In Modern, I usually just pick the most fun deck; that has always worked out well for me. To be honest, I do that with every format," Dickmann admitted with a sheepish grin. "But seriously, at Grand Prix Madrid I constantly found myself with a full graveyard, even with all the digging and delving."

And let's not forget that Grand Prix Madrid was won by Immanuel Gerschenson who also went against conventional wisdom and included [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) in his Blue-Red Delver deck, despite his full four [Treasure Cruise](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasure+Cruise)s.

"Another reason to pick Tarmo-Twin over straight Blue-Red [Splinter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter) Twin is that the deck has more play against [Choke](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Choke), both because of [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) itself and because of the lands in the green splash," Dickmann explained. "In Madrid, [Choke](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Choke) had easily been the single biggest problem for me."

Green also gave Dickmann access to valuable sideboard cards like [Nature's Claim](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nature%27s+Claim) and [Ancient Grudge](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancient+Grudge). "Affinity may not be the most popular deck at the moment, but it is insanely powerful," said Dickmann. "I specifically expected a lot of strong players, Justin Robb and Frank Karsten to name but two, to run the deck and wanted to hedge against it."

To make room for [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), Dickmann went down to just four [Deceiver Exarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deceiver+Exarch)s and two copies of [Splinter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter) Twin. That might seem very little, but Dickmann wasn't worried. "In reality, the deck still has a better shot at assembling the combo than previous versions, simply because of [Dig Through Time](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dig+Through+Time). That card is quite ridiculous," Dickmann stated. Other changes to the deck included cutting all sorcery-speed removal spells in favor of [Electrolyze](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Electrolyze) and [Burst Lightning](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burst+Lightning). "I'm still not entirely sold on the power of [Jeskai Ascendancy](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jeskai+Ascendancy), but obviously [Flame Slash](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Slash) and [Fork](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fork)ed Bolt interact pretty poorly with an unearthed [Fatestitcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fatestitcher)."

Dickmann again stressed the importance of a good sideboard in Modern. "I included some more cards for the [Scapeshift](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scapeshift) matchup there," said Dickmann. "The tempo plan, playing aggro-control, is better now obviously, with a much faster kill, but when you encounter another unfair deck, the lack of combo parts in one's own deck necessitates the addition of further protection."

Mirroring the return to [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), Dickmann was also back to using [Batterskull](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Batterskull). "I always used to have some midrange threats in the sideboard. I tried [Keranos, God of Storms](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Keranos%2C+God+of+Storms) for a while, and against Jund or Blue-White-Red Control Keranos was insane, but the format has turned more aggressive and those decks don't really exist anymore," Dickmann said. "So [Batterskull](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Batterskull). It's almost as hard to remove and has a much more immediate impact."

With only six combo cards and a grand total of two [Splinter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter) Twins in the deck, some might question whether the deck can even be called a Twin deck anymore. Dickmann wasn't worried, however, that opponents might give less respect to his ability to finish games quickly with an arbitrarily large number of [Deceiver Exarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20141213000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deceiver+Exarch) copies. "The combo kill has never been a big factor in my games," he said. "I think people know by now."
