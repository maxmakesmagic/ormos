When it comes to tournament *Magic*, sometimes the Top 8 tells you the whole story, and sometimes it doesn't. The Pro Tour this past weekend was one of the cases where the whole story wasn't obvious; even though Red-White Aggro was over-represented in the Top 8, it didn't actually dominate the tournament, and players (and wins) were split between the major archetypes of Izzet Drakes, Golgari Midrange, Red-White, Mono-Red, and Jeskai Control, with a couple of guest appearances by Boros Angels, Mono-Blue, Esper, and different flavors of Selesnya.

Of the major archetypes that didn't make the Top 8, Golgari Midrange is the most customizable one, and the one I feel has the best chance of success going forward, so it's the one I will focus on today.

Here's what I believe is the best performing Golgari Midrange list from the tournament, from Top 16 finisher Michael Kundegraber:

Golgari Midrange is a deck that has existed since the dawn of time, and its game plan has always been relatively consistent: to pair the overpowered green creatures with the overpowered black disruption. In Modern, this is done mostly in the form of discard spells and cheap removal; in Standard, we don't have the discard spells, but we get to play more removal and then have a better late game because of planeswalkers.

Every deck that isn't trying to close out the game quickly needs some form of card advantage, and in this iteration of black-green, it comes mainly from the explore creatures and the planeswalkers. There isn't an [Expansion // Explosion](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Expansion+%2F%2F+Explosion) type card that will provide you all the advantage you need in a single turn—instead, you get to grind your opponent with incremental two-for-ones. This is also one of the few decks in the format that gets to play a good utility land ([Memorial to Folly](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Memorial+to+Folly)), though there is only one copy here.

As far as the specific list is concerned, the thing that jumps out to me is the lack of [Wildgrowth Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wildgrowth+Walker) (and its replacement by [Druid of the Cowl](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Druid+of+the+Cowl)). While I understand what [Druid of the Cowl](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Druid+of+the+Cowl) does for you, I think [Wildgrowth Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wildgrowth+Walker) is just too important in this metagame, and I'm surprised Michael is not playing any. In fact, I'd go as far as saying that I just wouldn't play the deck without four [Wildgrowth Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wildgrowth+Walker)s in it (KMC-Genesis and Ultimate Guard Pro Team, for example, played two). It's literally your best card versus White Weenie, Mono-Red, Drakes, and Mono-Blue, since the combination of life gain plus a big body ensures you're very hard to race.

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Wildgrowth+Walker)](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wildgrowth+Walker)

Another card I like that Michael isn't playing is [Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer). It's not that good in the mirror, since it can just be killed (as opposed to [Carnage Tyrant](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carnage+Tyrant), which Michael has four copies of), but it's great versus White Weenie, Mono-Red, and some draws from Drakes (it's always excellent unless they draw [Beacon Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Beacon+Bolt)). It's cheaper than [Carnage Tyrant](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carnage+Tyrant) and a better blocker, since it flies, and it also helps you find whichever removal spell you need—[Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer) into [Find // Finality](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Find+%2F%2F+Finality) is a powerful curve.

When you build this kind of deck, you always have to decide what you want to beat. If you want to beat control and the mirror, then [Carnage Tyrant](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carnage+Tyrant) is the superior choice hands down. Personally, right now, I want to beat Drake, Mono-Red, and Red-White Aggro more than I want to beat the other two, so my choice would be [Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer). Then, I would try to shore up my weaknesses to control and the mirror in my sideboard.

I like Matt Nass's list because it's very well tuned to beat what I perceive to be the most popular decks going forward. Having three copies of [Cast Down](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cast+Down), for example, is excellent against Drakes, Mono-Red, and Red-White Aggro—almost everyone else plays one or at most two copies, relying on [Assassin's Trophy](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Assassin%27s+Trophy) instead, which is worse versus all of those. On top of that, he also has [Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer)s and the full four copies of [Wildgrowth Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20181116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wildgrowth+Walker). The downside, of course, is a worse mirror and control matchup, but I think this dedicated approach is better than trying to be decent against everything, which often results in beating nothing.

In the wake of the Pro Tour results, I think this list is a very good choice. It's the best version of Black-Green versus Red-White Aggro, and it's also good against Mono-Red, which might be everyone's go-to deck to beat Red-White Aggro. If I were playing a Standard tournament next weekend, this exact list would be a strong contender.
