The first list of Esper Tokens I saw was from a *Magic Online* user named Hogpog\_98, who went 9-0 in the Swiss rounds of a PTQ before ultimately falling to Temur Energy in the quarterfinals. Since then, the deck has been picking up steam, though it has yet to break through to the Top 8 of a major event.

At US Nationals this past weekend, we saw at least two famous players playing a list of Esper Tokens: Ali Aintrazi and Brian Braun-Duin. Both players wrote articles about the deck beforehand, so we have an idea of what lists they ended up playing. Here are the three decklists for comparison:

The core of the three lists is the same—they want to overwhelm their opponent with tokens, which you can generate via [Hidden Stockpile](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hidden+Stockpile), [The Scarab God](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Scarab+God), embalm creatures, [Legion's Landing](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Legion%27s+Landing), and [Start // Finish](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Start+%2F%2F+Finish), and double with [Anointed Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anointed+Procession). If you're constantly scrying with [Hidden Stockpile](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hidden+Stockpile), you'll eventually find a second [Anointed Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anointed+Procession), and then you get four tokens instead of one and quickly run away with the game.

The biggest difference among the lists is that Ali Aintrazi runs three copies of [Search for Azcanta](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Search+for+Azcanta), whereas the other two players run [Treasure Map](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasure+Map) instead. I like Search, but I wouldn't play it as a three-of, and I wouldn't cut an [Anointed Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anointed+Procession) for it.

I think that, in Game 1s, this deck is good against most of the field, regardless of which build you choose. Against Ramunap Red, you have a lot of blockers and some life gain. Against Temur and Sultai, you have [Fumigate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fumigate)s and [The Scarab God](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Scarab+God). Against control decks, you have threatening enchantments that dodge both [Vraska's Contempt](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vraska%27s+Contempt) and [Essence Scatter](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Essence+Scatter). If *Magic* was all about Game 1s, then I think this deck would have pushed through already.

I think this deck's biggest problem is with sideboarded games, once everyone gets to take out bad removal spells for more powerful cards. Red decks can bring in [Rampaging Ferocidon](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rampaging+Ferocidon), [Glorybringer](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glorybringer), and [Chandra, Torch of Defiance](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Torch+of+Defiance)—all of which bypass your game plan of life gaining and small blockers. Temur, Sultai, and control decks all get to add [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate) and/or [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress), which deal with both your threats and your answers. As such, the biggest advantages of the deck—dodging commonly used interaction and having game-breaking sweepers—disappears after sideboarding.

I think that, for the deck to break through, it needs a better sideboard plan. A sideboard for this deck can't be just a collection of cards that are good against certain archetypes, it has to accomplish four things:

1.  It must have a way to beat the control decks when they bring in [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) and [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate) to stop your engine.
2.  It must beat [Chandra, Torch of Defiance](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Torch+of+Defiance); [Glorybringer](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glorybringer); and [Rampaging Ferocidon](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rampaging+Ferocidon) out of the red decks.
3.  It must be able to beat the tokens mirror.
4.  It must have a plan against Temur and Sultai when their plan is to stop your [Fumigate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fumigate).

So, how do we go about accomplishing those things?

Number 1 is already there; I feel that, if you have your own [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate)s and [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress)es, you can fight that fight.

*"Well, this is awkward . . ."*

Number 2 is where I think the deck can be most improved. Right now, both BBD's and Hogpog\_98's lists run [Sunscourge Champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sunscourge+Champion) as their card against Ramunap Red, and I feel that this is a card that would be effective against their Game 1 configuration, but that isn't actually good after sideboarding, since it doesn't match up well against any of the three cards that beat you in Game 2s ([Chandra, Torch of Defiance](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Torch+of+Defiance); [Glorybringer](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glorybringer); and [Rampaging Ferocidon](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rampaging+Ferocidon)). I would much rather have an answer to those cards—[Vraska's Contempt](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vraska%27s+Contempt), [Cast Out](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cast+Out), and [Ixalan's Binding](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ixalan%27s+Binding) come to mind. I like [Ixalan's Binding](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ixalan%27s+Binding) the most, since it doubles up as a card for the mirror.

Number 3 is easy, but demands more slots. All the lists have [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) and [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate), and BBD even has [Demystify](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Demystify), but I think the best card in Esper for the mirror is [Lost Legacy](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lost+Legacy), which Hogpog\_98 used but both BBD and Ali cut. If you manage to take away their [Anointed Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anointed+Procession)s while keeping yours, it's very hard for your opponent to win. [Demystify](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Demystify) gets rid of one Procession, but they can just draw more since games go long, so I like getting rid of all of them. As a bonus, it's also good against White-Blue Approach. It's possible to play a mix, so that you can deal with resolved Processions and eventual [Ixalan's Binding](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ixalan%27s+Binding)s, but then I prefer [Fragmentize](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fragmentize) over [Demystify](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Demystify), since it can be boarded in versus decks like Mardu.

Number 4 is the hardest to accomplish. Temur and Sultai don't have as many spells as blue-black does, so you can't fill your deck with [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress)es and [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate)s to try to beat their counterspells. I think the best solution here is to bring in a powerful creature, since it dodges both [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) and [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate). Ideally, it has 5 toughness or more, to survive [Chandra, Torch of Defiance](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Torch+of+Defiance) and [Glorybringer](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glorybringer). [The Scarab God](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Scarab+God) is probably the best card for the job, and I wouldn't mind going up to four, because it should be your main game plan against those decks post-board. I've played four [Dragonlord Ojutai](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragonlord+Ojutai)s before and had no trouble with that, but if the idea of playing four of a legendary card scares you, then another viable option is [Crested Sunmare](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crested+Sunmare), which is easy to trigger with [Legion's Landing](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Legion%27s+Landing), [Sacred Cat](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sacred+Cat), and [Anointer Priest](https://web.archive.org/web/20171020000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anointer+Priest), assuming all those are still in your deck.
