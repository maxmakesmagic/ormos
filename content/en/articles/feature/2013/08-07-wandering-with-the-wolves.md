Green-blue aggro is not the most popular archetype, at least not since the days of Green-Blue Madness (which was more on the side of aggro-control, anyways). This list from a **Magic Online** Daily Event is light on the finesse and heavy on the aggro, leaving no doubt as to what the game plan is: cast resilient threats and [Giant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Growth) them. 

![Wandering Wolf](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Wandering+Wolf)

With twelve one-drops and twelve two-drops, as well as seventeen(!) pump effects, this deck goes for the throat. It's almost a hybrid of Mono-Red and Bant Hexproof, as it combines approaches from both decks into a slightly different angle than either.

Like Mono-Red, it has a million cheap creatures, and even has [Burn](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burn)ing-Tree Emissarys. [Giant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Growth)s are also dangerously similar to burn spells, particularly when combined with [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor). The clock this deck presents may even be faster, with [Wandering Wolf](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wandering+Wolf) being a big part of that. 

Speaking of wolves, the Bant Hexproof part of this deck lies in its resilient and hard-to-block creatures. [Experiment One](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Experiment+One) regenerates, there are eight undying creatures, and [Wandering Wolf](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wandering+Wolf) is nigh-impossible to defend against. It trades the invulnerability of hexproof for vastly better stats and lower casting costs, going for speed over protection. 

Against a field of aggressive decks, I actually like this deck a lot. It doesn't fare quite as well against removal, but it doesn't have a problem racing, and it even is better against [Supreme Verdict](https://web.archive.org/web/20130807000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Supreme+Verdict) than Hexproof due to all the undying guys.
