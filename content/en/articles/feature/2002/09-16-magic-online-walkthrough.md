### Day 2: Display and Game Play Settings

* * *

Sorry about the delay getting Day 2 out, but I figured everyone would like to see the information about the upcoming *OnslaughtTM* release. So, without further ado, let's talk about ***Magic** Online* settings.

No matter how much experience you have as a **Magic** player, there are quirks to playing ***Magic** Online* that can trip you up if you're not aware of them. Most of these things are handled through the **Settings** screens.

Scrolling through the tabs, you'll see there are many options for you to play with. You should take some time to look through these and try out the different options. However, the two screens that'll help (or hurt) your games the most are <b>Display Settings</b> and <b>Game Play Settings</b>.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

**Display Settings**  
 The **Display Settings** screen is all about making it easy to see what you're doing in ***Magic** Online.* You can get detail about all the options in the ***Magic** Online* help files, but I'll go over some of the ones that have the most effect on your game.

*Automatically resize cards*  
 Select this option to have ***Magic** Online* shrink cards when there are a lot on a player's side of the in-play area. When a game begins, this option won't have much affect on what you see. Once you get a lot of permanents in play, the cards are switched to the next smallest size available.

If you don't select this option, the cards will stay at the size you set in the **Your cards in play** and **Other players' cards in play** options. When a player gets a bunch of permanents in play, you'll have to scroll the in-play area to see everything on the board. You might forget that there are other cards in play that you can't see, which could affect your decisions.

*Shade keywords and show definitions*  
 This option is a must-have for new players. Words on cards that have specific **Magic** definitions will appear shaded. If you're not sure what the word means, click it and a definition will pop up. Once you're more familiar with the special terms you'll come across, feel free to clear this option.

*Show flavor text*  
 Many players may think this option has no effect on playing cards. In most cases, they're correct.

While it's cool to get a little feel for the **Magic** multiverse, some players get confused by the flavor text. Flavor text has no effect on a card's abilities, so hiding it will keep you from making that mistake. If you find that you expect the card to do something because of what's in the flavor text, clear this option.

*Use abbreviations on small cards*  
 This option is in the same ballpark as the **Shade keywords and show definitions** option. When cards are reduced in size, ***Magic** Online* will try to keep as much of the text on the card as possible. In some cases, this means some words will be abbreviated. If you're unfamiliar with what certain cards do and what some **Magic** terms mean, clear this option.

*Pop-Up Card Text Delay*  
 If you find yourself always pointing your mouse at a card to get the full text on the pop-up window, move the slider to the right. This makes the text come up quicker so you don't delay your play too much.

On the other hand, if you already have a handle on most of the cards and don't use the pop-up text much, move the slider to the left. That way you won't have pop-up windows appearing all the time when you're looking at the screen.

*Card Size Options*  
 There are five places you can adjust the size of the cards. Three of them (**Size of Cards in Hand** and **Card Size** under **Your cards in play** and **Other players' cards in play**) affect the size of cards on the **Duel** screen. The other **Card Size** option changes the size of cards on the **Deck Editor** and **Deck Builder** screens.

All of these settings depend on your comfort level (and eyesight). Play around with different sizes while you're in a game until you can see enough of the cards to make yourself happy.

**Game Play**  
 The **Game Play** options affect everything that goes on in the middle of a game. There are quite a few settings on this screen, so I advise you to take a look at the help file. I'll go over some of the most important ones to keep track of.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/77/12/77126cd3109c669385f4355c1a56658a5b20543d3b010f60d113a5acf0bd62fd.jpg"></td>&#10;<td><p><i>Set Stops</i><br> Your stops are what tell <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> when during the turn you always want to have a chance to play a spell or ability. Normally, whenever your opponent plays a spell or ability, you'll have a chance to respond. However, there might be times when your opponent doesn't do anything and you'll want to play something.</p>&#10;<p>There are two sets of stops: one for your turn, and one for your opponents' turns. While you can change these settings on the fly from the <b>Duel</b> screen, it's best to set them ahead of time.</p>&#10;<p>My advice is to begin with the default settings and play some games in the <b>Casual Play</b> area. Keep track of times your game skips past a step you'd like to have the chance to play something, then set a stop for that step.</p>&#10;<p>For example, if you want to play <a>Orim's Chant</a> to keep your opponent from playing cards during his or her turn, you'll need to set a stop for your opponent's upkeep step. Another common example is if you're playing any creature that has the ability to tap another creature (<a>Nomad Decoy</a>, <a>Stormscape Apprentice</a>, etc.). If you want to use the ability to tap a creature before it can be declared an attacker, you'll need a stop set for your opponent's beginning of combat step.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

You might wonder why you don't set stops for every step just to be sure. Well, there are two reasons. The first is that you'll end up clicking **OK** every step of every turn. It gets annoying quickly, trust me. The second is for when you play any sanctioned game or in a league. Those games use a game clock that ticks away whenever the game is waiting for you to do something. You'll lose a lot of time just clicking **OK** and may end up in a time crunch.

So that's what the stops are all about. Now let's take a look at some of the other options on the screen. Remember, if I don't cover it here, check the in-game help.

*Yield to combat damage that doesn't affect you or your creatures*  
 This is an important option to pay attention to for multiplayer games, and also has some affect on two-player games. All this means is that, if combat damage doesn't do anything to you, the game doesn't pause to see if you want to play something. Unless you have a spell you want to play on an opponent before combat damage goes through to him or her or your opponent's creatures, make sure this is selected.

*You can only announce a spell or activated ability if you have the mana in your mana pool to pay for it*  
 If you always tap land for mana before you select a spell to play, then you can ignore this option. All it means is you can't click cards in your hand to play them until you've paid the mana cost. When you play a game with this option selected, watch the names of the cards in your hand. As you add mana to your mana pool, the card names will change colors to show you've got enough mana to play the spell.

Clearing this option lets you click any card in your hand, then you'll be asked to pay the mana cost.

*Yield to your own madness triggered ability*  
 Madness is a mechanic on some *TormentTM* cards that lets you play the card whenever you'd normally discard it. Selecting this option means you don't automatically get the chance to play something else when you play a card using its madness ability.

*Yield to your opponent's madness triggered abilities*  
 Selecting this option means you don't automatically get a chance to play something when your opponent plays a card using its madness ability. There are probably times you'll want to respond, so leave this cleared unless you're running short on time.

*Always choose to remove madness cards from the game when discarded*  
 The only time you'll need to select this option is when you're playing a deck that uses the madness mechanic a lot. With this option selected, you won't be asked if you want to play the card using its madness ability and it'll save you some clicking. Otherwise, leave this option cleared.

**Conclusion**  
 The best way to decide what options you want selected and which ones you want cleared is to experiment. Set them up how you think you want them, then play some casual games. Eventually you'll get a feel for the best settings for your play style.
