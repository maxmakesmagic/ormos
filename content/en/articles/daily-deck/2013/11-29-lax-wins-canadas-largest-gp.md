It's been a long time coming for Bostonian Ari Lax. The Gold-level pro has been seeking his first Grand Prix title for years, coming close in both Nashville back in 2010, and again in Providence earlier this year. Grand Prix Toronto marks his seventh Grand Prix Top 8, but more importantly, it also marks his first win. 

Ari Lax navigated a field of 1603 players this weekend, in what became Canada's largest Grand Prix ever held. The field of over 1600 players cut down drastically going into Day Two, until only eight players remained. The Top 8 featured five players who have had multiple Grand Prix Top 8 finishes in 2013, including Hall of Fame member William "Huey" Jensen. However, it was Ari Lax who inevitably took down the whole tournament with an unconventional green-black deck that sported oddball stars such as [Bronze Sable](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bronze+Sable) and the [Loathsome Catoblepas](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loathsome+Catoblepas). 

Grand Prix Toronto also marks the end of the qualification season for Pro Tour *Born of the Gods*. Robert Smith, Rich Kraupa, Edgar Magalhaes, and Seth Manfield earned themselves airfare and an invitation to the big event in Valencia next year. 

But for now, the weekend is about Ari Lax and his big victory. After years of close calls, it has been a long time coming.

*   ### Quarterfinal - Rich Kraupa vs. Seth Manfield

    *by Josh Bennett*
  

The Story

Rich Kraupa spent his qualifying season in a series of near-misses, only to earn an invitation to Pro Tour *Born of the Gods* the hard way with his Top 8 appearance here at Toronto. Two-Time Grand Prix Champion Seth Manfield also scored an invite to Valencia, which lets him defer his Silver Status invite to Pro Tour *Journey into Nyx* in Atlanta. Now it's time to battle for gold and glory. 

![](http://wizards.com//mtg/images/daily/events/gptor13/qf_kraupa.jpg)

*Richard Kraupa*

  

Kraupa has a stocked blue-black deck that can bury the opponent in card advantage. Manfield's red-green aggro deck hopes to end the game before that can happen.

The Match

Game one was a brutal lopsided affair. Kraupa's first play of [Returned Phalanx](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Returned+Phalanx) fell to [Lightning Strike](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Strike), then he took four from [Minotaur Skullcleaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Minotaur+Skullcleaver). He played out a [Triton Fortune Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Triton+Fortune+Hunter), but Manfield suited up with [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad) and made it another four. Kraupa tried to fight back with [Griptide](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Griptide), but Manfield just rebuilt his board and then served up [Portent of Betrayal](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Portent+of+Betrayal) to put the game away. 

Game two was more Kraupa's speed. Manfield stumbled off the starting blocks, and after trading [Sedge Scorpion](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sedge+Scorpion) for [Fleshmad Steed](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fleshmad+Steed) he couldn't find another threat until [Ill-Tempered Cyclops](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ill-Tempered+Cyclops). When Manfield went for the monstrous, Kraupa was ready with [Lash of the Whip](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lash+of+the+Whip). [Disciple of Phenax](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disciple+of+Phenax) made sure the path was clear, and then Kraupa started chaining enchantments on [Triton Fortune Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Triton+Fortune+Hunter). The game was soon his. 

![](http://wizards.com//mtg/images/daily/events/gptor13/qf_manfield.jpg)

*Seth Manfield*

  

Manfield was back on the play and made the most of it. He got a quick six in with [Arena Athlete](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arena+Athlete) and [Minotaur Skullcleaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Minotaur+Skullcleaver) before Kraupa could get on the board. Kraupa summoned [Triton Fortune Hunter](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Triton+Fortune+Hunter), but was forced to block a second Skullcleaver and fell to just ten. 

However, Manfield had stalled on three mountains. Kraupa played [Disciple of Phenax](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disciple+of+Phenax), stealing [Destructive Revelry](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Destructive+Revelry). Manfield untapped and drew. He swung in with his Skullcleaver but couldn't punish a block. He passed the turn without playing a card. Another Disciple from Kraupa laid his secrets bare: A hand full of green cards: [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad), [Feral Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Invocation), [Savage Surge](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Surge) and [Fade into Antiquity](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fade+into+Antiquity). A forest would make all the difference, but it was not to be. Kraupa bestowed a pair of [Nimbus Naiad](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nimbus+Naiad)s on a second Fortune Hunter, and soon he was on to the semifinals. 

The Aftermath

Kraupa seemed almost apologetic about the conclusion to the match. Nobody likes a non-game, even when it ends in your favor. Manfield was gracious in defeat, shaking Kraupa's hand and wishing him luck in the Top 4.

*   ### Quarterfinal - Ari Lax vs. Ben Moir

    *by Mike Rosenberg*
  

Ben Moir was one of five players in this Top 8 that has earned multiple Grand Prix Top 8 finishes this year. His last breakthrough performance was in Grand Prix Detroit earned him a Top 4 finish, and his Top 8 finish here ensures that he will be able to compete in more than just Pro Tour *Born of the Gods*. 

His opponent, Gold-level Pro Ari Lax, is seeking his first Grand Prix victory. He earned the silver trophy with his team in Providence earlier this year, but now, he wants the trophy to match his Professional Point status. 

The Games

Lax kept a six card hand while Moir went to five. Lax had turn one [Tormented Hero](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tormented+Hero) into a second-turn [Ordeal of Nylea](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ordeal+of+Nylea). [Purphoros, God of the Forge](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Purphoros%2C+God+of+the+Forge) from Moir came down on turn four for Moir, and it was far away from becoming a creature. Things continued to go from bad to worse for Moir when he had to chump block each turn... 

...until he hit land #6 and cast [Elspeth, Sun's Champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elspeth%2C+Sun%27s+Champion). She made three tokens, and Lax suddenly froze. 

![](http://wizards.com//mtg/images/daily/events/gptor13/qf_moir.jpg)

*Ben Moir*

  

Life totals sat with Moir at 5, and Lax at 7, with Lax taking some early damage while his [Tormented Hero](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tormented+Hero) did some early beats. Lax's attacking 5/4 [Tormented Hero](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tormented+Hero) and [Erebos's Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Erebos%27s+Emissary) took out two tokens, but Elspeth made some more on the next. In turn, those tokens could trade with Lax's creatures thanks to Purphoros's pump effect. 

The two-card mythic combination was enough. Lax took a good look and proclaimed, "Yeah, I'm dead." Moir won a game that looks far out of his reach. 

As the two shuffled up for the second game, a little banter was shared.

Moir: "Yeah..."

Lax: "That did not feel good."

Moir: "No..."

The first two plays from Lax in the second game were [Erebos's Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Erebos%27s+Emissary) and [Nessian Asp](https://web.archive.org/web/20131129000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nessian+Asp).
