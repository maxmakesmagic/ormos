The new wave of green decks at Seville continues. Marcio Carvalho's choice was also Green Devotion. In this case, Mono-Green Devotion. By dismissing red, the deck gained a more distinct game plan: mana creatures ramping into huge creatures. The deck has the full four [Whisperwood Elemental](https://web.archive.org/web/20150225000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Whisperwood+Elemental)s, a card I discussed yesterday.

But this is just the beginning.

The deck has [Ugin, the Spirit Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20150225000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ugin%2C+the+Spirit+Dragon), a card often only seen in Blue-Black Control decks due to its high mana cost. Ugin's minus ability doesn't seem well matched with the deck. However, eight mana isn't a problem for this deck, and Ugin's plus and minus abilities are powerful enough that he makes losing very difficult once summoned.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ugin%2C+the+Spirit+Dragon)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150225000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ugin%2C+the+Spirit+Dragon)

If it's difficult to protect Ugin, that means the board is unfavorable anyway. In that case, why not reset it altogether, creatures and all? I never thought Ugin would fit in a Green Devotion deck, but Ugin gives the green deck a way to break through a situation it could not win otherwise.
