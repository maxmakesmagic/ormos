Four players who nobody had reckoned with make up the German National team. Bodo Rösner is an old-timer from Bremen, who fought his way from a 4-3 start right to the top. Rösner won 17 games straight between his last loss and the moment when he was National Champion. His final opponent was Dennis Johannsen, who is a semi-pro with a couple of Pro Tour appearances under his belt, but no successes on the big show yet.

Rösner carried an Angelfire deck to the Standard portion that he had built just before the tournament. He didn't like his chances and decided on the last minute-switch, which obviously served him well. The story of the Top 4, though, was Paul Borczyk, who had gone undefeated in draft without any preparation and piloted his way through the Standard portion with a green-black [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) deck supported by a white splash. His decklist has only 3 [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf)s, in his own words, "because everybody else played four and I wanted to do something different".

The last member of the team is Tobias Gräfensteiner. He and his brother Daniel are part of the young group of competitive Germans who seek to eventually become the heirs of Kai, Marco and Dirk. Worlds in New York may just become the springboard for Gräfensteiner to reach a higher level (quite literally), but he has quite a long way to go before he could ever be considered the next Marco, let alone Kai.

Everybody is looking forward to Worlds with mixed feelings about what the team can do. But Dennis Johannsen, well on his way to become the team's joker, already gave out the team order: "Stay ahead of the Japanese!" If they can do that, German spectators have nothing to worry about.

**Congratulations to Bodo Rösner the 2007 German National Champion!**

## Sunday, Sept 1: 12:20 p.m. - The Quarterfinal Round-Up

*by Hanno Terbuyken*

  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/dc/09/dc09e8d6618c33b58316ffdbb64b4d3227fab01cf61896460cb151356f32e69c.jpg)*Pro player Jim Herold assists with the German coverage, watching the match with head judge Falko Görres.*

I wish my eyes were a four-way split-screen with four pairs of hands. Then I could set each of them at a feature match table and write four parallel accounts of what happened. Alas, I cannot splinch myself, so here goes the two-eyed, ten-fingered walk-about round-up of the four quarterfinals. Prepare yourselves for a journey across four tables in one room!

The match between Tobias Gräfensteiner and Michael Müller was the first to finish a game. While Rösner and Johnson had just finished their pre-game procedure, Gräfensteiner was already one game up. By the seventh round, he had Michael Müller under Elemental-Shapeshifter lock and defended it with [Remand](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remand) and [Rune Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rune+Snag) until he had the win in the bag.

Michael Müller 0 - 1 Tobias Gräfensteiner

Tobias Rasir followed suit, beating Paul Borczyk after his opponent mulliganed to five. Yesterday night's estimates had Bordzcyk beating Rasis after sideboarding, though, but we would see. Sven Johnson with his TarmoRack was still deep in combat with Bodo Rösner, and under the camera, Thomas Koch defeated Dennis Johannsen in the first game.

Tobias Rasir 1 - 0 Paul Borczyk  
 Dennis Johannsen 0 - 1 Thomas Koch

We jump in with Tobias Rasir and Paul Borczyk, pitting Top 8 rookie against Top 8 rookie in the second game of their match. Rasir lead 1-0. Borczyk had been the story of the day yesterday with his Top 8 finish from a status of nearly no preparation, but the crowd favorite was Rasir. Borczyk kicked things off with [Ravenous Rats](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravenous+Rats), making Rasir discard [Teneb, the Harvester](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Teneb%2C+the+Harvester), and equipped the puny beater with [Loxodon Warhammer](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loxodon+Warhammer). [Castigate](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Castigate) from Rasir revealed Crime // Punishment, [Skeletal Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skeletal+Vampire), and [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) in Borczyk's hand. Rasir took the Crime away, and went for land with [Edge of Autumn](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Edge+of+Autumn).

Rasir 15, Borczyk 22, that was the **Magic** of [Loxodon Warhammer](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loxodon+Warhammer) right there. [Harmonize](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonize) for Rasir gave him more options than just the [Loxodon Hierarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loxodon+Hierarch) in his hand. Borczyk failed to bring on more action. The difference between the players was visible: Rasir had crisp, precise play going on, keeping track of the gamestate in his head, and played more like in a chess match than Borczyk, who looked as if he felt out of place on the final tables.

Rasir used [Wrath of God](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wrath+of+God) to clear away the [Ravenous Rats](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravenous+Rats) and [Birds of Paradise](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Birds+of+Paradise), and [Faith's Fetters](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Faith%27s+Fetters) to incapacitate Borczyk's [Hypnotic Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hypnotic+Specter). [Loxodon Hierarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loxodon+Hierarch) came down for Borczyk, Rasir decided on another [Harmonize](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonize) which brought him [Putrefy](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Putrefy) to kill Hierarch. The third [Harmonize](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonize) of the game gave Rasir [Troll Ascetic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Troll+Ascetic). Borczyk, though, used Crime to acquire the [Teneb, the Harvester](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Teneb%2C+the+Harvester) Rasir had discarded a while ago. Rasir's solution: playing Teneb himself, invoking the Legend rule and making them both die.

The next creature to appear on the board was Borczyk's [Grave-Shell Scarab](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grave-Shell+Scarab). But the toolbox Rasir had brought delivered: [Glittering Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glittering+Wish) for [Angel of Despair](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Despair) took out the Scarab for a turn, even though Borczyk had [Putrefy](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Putrefy) for the mighty sword-wielding lady. Despite all the fuss, nothing was really changing: Rasir: 15, Borczyk: 22. An attack with [Troll Ascetic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Troll+Ascetic) and playing [Loxodon Hierarch](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loxodon+Hierarch) let Rasis equalize the life totals.

Bordzyk put pressure on with [Skeletal Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skeletal+Vampire). Rasir attacked with Hierarch and Ascetic, prompting Borczyk: "Wrath in hand…" The hunch was good, as Rasir cleared the board and layed [Troll Ascetic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Troll+Ascetic). "Three more Wishes, and two Angels, then I have made it", said Borczyk, declaring his primary targets to attack in Rasir's deck. Rasir simply pointed at [Troll Ascetic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Troll+Ascetic). Borczyk: "Yeah, I can handle that one!"

"All in", said Rasir, and get Debtor's Knell with [Glittering Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glittering+Wish). Looking at the [Angel of Despair](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel+of+Despair) and two Tenebs in his 'yard, Rasir asked: "Do you have it?", meaning Borczyk's single [Mortify](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mortify). Borczyk drew it one turn later. Rasir's position had improved enough, though, that he could beat Borczyk to zero.

Tobias Rasir 2 - 0 Paul Borczyk

On the other tables, Dennis Johannsen had equaled out to one game each against Thomas Koch, and Tobias Gräfensteiner had taken a 2-0 lead over Michael Müller.

Michael Müller 0 - 2 Tobias Gräfensteiner

Bodo Rösner versus Sven Johnson saw Rösner in the lead with a comfortable 2-0. The third game started with a [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare) from Rösner on [Smallpox](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smallpox), [Lightning Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Helix) on Johnson's [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) and two [Dodecapod](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dodecapod)s that Rösner put into play thanks to Johnson's discard spells. Two turns later, Rösner had [Faith's Fetters](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Faith%27s+Fetters) for Johnson's [Tombstalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tombstalker), and that was the fastest game and match in the Top 8 so far.

Bodo Rösner 3 - 0 Sven Johnson

Both players talked about their sideboard strategies. ## Sunday, Sept 1: 5:38 p.m. - The Legacy Championships, and Volkan Baga

*by Hanno Terbuyken*

 

A National Champion has been found, the team is set to prepare for the World Championships, but the event is not over by far. 175 players met this morning to conquer the main hall, more players than in the actual German Nationals. They wanted to test their skills with the old cards, playing in the open German Legacy Championship. Eight rounds of Swiss are still being played, with a cut to Top 8 to happen sometime in the future. The Legacy metagame here seems to be two-sided: On one hand, players win around turn three with a giant [Empty the Warrens](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Empty+the+Warrens), on the other hand, they beat down with the hottest card in **Magic**: [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf).

Also, artist Volkan Baga is still on site, signing cards, binders and playmats to those who want it. He's a very recent addition to the **Magic** art portfolio, having illustrated just eight cards so far, among them [Stromgald Crusader](https://web.archive.org/web/20170412185239/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stromgald+Crusader). The original art is on display here, and it's gorgeous. Take a closer look at the card: You may not be able to see it, but the grin of the Crusader is the most mischievous I've ever seen on a skeleton, Terry Pratchett's Death included.
