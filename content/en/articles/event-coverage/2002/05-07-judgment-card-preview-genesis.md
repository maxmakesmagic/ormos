Traditionally, green five casting cost 4/4 creatures were only exciting when played in your draft deck. For five mana, you can either get a larger monster (Shivan Wurm, Kavu Titan), or play with smaller creatures and cast a devastating five-mana spell like Overrun. So what makes Genesis different and worth considering for your post-*Judgment* decks? It is one of few large green creatures whose special ability is more important than their raw power.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1a/6b/1a6b91c1e1eb90bafc5b08dad103379e9909f2c9dc2bb76492b21af637a70074.jpg)

Genesis represents one of the new mechanics in *Judgment*. It and several other creature cards like it provide powerful special abilities or enhance the creatures you have in play - but only after the creature itself has been sent to the graveyard! Once there, Genesis is your path to victory if only the game lasts long enough. Just like almost any other card, there are times when you will not be able to take full advantage of Genesis. You may be really far behind, and cannot afford to spend a turn reanimating a creature. In most cases though, Genesis can win you the game if you just get enough breathing room.

Suppose you are playing a red-green mirror match. You've traded off the early creatures in combat, or by casting Flametongue Kavus on each other. Eventually, you get to cast Genesis. Your opponent can choose not to kill the Incarnation in order to stop you from getting an extra card out of your graveyard every turn you like, but that would come at a hefty price. If you are ahead or the situation is even, you can start attacking for four points of damage every turn. There is no way any deck can afford to take that abuse for more than a couple of turns total. If you are falling behind, Genesis is a perfect blocker. Your opponent won't be able to attack with anything big without sending it off to the bin. Once there, Genesis can return Flametongue Kavu to your hand and let you take out an opposing creature. Once in play, Kavu will present the same problem. Will your opponent take four points of damage every single turn, or will he let you keep hitting him?

While certainly useful in that scenario, Genesis becomes even more impressive when used against a control deck. Be it Psychatog or blue-white control - they rely on being able to counter or kill your creatures and eventually pulling ahead thanks to the card economy provided by Fact or Fiction, Wrath of God, and other powerful spoilers. Outside of using Syncopate, or resetting the game with Upheaval, what solution do these decks have for Genesis? If not countered or killed, it will quickly demolish the opponent, whose life total should already have been reduced by the onslaught of your early creatures. If countered, Genesis will relentlessly bring back a creature per turn, and no amount of card economy that a control deck can generate will stop it.

Genesis truly shines in Limited play, where it is an unquestionable first pick in a draft and a very strong argument for playing green if you get it as a part of your sealed deck set. Whereas speed can be more important than card economy in most Constructed games, the opposite is often true in Limited, where card economy and individual ultra-powerful "bomb" cards are king. A large creature that provides that card economy and is easy to splash for even if green isn't one of your main colors will see plenty of play.

Extended opens a whole new realm of possibilities for Genesis. One of the most exciting is combining it with creatures with echo such as Bone Shredder and Radiant's Dragoons - that way you can recur a creature for its comes into play effect and not have to worry about getting it back to the graveyard at all to do it again.

It remains to be seen just how much Genesis will influence the way green decks are built this summer. One thing is for certain, it will be making an appearance in tournament play.

Helpful Genesis tips:

*   When no other creatures are available, you can always use Genesis to reanimate itself.
*   Unlike Genesis, cards like Blurred Mongoose lose their creature abilities when in graveyard, so you can reanimate untargetable creatures, or creatures that have protection from green.
