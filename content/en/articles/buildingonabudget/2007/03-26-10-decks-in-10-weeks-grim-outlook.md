![](https://i.ormos.uk/ce/17/ce17ed7b2d1ea108ad0cbe796ed4fb7347dd80e06614d364ebdd1a73e145830a.jpg)Welcome back to Building on a Budget! We're on week four of ten on our quest to jump from one deck to the next! For those just joining us – Ten Decks in Ten Weeks is an experiment in which I build a two-color deck, test and tune it, and then keep all the cards of one of the colors as my starting point for the following week. Last week's deck was a Red/Blue [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) deck. Before we get started on this week's evolution, let me address one concern about the Ten Decks in Ten Weeks project. There have been two concerns expressed, which are seemingly at odds with one another:

*You don't keep enough of the cards from the beginning color of the build in the end result of the build.*

and

*You start out by keeping too many cards from the beginning color of the build when they are obviously not optimal for the new deck.*

To this I answer: You know those theme deck evolutions I've done in the past? The 10 Decks in 10 Weeks owes a lot to those columns. How so? Well, take a look again at this week's deck. I'm starting with 47 of the 60 cards for my deck! Some of these might not be the best choices when I'm transitioning from a blue-red control deck to a blue-black discard deck, but it's a really good jumping-off point to build a deck from. If a card blatantly doesn't belong in the deck, it usually gets taken out by the first changes to the deck. Sometimes a card might surprise me. Either way, this method is similar to how I used to build decks back in the day – take cards from a previous deck that I liked, and see how they operate when partnered up with a new color.

For reference, here's what I took out of the deck: 

Out: 4 [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), 4 [Izzet Signet](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Izzet+Signet), 4 [Izzet Boilerworks](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Izzet+Boilerworks), 1 [Invoke the Firemind](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Invoke+the+Firemind), 4 [Pyroclasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyroclasm), 1 [Shard Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shard+Phoenix), 1 [Jaya Ballard, Task Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jaya+Ballard%2C+Task+Mage), 4 [Conflagrate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Conflagrate)

For simplicity, I also swapped out the appropriate basic lands, signets, and bounce lands from Izzet to Dimir.

In: 4 [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp), 4 [Dimir Signet](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Signet), 4 [Dimir Aqueduct](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Aqueduct)

For those who have been long-time readers of Building on a Budget, [my first column ever](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=mtgcom/daily/bb53) was a black-blue discard deck. I built that deck around [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim), and it didn't work out too well. Here's what I had to say about [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) last year:

> *"..."*

In reviewing that column, I realized that I didn't really do a good job of explaining why [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) did not work in that deck. Allow me to clarify: [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) is a do-nothing card. That is, in-and-of itself, it does nothing. If you play [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) on turn three, all you've done is put an enchantment on the board that needs other cards to work. [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) is entirely passive, and relies 100% on other cards to do anything. 

![Megrim with a large red X](https://i.ormos.uk/fc/56/fc567362d5a23cbe7db00f9f71ef8c2bd77069f7d59e8dc8d0f1d33a8b87ca79.jpg)Moreoever, most good discard spells cost one, two, or three mana. Discard is maximally effective early in the game – the longer the game drags on, the quicker your opponent can empty their hand by casting spells. If your opponent has four cards in hand on turn three, would you rather play [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) and let him play three of those spells the following turn, or play [Stupor](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stupor) and strip out half of his hand? Do you hold off casting [Cry of Contrition](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cry+of+Contrition) on turn one so you can cast it on turn four after [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) is on the board? Holding back early-game discard seems counterintuitive when your game plan is to knock out your opponent's hand as quickly as possible and send them into top-deck mode.

For these reasons, I made a conscious decision to completely avoid [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) this time around. Now, before you break out the pitchforks and protest boards, fear not! You wanted a black-blue discard deck, and you're getting a black-blue discard deck.

Enter the [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack). Here are 5 reasons why I like [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) better than [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim):  

*   The Rack takes less color-specific mana to play.
*   The Rack costs less mana to play.
*   You can drop The Rack on the board after your discard spells, and have it deal maximum damage. Megrim is strictly beforesies.
*   The Rack features art from Stuffy Doll. Megrim artworks generally resemble visualized headaches.
*   I actually have a long history with The Rack. Grok this:



Yup, back when Magic was first in its infancy, I was playing a Black/Red discard deck centered around [Hypnotic Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hypnotic+Specter), Hymn to Tourach, and [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack)! You can find a description of the deck, written by yours truly on December 12th, 1994, [here](http://groups.google.com/group/rec.games.trading-cards.magic.strategy/browse_thread/thread/32e7b95b373cbbda/6d80c49904e7b9c8?lnk=st&q=bleiweiss+the+rack&rnum=4&hl=en#6d80c49904e7b9c8).

**Okay, be honest here. Were you alive when I built that deck in December of 1994?** Yes, I was alive and kicking!No, I wasn't even born yet!

In: 4 [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack)

Another advantage [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) has over [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim) is that it is harder to play around. With [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim), your opponent can empty their hand, and dodge your discard spells. Can't make someone discard from a hand of zero! [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack), however, causes tension. Let's say your opponent has two cards in hand, and you've got [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) on the board. Do they play those cards, and risk taking more damage? Or do they hold the cards, and hope you don't draw a discard spell? [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) forces your opponent into a disadvantageous situation, which can cause them to make bad judgment calls based on the game state.

The first thing I did when making up the Grim Outlook deck was to make a list of all the discard spells available in Standard. The second thing I did was make a list of all the cards in Standard that forced an opponent to remove a card in their hand from the game. Remember – the goal for [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) isn't necessarily to have an opponent send a card from their hand to the graveyard – it's to make them have no cards in hand. [Castigate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Castigate) and [Coercion](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coercion) work equally well in this regard!

For the first build of Grim Outlook, I was torn between wanting to put in powerful one-shot discard spells ([Stupor](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stupor), [Persecute](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Persecute)) or powerful repeating discard spells. Due to my low creature count (the only creatures left in my deck were four [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler)s and a [Riftwing Cloudskate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Riftwing+Cloudskate)), I decided to go the latter route. This left me the following choices:

[Blizzard Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blizzard+Specter)  
[Dementia Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dementia+Sliver)  
[Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse)  
[Dimir Guildmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Guildmage)  
[Hypnotic Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hypnotic+Specter)  
[Lore Broker](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lore+Broker)  
[Riptide Pilferer](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Riptide+Pilferer)

[Hypnotic Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hypnotic+Specter) was immediately out because of budget – they run around 8 tickets each on Magic Online, so a playset of four would be more than the entire cost of this deck! I also didn't want to run [Dementia Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dementia+Sliver) or [Dimir Guildmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Guildmage) – I've had experiences with the latter in other BoaB decks, and I've found it to be too slow. The Sliver would be equally torturous to use. [Lore Broker](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lore+Broker) would help my opponent as much as hinder them, and doesn't actually make them lose cards from the hand – it's suited for [Megrim](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Megrim), but not [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack).

This left Blizzard Specter, [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse), and [Riptide Pilferer](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Riptide+Pilferer) as my choices. I decided to start [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse), since it swings a two-card advantage – your opponent discards, *and* you draw a card. This combines well with [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler), which is already in the deck.

In: 4 [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse)

While deciding which creature to use for the last three slots, inspiration hit. I remembered that for a brief time, there had been a card used frequently with the Urzatron in white-blue decks. This card was one near-and-dear to my own heart (in fact, my use of it predated more widespread use), and would be perfect for this deck. It strips the opponent's hand, is mana-hungry for an Urzatron, and doesn't have color requirements.

Figured it out yet?

![Muse Vessel](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Muse+Vessel)

That's right – my [old friend](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=mtgcom/daily/bb70) [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel)! It was time to dig the ol son-of-a-Scepter out of retirement and give him a dusting off. Last time I played with [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel), my goal was to deny my opponent lands, use [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) to strip their hand, and then beat them to death with their own kill spells. 

This time around, [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) is an improved version of [Disrupting Scepter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disrupting+Scepter). Although it costs one more mana than the Scepter, [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) has the same activation cost once it hits the table. For that one mana up-front investment you get the following improvements:

*   Removing cards from the game rather than discard – important for Dredge cards, Reanimator decks, or decks that can Recollect spells.
*   The ability to, once in a while, use your opponent’s cards.

Note that this time around, [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) is there to strip an opponent's hand for [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) – if #2 happens, it's just gravy on the train!

In: 3 [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel)

The Blue/Red [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) deck was focused solely on winning through drawing lots of cards and killing with either [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) or [Conflagrate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Conflagrate). This blue-black deck still leans heavily on [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) for the win, but seeks to win through card advantage rather than pure card drawing – I have [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) and [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) as an alternate win condition, and so this hybrid discard/card-drawing deck has two routes to victory, rather the just the one from last week.

Game 1: Ickiwon (Red/Blue/Green Land Destruction)  
 I get all three Urza Lands by turn three, with two [Dimir Signet](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Signet)s to boot. This allows me to both suspend two [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler)s and Rune [Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snag) his land destruction spells, ending the game in a hurry.  
 Record: 1-0

Game 2: Scree (Black/Blue Milling)  
 He gets stuck with only blue mana, and I am able to take advantage of his slow start by dropping a third-turn [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) and ripping his hand apart. By the time he draws a black mana and plays [Evil Eye of Urborg](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Evil+Eye+of+Urborg), I have removed his entire hand with [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) and the Cutpurse, and have [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) on the board to eat his life total.  
 Record: 2-0

Game 3: Tiddlyflop (Mono-Red Burn/Storm)  
 He starts by burning me down to 10, but I get an early Urzatron thanks to [Compulsive Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Compulsive+Research) and drop back-to-back [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel)s. He tries to go off with storm by play [Seething Song](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seething+Song), but I counter it to keep him from getting his mana count up. I then drop [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack), hit him for three for five consecutive turns, and then finish him off with a second copy of [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) on turn number six.  
 Record: 3-0

Game 4: Meshin (B/U/G Control)  
 He [Boomerang](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boomerang)s my turn-two [Dimir Aqueduct](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Aqueduct) and plays double [Farseek](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Farseek), which puts him a full three land drops ahead of me! I manage to drop the first creature with a [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse), and he answers with [Plague Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plague+Sliver). I attempt to [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal) the Sliver, but he has [Remand](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Remand). He drops [Coiling Oracle](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coiling+Oracle) and swings in for 5. I swing in with my Cutpurse, draw a chump block, and [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal)[Plague Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plague+Sliver) off the board. He plays [Assassinate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Assassinate) on my Cutpurse on his turn, but walks his [Plague Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plague+Sliver) into a [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak).

![Aeon Chronicler](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Aeon+Chronicler)

With the board back at parity, I suspend [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) for three and draw into the Urzatron. The Chronicler hits once and is then [Assassinate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Assassinate)d, but I draw [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) and begin taking out Meshin's hand. [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) #2 joins the party, and Meshin is left with three cards in hand when I first swing. He attempts to get [Ashcoat Bear](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashcoat+Bear) into play, but I [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal) it *twice* to allow my Cutpurse to knock him down to one card – and then I drop all three copies of [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) I had been holding in my hand. With the [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) already in play, Meshin is unable to dig himself out of a hole, and loses shortly thereafter.  
 Record: 4-0

The blue-red [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) deck took advantage of the three Urza Lands mainly through [Conflagrate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Conflagrate) and [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler). This deck was taking advantage through [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) and [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel). Why not throw in a ridiculously expensive yet game-altering spell in-theme that can feed off of the seven mana produced by the Urzatron?

Out: 1 [Riftwing Cloudskate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Riftwing+Cloudskate), 1 [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp)  
 In: 2 [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End)

That's right – [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End)! For when you want to make sure your opponent doesn't have a hand, period, end of story. The [Cloudskate](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudskate) wasn't really doing anything in this deck, and I felt like I could lose a basic land given the make-up of the colors of this deck. If I could power out a turn four or five [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End) with Signets and the Urzatron, that would spell game over for many opponents – plus it would hasten a late-game win with [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) if necessary.

Game 5: Luckzach (B/G/R/U Control)  
 We go back and forth dropping 2/2 creatures to the board – my two [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) to his morphs. He tries to play [Indrik Stomphowler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Indrik+Stomphowler) to take out a [Dimir Signet](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Signet), and I attempt to [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) it. Luckzach then flips [Willbender](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Willbender), but cannot change the target of my [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak).

Rules Lesson: A spell cannot target itself. The only two spells on the stack were the Stomphowler (which the [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) was already targeting), and the [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) itself. [Willbender](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Willbender) can't make the [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) target itself, and it's already targeting the Stomphowler. This lead to both a wasted [Willbender](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Willbender) and a resolution of my [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), stopping his beast.

I added insult to injury by playing [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End) on my turn (which *would* have been directed right back at me with [Willbender](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Willbender)!), taking out [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), [Savage Twister](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Twister), [Harmonize](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonize), and [Deadwood Treefolk](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deadwood+Treefolk). One [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) and one Chronicler later, and that was all she wrote.  
 Record: 5-0

I won't complain about a 9 and 1 record, but I will make some changes to the deck! Although the deck was working well, I've always been curious about Blizzard Specter. On paper, he's [Abyssal Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abyssal+Specter) with perks – if your opponent has no hand, you get to [Sigil of Sleep](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sigil+of+Sleep) their worst permanent (since they choose what bounces). This overcomes one of the big disadvantages of discard creatures – they are useless and overcosted if your opponent empties their hand. Nobody wants to pay four mana for a 2/3 vanilla flyer. Everyone wants a 2/3 flyer with perks regardless of opponent's hand size.

Blizzard Specter has the added bonus of working well with [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel). In my old [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) deck, I used [Temporal Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temporal+Adept) to both lock down my opponent's mana and feed cards into [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel) against an empty hand. Blizzard Specter *can* serve the same purpose, but again it's mainly there for discard, with the *option* of bouncing permanents. If I hit an opponent with both Blizzard Specter and [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) at the same time, I can stack the abilities in any order I want – meaning I can have the Specter bounce a permanent first, and then have the Cutpurse make my opponent discard that card.

In short, Grim Outlook is a synergy bistro.

I also wanted to add two [Gemstone Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gemstone+Mine)s to the deck. [Gemstone Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gemstone+Mine)s run about 3 tickets each on Magic Online right now, and the rest of the deck can be gotten for under 10 tickets. [Gemstone Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gemstone+Mine) is a good land to have for any variety of decks that need multiple colors of mana, and so I don't mind throwing a couple into this deck. If you were to go out and buy/trade for [Gemstone Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gemstone+Mine)s, you'd be able to use them in virtually any deck you wanted (unlike a higher dollar, niche/color specific card such as [Persecute](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Persecute). It also costs 3 tickets, but I avoided for this deck for that reason – it's specific to one color or deck type). 

Two [Gemstone Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gemstone+Mine)s are fine, because they are there to capitalize on [Muse Vessel](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muse+Vessel). They work well with [Dimir Aqueduct](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Aqueduct), because I can remove two counters from my Mines, and then bounce them with the Aqueduct, allowing me to play them fresh with three counters the turn after.

To make room for these cards, I took out an [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island) and a [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) (for the Mines), and one Rune [Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snag), one Chronicler, and one [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal). I chose those three cards because I figured I could lose a little utility in exchange for another creature/win condition. While it might seem counterintuitive to cut a Rune [Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snag), they are in there for the first Rune [Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snag) (pay 2 mana) – any extras drawn are gravy, but I'm relying on them being [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak)s 5,6, and 7, and not as an über-counter.

 We both build up mana, and I make the first move by suspending [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler). The Cutpurse dies to Lighting Helix, and Celalsson tries to play Numot, The Devastator, but that walks it right into a Rune [Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snag). He runs out of gas, and I get another [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler), a [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse), and a Blizzard Specter. After I strip his hand, he casts [Windreaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windreaver), but I'm already too far ahead for him to catch back up. After playing double [Compulsive Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Compulsive+Research), he concedes.  
 Record: 10-2

 He suspends [Greater Gargadon](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Greater+Gargadon), and I get an early Urzatron.  Record: 11-2

Game 14: ickiwon (R/U/G LD)

Game 14: Lajoy Lord (Mono-Green)  
 He gets [Silhana Ledgewalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silhana+Ledgewalker), [Giant Spider](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Spider) and [Elvish Bard](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elvish+Bard) against my board of Blizzard Specter and triple [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse). When he attempts to play [Moldervine Cloak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Moldervine+Cloak), I counter it. Lajoy then plays scared of counters the rest of the game, never dredges the Cloak, and dies when I empty his hand with [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End) and bounce his Spider with [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal), allowing my Specter to come through. If he had dredged the Cloak at any time after my first [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal), his Bard would have decimated my team.

Remember: [Dredge](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dredge) cards don't fear countermagic. If a Blue player needs to [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell)[Moldervine Cloak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Moldervine+Cloak), most times you want to keep dredging it back until it sticks. They have a limited number of counterspells (unless it's [Spell Burst](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Burst) with buyback). You don't really have a limit on the number of times you can dredge your Cloak!  
 Record: 12-3

Game 15: NickD316 (Mono-Black Control)  
 He plays Will-O'-The-Wisp, [Royal Assassin](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Royal+Assassin), and [Hypnotic Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hypnotic+Specter). I drop a bunch of creatures but can't attack through his defenses. He gets [Loxodon Warhammer](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loxodon+Warhammer), trades his Specter for a Blizzard Specter, and then drops Herald of Leshrac. The Herald hits me for 15 before it dies to its own cumulative upkeep, bringing me to 3. Before I can draw an answer, he gets another [Hypnotic Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hypnotic+Specter) and flies in for the win.  
 Record: 12-4

This is a third game that I've lost to one-toughness creatures. It's definitely time to address this issue. I know that there are spells suited for the task of dealing with a first-turn one-toughness creature, and I'm determined to bring them in at this point. The question is: Do I feel like a Funeral or a Pirate? 

[Funeral Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Funeral+Charm) and [Piracy Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Piracy+Charm) are nearly identical, with the former giving [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp)walk, and the latter [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island)walk. However, if you have a choice between the two, [Piracy Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Piracy+Charm) is almost strictly superior. Why? There are a *lot* more protection from black creatures out there than protection from blue, and for that reason, a blue removal spell that mirrors a black removal spell is more valuable. It helps that [Piracy Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Piracy+Charm) also doubles as discard in a pinch, no?

I also add back in the fourth [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal) (oh, how I missed you!), and finish removing Rune [Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snag) to make room for these cards. I also yank out one [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End) – while it's a nice trick to draw, a lot of the time I am already facing an opponent with an empty hand when I draw it. One seems like enough.

Game 19: biljac (Mono-White Aggro)  
 This is a really close game – he gets an early [Knight of the Holy Nimbus](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+the+Holy+Nimbus) and [Paladin en-Vec](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Paladin+en-Vec), but I get double [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) and he needs to hold back the Paladin to block. He gets two copies of [Glorious Anthem](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glorious+Anthem), but I repeal his Paladin on back-to-back turns, and start getting card advantage. He suspends [Ivory Giant](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ivory+Giant), and we reach a stalemate were I take out his entire hand and drop a copy of [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack), hitting him a couple of times. His Giant unsuspends and he takes me to two, but I have [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) on the board. On my turn, I play [Compulsive Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Compulsive+Research) and end up with a hand of [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), Blizzard Specter, [Compulsive Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Compulsive+Research), and [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) before discarding. His board is a [Knight of the Holy Nimbus](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+the+Holy+Nimbus) and the [Ivory Giant](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ivory+Giant), and mine is just the Chronicler and [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack). I choose to discard [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) and [Compulsive Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Compulsive+Research), and then drop Blizzard Specter to chump block, and [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack) to race him for 6 damage this and next turn, which would win me the game...

Except, if you're paying attention, you'll notice that this left me with no cards in hand, making this the first time in two weeks that I killed my own Chronicler by having zero cards in hand. I got greedy for the win when I saw the second copy of [The Rack](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=The+Rack), and instead of correctly discarding it and keeping the [Compulsive Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Compulsive+Research) and Blizzard Specter, I kept it and lost. D'oh!  
 Record: 14-5

Game 20: Rep177 (B/W Orzhov)  
 He gets early guys creatures, but I get double [Dimir Cutpurse](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dimir+Cutpurse) and multiple [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal)s. He plays [Wrath of God](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wrath+of+God), and I answer with [Wit's End](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wit%27s+End), taking out his hand of expensive fatties and Debtors' Knell. I then draw back-to-back-to-back [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler)s, arrange all three to unsuspend at the same time, and hit him with a trio of 10/10 creatures.  
 Record: 15-5

Of all the decks built for the Ten Decks in Ten Weeks experiment, this one and the previous one held over the highest number of cards from one week to the next. Despite this, both decks played completely differently – the red-blue [Aeon Chronicler](https://web.archive.org/web/20070326000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aeon+Chronicler) played control, creature kill, and then finished the game in one blaze of glory. Grim Outlook is a lot more foward – I dropped early creatures, attacked my opponent's hand aggressively, and left them with nothing in the end. If last week's deck could be categorized as combo-control, then this one would be an aggro-control deck – I won through aggression, but had control elements (some countermagic, some discard) to facilitate the victory.
