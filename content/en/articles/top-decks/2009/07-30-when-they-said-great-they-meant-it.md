Three more National Championships have hit the books, and in light of these and other recent tournament events, we can start to make some pretty bold claims. Like: "Five-Color Control is the best deck in the format."

Skeptical?

Understandable.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7a/32/7a320b02768c8bec8d1ddccf0f704ac14eebe9c432c1d7f68f12ce486027ecb4.jpg)  

It is certainly arguable that we can't make the above statement (even given some pretty amazing finishes, especially in the U.S. and France) ... but how about this one: "[Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) is the best sideboard card in the format, and maybe ever."

Less skeptical?

Let's take a look at the [U.S. Nationals](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/usnc09) Top 8:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/12/7f/127f2908047a0ce4af48edf4ce11c635b75e86e12bd47847d0b588f290157d29.jpg)  

But the list of decks only tells half the story. The other half is told in the decks' sideboards. It's almost like [Bloodbraid Elf](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodbraid+Elf) in our pre-[Pro Tour–Honolulu](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/pthono09) deck [roundups](/en/articles/top-decks/2009/06-04-five-faeries-doran-and-32-bloodbraid-elves): thirty-two ever-loving copies of [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag). Absolutely astounding!

![Great Sable Stag](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Great+Sable+Stag)

In half of these decks, [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) pushes Five-Color Control past its most dangerous rival, Faeries, in the heads-up matchup. [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) has been described in many ways across multiple contexts, but as a Faeries-hating measure, I think I like Brian Kowal's description the best: "It's basically a [Sulfuric Vortex](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sulfuric+Vortex) that only hits them." This is a somewhat apt description of [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) once it has already hit the table (where it is relentless in its offense against blue-black Faeries players), and it does 50% more damage, most turns, than even the much feared Vortex. But unlike [Sulfuric Vortex](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sulfuric+Vortex), [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) carries with it a degree of absolute certainty. Because it can't be countered, the Stag always hits the table.

But beyond its efficacy against the fearsome Fae, [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) is just *a* drop—great or simply available—for the decks that choose to play it (and given this Top 8, that is "most" decks). Consider the Top 8 matchup between eventual winner Charles Gindy and Standard-dominating Grinder winner Mark Hendrickson: Both players brought their Stags in for Game 2, despite neither player piloting Faeries. In fact, Hendrickson's deck played neither black nor blue!

The performing Five-Color Control decks seem to be currently enjoying an environment in stark contrast to that just a few months ago during the Regional Championships; that is, one that is [Anathemancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anathemancer)-light if not [Anathemancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anathemancer)-free. This is a metagame windfall in much the same way that the presence of [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) almost everywhere is a deterrent against Fae.

In general the Five-Color Control decks seem to have adopted a mix of [Volcanic Fallout](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Fallout) and [Hallowed Burial](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hallowed+Burial) to replace the loss of [Wrath of God](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wrath+of+God). In some cases you will see something like seven copies of these sweepers main deck; essentially all of the decks played redundant copies of sweepers (in some cases spreading out to [Infest](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Infest) and [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout)) in their sideboards.

The jury seems to be in / back / quite stably decided on the matter of [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum); looking at this Top 8, Todd Anderson is the odd mage out with only two copies of the devastating seven-mana sorcery. The other three players all packed three copies main deck. 

![Cruel Ultimatum](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Cruel+Ultimatum)

  

All in all, Five-Color Control seems like one of the best positioned decks for aspiring PTQ winners right now. Last week we decreed White Weenie / Kithkin as a—or the—deck to beat, and Five-Color Control came out to prove us liars. Eventual U.S. National Champion Gindy even joked that [Honor of the Pure](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Honor+of+the+Pure) was one of the best ***Magic** 2010* cards for him (White Weenie being a favorable matchup for Five-Color Control). The control deck itself features lots of land, card drawing from [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter), [Esper Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Esper+Charm), or [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) to help hit land drops (which itself, as a process, is quite happy to be working with 26 lands), and powerful answer spells (most notably [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command)), culminating in [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum), which addresses the most glaring problem with the strategy ... by handing over 5 life.

This deck is essentially this year's take on the [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin) strategy that was so successful at States 2007. Rather than focusing on black for [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin) and blue for most of the rest of its spells, this deck, designed by Conley Woods, is a base-Jund board control deck whose only ties to blue are its [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter)s. 

This kind of a deck can take full advantage of the evoke mechanic; for instance it can be very profitable for the deck to run out [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) on turn two or [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) on turn three rather than waiting until it has sufficient mana for the full cast. With one of the Elementals in the graveyard, [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin) comes online that much more quickly.

![](https://media.magic.wizards.com/image_legacy_migration/mtg/images/daily/td/td49_3Cards1.jpg)  

Almost all of the creatures in the deck have some kind of a profitable two-for-one "enters the battlefield" ability. [Caldera Hellion](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Caldera+Hellion) and [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher) are reminiscent of [Wrath of God](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wrath+of+God), [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) gains life, and [Bloodbraid Elf](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodbraid+Elf) sets up another card; only [Putrid Leech](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Putrid+Leech) lacks that angle, and it is nevertheless a hugely effective offensive weapon in its own right. By choosing specifically these kinds of creatures, the Conley Woods Special improves the potential of future [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin)s, especially when playing them during, say, combat.

One of the most impressive decks on the U.S. Nationals weekend was Mark Hendrickson's Red-White Control. Hendrickson won a grinder on Thursday night to qualify for Nationals and did not drop a Constructed match from the start of the grinder until his unsuccessful battle with eventual U.S. National Champion Gindy.

Like the Spanish Inquisition itself, no one expected an assault—successful or otherwise—from [Armillary Sphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armillary+Sphere) (this deck's "[Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter)") or certainly [Call the Skybreaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Call+the+Skybreaker). More conventional power cards included a variety of planeswalkers and the planeswalker-like [Obelisk of Alara](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Obelisk+of+Alara). The Spanish Inquisition combines the relentless offense of [Goblin Assault](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Assault) with an unending number of creature kill spells. Poison for some decks ... but not really dangerous for a Top 8 full of creature-poor Five-Color Control opponents.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0b/fb/0bfb2c9d133d12030fb508a1e852160448a972e920b61d2831e8faa6ae91bc35.jpg)  

While we call the deck red-white, it is important to note that Hendrickson dipped into all five colors via the sideboard. [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) represented green, [Hindering Light](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hindering+Light) blue, and [Identity Crisis](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Identity+Crisis) black; while his deck lacked a [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum), [Identity Crisis](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Identity+Crisis) is Mark's slightly faster, still tremendously damaging, big sorcery. Stick [Identity Crisis](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Identity+Crisis), and the opponent will probably have to topdeck [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum) to get back in it.

Luca Ravagli's win at [Italy Nationals](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/ravagli-ravages-italy) with yet another Five-Color Control deck did little to shake the apparent dominance of the archetype we described in the U.S.-focused section. Many of the same themes appear in Ravagli's deck, including [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag), a heavy reliance on sweepers, and three main-deck copies of [Cruel Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Ultimatum) (some might "blame" reigning Player of the Year and newly minted [Japan National Champion](/en/articles/event-coverage/2009/07-17-coverage-of-japan-national-championship) Shuhei Nakamura for these developments).

Interestingly, Ravagli tapped into a variety of different threats for his offense. [Baneslayer Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baneslayer+Angel) is the most obvious and powerful for its cost. On the flip-side, [Bogardan Hellkite](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bogardan+Hellkite)—exactly the kind of expensive Dragon [Baneslayer Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baneslayer+Angel) is proof against—shares deck space with the sleek new white weapon.

![Time Warp](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Time+Warp)

  

I bet that sub-head had most of you pegging this section on Hall of Famer Olivier Ruel and his [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve) decks. But nope, we are highlighting the [Time Warp](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Warp)s in certain Faeries Top 8 decks. A big part of the danger that Faeries decks pose comes from the [Time Walk](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Walk)-like power of their [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique)s ... Not only is a [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) a lethal 4/4 flyer for four mana, but it typically robs the opponent of the fourth turn. [Time Warp](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Warp)*literally* robs the opponent of a turn. While it is not 100% proof against [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag), if there is one thing that can help win a race, certainly it must be preventing the opponent from being allowed to participate in said race.

**The World's Most Awkward (And Currently Only) Diabolic Edict**

![Warren Weirding](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Warren+Weirding)

  

Deltour had a very interesting solution to the [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) problem: Goblin sorcery [Warren Weirding](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warren+Weirding).

Unlike targeted removal like [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) or [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt), [Warren Weirding](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warren+Weirding) is a kill spell that Faeries can use to eliminate [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag), without dipping into red. If the [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) is the only sailor on (the other side of the) board, [Warren Weirding](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warren+Weirding) can actually throw it off the prow. Since the goal is to bag an Elk rather than a Goblin, there is typically no downside to this spell (though it certainly can be sticky if the opponent runs out a Sable Stag post-[Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks), or perhaps flips it over with a [Bloodbraid Elf](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodbraid+Elf)). Those situations, should they come up, will require a little more maneuvering, but [Warren Weirding](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warren+Weirding) can still give Faeries a chance against this now most potent of sideboard spoilers.

One of the coolest decks to come out of ***Magic** 2010* was the [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve) / [Open the Vaults](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Open+the+Vaults) combo that Mikko Airaksinen used to take Finland Nationals. Olivier Ruel sleeved up a version and played [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve) to France's Top 8 last week.

This strategy has two things that make it get up and go. The first is the many cantrip artifacts like [Elsewhere Flask](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elsewhere+Flask) and [Kaleidostone](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kaleidostone). These cards draw a card when they come into play, and therefore mitigate the potential loss of card advantage represented by using [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve).

The second half is new floor general [Open the Vaults](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Open+the+Vaults). This [Replenish](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Replenish)-like card returns all the sacrificed [Elsewhere Flask](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elsewhere+Flask)s and [Kaleidostone](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kaleidostone)s to play, filling the [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve) player's hand with more action, and giving him another set of artifacts to sacrifice to [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve). With extra turns from [Time Sieve](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Sieve) and [Time Warp](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Warp), the deck can set up for a kill with [Tezzeret the Seeker](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tezzeret+the+Seeker) or just draw lots of cards to continue to fuel its turn-stealing mission.

The rest of the time the deck operates much like a conventional [Fog](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fog) deck, using [Howling Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Howling+Mine) and [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) to draw sufficient [Fog](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fog) effects to "not die" even as the opponent expends energy, cards, and little bits of his soul (maybe even future years of his life, doubtlessly stress-filled ones) to kill the frustratingly slippery [Fog](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fog) player.

So if there is one thing we can draw from this past week's big tournament results (besides the unbelievably surprising efficacy of [Great Sable Stag](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Great+Sable+Stag) in the U.S.), it is that there is *still* a tremendous number of viable decks that you can choose to try to win a Standard PTQ. If it is true that Five-Color Control is a far-and-away winner in the metagame, you may want to try one of the [Blightning](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blightning) Beatdown decks that did well in France. [Anathemancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anathemancer) is a solid main-deck card in a default Five-Color metagame, and [Banefire](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Banefire) (as seen in Antoine Menard's deck) tears right through the Five-Color Control defense, along with the hard-to-deal-with and positively (and we mean *positively*) confusing [Demigod of Revenge](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Demigod+of+Revenge).

You can find the rest of the [France Top 8 decks](https://web.archive.org/web/20090730000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/mongilardi-leads-france) in the coverage.
