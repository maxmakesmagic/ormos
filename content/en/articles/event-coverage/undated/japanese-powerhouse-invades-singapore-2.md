**Itaru Ishida Is the Grand Prix Singapore Champion!**

Itaru Ishida is one the most long-tenured Pros in the Japanese **Magic** community, and with his Top 8 appearance he pulled into a tie with none other than Kai Budde for the second-most Top 8 appearances in Grand Prix competition. The taciturn Ishida dubbed his deck the Ink-Eyes Special and he piloted it through a pair of players with Temporary Solution, with one Rock player squeezed in between, for the Grand Prix-Singapore title.

This was the largest Southeast Asian Grand Prix in history and much of that was due to an unusually large Japanese presence, with 35 players making the seven-hour journey. There were more Japanese players than Singaporeans on Day 2, and four of them (three if you don't count Australian ex-pat Oliver Oks) reached the elimination rounds.

Congratulations to everyone who did well this weekend and condolences to Sukhum Kiwanont of Thailand who could not quite get over a wall of three straight Japanese opponents and just missed the cut in ninth place.

## Sunday, March 20, 7:08 p.m. - Quarterfinal Round-up

**Itaru Ishida vs. Oliver Oks**

Ishida took some early beats from Oks' [Silver Knight](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silver+Knight) before he slowed things down with his [Psychatog](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Psychatog). Oks played a [Gilded Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gilded+Drake) to steal the Tog and got in seven more points on his next attack but Ishida had access to [Echoing Truth](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Echoing+Truth) and returned the Tog to its rightful owner. He was able to get it to lethal range in pretty short order.

![Echoing Truth](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Echoing+Truth)

In the second game, Oks drew land where Ishida was drawing [Accumulated Knowledge](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Accumulated+Knowledge)s. Ishida advanced to the semifinals taking the match two games to none.

**Sun Kit Yeung vs. Dennis Yuliadinata**

Sun's maindeck Swords and sideboarded [Engineered Plague](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Engineered+Plague)s made this a completely lopsided affair with Sun taking the match in two blisteringly fast games. ## Sunday, March 20, 7:22 p.m. - Semifinals: Itaru Ishida vs. Sun Kit Yeung

Semifinals: Itaru Ishida vs. Sun Kit Yeung

Sun came out of the gates fast in Game 1 with [Llanowar Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Llanowar+Elves) and a [Sword of Fire and Ice](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sword+of+Fire+and+Ice). He equipped and elf and began beating down for five -- and a card -- per turn. Ishida bided his time with card drawing spells and working around [Cabal Therapy](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cabal+Therapy) until he found Tog. Tog could not block the elf but Ishida cast a flurry of [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush)es leaving himself only one [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island) in play. He added up all the damage he could manage with his flying Tog and it was two points shy of being lethal.

"Good game," conceded Ishida.

Ishida mulliganed to open Game 2 and was fine with the next six. Sun tried to slow down his foe's mana with a pair of Ports while beating with an elf. On turn five, Ishida floated one mana and drew a card. He burned for one and passed the turn after playing a land. Sun prompted Ishida to break a Delta for a [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) by Porting it EOT.

Ishida cast FoF at the end of Sun's turn and took a three card pile with land, [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell), and [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) over [Intuition](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Intuition) and [Cunning Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cunning+Wish). Sun kept Ishida from black mana to cast his Tog. He tutored up a [Cabal Therapy](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cabal+Therapy) and cast it naming [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell). He saw Meloku, the desired counter, [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) x 2, [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush), and Tog. He played a land and cast [Troll Ascetic](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Troll+Ascetic). Ishida [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak)ed it twice and Sun flashed back Therapy naming Tog.

![Psychatog](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Psychatog)

Ishida untapped and played his Meloku. Sun played [Braids](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Braids) and the legend battle was on. Ishida made two spirit tokens EOT and untapped to swing for four. He killed one of Sun's permanents by putting engineering a plague on elves. Sun continued to port Ishida's dwindling mana and the Japanese player kept making additional tokens in response to being ported.

Sun drew a Sword and equipped [Braids](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Braids) and attacked. He used the ability from the Sword to shoot down a token. Ishida was down to one land and five guys and would kill Sun on the next turn. Sun sighed as he drew his card -- a second Sword. He equipped his [Braids](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Braids) and attacked only to come up two points short.

If he had done the two to Ishida last turn he would have won.

Sun mulliganed a perfectly reasonable hand if you didn't account for the fact that it had no [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest)s. He liked his next six and led with an elf. Turn two was [Cabal Therapy](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cabal+Therapy) on [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell). He hit and saw two [Cunning Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cunning+Wish), three land and [Deep Analysis](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deep+Analysis) as well.

Sun took the Wishes. His [Eternal Witness](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eternal+Witness) was [Counter](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counter)ed on the next turn. Ishida tapped out to [Deep Analysis](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deep+Analysis) and Sun had a window to cast [Braids](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Braids). [Ravenous Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravenous+Baloth) was countered a turn later. Ishida had a pair of [Deep Analysis](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deep+Analysis) keeping his at four lands while Sun was pressing up against his own [Braids](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Braids) lock.

The Rock player tapped out to cast [Troll Ascetic](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Troll+Ascetic) and then sacrificed his elf -- he had two lands left -- to name Tog. He whiffed. Ishida was locked at four lands and could not get out from under to cast Meloku.

Ishida [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm)ed in response to [Cabal Therapy](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cabal+Therapy) he tucked two Togs on top for safekeeping. ## Sunday, March 20, 8:18 p.m. - Finals: Shih Chien Chan vs. Itaru Ishida

The largest GP ever held in Southeast Asia came down to a contest between Shih Chien Chan of Malaysia with Temporary Solution and Itaru Ishida of Japan with tried and true [Psychatog](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Psychatog). It was only fitting that the young upstart was playing one of the newest decks on the Extended block while the seasoned veteran with fourteen Grand Prix Top 8s and one Pro Tour Top 8 on his lengthy resume should be playing one of the format's old standards.

Chan led off with [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) -- off [Adarkar Wastes](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adarkar+Wastes) -- for [Ancient Den](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancient+Den). That allowed for a turn two [Meddling Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Meddling+Mage) naming [Cunning Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cunning+Wish).

As usual, Ishida gave away nothing by his expression. He merely played lands, did not comment or even show a flicker of joy as his opponent hiccupped on his third land drop. Chan plugged away with the Mage and Ishida [Intuition](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Intuition)ed for three [Accumulated Knowledge](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Accumulated+Knowledge). Chan missed another land drop and Ishida drew three cards. He played his fifth land and calmly announced "discard" as he threw away two lands.

Chan tried a morph and Ishida paid full price to [Foil](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foil) it. Ishida seemed to be in control but his life was below the halfway mark from the Mage. He played his sixth land and had to return two of them for [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush) to find an answer for another morph. He then had to [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm) but eventually found a [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell). He [Intuition](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Intuition)ed at the end of the turn for three copies of Tog and played the toothy threat when he untapped leaving the always intimidating UU available.

Chan took a point from a painland and cast his second [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) of the game to find a [Parallax Wave](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Parallax+Wave). Ishida tapped his two blue and countered the enchantment with a pair of [Force Spike](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+Spike)s. Ishida untapped and discarded [Wonder](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wonder) to his Tog and proceeded to use the alternate casting cost to play a pair of [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush)es and put six extra cards in his hand.

I have to say it was one of the few times that Ishida showed some emotion in a feature match. He looked positively gleeful -- if you can imagine that -- as he picked up his lands and ripped four cards from the top of his deck. It almost seemed like he was having fun.

Both players kept their hands for Game 2. Chan tried to craft his hand with the Vintage play of [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm) and [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand). He tucked two cards and top and quickly shuffled them down into his deck to find a [Plains](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plains). His [Meddling Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Meddling+Mage) was [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell)ed. He attempted another on the next turn and Ishida [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm)ed. The Mage resolved and Chan named [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) but he missed his fourth land drop.

Ishida just developed his mana. When Chan --still missing a fourth land -- attempted to play a Bouncer, Ishida broke a Delta and [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm)ed again to find a [Counterspell](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell) for the spellshaper. Ishida untapped and played [Psychatog](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Psychatog).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/50/d9/50d9ca0287afdd23be2843f248b6012c7300b98b96068c8b35f29b5358cffa4b.jpg)*Itaru Ishida is going to Disneyland!*

Chan drew his card and slowly panned through them hoping to find a fourth land. He did not and offered a morph for Itaru to [Force Spike](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+Spike). Chan gestured for Ishida to get along with it and kill him but the math was not right. Ishida [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm)ed again and sent his Tog into battle. [Meddling Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Meddling+Mage) stepped in the way and Ishida pumped from his bin. Another Tog joined the team.

Still no fourth land for Chan. He braced for lethal from Ishida but took only two. He cast [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) EOT -- "Give me a white source." He searched up his [Ancient Den](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancient+Den) and tried a [Parallax Tide](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Parallax+Tide) which was [Foil](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foil)ed. He took two more from the looming threat of the Togs.

Another Tide was [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak)ed and tie was running out for the Malaysian player as the Tog math crept closer and closer to being in the black. Ishida did some quick tallies and pushed them into battle. He cast [Intuition](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Intuition) for [Cunning Wish](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cunning+Wish) and then Wished for a [Gush](https://web.archive.org/web/20210502063103/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gush). He paid the ACC and began dumping his hand. Chan double checked the math on the off chance that Ishida was wrong but the crowd had already double checked Ishida's sums and they poured past the makeshift barriers to rain their congratulations down on the popular player's back.

Itaru Ishida is the Grand Prix Singapore Champion!
