## Grand Prix Hannover 2009

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1a/c2/1ac20f20e1bc6039df7dcf6ce4e8ecee218262dc5abeb95f408e931d2248433a.jpg)

Congratulations to Lino Burgold the Grand Prix Hannover Champion! Local boy Burgold was the only German player in the top eight, but with his Elves deck he pulled off a trick reminiscent of Pro Tour Berlin, in defeating all comers on his way to the top. Kenny Oberg was on for a repeat of Berlin also, having demolished the Swiss portion of the competition, but unfortunately for him could do no better than he did there, losing out in the Quarter Finals. The final match saw the raider from America Gaudenis Vidugiris taken down in a quick pair of games.

While Elves hasn't performed massively well in many PTQs, it turns out that on any given Sunday, with the right pilot it can still be a force to be reckoned with. Thanks for reading the coverage, and congratulations again to Lino Burgold, this weekend's champion!

## Quarterfinals – Kenny Öberg vs. Karim Bauer

*by Tobias Henke*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/cb/74cb1fd2b159cf43f456a3f27c54e1c0ec145b39822842656e81be1dd2bc5942.jpg)

We're off to single elimination now. Two players enter the fray, one leaves. (Actually, two leave, but only one *advances*.) The combatants for this quarter final are Kenny Öberg from Sweden, playing his trademark Tezzerator deck, matched-up against Austrian Karim Bauer, running Loam Rock.

Game 1 began with a mulligan from Öberg and a [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) from Bauer. That revealed [Tezzeret the Seeker](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tezzeret+the+Seeker), a land, [Chrome Mox](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chrome+Mox), [Trinket Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trinket+Mage), and [Thirst for Knowledge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thirst+for+Knowledge). The Mage went to the bin.

Öberg imprinted Tezzeret into the Mox to have turn-two [Thirst for Knowledge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thirst+for+Knowledge) available. When Bauer cast [Raven's Crime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Raven%27s+Crime) on his next turn, Öberg responded with the card drawing as expected; unexpectedly though he had to discard two cards! [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare) and [Vendilion Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vendilion+Clique) went to the graveyard and were joined by yet another Tezzeret as well as [Academy Ruins](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Academy+Ruins), when Bauer retraced the discard spell. No cards left in hand on turn two! Things really were looking dim for Öberg.

He did have another [Academy Ruins](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Academy+Ruins), while Bauer summoned [Knight of the Reliquary](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+the+Reliquary) for some major beatings. One turn (of the Knight to the right) later Öberg went to 10.

Öberg passed without play and went to four. Bauer had [Life from the Loam](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Life+from+the+Loam) and retraced [Raven's Crime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Raven%27s+Crime) once more, to which Öberg responded with [Condescend](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Condescend), just for the scrying. Öberg kept [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) and [Thirst for Knowledge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thirst+for+Knowledge) on top of his library. The Command bought another turn, and [Thirst for Knowledge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thirst+for+Knowledge) might have found [Ensnaring Bridge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ensnaring+Bridge). Apart from that there was not much he could do... and as his deck did not comply with these rather hefty requirements, Öberg quickly shuffled up for Game 2.

**Kenny Öberg 0 – 1 Karim Bauer**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ee/16/ee16603e75fdffa978831da3186d37589b776c4ca033743fe351511b80968cd6.jpg)

The second game started much worse for the Austrian player, who didn't have a play on turn one, while Öberg was blazingly fast, once again thanks to [Chrome Mox](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chrome+Mox). In Bauer's second turn's draw step, [Vendilion Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vendilion+Clique) came down and took [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) out of a hand that contained another copy of [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks), [Pithing Needle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pithing+Needle), [Knight of the Reliquary](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+the+Reliquary) and lands...

Bauer put down [Pithing Needle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pithing+Needle) on [Tormod's Crypt](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tormod%27s+Crypt) and passed right back, Öberg attacked and seemed considerably more happy to pass *his* turn. [Knight of the Reliquary](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+the+Reliquary) entered play on Bauer's side uncontested, while Öberg's second attack brought Bauer to 10. The Knight grew to 5/5 with the help of another [Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath), but that meant Bauer would be in danger of dying to Öberg's 3/1 attacker within three turns. With both players apparently sporting a rather weak draw, the race was on.

The race turned into an increasingly one-sided affair, when Bauer added another Knight to his board. Öberg now stayed behind with his faerie and made the rather unusual choice of casting [Chalice of the Void](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chalice+of+the+Void) for three to at least keep the remaining [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) in Bauer's hand at bay...

Öberg accumulated more and more lands. He put down Future Sight in a last-ditch effort, slowly turned over the top card of his library... It didn't provide an answer for the imminent 5/5 beats, and he extended his hand in concession.

**Kenny Öberg 0 – 2 Karim Bauer**

  

## Quarterfinals – Gaudenis Vidugiris vs Helmut Summersberger

*by Tim Willoughby*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a4/27/a427cf5a3627f45fb9f53da97562be296a6adf060e5bef51527632a746832539.jpg)

Coming into this top 8, Gaudenis Vidugiris and Helmut Summersberger are both big names to contend with (and not just from a typing standpoint). Vidugiris is a Madison player (albeit originally from Lithuania) who is on a big spring break trip that will see him in Singapore next weekend. He finished second in GP Indianapolis and would dearly love to go one better this weekend. Helmut Summersberger is the perennial Austrian success at GPs and Pro Tours all over the world. Each came to the table with their own blue control deck using [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf)s, but it would be a slightly unusual mirror, as Summersberger leans on [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) and [Riptide Laboratory](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Riptide+Laboratory), making him more classically Faeries, while Vidugiris has the [Vedalken Shackles](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vedalken+Shackles) and [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command)s of some level or other of Next Level Blue.

Both players kept their openers, and each had an [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision) to suspend on turn one, setting up for a real battle around 4 turns later, when the big card drawing would finally make it as far as the stack. Summersberger had a turn two [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), but the powerful two drop was hit by a [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), keeping the board clear. Vidugiris had no play for his turn beyond a tapped [Breeding Pool](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Breeding+Pool), and had to watch on as [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte) hit play for Summersberger the very next turn.

Gaudenis suspended a second [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision), played a land and passed. With Gaudenis' first [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision) ready to happen the next turn, Summersberger didn't play anything for his turn, preferring to keep mana up to fight over the spell. A [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) was his answer, and Vidugiris, after a little thought, played [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) as a [Dismiss](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dismiss) on the faerie. This did tap him out, but meant that his card drawing worked. There was no fight possible now for Summersberger's [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision), meaning that for the time being at least, no player had a big advantage in cards beyond the extra one that Vidugiris had netted from his Command.

Summersberger didn't have big spells for his turn, but did animate a [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault), give it [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte), and swing. With counters on his [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Umezawa%25E2%2580%2599s%2BJitte), but only [Breeding Pool](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Breeding+Pool) untapped, Summersberger passed. A [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) came from Vidugiris, which met a [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare). After some thought, Vidugiris played [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) to force through his [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) and bounce [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte). Summersberger cashed in the counters on his [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Umezawa%25E2%2580%2599s%2BJitte) for some life, and picked up the sword.

![Engineered Explosives](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Engineered+Explosives)

The very next turn Summersberger used [Engineered Explosives](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Engineered+Explosives) to kill [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf), before replaying his legendary equipment. The game looked tight at this point, but had every likelihood of swinging in Vidugiris' favour if he could resolve a second [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision). The Austrian's plan against this? He suspended what was the fourth [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision) of the match. When Gaudenis' second copy came off suspend, he had a [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite), which prompted another [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) to counter [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) and draw a card.

Drawing 5 cards in a single turn tends to prove decisive in control mirrors, and it seemed that Vidugiris was happy to go for some more, casting first [Vedalken Shackles](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vedalken+Shackles), before suspending another draw 3. Summersberger had the end of turn [Repeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Repeal) for the artefact, but still had cause to worry. He decided to be the beatdown on his turn, running in for a second time with [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) swinging a [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Umezawa%25E2%2580%2599s%2BJitte). He then suspended another [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision), making the count 6 of them in Game 1 thus far.

Gaudenis on his turn played that [Vedalken Shackles](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vedalken+Shackles) again. As many blue mirrors can be, the match had become something of a staring contest. Over Gaudenis' shoulder, Sam Black and Brian Kowal were smiling. It soon became clear why, as Vidugiris cast [Meloku the Clouded Mirror](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Meloku%2Bthe%2BClouded%2BMirror). This was a threat which the group of Madison players had identified as one that was hard on many faeries decks if it resolved. A [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) came from Summersberger, but [Spell Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Snare) made sure it would do just that. Even with [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte) holding a pair of counters, that [Meloku the Clouded Mirror](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Meloku%2Bthe%2BClouded%2BMirror) seemed capable of making the entire pace of the match change.

[Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) picked up an [Umezawa's Jitte](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umezawa%27s+Jitte), but Gaudenis just tapped [Vedalken Shackles](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vedalken+Shackles), forcing Summersberger to bounce his own land with [Riptide Laboratory](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Riptide+Laboratory). Gaudenis made one illusion token, such that on his turn he could attack in the air for three. On Helmut's turn, he resolved [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision)s, meaning that there were only two left on suspend, one for each player, each only a turn away.

At the end of that turn, Gaudenis tried for a [Vendilion Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vendilion+Clique). This met the first [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) from Summersberger of the match. Vidugiris turned three more lands into illusions at the end of turn, and in his upkeep still had the mana ready to be able to [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) the [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) that came from Summersberger in his upkeep when [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision) came off suspend. Attacks in the air from Vidugiris' tokens took Summersberger to 10. He did get to resolve [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision) without incident, but ultimately drew nothing of value off it, and scooped up his cards.

Helmut Summersberger 0 – 1 Gaudenis Vidugiris

In the second game Summersberger would be on the play, and looked carefully at his hand before keeping. Vidugiris was certainly not one to slowroll the mulligan, and started shuffling up immediately, having deemed his seven not good enough.

![Ancestral Vision](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ancestral+Vision)

For Game 2 neither player had an [Ancestral Vision](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancestral+Vision), but more than that, Summersberger seemed inclined to be the beatdown, leading with a [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) to give himself the potential to start attacking from turn two. He played a [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808205057/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) on the second turn, and got to crack in for one with it, as there was no response from Vidugiris. This control match didn't feel quite the same as the first. There wasn't card drawing, just 1/2 creatures for 2, and a variety of fetchlands. Karim Bauer 0 – 1 Lino Burgold

As it turns out, game 2 never quite got started. Each player sideboarded, shuffled and presented, at which point Karim Bauer was issued a game loss for insufficient randomization. Specifically, Bauer was witnessed shuffling his deck face up / towards him as the only shuffling operations he performed aside from performing pile shuffles.

The DCI does not view shuffling face up as sufficient randomization, as there will be some known information about the location of a card or cards in the deck. Pile shuffling does not count as randomization, as it is changing the order of the deck in a non-random fashion, such that the position of an individual card could at the end be known if it were known at the start. Put the two together, and in total neither of Bauer's shuffling operations were creating an appropriately randomized deck. The game loss did not carry with it any suggestion of cheating, but Head Judge Seamus Campbell was quick to point out that these bad shuffling habits can only ever lead to game losses and should be avoided at all costs.

Lino Burgold wins an unorthodox 2 – 0.

Life from the Loam was once again stopped, this time with Flashfreeze, but that left Meloku unprotected. The dangerous legend promptly caught another Path to Exile, leaving one lonely token behind.

And then there came Relic of Progenitus. This left Kraft with two cards in hand and nothing else. No graveyard meant no Life from the Loam, no cycling, no Raven’s Crime, no business, while Vidugiri’s still had a full grip.

Kraft never really got back into the game. Vedalken Shackles and Vendilion Clique took care of the technicalities, but Relic of Progenitus was the back-breaker this time.

**Gaudenis Vidugiris 2 – 1 Lukas Kraft**

## Finals: Gaudenis Vidugiris vs Lino Burgold

*by Tim Willoughby*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3e/25/3e25d32a75da50f37577218a99493355b95dc30c270b36283155e713b366673f.jpg)

Elves feel very at home in Germany it would appear. The finals of Berlin were all Elves, and while they have certainly seen a drop in popularity in the PTQ season as plans have been made against them, they have made it back to a finals here, under the command of Lino Burgold. If they are to have a lone opponent though, they could likely wish for easier than Gaudenis Vidugiris. The Madison Wisconsin resident is on a big Spring Break trip around the world with Sam Black and Brian Kowal, and has been tearing things up with his Some Level of Blue or Other deck. Next weekend he’ll be in Singapore, quite possibly again casting Meloku, the Clouded Mirror with a big smile on his face. It has worked for him so far in Europe.

Gaudenis was on the play, and happy to keep a busty hand that allowed him to suspend an Ancestral vision on turn one. He saw Llanowar Elves and little more from his opponent from the first turn, and cast a Tarmogoyf on turn two. Against Elves, his start wasn’t quick. This was potentially a concern, but if Burgold stumbled, there was a threat plus card drawing to punish him with.

Burgold played a Morph on turn two, while Vidugiris just suspended a second Ancestral Visions. The morph from Burgold was Birchlore Rangers, but while it sat there face down as a 2/2, it was holding off Tarmogoyf – no mean feat. Burgold played Nettle Sentinel and passed. Vidugiris didn’t even do that much. He let his suspend spells tick down, played a land and said go.

Burgold started doing some math. Vidugiris face was a closed book, and the German player had to work out how best to try going for it. Could he wait? He left things until Vidugiris’ upkeep to play Chord of Calling for three. Vidugiris let this happen. Mirror Entity came down, suggesting that Burgold was looking to eke out a beatdown win rather than anything more spectacular.

Vidugiris resolved his first Ancestral Visions. He had a little think, played a land and passed the turn. Burgold attacked in with Mirror Entity, Nettle Sentinel and the morph. Vidugiris flashed in Vendilion Clique, seeing a pair of Summoner’s Pact, and Heritage Druid. He took the druid. With two blockers now, Vidugiris elected to block only the Mirror Entity, using the Clique. Burgold made his team into 6/6’s and knocked Vidugiris down to 3. Things looked very bad for the American, but he stayed calm. He watched as Burgold played Summoner’s Pact for Wirewood Symbiote, waiting for the moment it was played to cast Mana Leak.

Throughout Burgold’s declare attackers, Vidugiris was a wall of calm, in spite of a dangerous team coming his way. Burgold seemed almost surprised when the attack worked.

Gaudenis Vidugiris 0 – 1 Lino Burgold

![](https://i.ormos.uk/aa/aa/aaaad2f55964f0dbf32cc861dd77a6ebd2784a8a16247026ca7970ce349ea5d6.jpg)

The Elves were now a single game from taking their master and commander to a home turf victory against the invading American/Lithuanian force of Gaudenis Vidugiris.

Vidugiris took a mulligan. His six card hand had no coloured mana sources, and he was forced to take another. The five only had one land in, but at least it was an Island. Gaudenis played it and looked on as Burgold played a Llanowar Elves. He had no land on the second turn, and played an Engineered Explosives for one. A Thoughtseize from Burgold compounded Vidugiris problems, taking Flash Freeze from a hand that only had a pair of Cryptic Command and a second Engineered Explosives as other options.

Burgold’s start wasn’t the one to be playing against with five cards. He had a Wirewood Symbiote and Wirewood Hivemaster to quickly build an attack force with, before Gaudenis could even hit his second land.

When Vidugiris did draw his land, a Steam Vents, he had a little think. He popped his explosives to clear things a little, though he couldn’t stop Wirewood Symbiote from bouncing one of the Llanowar Elves that was destined for the graveyard. Burgold had a Glimpse of Nature that let him rebuild well. It seemed likely that there was no way for Vidugiris to get back into the game, and this was only compounded by Burgold’s Chord of Calling for Mirror Entity.

Even casting his Engineered Explosives for zero, and killing a swarm of insect tokens, Vidugiris would be dead to another mana source to pump the remainder of Burgold’s team. When that mana source came the following turn, Gaudenis scooped up his (two) cards.
