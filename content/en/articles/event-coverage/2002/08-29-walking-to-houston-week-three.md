The third week of PTQ season rolled around and I had only played in one PTQ and a Grand Prix Trial to that point. On the plus side the u-g threshold deck that I designed with the help of Mike Flores had been doing exceptionally well. I had yet to lose before the fifth round of a tournament with it and both Matt Boccio and Justin Polin had put up back-to-back Top 8s the previous weekend.

On the minus side, everyone seemed to be playing the deck. If they weren't playing it they were playing some other version of u-g. In fact it seemed as if there were as many builds for u-g as there were shrimp dishes for Bubba in the movie "Forest Gump" (u-g Scampi...u-g Cocktail...Coconut u-g...) and I was getting tired of playing the funhouse mirror matches.

I had been hoping that Worlds would present a new deck to shake up the field, but all it seemed to accomplish was the introduction of yet more u-g recipes. I considered playing the u-w Walamies deck for a while but actually did not have the Spurnmage Advocates. Instead, I turned to the winning deck from the previous weekend's Harrisburg PTQ ...

I altered the land base some and tried to find a sideboard other than the transformative version Jordan played in Harrisburg.

The tournament was at Neutral Ground this week so I did not have to concern myself with getting a ride beyond making sure I caught an early bus into the city. The only problem with that strategy is if you play someone playing a deck other than those two - like someone else playing Solitary Confinement. I was excited about having Cleansing Meditation in my board for the mirror match (I was being too cute. Tony won the roll and elected to play first. He sent back the fist seven cards because there was but one forest among them. The next six cards contained 5 land and an Arrogant Wurm and Tony kept it, hoping to draw a madness enabler within the first few turns. Chunk seemed content with his cards and the match got underway. The Shark drew and played an Aquamoeba on the second turn which Chunk answered with a Wild Mongrel.

Aquamoeba attacked into the Mongrel on the next turn. Chunk blocked and Tony flipped the 'Moeba and cast his 4/4 trampler with madness. Inexplicably, Chunk let his Wild Mongrel die in battle despite a hand full of cards. He untapped and cast a Catalyst Stone.

Tony attacked his opponent to 16 and added a Wild Mongrel and a Centaur Garden to his side of the board. Mike missed his fourth land drop but made his second Mongrel drop of the game. On Tony's next turn after Studying a Basking Rootwalla and another Arrogant Wurm into play, the two Mongrel's got into a fight. Their controllers did as well. Mike went on the offensive starting on his next turn and Tony succumbed to the larger Wurms.
