Hello and welcome to another edition of Building on a Budget. For [the last](/en/articles/buildingonabudget/2009/07-08-warp-world-revolution) [two weeks](/en/articles/buildingonabudget/2009/07-15-warp-world-2010) I've tried to help you find the best way to warp the world. This week is Favorite Flavor Week here on **magicthegathering.com**. I've always been a huge fan of the tribal mechanic. Long before *Lorwyn* was a whisper I had been playing Elves, Goblins, Slivers, and Merfolk on kitchen tables with my friends. I always loved the concept of tribal mechanics and thought they had excellent flavor. Elves work well with other Elves and Goblins work well with other Goblins. It makes sense on a very intuitive level. *Lorwyn* brought tribal mechanics to a whole new level, but recently there has been a lot of talk about what tribes might survive after that block rotates out in September. A quick look at the ***Magic** 2010* spoiler will point you in the direction of Soldiers.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a6/55/a65534d0f513b6639a1c75d9ba5345ef608a5322bb43dcc87886764df5a054c3.jpg)  

A lot of my readers opened up a [Captain of the Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Captain+of+the+Watch) and got really excited about the new ***Magic** 2010* Soldier lord. I can see what everyone is so excited about. I actually lost a match at the Prerelease when my opponent played a turn-five [Captain of the Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Captain+of+the+Watch) (thanks to rampant growth) followed by a turn six [Captain of the Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Captain+of+the+Watch). It's clearly a very powerful card, but how can we best abuse it?

When I first saw [Captain of the Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Captain+of+the+Watch) I looked at its creature type, which seemed like it would be extremely relevant so long as *Lorwyn* block was Standard legal. Soldier seems like a pretty good creature type when you realize that [Preeminent Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preeminent+Captain) can just put the Captain into play for free.

![Preeminent Captain](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Preeminent+Captain)

  

Many people would probably want to fill this kind of deck up with [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession)s and [Honor of the Pure](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Honor+of+the+Pure), but I want to stick with the Soldier theme and see how powerful the deck can be. I made a few lists in my notebook and ultimately decided that this seemed like a good route to go with the deck.

I'll explain each card's purpose in the deck.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/27/4f/274febc170ce9c6f47f882d8d79ac9242adb3cb1c03362bb4cc29a60fa68c9ea.jpg)  

**[Captain of the Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Captain+of+the+Watch):** Many of you were so excited about this card that you asked me to make a deck with it. I've been extremely happy with this card in testing and I'm confidant that four of this belong in the deck.

**[Preeminent Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preeminent+Captain):** This is the sleeper here. You might not be overwhelmed with this card's power level in the abstract, but in this deck [Preeminent Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preeminent+Captain) is just bonkers. The tempo advantage you get when you put a [Captain of the Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Captain+of+the+Watch), [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity), or [Veteran Armorsmith](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Veteran+Armorsmith) onto the battlefield is pretty absurd.

**[Elite Vanguard](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elite+Vanguard):** [Savannah Lions](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savannah+Lions) has always been good enough. [Elite Vanguard](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elite+Vanguard) one-ups its feline predecessor with an awesome creature type.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cc/2e/cc2ef2d6d26901e56d509ce15e8bc1a67f3cafa62a81534326f64b6151fd19fd.jpg)  

**[Veteran Armorsmith](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Veteran+Armorsmith):** An obvious four-of in a Soldier-themed deck. A 2/3 for two mana is a fine deal no matter how you cut it. The fact that it makes most of your team survive a [Volcanic Fallout](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Fallout) or [Infest](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Infest) is also extremely relevant. 

**[Veteran Swordsmith](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Veteran+Swordsmith):** Another obvious inclusion. This type of card really shines against control decks that you want to finish off very quickly.

**[Ballyrush Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ballyrush+Banneret):** When I typed "[Soldier](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?type=+%5Bsoldier%5D%7C%7Csubtype=+%5Bsoldier%5D&color=+%5BW%5D&format=%5B%22Standard%22%5D)" into [Gatherer](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Default.aspx) I was delighted to see one of my favorite fifth to ninth picks from *Morningtide* draft. [Ballyrush Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ballyrush+Banneret) lets this deck get some pretty absurd draws. I like cards like this because they let me keep more hands than usual. If I'm on the play and have a hand with two cards that cost two mana, one card that costs three mana, two cards that cost six mana, and two lands I'm likely to mulligan to a hand that actually has a chance to play its spells. If one of those two drops is a [Ballyrush Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ballyrush+Banneret) I can happily keep a hand like that and take the gambit that my opponent will kill the first two-drop I play and not have a high density of removal in hand. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6d/d3/6dd34c957ac319840fc0cd8ab5249a269e874c06e15ab0bec0b46ea9f72127b6.jpg)  

**[Path to Exile](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Path+to+Exile):** There should be little question by now as to whether this card deserves a slot in white decks. It's extremely important that our deck has cheap answers to [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique), I don't want my opponent to fearlessly timewalk us with four mana 4/4s.

**[Ethersworn Canonist](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ethersworn+Canonist):** This is a nice piece of tech that was brought to my attention by Cedric Phillips. The Elf combo deck has been popping up a lot lately and this seems like a really solid answer. Most of the Elf decks won't be packing any main-deck removal so you should be able to get some free Game 1 wins with your Canonist. You can do a lot more with one spell a turn than they can thanks to [Preeminent Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preeminent+Captain).

**[Unmake](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unmake):** Another solid removal spell. I really want more than four spot removal spells so we can clear the way for our Peeminent Captain. I also want to make holes in the opponent's team to attack [Ajani Goldmane](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Goldmane). We don't have the luxury of playing Ajani ourselves (because of cost restrictions), and that gives other white decks a pretty big edge in the mirror. 

**[Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity):** This is the abosolute stones here. We may not have [Ajani Goldmane](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Goldmane), but [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) actually seems better a lot of the time in this deck. If we put a [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) onto the battlefield off our [Preeminent Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20090722000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preeminent+Captain) and pump up the team it will probably result in a lot of turn-four wins.
