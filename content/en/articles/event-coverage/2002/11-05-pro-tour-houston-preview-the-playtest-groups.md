Previews for Pro Tours can come in many forms. Some people preview formats and predict the prevailing metagame. Some preview players and predict top finishers. I am more interested in focusing more on the socio-political aspect of Constructed Pro Tours.

Test groups, while not strictly as politically cutthroat as team can be, have many facets that can turn friends against one another. This doesn't happen nearly as much as it used to. Most players have grown accustomed to their friends withholding information for Constructed events. These days testing groups are formed as much on the basis of friendship as anything else, so again the withholding of info has become slightly less of an issue.

There have been some interesting groups formed this year. There are a lot of the old staples you would expect. There are also some that seemingly came out of nowhere with no discernable connection other internet chatting. I apologize if I leave people out of these groups, but some are so large it is hard to list everyone. 

### The Germans +

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNOR01/662.jpg)

Kai Budde, Patrick Mello, Dirk Baberowski, Marco Blume, Gary Wise, John Larkin. The names on this team would point to extremely solid finishes. John Larkin is a Constructed specialist, and Gary Wise has become an unstoppable force in Constructed since his move to England. Gary is back in North America now, but the odds of his Constructed game slipping that much in such a short period of time are slim. It is not a surprise that the recently dominating Germans added these two players to their roster.

These guys are known for finding degenerate combos and almost always end up with close to the best deck. However those were in the days of the English/German alliance. With Gary the only remaining player from that contingent, can they keep up their Constructed run?

There were talks of Eric Taylor and Pat Chapin joining this group late in the season, but as of this article that union has not solidified.

### Punisher

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTOSA02/856.jpg)

Jens Thoren, Anton Jonsson, Tomi Walamies, Johan Sadeghpour, Mattias Kettil, Mattias Jorstedt. This is another group that has recently been flexing its Constructed muscles. Last year, Tomi took his elephant control deck all the way to the finals, where he barely lost to Kai. They were also able to quiet many of the critics by developing an OBC deck containing neither green nor black that was still highly competitive.

These guys are very close geographically and as such will have very productive testing sessions. Expect good things from a team that has 2 Invitationalists and a handful of Pro Tour Top 8s.

### Your Move Games

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2c/41/2c4153462c0c1df20291d12d90c45aa7dce81d8eca8efb3aa575ad902e2425df.jpg)

Dave Humphreys, Rob Dougherty, Justin Gary, Zvi Moshowitz, Darwin Kastle, Alex Shvartsman, Matt Rubin. These guys have busted Extended so many times it is absurd. Alex and Zvi were added to the roster for Worlds and apparently YMG liked what they saw. They lack the depth they had before, which can certainly hurt in an environment such as Extended. I wouldn't count out Rob Dougherty though. He has come up with some interesting and powerful decks in wide open formats like this one with few test partners. Even in Extended, you don't need an army of testers.

This is another group that had expressed interest in joining with Taylor and Chapin, but that deal as well fizzled.

### TOGIT, CMU

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/STC01/976.jpg)

Osyp Lebedowicz, Mike Turian, Brian Davis, Eugene Harvey, Gerrard Fabiano, Antonino Derosa, Ben Stark, Peter Szigeti, Ken Krouner, Ed Fear, Adam Fischer, Craig Kremples, Adam Horvath, Patrick Sullivan, Franck Canu, Christophe Haim, Tom Martel, Andrew Cuneo, Carlos Chada, Jeff Magid, Jon Sonne, Andy Stokinger, Chris Leather, Brett Shears. There may well be more, but I am not sure. This group may seem unwieldy, but don't take it at face value. When the season started, TOGIT and CMU intended to have the same agreement that we had for Worlds and Nationals. We had had some success testing together. However the group kept growing and growing and just got out of hand. So early on we decided to split up. TOGIT went their way and we went ours. 

Before Philly, we realized that this format was a lot of work. Plus there were many strong friendships between the groups so we decided to join back up. In Philly, Osyp invited Franck and Christophe to stay with them and they wound up testing Extended and joining the group. Peter was added during this weekend as well. 

Originally we were working with Liz Lempicki. She still has most of our decks, but is unsure if she is attending and as such hasn't tested at all. This would have also brought in Taylor, Chapin, and Chris Benafel, but at this late juncture these scenarios seem unlikely. 

I am expecting some good finishes here. We have a number of good decks, and sheer odds say some of us will break through.

### The Usual Suspects

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MASTERSBAR01/543.jpg)

Brian Kibler, Ben Rubin, Baby Huey, Matt Linde, Zack Parker, Brock Parker, Eric Froelich. This crew is what is left of the Mini-Colony. There is no living together this time, unless you count Huey, Brock and Linde. Kibler and Rubin have been working together since the dawn of time. Rubin has been having difficulty with his online Magic-ing, so they haven't tested as much as they have in the past. Huey assures me that a lot is getting done on their end and Kibler is getting some testing in with local prodigy Zach Parker (a.k.a. the Baby-Faced Assassin).

### The Dutch

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/713.jpg)

Noah Boeken, Jelger Wiegersma, Kamiel Cornelissen, Victor van den Broek, Frank Karsten. While all strong players, this group was seen several times at Worlds trying to beg, borrow or steal any deck they could. Jelger may or may not have done a file search for .dec on Alex Borteh's laptop while claiming to use it for checking his email. In any case, these are strong players with strong histories in all formats. I wouldn't count them out simply because they were less than prepared for Worlds.

### The Japanese

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a897.jpg)

Satoshi Nakamura, Jin Okamoto, Itaru Ishida, Katsuhiro Mori, Tsuyoshi Fujita. These guys, while not the finest technical players in the world, are always ahead of the game in technology. Remember that at the tail end of the OBC GP season, two late-blooming decks that rocked the scene: Infestation/Upheaval and Pirates. The Japanese had these decks at Worlds. Remember the Trix deck that Kai won New Orleans with? The Japanese had it a year before. Theirs was monoblue, and Necro, Ritual, and Mana Vault were still legal, but the point is, they know a good deck when they see one.

### Courtney's Boys

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPMOS01/944.jpg)

Bob Maher, Neil Reeves, Dave Williams. I haven't been able to pin down final answers from these guys, but I can only assume they will be working together. They are as good friends as you will find on the Pro Tour as well as top flight players. Though as I am sure they would be the first to tell you, they are Limited specialists.

### Deadguy

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a740.jpg)

Chris Pikula, Dave Price, Tony Tsai, Jon Finkel, Steve OMS. There are other players at Neutral Ground who are working with these guys, but I was unable to pinpoint exact names. These guys may seem over the hill, but I wouldn't count them out just yet. I played Dave at Worlds, and whatever anyone wants to say, he was my toughest opponent in that tournament. In a format such as Extended, aggressive decks can flourish or flounder. We shall see how this strategy treats its oldest users if they decide to knock on that door again.

### South America

  

Carlos Romao, Diego Ostrovich, Gabriel Caligaris, Gabriel Michelena. These rowdy upstarts made a lot of noise at Worlds. Diego couldn't follow it up at the Invitational, but Carlos mad his presence known. Can this conglomerate produce results at the next Constructed Pro Tour? Only time will tell.

### Canada's Best

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/WORLDS2000/images/126.jpg)

David Rood, Elijah Pollack, Gab Tsang, Jason Howden, Jon Stern. These guys are also better known for their talents in Limited play. However, Gab has been playing host to these guys for quite a while creating a far less publicized Magic Colony. It should be interesting to see if Gab's finish in the last 2 U.S. GPs were flukes, or if the man is back in his old form. We can also watch Team 2020 in individual action and see if the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

### The French

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/719.jpg)

Gabriel Nassif, Raphael Levy, Pierre Malherbaud, Amiel Tannenbaum, Olivier Ruel, Antoine Ruel. While Christophe and Franck have seemingly defected to the U.S., there are several strong players left in France. While the decks that come out of France are usually on the quirky side, it doesn't stop solid finishes. Gabriel Nassif has made his presence known in a big way. He is strongly supported by old timers like Raphael Levy and Pierre Malherbaud.

### Michigan

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphou02/978.jpg)

Eric Taylor, Pat Chapin. These two are going it alone. Despite the extremely small group of two, these guys are convinced they have broken the format wide open. I would like nothing more than to see these two great guys take the tournament by storm the way they took GP-Milwaukee.

### Missing in Action

Brian Hegstad, Ken Ho, Joe Crosby, Matt Ranks, Bryan Hubble, Jeff Cunningham, Chris Benafel, Jeff Fung, Terry Tsang, Dan OMS, Eric Franz, Blake Quelle. I am told that Terry will not be attending this tournament. I can only assume the man who won the last Constructed Pro Tour (Ken Ho) will not be attending this one as not one person, including friends and teammates, have heard from him since Boston. The others are all planning on attending, but I am not sure who they are working with, if they are working at all. It should be interesting to see the last minute scrambling that will be going on for these unfortunate souls. Some of these names may have fallen off the train, or not quite had the Constructed rating to get there.

So these are the groups that have worked together in preparation for Pro Tour-Houston. Some new alliances, some old friendships, some national pride, some continental allegiance. With so many different types of groups and so many different sizes it will be interesting to see which type and size can produce the best results.
