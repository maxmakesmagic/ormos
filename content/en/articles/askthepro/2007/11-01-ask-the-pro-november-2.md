![](https://i.ormos.uk/a1/c3/a1c32cb4cea706bb480729e50669771d75d76b266de86e82df6e9d8f4d16d6ec.jpg)Wizards of the Coast welcomes Hall of Famer Raphaël Lévy as the featured player here at "Ask the Pro." A fixture on the Pro Tour for the last eight years, Raphaël is uniquely positioned to answer your questions about the life of a professional **Magic** player, give a historical perspective on the game and high-level tournament scene, share stories about travelling the world, and talk about the role **Magic** plays in his life.

Fifth on the lifetime Pro Points list, Raphaël began his Pro Tour career back at Pro Tour-Paris in 1997. He became a regular on the tour starting at the 1998 World Championships. Since then he hasn't missed a Pro Tour, an astounding streak cresting 50 consecutive events. He was recently honored as a member of the [**Magic** Pro Tour Hall of Fame](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/tournaments-events-trading-card-game-magic-the-gathering-2)'s 2006 class and was inducted at the 2006 World Championships in Paris. He was recently named the 2007 [Road Warrior](/en/events/2007/mi07/raphael-levy-is-the-2007-road-warrior) by a vote of **Magicthegathering.com** readers, earning a spot at the [2007 Magic Invitational](/en/events/2007/mi07/2007-magic-invitational-voting) in October. 

Send your question, along with your name and location, via this email form. Answers will be posted every Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday. 

Note: This feature isn't intended for rules-related questions. For those questions, try our [Saturday School archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20071016101738/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/columnarchive&column=SaturdaySchool), the [Rules Q&A message board](https://web.archive.org/web/20071101000000/http://boards1.wizards.com/forumdisplay.php?f=154), or visit irc.efnet #mtgjudge.

*(Editor's note: While Raphael is globe-trotting this month leading up to the World Championships in December, there may be some interruption to his normal Tuesday-Thursday-Saturday update schedule. Rather than answer individual questions, he'll be giving updates from the road about his tournament success, travel stories, and the new parts of the world he's seeing.)*

The song I had in my head during my whole stay. It describes perfectly some of the players, coming to any place for a tournament, without really having a look around. It is from the musical &quot;Chess,&quot; telling about world-class chess players going to Bangkok for a tournament (notice any similarities?). </p>&#10;<p>... But I am glad I am not one of these players. I am writing these lines from Bangkok Airport, where I have a few hours to wait, waiting for my next flight on my way to Krakow. In a few words, I had a blast here. </p>&#10;<p>I did not really know what to expect of Thailand. I had heard so many people praising the land, telling stories about their endless parties. Sure I would have loved to take part in one of these, had I had time to travel south to the islands. </p>&#10;<p>I arrived on Thursday, and met Olivier and Mathieu Le Montagner (a French player who had made the trip from France to both Brisbane and Bangkok with Oli) at the hotel. A high-standing hotel, but that was cheaper than any of the ratholes you can find in the U.S. or in Europe. We took off to walk around the city for the afternoon, scheduling to visit the temples and all of the tourist-y places on the day after. What I found was an over-crowded, over-polluted city in which I felt totally oppressed. Quite a bad first impression, but I had seen nothing yet. </p>&#10;<p><img src="http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/tournamentcenter/2007/atp_buddha.jpg">On Friday, we saw what we came here for. The Buddhas, the temples; the Tuk-tuk drivers taking you around to the stores in which you have to spend 10 minutes, so he can have his &quot;gazoline&quot; sponsored, the classic rip-offs from about anyone trying to sell you something, but hey, that's how it works here.</p>&#10;<p>But overall, it was worth every dime I gave away to merchants. In about a couple of hours, I discovered how the country worked. How everything is about the money brought by tourists, how everyone wants a piece of you. But also, I saw some of the beauties the country had to offer...</p>&#10;<p>I had not forgotten the purpose of my trip. I am travelling to rack up Pro Points to level up, and Grand Prix–Bangkok was the first stop. The first day went smoothly. I opened a mediocre+ sealed deck, featuring <a>Austere Command</a> and <a>Epic Proportion</a>. The green-white deck I built was decent, but could not beat a blue-based deck. My sideboard plan was to replace the green (too slow) for blue (four islandwalkers) against blue decks. </p>&#10;<p>My plan worked out fine as I finished the day at 7-1, losing the last round to Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa. I would need to 4-2 or 4-1-1 to Top 8. I did 4-2 on the second day, drafting a very average white-green kithkin/elves deck in the first pod, going 2-1 with it, and drafting a huge goblin deck featuring <a>Fodder Launch</a>, <a>Wort, Boggart Auntie</a>, <a>Tar Pitcher</a> in the second pod. I managed to beat Takuya Osawa in the fourth round of the day, but lost to Masahiko Morita for the Top 8 in the next-to-last round of the day. His draw in the third game was just unbeatable: turn-two <a>Stonybrook Angler</a>, turn-three <a>Merrow Reejerey</a>, and turn-four <a>Merrow Reejerey</a> No. 2 and a 3/3 <a>Amoeboid Changeling</a>... I would win the final round and end up ninth, collecting 3 Pro Points, and getting closer to my goal. I did not really have a shot at Top 8 during the last round, so I do not really feel bad about missing the cut.</p>&#10;<p>Oliver and Mathieu left Thailand on Monday, so I had three days in the country on my own... and I am happy I did stay extra time. I had booked a trip to Kanchanaburi, three hours away from the capital city. <b>Magic</b> took me to nice places, but that one was probably one of the best. Splendid waterfalls, elephant riding, tiger stroking, cave exploring, bamboo rafting; that is what Murray Head, and players who decided not to stay a bit longer, missed.</p>&#10;<p>I will soon pull my jacket out of my bag (soon meaning a mere 20 hours) as I will land in Krakow on Thursday.
