Which was better: 1999 Worlds or the Pro Tour in 2003?

I have to go with the event almost four years ago here. The Exhibition Center surely wasn't as good to me as it was four years ago when I won the World Championship in the very same location. 2003 Pro Tour-Yokohama was still a good experience.

I left Hamburg on Tuesday. The plane was crowded with various gamers from Germany, the Netherlands and Sweden. Unfortunately it was overbooked and Mattias Jorstedt and one or two other Swedes jumped at the opportunity to fly through Frankfurt while getting 600 Euros for their trouble. How very lucky. They even got tickets for a better company and their flight was pretty empty while ours didn't have a single empty seat. That meant that drafting was impossible. The movie selection wasn't exactly great either and the flight ended up being pretty boring. I guess we could have playtested Extended for the Masters as we had absolutely no technology at all, but it still seemed like a bad idea to show everyone what we were planning to run. We eventually arrived in Japan at 9 AM. I usually don't have problems with jet lag at all, but traveling to Asia is just awful. Losing a night is pretty rough. The other bad news was that my hotel was quite far from the site. As I am not a fan of Japanese food, I convinced the others to eat at the Hard Rock Café, which was alright and wasn't too expensive as the Yen/Euro-exchange rate is currently pretty good for us. When we got back I fell asleep in Dirk's room and woke up later on. We played a few games of Extended, did a draft and eventually I just couldn't stay up any longer.

The next day was mainly Masters playtesting. And by playtesting I mean “watching the Gateway”. The first thing we saw was that lots of people ran blue-green madness and goblins. We were pretty sure that that would reverse for the Masters itself. It was also quite easy to guess what most people were running anyway. I mean Rob Dougherty was certainly running Reanimator, Jeroen Remie and Darwin Kastle Rock, Zvi Turboland, Justin Gary Oath and so on. We came to the conclusion that there shouldn't be all that much Psychatog and decided that we would just run an updated version of the blue-black Reanimator we played at PT-Houston.

After five rounds of single elimination in the Gateway, Jeff Cunningham, Matthias Veron, Matias Jorstedt and Gabriel Nassif grinded in. They ran blue-green, Aluren and Enchantress and except for Enchantress, which is an auto-loss, the other three decks are pretty good matchups, so we were quite happy with that. When the pairings went up I was close to getting sick though. Even in the very last Masters event we were almost as unlucky as possible. John Larkin played against Nassif, the worst matchup for our deck and it was also the only Enchantress deck in the tourney. Dirk got an okay matchup against Antoine Ruel but Mark Ziegner, who chose to run Sligh, got paired up against Jeroen Remie. Jeroen ran his Houston deck, which featured Spike Feeder and Ravenous Baloth both in the main. That version has exactly one favorable matchup – Goblins. Mark still won a game and the third was pretty close, but in the end the life gain deck prevailed. John lost in two quick games against Nassif, who didn't only have a good matchup but got quite fortunate as well. When John Duressed him in game 1, he just showed him two Seal of Removals and when he used Cabal Therapy naming Seal in game 2, he didn't have any in his hand but he drew one turn 1.

Marco and I weren't any luckier, as we had to play each other. Marco opened both games with turn 1 Duress while I didn't draw either Therapy or Duress. In the meantime, he drew both the single Guilded Drake and Llawan, Cephalid Empress. I still could've won game 2, but a timely Reanimate for a Verdant Force was too much. Another (but at least the last) round 1 exit for me. Marco then lost to Diego Ostrovich's elf deck, which is a pretty good matchup with maindeck Thrashing Wumpus, but Diego got the Bird, Cradle, Tangle Wire, Rishadan Port draw in the two games that he played first and that was a bit too much. After Dirk won his round and John lost to Gabriel, Dirk naturally had to play Nassif in the second round. Another Masters, another excellent 2-5 performance. Frown.

After a pretty short night, the drafting tables were waiting. My first table seemed pretty average. I was feeding Zvi and two Germans without too much PT experience were across the table. My opening pack was pretty bad. I had a choice of Pinpoint Avalance, Gustcloak Harrier, Wirewood Elf and Festering Goblin. Drafting white cards is generally a gamble but you can end up with a really good deck if it works out. I like drafting white when I open mediocre packs for that reason – if you don’t have a great first pick, you can just take the risk. I put the Harrier into my pile and hoped for the best. It was to no avail though. Alessandroto Torresan on my right ended up white-blue and there were no white cards passed to me at all. I picked up a Wirewood Savage and some pretty late black stuff, but I saw no removal at all. The second pack didn't really help me there as I opened another weak booster and had to settle for a first pick Dirge of Dread. Zvi behind me apparently figured that the Wretched Anurid I passed to him 10th pick was a signal that I don't draft black cards, while I just don't consider that card playable in anything but black-red and black-blue. And I don’t like those color combinations a lot. Zvi picked up two Cruel Revivals in pack 2 while I still didn't see a single card capable of dealing with Sparksmith or Timberwatch Elf. A second pick Hystrodon gave me a nice rare though. After two packs, I was pretty much mono-green and it looked like I had to run two Heedless Ones, which I don't really like at all. My Legions pack had some good cards at least. I could either abandon the three black cards I had and pick up a Hellkite or go with a Canopy Crawler. After I got some good black rather late in pack 1, I figured the dragon wasn't worth it and I grabbed the beast. Unfortunately I didn't get to see any more good black or green cards and the highlights were a few bears.
