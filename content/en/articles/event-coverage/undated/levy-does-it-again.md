In Singapore, Hall of Famer Raphael Levy became one of the few players in **Magic** history to win back-to-back Grand Prix titles, and he beat a stellar Top 8 to do it. Since the Frenchman picked up Oli Ruel's copy of Domain Zoo back in Dallas, he has been nearly unbeatable, and not even the likes of both Ruel brothers, Tomoharu Saito, Osamu Fujita, Ryo Ogura, or Japan's newest force Shingo Kurihara, could defeat him here. Levy was the last player with an unblemished record in the swiss and when the dust cleared from the elimination rounds, he was also the last man standing.

Levy pulled out an extremely close match in the quarters, defeating Ogura 2-1 despite Ogura having a Counterbalance on the table for most of Game 3. He then mauled Antoine Ruel in a semifinal mirror match, setting up his shot at history and making him the unquestioned master of the 2007 Extended season.

On the other side of the bracket, Shingo Kurihara was busy showing just how good the latest version of Balancing 'Tings can be. He defeated Tomoharu Saito's U/W Tron deck in a marathon quarterfinal, and then managed to defeat Osamu Fujita despite Blood Moon hitting the board, giving himself a chance to play spoiler for Levy's run. Unfortunately for the young Japanese player, today was Levy's day and nothing would stop him from winning. Much like the semifinal against Antoine Ruel, Levy was never in any real danger of even having a close game in the finals, and he carefully savaged Kurihara 2-0.

Congratulations to Raphael Levy, back-to-back Grand Prix champion and the winner of Grand Prix-Singapore 2007!

Joined by his brother and Raphael Levy in the Top 8, they now have to do battle against 5 Japanese players, making this a pure Japan vs. France battle. Ruel's deck is a spicy one, sporting all sorts of new Balancing Tings technology, but sitting across from him is a Fujita, and &quot;technology&quot; is the family's middle name. Tsuyoshi's latest creation is Deadguy Boros, yet another Extended beatdown deck meant to take advantage of metagame inefficiencies, and it looks like it is working. Ruel faced Tsuyoshi Ikeda earlier in the weekend (who played an identical 75 to Osamu), an managed to defeat him 2-0, but admitted that he might have just gotten lucky. He'll have to do it again if he hopes to progress past the quarterfinals.</p>&#10;      <p>Fujita had to mulligan his first hand, but still was able to play <a>Isamaru, Hound of Konda</a> on turn 1 followed by Icatian Javlineers and <a>Grim Lavamancer</a>. Oli was happy to see a pair of <a>Lotus Bloom</a>s in his opening hand, but would need to draw some business spells soon with Osamu getting off to a fast start. Osamu got Oli down to 6 on turn 3, and then Ruel made a small mistake with which mana he used to activate <a>Sensei's Divining Top</a>, cast <a>Balancing Act</a>, sacrificed all his lands in response, and cast Anurid Brushopper. Osamu responded by casting <a>Lightning Helix</a>, and flashing back <a>Lava Dart</a> to drop Ruel to 2. It took Osamu four turns to draw the two lands he needed, but Oli died to <a>Lightning Helix</a> before the Brushopper could get there.</p>&#10;      <p>Fujita 1 - Ruel 0</p>&#10;      <p>Both players kept their opening 7 in game 2, with Ruel again suspending <a>Lotus Bloom</a> on turn 1, matched by <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> from Fujita. Ruel suspended a second Bloom on turn 2, but <a>Kataki, War's Wage</a> would make their use a bit more interesting. <a>Insidious Dreams</a> on turn 3 from Ruel showed how Tings has changed recently, since he stacked <a>Draco</a> on top of his deck and then used the <a>Erratic Explosion</a> already in his hand to make Osamu pay for using <i>Ravnica</i> dual lands in his manabase. &quot;Take 16!&quot; almost invariably seems to bring a quick end to games.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/f7/5a/f75aaa7d9871ce700f1b88c8e9a18c795027e0644bf55368d876d1875674c342.jpg">&#10;            <i>…but could not overcome clan Fujita.</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Fujita 1 - Ruel 1</p>&#10;      <p>Fujita was undeterred by the outcome of the last game, starting off by taking 3 from his mana base and casting <a>Savannah Lions</a> on turn 1, and double suspending a pair of <a>Rift Bolt</a>s on turn 2. <a>Orim's Chant</a> during Fujita's upkeep was a filthy double <a>Healing Salve</a>, and saved him from at least one extra turn of <a>Jotun Grunt</a> beatings. <a>Balancing Act</a> on turn reset the board with Ruel at a healthy 14. Both players quickly rebuilt, with Fujita using <a>Molten Rain</a> to try and buy that one extra turn he would need to burn Ruel out. With his life at 5, Oli was forced to act, or at least it looked like he was, but he simply Topped and passed the turn. Luckily Fujita also did not have any action, swinging for 1 with his Lavamancer before saying , &quot;Go.&quot; Oli scoffed. &quot;Exactly what I did not want to hear… 'Go.'&quot; At the end of Fuijta's turn, Ruel cast <a>Insidious Dreams</a> for 3, but Osamu responded with a slowrolled <a>Char</a> for Oli's last 4 life.</p>&#10;      <p>Fujita 2 - Ruel 1</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday March 4: 7:31 p.m. - Semifinals: Rapael Levy vs. Antoine Ruel</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Jun-Wei Hew</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/95/39/95390b2179e211eb9f5293d7d5f61975cb9ebb90f1cc933416e1457c77b0df79.jpg">&#10;            <i>Raphael Levy vs. Antoine Ruel</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>They traveled half way across the world to play. Neither player needs much of an introduction, with Raphael Levy coming off a hot streak from a victory in Dallas. His Top 8 presence here gives him the chance to clinch back-to-back Grand Prix titles, but in the meantime, he had to deal with Antoine Ruel. This semifinal match features the Domain Zoo mirror, undoubtedly a matchup that has seen play several times over this weekend.</p>&#10;      <p>This particular match is the pure 75-card mirror, making things even more interesting.</p>&#10;      <p>One facet of the Domain Zoo mirror is that, at least for the first few turns of the game, it's arguable whether you deal more damage to yourself than your opponent. Mana management becomes an understudy of life and time management even more so in this matchup than any other.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Grim Lavamancer</a>s came out on both sides, Antoine's having the advantage from two fetchlands compared to Raphael's one, but Raphael's <a>Tribal Flames</a> took care of the opposing Lavamancer before it came online.</p>&#10;      <p>Over the next few turns, the board developed into a biological food chain of sorts- Antoine's Isamaru would not get past Raphael's <a>Kird Ape</a>, which in turn would not get past Antoine's newly summoned <a>Wild Mongrel</a>. However, Raphael still held onto his advantage outside of combat in his <a>Grim Lavamancer</a>, taking out Isamaru before <a>Watchwolf</a> joined in.</p>&#10;      <p>Sending <a>Wild Mongrel</a> in, Raphael made the logical block of the sole <a>Watchwolf</a>, which was then disposed of courtesy of <a>Gaea's Might</a>. His own <a>Wild Mongrel</a> came in to replace the fallen wolf, along with <a>Kird Ape</a> to help things out.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Wild Mongrel</a> smashed into <a>Wild Mongrel</a>, Raphael's taking out Antoine's without much of a fuss. He then used <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> to take out Antoine's Swiftblade, leaving him with just a lone <a>Kird Ape</a>, before going in for the kill with <a>Jotun Grunt</a> and Isamaru.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine, still stuck at two hand, revealed the creatures still stuck in his hand.</p>&#10;      <p>Levy 1 - Ruel 0</p>&#10;      <p>Sideboarding in any mirror is always interesting, particularly when both players are bringing the exact same 75 to the table.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Sudden Shock" src="https://i.ormos.uk/8b/73/8b73c45d32dfc7e425dc5e5746a080d18aab8fb879d55eed903bdf1eed94e5e2.jpg">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Surprisingly enough, both players sideboarded nearly exactly- they each took out one copy of <a>Boros Swiftblade</a>, <a>Savannah Lions</a>, <a>Sudden Shock</a>, but Raphael took out <a>Brute Force</a>, while Antoine chose <a>Breeding Pool</a>. The choice of a single card now separated the two decks.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine started out the second game drawing out a <a>Firebolt</a> from Raphael's hand via Isamaru, before his <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> hit the table. Unfortunately, Raphael took that out with a <a>Tribal Flames</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Jotun Grunt</a> came down on Antoine's side, which was then matched by Raphael's own <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> and Isamaru cheering him on.</p>&#10;      <p>It was then that the realization that this was the first time Antoine has brought a pure aggressive deck to the table hit, as he commented at how disoriented he felt at having just three cards in hand on turn two.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine sent the <a>Jotun Grunt</a> into the red zone, daring Raphael to block. Both players had green up, suggesting a tradeoff in <a>Gaea's Might</a>. Raphael chose to play it safe, blocking with Isamaru before using <a>Gaea's Might</a> to save it after damage was put on the stack. That left his <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> open for Antoine's <a>Lightning Helix</a> to take out.</p>&#10;      <p>The life totals stood at 18 to 14 in Antoine's favour.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/39/32/393244d01118b3c5c6f55591c59a9f9061ddce0a2836ba35842e756ec5fdd661.jpg">&#10;            <i>Raphael Levy can't be beat.</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Looking to even things a little, Raphael sent Isamaru into the red zone, playing <a>Boros Swiftblade</a> and <a>Kird Ape</a> to add to his forces. Antoine's <a>Firebolt</a> took care of the Swiftblade, and he served with his Grunt, eager to milk it before the cumulative upkeep clock struck. With no blocks, Antoine <a>Gaea's Might</a>'ed into a free 8 damage, taking Raphael down to 6.</p>&#10;      <p>Raphael then drew an <a>Umezawa's Jitte</a> to swing back into the game, but did not have the mana to equip it onto anything just yet. Antoine paid for his <a>Jotun Grunt</a> for the last time, before deciding that he ought to Cloak up the Grunt to force through what damage he could, and give him a larger buffer before the Jitte got too out of hand. <a>Kird Ape</a> soaked up some of the damage from the impending Grunt, leaving Raphael at 5. </p>&#10;      <p>Isamaru took up <a>Umezawa's Jitte</a> and swung. After Raphael used one counter, Antoine looked to possibly hold things off a little bit more with another <a>Jotun Grunt</a>, but <a>Tribal Flames</a> dispatched it, leaving Raphael with a very angry Isamaru with a Jitte, now standing at three counters.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine, now exhausted out of resources, could not stop the Jitte from doing its work. His last draw step coughed up <a>Gaea's Might</a>, which he decided would be put to better use on Raphael's impending Isamaru. This is Kurihara's first Grand Prix Top 8, but it comes directly on the heels of a Pro Tour Top 8 he earned last month in Geneva, in an entirely different format, no less. Kurihara is playing the other story of this event (besides Levy's run), the latest version of Balancing 'Tings. Akira Asahara's favorite deck has once again hit the big time, placing all three players running the deck in the Top 9 (Shuuhei Nakamura missed the Top 8 with it on tiebreakers). Everyone running the deck has admitted it is extremely difficult to play correctly, but the performance of those players cannot be denied - like Domain Zoo, it's a clear player in the Extended metagame.</p>&#10;      <p>Kurihara won the die roll to start the finals, but had to mulligan his first hand. An attack from the animals and then <a>Tribal Flames</a> for 5 dropped Kurihara to 8 before the young Japanese player wiped the board with <a>Balancing Act</a>. Levy rebuilt, but more land damage evened the life score at 8 a piece while allowing Levy to cast <a>Jotun Grunt</a>s. A swing from the Grunts and <a>Gaea's Might</a> made Levy's low life total irrelevant.</p>&#10;      <p>Levy 1 - Kurihara 0</p>&#10;      <p>Kurihara didn't appear flustered while shuffling for game 2. One more game, and another title would be his.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/8f/88/8f88b0788a3e1fe0d45d459ba673365b85ee7376aaf57ae5c64e157782c17e9b.jpg">&#10;            <i>Raphael makes it two!</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Levy looked at his opening five cards of <a>Boros Swiftblade</a>, double Might and double <a>Tribal Flames</a>, and shed a tiny little tear when his final two cards did not turn up a land. &quot;Well, that's a heartbreaker right there.&quot; His next six cards delivered the goods, and Raph cast <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> on turn 1, then <a>Meddling Mage</a> naming <a>Insidious Dreams</a> on turn 2. <a>Gaea's Might</a> on the attacking 'Mancer and Mage threatened to hit for 8, but <a>Moment's Peace</a> fogged the way.
