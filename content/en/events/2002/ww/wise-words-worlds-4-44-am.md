Jetlag sucks. I've said it before, but here I am, 4:44 AM on Friday, four hours and a few from playing Day 3 of a tournament in which I'm a sad 6-6 and I can't sleep. Last night I woke up at 2:30 AM. The night before it was midnight, and while my lack of preparation for this tournament is the reason for my downfall, it's still frustrating to find yourself unable to REM, **Magic** or no.

With roomie Kevin O'Connor fast asleep, there was little to do in the hotel room, so I'm sitting in a Kinkos thirty meters up the street from the hotel with my muse being the goings on of the past few days. Worlds always has been and will be the event to end all others in **Magic** circles, and I've made a few observations and picked up a couple of interesting tidbits over the last few days that warranted a Wise Words. Hopefully, I can get my fingers to the keys before collapse.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1b/8b/1b8b240403a78c5a7938276cc45d66e9bd7b100fdf1248f985d3270d24853189.jpg)Today we're running *Odyssey* Block, and I have to be honest, I could be more thrilled. Professor Craig Jones sent an email out to the Team England mailing list recently about this format that pretty much summed it up, saying 'This would actually be a pretty interesting format if there weren't decks that made 6/6 creatures on turn three and that Mind Twisted your hand before drawing eight cards'. Not surprisingly then, my choices came down to monoblack or blue-green madness, and while I think madness might be the better choice overall, I'm running black. I played it in Osaka with some success, played it every night for two months before that tournament, and while there are obviously changes to the deck and environment, I'm confident in my ability to play it well. My record has left me with little to play for on an individual level, but John Ormerod and I are playing for Boston qualification, needing a combined ten or eleven Pro Tour points to get Ben Ronaldson to the Team Pro Tour. If we fail, we start searching for a third with most of the pros already committed, or we go our separate ways and risk facing one another at the table. I don't want to face The Rod. He's a good friend and a great teammate. That's incentive enough to win.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds02/a728.jpg)The big story thus far has to be Cole Swannack, and I'm stuck right in the middle of it. A week ago, I wrote that I wanted to see Cole go head to head with Kai Budde, figuring his youth might protect him from the nerves that seem to get to many of the big man's older opponents, and instead I drew the kid myself, watching helplessly as two Roar tokens flew over my army's head to hit me for twenty-four over two turns. If I were alone in this losing distinction I'd be embarrassed, but I'm not close. The kid beat Albertus Law on Wednesday. He beat Dan Clegg on Thursday and did the same to Anton Jonsson, a guy I figured had as good a shot as anyone at taking the whole thing. At the end of Day 2, Cole dropped to 8-4 with two losses on the tail end, but if he's set for block, 5-1 can be done. That'll get him into Top 8. Cinderella's a weak story if that happens.

While I'm no cool customer, I'm pretty good at knowing when I need to bear down and get tough for a match, but I just couldn't do it in round five. I heard the Feature Match announcement and tried to do so, but when you're looking at a player one-third your size, who's wearing a big smile on his face and loving **Magic** while being boosted by 30-40 pit dwellers that, at the same time, have no real hopes for your downfall, it's hard to get mean. I bore down and played to win, but the determination and fury just wasn't there. Cole enters every match with a distinct advantage: his opponents either underestimate him or like him with kid gloves. Now all he has to do is find the game to turn that into a win.

Upon the conclusion of our match, I extended the hand and told him that he'd be the coolest World Champion ever, but the truth is I don't want Cole to win like some of my fellow *Sideboard* reporters do. No, I'm not bitter about the loss. That happens in competition, but the truth is that to me, **Magic** is a game of skill and Cole will have to get very lucky to take this kind of crown. Kai wins more than everyone else because he's better than everyone else (except for this week. He's 6-6. I'll probably get him in round thirteen), and while Cole's a great kid, I don't see him as one of the world's best players, at least not top hundred or the like. Sure, it would be a lot of fun to have him as champ for a few days, but once the celebrations stop, we'll all be forced to start asking ourselves questions about this game. I really want him to do well, but the truth is that I'd rather see Chris Benafel win this tournament. He's a better player, so he deserves it more. I'll still cheer if the kid goes all the way though.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/USNAT01/917.jpg)While Cole's here, a lot of people aren't. Two of this year's Pro Tour winners opted out of the Sydney trip, with Ken Ho and Farid Meranghi absent from proceedings, but for my money, the biggest omission fro the worlds roster is Alan Comer. Alan's last job saw the maturation of stock options leave him comfortable, so the cost wasn't the issue, but his entry into the tournament was against the DCI Floor Rules as he's just been hired by WotC to work with **Magic** Online, and I can't think of a better choice. He mixes youthful exuberance with obvious intellectual strength, and in his ascendance, the Pro Tour should find itself a sigh of relief. My only regret is that he leaves our ranks without an appearance in the Invitational, and there's no doubt in my mind that he's the greatest player in the game's history to not receive the honor. Alan made Top 8s at LA 2, Paris, Worlds '98, Chicago 3 and Barcelona. He created incredibly important, environment-altering decks like Godzilla, Turbo Xerox and Miracle Grow. He was a member of two of the most powerful teams - Godzilla and Mogg Squad - in the game's history and he did it all with a huge smile on his face, because in the end, it wasn't the money or fame or travel that propelled him: it was his love for the game. Over the years, Alan and I have met four times with a now-final tally for 2-2, and I have to count myself lucky, because I don't have to have years of objective assessment of my game under my belt to know that he was the better player of us two. Honest, sportsmanlike and just a little lovable-insane, the Pro Tour needs more like him. It's too bad his departure leaves one less, but I can't think of a more appropriate job for the man. Good luck Alan, not that you'll need it.

Alan's departure coincides with Bob Maher's return, and while this hasn't been the Great One's tournament, I for one can't help but feel like the earth has been returned to its axis. Last night, Bob and his wife Courtney joined Dave Price, Zev Gurwitz, Andy Wolf, Neil Reeves and I for dinner and it was great to talk about everything from Truc Bui's drafting to Ben Rubin's travel experiences to Mark 'the bird' Fydrich. It's been especially nice to see Courtney, who Bob brings out of the woodwork from time to time to add a touch of grace to our plodding PT existence. She's the greatest of all PT wives, always bearing stomach-oriented gifts for anyone wanting to chat. She's gained me 27 pounds since Wednesday, and while anyone else doing so would frustrate me to no end, she's an awesome lady and I can't hold a grudge.

Getting to know Reeves a little better has been an experience to remember. The guy is brash, honest, funny and talented beyond belief, managing to mix the intellect needed to thrive in this game with the physical prowess that made him an all-state catcher back in his Arkansas days. That's a tough combination of skills to find in one package, and the way people have been talking about him around here, he's going to be around for a long time.

5:32 AM. Getting tired again. I really should be sleeping, but the muse pushes me on. I'm going to suck today. Congrats to my opponents. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a7/d9/a7d9383c807864b03906a97090b260137c7b7dd92f6ee2b213b965cdbfc9fba6.gif)The team competition is starting to heat up. Before the tournament, the teams being talked about were France, Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands and the good ole US of A, but there have been some massive surprises this year. Worlds usually sees a couple of Standard upstarts find reality in Limited, suggesting that frontrunner Ireland and contenders Malaysia and Argentina are for real. Ireland's run has been remarkable in that they've been in first the whole way, led by comeback kid John Larkin, one of my personal favorites. Larkin stands in at about five foot seven, but he's tough as nails. You know those jerks you see in movies telling people who can and can't go into clubs? He was one of them for years, but in Ireland that kind of a job isn't quite so glamorous, with John taking a pipe to the skull on more than one occasion. He's possessed of the Irish love of drink and good times, and for this week, perhaps the luck as well. In case you weren't around back then, John, playing in his eighth PT, dominated the Swiss of rebel-2000 PT-NY before bowing out to teammate Mattias Kettil in the quarterfinals there, so don't think this guy's not for real. Ireland leads the pack by three points. I'm hoping it stays that way, because frankly, it's always fun to watch the Irish win, because you know the celebrations will be amazing. Larkin makes a pretty good Robbie Keane for my money. 

As if the Irish weren't botching up my pre-tourney predictions enough, I'm getting killed on my solo picks. Olivier Ruel can only hope his brother repeats last year's semifinal performance, because he's 5-7. Rob Dougherty came to me laughing before the tournament and informed me he'd done a total of six hours of testing in his newfound marital bliss. Jens and Anton are doing okay even if Jonsson can't beat a thirteen-year-old kid, but Kai is mired in the same 6-6 muck I am, already eliminated from Top 8 contention. Prognostication is a tricky game I may not be too good at. Truth be told, Chris Ronaldson hasn't picked up a Magic card in seven months plus and Katsuhiro Mori hasn't come to close to that Top 8 I called for in January. Maybe I should just stick to the present. 

6:01. Got to wrap this up so I can power nap before the day starts. I'm knackered.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5a/48/5a487069d9fc2c7a1ecece659d5f148fe7e62be20971e10ca589d784c729e925.jpg)One little **Magic** Online story before I go. Dave Price is playing under a nickname that hides his identity (it's really hard to play while being asked questions by players you don't know, and Dave would get asked a lot), and while he'd never gotten into the trash talk, one opponent forced him to it. Dave was in the final of a draft, light on the mana and frustrated because when his opponent attacked with Aquamoeba and discarded to change its power and toughness. After damage was on the stack, his opponent discarded again at which point Price cast Flame Burst to make the guy discard an extra card, and his opponent suggested that perhaps he should have cast the Burst before damage, also informing Price that he was happy to help people improve their game. That was the last straw: Dave informed the guy that if he needed help winning a PT he should ask for it, and until then he should keep his opinions to himself. The mystery opponent replied that he really didn't need much help in that area. "Who is this?" Dave asked meekly. The answer came.

"Kai"

Have a good week
