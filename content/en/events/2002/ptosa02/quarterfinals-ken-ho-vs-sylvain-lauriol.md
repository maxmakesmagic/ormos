Both Ken and Sylvain both piloted Blue Green to a finals berth, but despite having the same color combination, their decks are vastly different. The Frenchman's deck is heavily based around the Torment card Breakthrough, which allows for not only a Madness outlet, but often provides a quick mechanism for Threshold. He does have a more unusual creature base, running Thought Eaters and a lone Nimble Mongoose amongst his army. Ho's deck is also atypical of the typical Blue/Green OBC deck, much more centered around Upheaval and Squirrel's Nest – two of the most powerful cards in the format, and garners its card advantage through setting a favorable Standstill.

### Game 1

Ken won the roll and choose to play first and decided to keep his hand, as does the Frenchman. Ho opened up proceeding with a 1st turn Rootwalla. When Lauriol declined to cast a corresponding first turn play, Ho saw his opening and cast Standstill. Knowing that he couldn't wait, Sylvain decided to cast Wild Mongrel, allowing Ho to draw 3 cards from Standstill. Ken then proceeded to add to his army and summons an Aquamoeba. Lauriol then decides attacks with Wild Mongrel, Ho declines to block, and there are other effects from the Frenchman. Lauriol then plays Centaur Garden, and casts Seton's Scout as well as a Nimble Mongoose. The American attacks with a Basking Rootwalla and Aquamoeba and Lauriol decides to block Rootwalla with the Mongoose and Scout.

Ho pumps Rootwalla and Aquamoeba by Discarding a Rootwalla. In an attempt to counterattack, Lauriol attacks with Mongrel pumps and discards a Roar of the Wurm and then flashes the Roar from the graveyard. Ho Casts Roar (after discarding it to Aquamoeba). Lauriol then casts Thought Eater in an attempt to attack from the skies. Ho attacks with his own Roar Token, Lauriol blocks with his newly created token. Lauriol attacks with Thought Eater which bring them down to 13 life. In his turn, Lauriol summons a Werebear and in the European's end step, Ken casts Anrrogant Wurm, paying the Madness cost. Ho attacks with Rootwalla, Aquamoeba and the Wurm. Sylvain's Wild Mongrel blocks the Rootwalla, but Ho pumps Rootwalla, forcing Lauriol to discard two cards to Mongrel and pumps Aquamoeba. Lauriol takes 7, halving is life total to 7.

Ho then summons an Aquamoeba. In an amazing topdeck, Lauriol draws Breakthrough. Lauriol casts Breakthrough for 3 (drawing Mongrel, Breakthrough, Island, Forest), and chooses to discard an Island. He sends the attack through the air with Thought Eater. Ho, sensing the match getting out of hand, casts Upheaval floating one blue mana, drops Forest and Aquamoeba. Ho discards down to 7 (Forest x 2). Lauriol casts Careful Study, draws two Rootwallas and discards them to Careful Study, paying their Madness cost. This elicited a groan from the American. It was then Lauriol's turn to discard down to 7 (Thought Eater, Island, Centaur Garden). It was potential a mistake discarding the Eater, as Ho had no easy way to deal with it main deck. Ho the attacks with an Aquamoeba, and when Lauriol declines to block, he switches the power and toughness bringing Lauriol to 4. Ho then casts an Aquamoeba, which Sylvain respondins to with a Werebear (at Threshold). Ken then casts Arrogant Wurm (paying Madness). Lauriol casts Wild Mongrel in defense. Ken finally draws and casts Squirrel Nest, giving Lauriol not many turns to find an answer. Lauriol casts Careful Study (and discards 2 Forests, which he drew), and sends into the Red Zone with a Wild Mongrel – Ken blocks with all his creatures (Arrogant Wurm and two Aquamoebas). Mongrel trades with Wurm. Lauriol replaces his Mongrel with one he just drew. But the squirrels were too much in the long run, and so after attempting to Breakthrough for 3, he conceded.

Ho leads 1-0

### Game 2

Lauriol decides to play first and leads off with a turn 2 Wild Mongrel. Ho matches him with his own Wild Mongrel. Not dauten by the prospect of trading dogs, Lauriol attacks with Wild Mongrel and adds a Seton's Scout to his side of the boards. Deciding that defense is necessary, Ken doesn't attack. Still undeterred, Lauriol again attacks with Wild Mongrel, but this time the American bites and decided to block, discarding a Roar, Upheaval, and a Forest - Lauriol didn't think that the Mongrel was worth saving so only discarded a Thought Eater and Roar of the Wurm. Lauriol then attempts to cast a Breakthrough for 3, but is thwarted by Ken's Circular Logic. The American decides that counterattack is the way to go, and send his Wild Mongrel into the Red Zone bringing Lauriol to18. He the flashes back a Roar. The Frenchman casts Werebear and then Breakthrough for 1, bringing him to Threshold. Lauriol then casts Stupefying Touch on Mongrel. Lauriol mounts his comeback by casting a Roar of the Wurm that he discarded to Breakthrough and attacks with it the very next turn. Ho chooses doesn't block But the real turning point of the game came when Ken cast a Squirrel Nest only to be stolen by the Frenchman's ONLY Aura Graft. After a series of alpha strikes by the Frenchman, Ho was forced to chump until the situation was irreparable.

### Game 3

Ho chooses to play and comes up with a turn 2 Wild Mongrel. Lauriol decided to Stupefying Touch the dog, and in response, Ho summons a Rootwalla with Madness and discards a Roar into the graveyard. Sensing an opportunity, the Magic Colony resident attacks and then sets down the devastating Standstill. Know that he can't wait to long, Lauriol casts Mongrel - allowing Ho to draw three cards. Ho casts Roar of the Wurm with flashback and Lauriol casts Seton's Scout and Werebear in defense. Ho attacks with Roar of the Wurm, and the Frenchamn decideds not to block bringing Lauriol to 11. Ken decides to cast a Squirrel Nest. Looking for an answer, Lauiriol casts Breakthrough for 4 (draws 3 land and a Rootwalla) and discards 2 lands and a Rootwalla (played with madness). Ho once again brings it with Roar of the Wurm and sensing his life is going to be low soon, Sylvain blocks it with Seton's Scout. Ho plays another Nest, and knowing that it could get out of hand quickly, Lauriol casts Upheaval, plays Island, casts Careful Study and discards a Forest and Rootwalla with Madness. Sylvain then discards down to 7, and soon is forced to do the same – discarding two Rootwalla, Upheaval, Aquamoeba and two Forests. Lauriol attacks with Rootwalla and casts Seton's Scout.

Ho responds with his own Wild Mongrel, but it is Stupifying Touched before to soon. Ho attacks with both his Rootwallas bringing Lauriol to 9. Ho casts yet another Mongrel but like his twin, he soon comes under the sway of a Stupefying Touch. Sylvain then casts his own Mongrel. Ho turns the tide with a Squirrel Nest. Lauriol then attacks with Scout and Werebear, and Ho decides to block with a "touched" Mongrel and a Squirrel. Ho attempts to turn the tide by casting a Persuasion on Lauriol's Werebear. Lauriol is forced to cast then second Upheaval of the game. But this time Ho comes back even stronger with three Rootwalllas and multiple Werebears. Sylvain only has one Rootwalla and is forced to trade and he casts Werebear in an attempt to retain parity.

Ho attacks with Werebear and Two Rootwallas, and their lycanthropes trade, and the attack brings Lauriol to 6. Ho passes the turn – Lauriol casts Rootwalla at end of turn. Lauriol then attacks with his Wild Mongrel and Ho casts Arrogant Wurm with Madness and then blocks with his larger dog. Lauriol discards two lands and Ho discards a Mongrel and after damage on stack, a Squirrel Nest. Ho keeps his Mongrel but Lauriol's dies. Lauriol casts Breakthrough for 3. Discarding Roar of the Wurm. Ho then attackls with 2 Rootwallas, Wild Mongrel and Arrogant Wurm forcing Lauriol to concede..

### Game 5

Both players are exhausted by this time as the match enters its 5th (!) hour. Ho chooses to play and keeps his hand, but this time Lauriol is the unfortunate one who is forced to mulligan. Ho starts the offense with a 2nd turn Werebear. Lauriol matches it with his own Werebear. Ho passes. Lauriol attacks with Werebear and then casts a Seton's Scout and Rootwalla. Ho responds with a hard cast Arrogant Wurm. Lauriol responds with a Stupifying Touch on a Werebear. Ho casts another Werebear and a Standstill. With the board locked up, both players pass and drop land for a few turns. Ho breaks the situation with an Upheaval, which leaves him with a land and a Werebear and an empty board on Lauriol's side.

Lauriol uses his first turn after the Upheaval efficiently casting a Nimble Mongoose and a Madness played Rootwalla on the first turn.Ho responds a Wild Mongrewl with an open blue representing a Circular Logic. A Stupefying Touch from Lauriol the next turn dumbed down a Werebear – which attacks next turn into the Goose and Rootwalla. the next turn is explosive with the Wild Mongrel being sent in being pumped with an Arrogant Wurm and a Basking Rootwalla. In the face of all these creatures, and with the Frenchman's last attempt at a creature Circular Logiced, he conceded and let the former rookie into the semifinals.

**Final Result:** Ho wins 3-2
