As for the individual top eight, well, you'll just have to wait for Part 3, won't you? In many ways, the team day of Worlds is the most fun. Even those lucky few who have made it to the Sunday showdown can relax somewhat in the company of their teammates. And although you'll never find a room full of magic plays where there isn't some degree of rivalry, the emphasis on team day is very much about sporting behaviour, friendly rivalry and a shared enjoyment of the great event at the sharp end. Where national teams were striving hard to make the final on Sunday, it was a frustrating day by and large, with 9 points for a team win only being secured each round if both 2 headed giant pairings won their jewel. Making progress up the leaderboard was extremely difficult. As an example, with only one defeat in six rounds, the British team managed to secure fewer points than if they'd won two matches and lost 4. The Swiss, who had gone into the day as joint leaders, quickly established a lead again, generating the full 18 points available from the first two rounds of the day. Since this left them a massive 19 points clear of the third place Tech Republic, it seemed almost certain that Switzerland would be into the final. Behind them are mighty scrap was developing between last year's finalists Japan and the Netherlands, plus the Israelis and the Czechs. With two rounds to go and the final pairs of teammates about to embark on the last draft of the day, the Swiss were indeed home and host, having frankly obliterated anything that moved. Israel and the Czechs finally fell away, and that left Austria, Japan and the Netherlands to compete for the second final slot in the penultimate round. The Japanese played spoiler, denying victory to their Dutch opponents. But if the Dutch could defeat the Swiss in a potential final preview in the last round, then it would all hinge on whether the Japanese could also defeat Austria. Well, in the event, neither contender could get a point in the final round. And so Austria advanced, but by the narrowest of margins from Canada, whose squad of Andrew Tingay, Daniel Kramer, Mike Arenson in a certain rich hound, had chomped their way up the table to finish an outstanding 3rd. And so the table was set for the final Super Sunday of 2007 with both individual and team World titles on the line.
