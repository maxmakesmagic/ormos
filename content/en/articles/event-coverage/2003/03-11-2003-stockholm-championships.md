About one month ago I checked my email and found this:

*Hi Kai.*

*It´s Peter Brottman from Decklab.com again. Just wanted to invite you to Stockholm 1-2 of March to the Stockholm Championship 2003. This is the third time I have arranged it.*

*I´ve heard you hate traveling a lot, so I don´t really expect you to come, but if you should, for any reason, be in the neighborhood (that is, in Sweden) you´re very welcome!*

*The first prize is approximately $ 1,100, with prizes down to the Top 16. Day 1 will be Standard with Legions and Day 2 will be Top 64 O-O-L Rochester draft, 2 pods. Top 8 will be Standard again. Day 1 is also a National Qualifier for Swedish Nationals (I think this is the first national qualifier for ANY Nationals, worldwide), but anybody can attend, even foreigners. Jens Thorén and Anton Jonsson will be there... wouldn´t it be nice to kick some Punishers?*

*So, what do you say? My guess is: "probably not", but at least you didn´t miss this one by not being notified.*

*Well, anyhow, good luck with school and everything and maybe I will see you in Sweden at our next GP this fall.*

*Cheers!*

*Peter*

*p.s. If you SHOULD be in Sweden and decide to come to this tiny, tiny tournament, then you can stay at my place, Thorén and Jonsson will.....*

I like playing tournaments after all, but the idea of going to Sweden for a small one wasn’t too appealing to me at first. I do like hanging out with Anton Jonsson, Jens Thorén, Thomas Rosholm, Johan Sadeghpour and the other Swedes that I have gotten to know. Then I learned that all my exams were done at that point and that there wasn’t anything of any importance that weekend. I checked flights and there was a direct connection from Hamburg to Stockholm which I could get with my frequent flier miles. By now, this whole thing sounded a lot more interesting, as it turned out to be a completely free trip.

I booked the ticket and left Hamburg on Friday before the tourney. A short flight later I arrived in Stockholm and Peter picked me up. When we got to his place, it was kind of crowded with the mentioned guys and several others staying there. One of them had booked a table at a place that was a combination of a sports bar and a club. The problem was that it happened to be in the outskirts of Stockholm and I’m not really much of a dancer. Anyway, the food was all right and the drinks not as expensive as I was afraid they would be. Anton ended up being rather drunk pretty soon and I spent the evening chatting with Thomas Rosholm and the Head Judge of the tourney, Matthias Waglin. When we were heading back, I was very impressed by a few Swedish girls, who left the club for a smoke wearing, well, not much. With the temperature being at roughly –15 celsius, I could hardly stand waiting 15 minutes for a cab and I was wearing a jacket. At least I learned that I really don’t have a right to complain about the –5 celsius we had in Hamburg at that time. I got a good four hours of sleep on a inflatable air mattress, which was much better then what I had expected, knowing that there were going to be a ton of people at Peter’s place.

We got to the site on Saturday morning and 160 people showed up. That meant 8 rounds and the Top 64 would advance to Day 2 to the Rochester draft. Sadly, Matthias Kettil and Matthias Jorstedt turned out to be very lazy and skipped the tournament. But Johan turned up and there were quite a few other guys I had met at European events before. The first day of the tournament was Standard. One of the old rules at European **Magic** events is that Scandinavians love their islands. Whenever you ran into one, they would be playing some sort of blue deck. With that in mind, I built a Psychatog version that was tuned to win the mirror match and smash anything like Wake and black control:

Johan made the Top 4 at a GP in Oslo some time ago and was running green-blue madness today. The first game ended up being pretty ridiculous. He Parised into an awful hand but I kept five lands, Counterspell and Upheaval going second. He used Careful Study to discard two forests on his first turn and still couldn’t find a two casting cost creature. He managed to draw some creatures at some point, but I countered them. My deck didn’t give me a ‘Tog or any sort of card drawing and many turns later, a Wonder was beating me down. I was at 8 life when he cast Roar of the Wurm. Still not having anything, I had to cast Upheaval to reset the board. I managed to topdeck a few important removal spells and many turns later, I got the second Upheaval and could follow it up with a Psychatog.

In game 2, I had to play a Lonely Sandbar turn 1 and Johan resolved a Compost. My hand was Deep Analysis, Smother, Edict, Innocent Blood and some lands. I ripped Callous Oppressor of the top. That game the little Cephalid displayed how broken he really is. Johan got to draw a ton of cards from his Compost, but it just didn’t matter. Innocent Blood was devastating and he always had to make sure that he could play two creatures to prevent me from just stealing one and attacking with that in case he only got one. While the Oppressor kept him busy, I drew tons of cards with Compulsion and Concentrate, which negated the Compost some more. Eventually, all his Logics were gone and I played Upheaval and followed it up with a lethal ‘Tog.

Johan impressed me by boarding out his main deck Upheaval. He had a second one in the board and I was 100% certain that he would bring it in. I let a few spells resolve in the second game that I could have countered to make things a lot easier for me, but I was always afraid of Upheaval and kept two Counterspells back in case he would topdeck them. By removing them from his deck, he left those counterspells useless as I was never willing to use them as I was afraid of something Johan didn’t even have in his deck any more.

Anton lost his quarterfinals against Andreas Persson who was playing green-red, the only matchup I prefer to avoid with ‘Tog right now. And, unfortunately, I was playing Andreas next.
