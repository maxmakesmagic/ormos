<table><tbody><tr><td><b>BLOG</b></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr><td><br><a name="1"></a><li><b>Saturday, January 8: 11:15 am - Kicking off the New Year!</b></li>&#10;<p>Here we go again. Osaka marks the first of two trips to Japan this month for me, racking up 26,000 frequent flier miles and many an hour of fitful plane sleep and jet lag. Thankfully, just being in Japan makes it all worthwhile. The people here are nice, the culture is fascinating, and even the weather isn't bad - 50 in Osaka is <i>much</i> better than the frosty 6 degrees at Grand Prix-Chicago. Minus the language difference, being in Japan is not unlike any other big city in America except in addition to the normalcy of city life, Japan adds a &quot;What in the world...&quot; factor of surprise and delight that I particularly enjoy. </p>&#10;<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gposaka05/b1.jpg"><br><i>Castle Osaka</i></td></tr></tbody></table>&#10;<p>I'm taking baby steps in learning the language (I bought some kid's books at the bookstore in addition to some beginner Manga and a kiragana/katakana study guide), I've successfully navigated around the two largest cities in Japan (Tokyo and Osaka) by myself, and I'm even starting to recognize the restaurants that serve great food without having to rely on the generous translation services of Ron Foster all the time. Being here is great. Yesterday I wandered around taking in sites like Osaka Castle and the Osaka Museum of History, while making sure to get some shopping in at the great underground shopping malls and department stores located around our hotel. </p>&#10;<p>Those of you who have read the Chicago coverage know that one of the best parts of traveling the world is finding great restaurants to eat at while you're out, and Japan has no shortage of those. Those looking for a connection to the world can usually find a McDonald's or a Wendy's around, but you won't find a teriyaki burger with a fried egg on top or pumpkin soup at any Mickey D's in the states. Instead of sticking to the known like American chains, you should try Mos Burger, a chain of Japanese burger places that serve you regular burger fare, organic style. If you want something a little more upscale, you should do what I did for lunch yesterday and track down a tonkatsu restaurant. There's something about a deep-fried porkchop dipped in sesame worstershire sauce served with shredded cabbage in citrus sauce that's irresistible. </p>&#10;<p>Even better though was last night's dinner at a Korean Barbecue joint down some neon-lit alleys not too far from our hotel. The combination of pickled vegetables and bite-size meat morsels that you cook yourself on a grill at your table makes for a great community meal, particularly when conversation is well-lubricated with beer and sake. </p>&#10;<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gposaka05/b2.jpg"><br><i>The Glory of Tonkatsu</i></td></tr></tbody></table>&#10;<p>After Yokohama, the judges and coverage staff all went out to party at local Japanese pubs, where I introduced my fellow attendees to the glory of <a href="/en/articles/feature/2005/01-04-a-vintage-year-in-review-2">Limited Infinity</a> before stumbling back to my hotel at 5AM, inebriated, exhausted, and really happy. I'm sure there will be more of that here, but Nagoya holds the prize this month of cool happenings and good times. I can't go into detail here, but word has it that Nagoya will be the starting point for a whole new class of Pro Tour perks that make it much cooler and more fun to qualify for and attend a PT. I'm sure we'll go over every new benefit that the folks at Wizards in our Nagoya coverage, so that's something to look forward to. Also on the agenda for Nagoya will be my first trip to a karaoke bar, and I'll be looking to rope fellow coverage reporters, players, and staff alike into an evening of partying and embarrassingly off-key singing on one of our free evenings. </p>&#10;<p>Believe me when I say that 2005 will be the best year yet to be a Magic player and particularly a Magic Pro (and it will still be a lot of fun to be a world-traveling coverage reporter as well).</p><br><a name="2"></a><li><b>Saturday, January 8: 12:20 pm - Taking the Pulse of Japanese Magic</b></li>&#10;<p>I know a lot about U.S. Magic and a little about European Magic, but when it comes to Japan, I was a blank slate before GP: Yokohama, knowing only what I'd seen occur at the Pro Tour and nothing more.
