Last week, I talked about how important the maintaining of our history is, and much to my appreciative surprise, a number of people voiced likeminded sentiments in response to the article. If you have no idea what I'm talking about, you can figure it out yourself by going [here](/en/events/2002/ww/wise-words-the-ghost-of-pro-tour-past). This week, we complete the rundown of past Pro Events, looking at what's transpired leading up to this season. Enjoy

### 1998 - 1999

Germany has always been among the handful of nations that were able to claim a serious challenge to early American supremacy, with players like Frank Adler, Peer Kroeger and Janosch Kuhn making serious challenges to Pro Tour thrones, but in '98-'99, a new breed of German **Magic** player would change the face of the Pro Tour for ever...

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPAMS01/975.jpg)

**Chicago** - The German invasion began with the first Pro Tour of the season. Playing in his first PT, Dirk Baberowski shocked the **Magic** world by emerging from a star-studded Top 8 to win this tournament, ending the American win streak at five Pro Tours by defeating Casey McCarrel in the final. Interestingly, Mike Turian and Darwin Kastle unintentionally drew a final round match that would have put either player in the Top 8 with a victory. Both players still maintain that they would have won given more time.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/887.jpg)

**Rome** - One major obstacle facing the game at the time was the fact that with no one player managing two Pro Tour victories, it was hard to prove **Magic's** base was in skill rather than luck. In Rome, that all changed. In danger off falling of the Pro Tour coming into the event, Tommi Hovi emerged victorious in the highest power level Pro Tour to date, piloting his Academy deck to his second PT win and cementing himself as one of the game's true greats. Hovi defeated newcomer Nicholas Labarre in the final despite a poor matchup after beating long time affiliate Olle Rade in the semis.

**Los Angeles** - With Rochester being considered by many to be the most skill-intensive format in the pro game, it should come as no surprise that the two men considered by many to be the best drafters in the world at the time would face off in the finals here. Always the bridesmaid, never the bride, Steve O'Mahoney-Schwartz finally secured his place in the game's annals with his finals win over friend and teammate Jon Finkel in a Top 8 that proved the value of friendly drafting. With the two finalists cooperating with neighbors Terry Lau and Worth Wollpert, each of the four won their semifinal matches over their less cooperative opponents despite their carrying names like Long, Geertsen and Chapin.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/13/61/1361ba4eb1011f6790b227560f8a78ccd090d23a440170f425f6c0834a253395.jpg)

**New York** - If nothing else, playing Block Constructed with Tolarian Academy makes for a lot of fun. There were more Academies played than swamps or plains in New York, and in the end it was the Jumble who proved most capable of taking advantage, placing Casey McCarrel, Shawn Keller, Terry Tsang and Ryan Fuller 1st, 2nd, 8th and 9th respectively. Your Move Games also had a strong showing, with both Dave Humphreys and Rob Dougherty earning their first Top 8 key chains. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/EURO01/927.jpg)

**World Championships** - Having won three European Grand Prix over the course of the year, Kai Budde had captured everyone's attention, but no one knew what was coming when World Championships came around. Without a Top 16 finish to his credit, Budde won the Player of the Year award and the World Championship crown in consecutive matches, defeating Raffaele Lo Moro in the semis and Mark LePine in the final. Budde's win helped him leapfrog PotY frontrunners Casey McCarrel and Jon Finkel, and helped the Americans realize that winning three Grand Prix in one season, regardless of the continent they're played on, is a remarkable feat attainable only by the very best. His win was dismissed as fluke.

### 1999-2000

With the first ever PotY/World Champ now a known entity, the Pro Tour had shifted back to a more international flavor. The key development of this campaign was the introduction of the Team Pro Tour, which would give the world's best the chance to publicly do what they'd done privately for years: teams draft. Instead of the popularized team booster format though, WotC decided to go with Sealed on the first day of the Tour and Rochester on the second.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2c/41/2c4153462c0c1df20291d12d90c45aa7dce81d8eca8efb3aa575ad902e2425df.jpg)

**Washington D.C.** - With this being the introduction to a brand new format, one had to figure the teams comprised of the strongest individuals would likely do well, and the results only backed up that theory. Possibly the strongest player by player team in the tournament, Your Move Games (Darwin Kastle, Dave Humphreys, Rob Dougherty) ran off with the crown despite Dougherty's near collapse from illness prior to the final. YMG defeated Antarctica (Jon Finkel, Steve OMS, Dan OMS) in the semis and Game Empire (Alan Comer, Kurt Burgner, Brian Selden) in the final despite the Californians' dominance in the Swiss.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/WORLDS2000/images/267.jpg)

**London** - Never one to say much, Kyle Rose always spoke with his actions. The first player to start a Pro Tour with 13 wins, a former U.S. champ and a multiple Top 8 finisher, Rose won his only Pro Tour crown in London, where, booster drafting Urza's Block, he defeated rookie Thomas Preyer in the finals. Notable here was the strong showing by New England players, with Darwin Kastle, Billy Jensen and Mike Bregoli all finishing in the Top 8. Joining them was Ben Rubin after an off year that saw him forced to go back (albeit briefly) to the PTQ circuit. With this Top 8 finish, Rubin was back on Tour to stay.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8d/96/8d96b3bfdf6b0eb6f3ecdf3d7da73b059182c0663c82595a8bb82668ff3af9ef.jpg)

**Chicago** - While Dave Price's victory was probably the most popular with Pro Tour outsiders, Bob Maher's had to be the player's choice. As good as gone from the Tour, the self-described 'has been' took the deck he knew better than any other, Oath, and outplayed the field, culminating with a 3-2 victory over rookie Brian Davis that observers felt the kid should have won 5-0. Upon the match's completion, Bob was embraced in memorable fashion by then fiancée Courtney. He then made his way to Seattle to win the first Extended GP there playing the same deck.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/634.jpg)

**Los Angeles** - After his Top 8 finish a year earlier, Mike Long decided it was time for a comeback, and he did it with his usual flare, scoring another Top 8 finish all the while creating an uproar in his crucial Swiss match against Darwin Kastle. Unfortunately for Mike though, his luck ran out in the semifinals when he was defeated by a then unknown kid named Chris Benafel. The people's new hero, Benafel then lost in turn to Trevor Blackwell, a player who, like Long, was making a comeback after a year away from the tour. Any suggestion that Blackwell's win was a fluke though was nullified by his win at U.S. Nationals a year and a half later.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/WORLDS2000/images/309.jpg)

**New York** - Like Bob Maher, Sigurd Eskeland was ready to quit the Pro Tour, giving himself one last chance at what has come to be known as 'the Rebel Pro Tour'. Playing a Rising Waters deck, Eskeland finally made his first Top 8, and once there didn't squander the opportunity, defeating Warren Marsh in the final. Inspired by his newfound wealth and fame, Sigurd stayed on Tour, where he's remained until today. Also of note, New York marked the debut of Kamiel Cornelissen, whose late swoon saw him settle for an 11th place finish in his initial Pro Tour.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/864.jpg)

**World Championships** - Jon Finkel's demise was the most talked about issue of the winter, and his resurgence was the most popular topic of the summer. Dominating U.S. Nationals, Finkel represented his country as team captain at Worlds, and in doing so became the first player to win the World Championship in singles and teams in the same year. Finkel beat long time friend Bob Maher in the finals playing nearly identical decks, but not all was lost for Maher, whose semifinal win over Dominik Hothow sent him past Darwin Kastle in the season's last tournament for Player of the Year honors. Kastle was seen waving a German flag during the Maher-Hothow match.

### 2000-2001

With **Magic's** greatest player back on top of the competitive world, and new monies making professional **Magic** a viable occupation, the Pro Tour was unprepared for the one-man onslaught that was about to occur. The 2000-2001 season will be remembered for a long time as the year of Kai Budde's emergence as the game's best player, no mean feat considering Jon Finkel would Top 8 twice during the season.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNY01/919.jpg)

**New York** - The team Pro Tour's second go saw the benefits of Pokemon as the prize purse doubled, and thank God! I mean, how would I have survived with $10,000? Mike Turian, Andrew Cuneo, Aaron Forsythe and Andrew Johnson worked together to gain as strong an understanding of the *Mercadian Masques* team Rochester format, and it paid off, as the latter three (Car Acrobatic Team) met Turian, Scott Johns and I in the final, with the good guys proving triumphant. Timing is everything, sports fans.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b4/6b/b46bb14699e51aa9d14c83a73ddc048b7f077fc33f94af47aaf675e80d373609.jpg)

**Chicago** - The Pro Tour, thanks in part to popular demand, returned to its Standard roots for the first time in years, with the results spawning what may be the strongest Top 8 of all time. In the end, Kai Budde, coming off a quiet and frustrating year, took the title, defeating Kamiel Cornelissen in the final, making him only the third player to win two Pro Tours. The Top 8 also included Jon Finkel himself, Rob Dougherty and Mike Pustilnik who was about to make some real waves...

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNY01/912.jpg)

**Los Angeles** - ...on the Californian Coast. Mikey P, who'd seemingly been around the Pro Tour since time began, hadn't made a Top 8 prior to Chicago in quite some time, but his perseverance paid off, with the little man from New York taking the title. Once again, the unfortunate loser of the final was Cornelissen, who could do nothing but shrug as Pustilnik cast Ravenous Rats, forcing the Dutchman to discard his one card: Rith, the Awakener. Pustilnik would go on to become the first player in the game's history to win a Pro Tour, a Grand Prix and a Masters Series with his win in New Orleans.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNY01/910.jpg)

**Tokyo** - When your team is named to suggest that you're going to win a particular Pro Tour, you'd better back it up. Figuring they'd walk all over Tokyo, Zvi Mowshowitz, Kai Budde and company formed Team Godzilla. Team Godzilla didn't quite dominate, but they did manage to walk away with this one thanks to Zvi. Godzilla's metagamed blue-white deck helped Zvi to the title in a Top 8 filled with AlphaBetaUnlimited members and affiliates playing red-green, including Ryan Fuller, who became the first player to finish the swiss rounds of a Pro Tour with a 14-0 record. Zvi's win came at the expense of prodigal son Tsuyoshi Fujita, who became the first Japanese player to appear in a Pro Tour on Sunday.

**Barcelona** - Not content with being tied for first with Finkel and Hovi for most PT wins, Kai Budde did it again, taking the *Invasion* Block booster draft format and making it his own with defeats over fellow Godzillans Alan Comer and Patrick Mello in the finals and semis. Comer's performance was especially notable as he'd only recently fallen off the gravy train for the first time in five years. Also making his emergence in this tournament was Dan Clegg, who would go on to major Grand Prix success and his first Invitational at Cape Town.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/782.jpg)

**World Championships** - With school, girlfriend and other pursuits ebbing away at his **Magic** time, Worlds 2000 top 8 finisher Tom Van de Logt didn't make his way to any of the Pro Tours during the 2000-2001 season, but with school out for summer, the 6'7" Van de Logt found the time to playtest and make the trip to Toronto and the rest is history. Van de Logt beat U.S. Nats breakthrough performer Alex Borteh in the final with his black-red deck taking out Borteh's Fish. Also in the Top 8 were Tommi Hovi, playing on his first Sunday since Rome and Mike Turian, finally playing in his first individual Sunday match, but the story of the tournament may have been Dave Williams, who was disqualified mid-way through his quarterfinal match with Van de Logt, giving England's John Ormerod his second Top 8.

...and that brings us to the 2001-2002 season, and if you're still reading, I think you know what's happened so far. Nice is the final Pro Tour before Nats/Continentals/Worlds season starts, meaning that the Player of the Year race (which is obviously Kai's to lose) will wrap itself up amid a number of stories like the ones above that we'll likely be talking about years down the line. That's about it for now. I hope you've had as much fun reminiscing as I have. Have a good week.
