It's been only a few months since Eli Loveman took Humans to victory at Mythic Championship II, but the Modern metagame has gone through substantial changes since then.

*War of the Spark* and *Modern Horizons* rank among the two most impactful set releases in the entire history of the format, and *Core Set 2020* has added several relevant cards as well. What's more, [Bridge from Below](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bridge+from+Below) was banned just a few weeks ago. With all these shakeups, where did players' Modern deck choices end up in Barcelona?

Even without [Bridge from Below](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bridge+from+Below), [Hogaak, Arisen Necropolis](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hogaak%2C+Arisen+Necropolis) remains a very powerful card that is well worth building around. Consider the following start:

*   Turn 1: [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire) and [Carrion Feeder](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carrion+Feeder).
*   Turn 2 [Stitcher's Supplier](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stitcher%27s+Supplier) and [Faithless Looting](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Faithless+Looting). Cast Hogaak from the graveyard, trigger [Vengevine](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vengevine), and pass with 14 power on the battlefield.
*   Turn 3: Attack for lethal.

While this is an exceptionally good start, it illustrates why so many players chose the deck. A metagame share of 21.4% is absurdly high for Modern. And this doesn't even include the Dredge decks (which sometimes had one copy of Hogaak) and the Hogaak Dredge decks (which run 3 Hogaak and 4 [Stitcher's Supplier](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stitcher%27s+Supplier)). Hogaak is certainly the card of the tournament.

While most players agreed on the core of four-ofs, there was no consensus on the final slots to fill out the deck. The above list runs [Insolent Neonate](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insolent+Neonate), [Lightning Axe](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Axe), and [Golgari Thug](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Golgari+Thug) in those last few slots, whereas other versions had graveyard setup cards like [Cryptbreaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptbreaker), [Lotleth Troll](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotleth+Troll), [Shriekhorn](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekhorn), [Altar Of Dementia](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Altar+Of+Dementia), and/or [Hedron Crab](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hedron+Crab) or interactive cards [Fatal Push](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fatal+Push), [Assassin's Trophy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Assassin%27s+Trophy), and/or [Leyline of the Void](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leyline+of+the+Void) instead. But while a consensus on the optimal build is lacking, a large part of the field agreed that returning Hogaak, [Vengevine](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vengevine), and [Bloodghast](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodghast)s for free is a powerful strategy.

The deck got several upgrades from the last few sets. [Aria of Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aria+of+Flame) is an alternative win condition that circumvents both creature removal and anti-graveyard cards. [Finale of Promise](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Finale+of+Promise) is also a strong addition because it returns [Arclight Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arclight+Phoenix) by itself. And one-of additions like [Lava Dart](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lava+Dart) or [Magmatic Sinkhole](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magmatic+Sinkhole) are easy to find with all the card draw spells.

These card draw spells, by the way, still form the core of the deck. They help find [Thing in the Ice](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thing+in+the+Ice) and [Arclight Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20190726000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arclight+Phoenix), enable them, and keep you churning through your deck. Some things don't change.
