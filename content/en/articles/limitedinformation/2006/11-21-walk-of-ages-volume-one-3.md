For this game, here is the deck used:

Yeah, triple Might Sliver. I don't get it either. My opponent this round is a gentleman with the handle of “Pantanonegro,” also known as Delfin Martinez of Spain. This is my third game of the match against Delfin, the first being a blowout with Might Slivers and the second having me crumble to color screw versus triple Keldon Halberdier. In the third game, playing first, my opening hand looks like this:

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_BMull.jpg)

Is this a mulligan hand? No way! Any opener with a one drop, two drop, and three drop, along with *all* the colors is just dandy. It's not a perfect hand, but it's definitely top tier. It would not be smart to throw this one back. I keep and start off with an Island and Viscerid Deepwalker. Delfin keeps his initial seven as well and leads with a Mountain and Keldon Halberdier. Can I reiterate how big playing first is in *Time Spiral* draft?

The next draw is an interesting one and prompts a big decision. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B2a-q.jpg)

Ah, Tromp the Domains. I told you I liked that card. The Tromp drawn this turn changes things a bit. My game plan with the opening hand was midrange, which is to say I was okay with the aggressive or control role. Depending on when the Might Slivers or Chronosavant or Griffin Guide or whatever show up, the deck's style could be shifted. Now, with a Tromp, the new plan is to deal as much damage as possible. I would gladly take 4 points of damage to deal 2, because my finisher is better and more immediate than anything my opponent can throw back. Overrun for the win, and all that. 

With that in mind, the unknown is which play would deal more damage: Spinneret Sliver or suspending Riftwing Cloudskate. I assume that between flying and the bounce effect, Riftwing is good for at least two points. I can't say the same about Spinneret Sliver. However, I *can* say the longer the game goes on, the less likely a 2/2 ground creature will deal damage. Normally I'm a fan of aligning suspend creatures. This deck could also want to save its Slivers, because with triple Might, they're more valuable than other creatures. However I'd be foolish to stay attached to those guidelines when our draw dictates a different course of action. I want to deal immediate damage to maximize Tromp later. Spinneret Sliver on turn two, while less strong for the late game, is better early. Luckily I already have the late game covered. This turn, this time, Spinneret Sliver gets the nod.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B2a.jpg)

As expected/hoped for, Delfin does not drop a Pit Keeper or Aetherflame Wall. Spinneret is free to come in for the 2 points, exactly what I wanted. After combat, Riftwing Cloudskate takes on his suspend. Why not hard cast it on turn five and get some surprise value? Because surprise value is far less valuable than maximizing mana. Remember that with the haste, my Riftwing still attacks for 2 on turn six. The difference is, this turn I make a play that uses my mana. On turn five, who knows what I may want to cast over a 2/2 flier? Why put myself in that potential conundrum? Suspend now for options later.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B3a.jpg)

Through sheer serendipity, my opponent again plays nothing on his turn. Spinneret deals another valuable 2 points. My draw is a land, so I have nothing else to do. After combat, back to Pantanonegro.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B4a.jpg)

Pantanonegro, finally tired of being slapped by Grizzly Bears, decides to make a play this turn. Actually, two:

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B4b.jpg)

Coal Stoker's a great card, even though Delfin doesn't use it to put a second blocker into play. Still, it's currently the largest creature on the board.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B5a.jpg)

My fifth turn has the Deepwalker enter play, along with a very nice draw in Walk the Aeons. The question for this turn was simple: What to attack with? The three viable options are:

*   Attack with both
*   Attack with Deepwalker
*   Attack with neither

Each option had its strengths and weaknesses. Attacking with both guarantees 2 or 4 points of damage. Damage, you may remember, is something this deck wants to dish out afore Tromp. Yet this option is merely a short term boost. I'll assume my opponent will put Coal Stoker on Spinneret Sliver, taking four. That's nice, except now I'm down a 5/5 trampler *and* I'll take more damage on the backswing. Dealing 4 now to forgo dealing 5 later is just bad math, especially when the attack still leaves Delfin with 3 toughness worth of D.

What about just Deepwalker? Perhaps Delfin would let it through, since that incoming Halberdier blocks the Homarid so effectively. Perhaps…but I'm not willing to take the chance. Coal Stoker blocks Deepwalker just as effectively and Halberdier blocks Spinneret fine, or maybe Halberdier just keeps attacking. 90% of the time, the right play for Pantanonegro *is* to block Deepwalker here, and again, my creatures are more valuable alive than dead.

So it's the third choice, attack with neither. A rare decision, but in this case it's warranted. As a side benefit, perhaps keeping the two creatures untapped will prevent some life loss, but that's not the goal. Mainly, I just want the guys alive to Tromp up later.

Delfin's Halberdier comes into play and immediately attacks, leaving Coal Stoker back for defense. It's unfortunate he left the Coal Stoker back, since I'm in a *very* good place to win if he attacked with both. Delfin made a good attack though. Any blockers? 

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B5b.jpg)

Technically I could double block and kill the Halberdier without losing card advantage, but by now I think I've shown why I won't be doing that play. Besides opening myself to Bad Things with a potential Strangling Soot or Cannonade, I'd still rather have both creatures alive. Taking damage here is not an issue. After combat, Delfin plays a second Keldon Halberdier.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B6a-1.jpg)

My turn sees the last big decision of the game. What to bounce with Riftwing Cloudskate? Clearly I want to do something untapped, and while the land is cute as some kind of Lightning Axe defense, it could and probably would be cast in response anyway. Normally the Halberdier would be bounced back. After all, Coal Stoker's ability makes bouncing it ineffective for tempo, and Delfin even has a mana sink in play. Normally the Halberdier, yes, but again why in the world would you ignore the cards in hand? The “obvious” play is just not correct here. I've said it before, but it bears repeating: There are very few automatics in Limited. Scenarios like this one make what seem like the clear solution totally wrong. In this case, I want Coal Stoker back in Delfin's hand. I don't want him to have another turn and I especially don't want that extra 3 points of toughness shielding his life total.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B6a-2.jpg)

I attack with Riftwing Cloudskate, un-axed, and after combat cast the underrated Walk the Aeons. The drawn Might Sliver is a green herring.

Next turn, down comes the powerful Tromp the Domains. (2+2+2) worth of base power plus (3+3+3) worth of trample bonuses (-1) for Halberdier's toughness = 14 points of damage. Rawrrr!

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B7a-1.jpg)

Delfin throws his guy in front of the speeding sliver, but the math is good, and Delfin goes from 14 to zero, granting me the game and match. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/limited/nw30_B7a-2.jpg)

I liked this game because the choices were against the grain of standard play and even this deck's philosophies. But what *should* happen doesn't matter nearly as much as what you actually have to work with. In both of these games, my draws and opponent's draws determined the right moves; nothing else. Did I get lucky to win here? Maybe, but I like to think I made my luck work for me. With awareness of the tenor of our games, I was able to seize the opportunities presented. Win or lose, that's the right way to play **Magic**. Finally, click [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20061121000000/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/daily/nw30c) to see a few cute single screens that had some fun decisions attached.
