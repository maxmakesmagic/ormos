Tatsunori Kishi defeated amateur Keisuke Hashimoto and Affinity with his red-green Goblin deck in two quick games to secure himself a check for $2,400 and his first Grand Prix title. Previous to this event, his best finish was 24th at Grand Prix Yokohama. 

Twenty-fourth was just enough to earn him a very costly Pro Tour point that meant that his opponent Hashimoto took home the $1,500 amateur prize in addition to his $1,700 consolation prize.

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>COVERAGE</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <hr>&#10;      <a name="17"> </a>&#10;      <h3>Sunday 8/29/04, 18:06 - Finals: Keisuke Hashimoto vs. Tatsunori Kishi</h3>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <i>by Eli Kaplan</i>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>The matchup seems hauntingly familiar. In Japan's last major tournament, red/green Goblins defeated Affinity to win the National Championship. Last time it was Tsuyoshi Fujita piloting the Goblins that defeated Kenji Tsumura, running the machines. This time Tatsunori Kishi is in charge of the mountain tribe, charging against Keisuke Hashimoto's artifacts. Kishi has had little experience at the top tables, with a mere top 32 performance at last year's Grand Prix Yokohama. Hashimoto's an amateur, so no matter what, he's walking away from this match with more money. But what price can be put upon a Grand Prix championship?</p>&#10;      <p>Kishi won the die roll and chose to play first. He kept his hand, while Hashimoto sent his back. His hand of six cards drew a frown, but he shrugged and kept it. Kishi kept Hashimoto from developing his board by Oxidizing a <a>Great Furnace</a>, though Hashimoto was able to play a <a>Chromatic Sphere</a>. Kishi played a <a>Goblin Piledriver</a>. Hashimoto prayed for land, but his prayers were unanswered, and passed the turn. Kishi ruthlessly played a <a>Goblin Warchief</a> and attacked with it and the Piledriver, dealing five. Hashimoto could do nothing but watch as he passed the turn and Kishi played a <a>Siege-Gang Commander</a>, attacking for 18. In a mere four turns, Kishi took Game 1.</p>&#10;      <p>The mood was tense as both players reached for their sideboard. Hashimoto removed three <a>Shrapnel Blast</a>s and a Cranial Plate for four copies of <a>Pyroclasm</a>. Kishi replaced his <a>Skirk Prospector</a>s with <a>Naturalize</a>s for more removal. After shuffling up, both players kept their initial hands.</p>&#10;      <p>Hashimoto started by rapidly slamming cards down onto the table. <a>Seat of the Synod</a>, <a>Welding Jar</a>, <a>Ornithopter</a>, <a>Pyrite Spellbomb</a>, and <a>Frogmite</a> came down in a matter of seconds. &quot;Whoa!&quot; Kishi was impressed with the quick start.
