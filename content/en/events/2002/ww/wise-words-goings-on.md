In the months since my return from England, I’ve done a lot of slacking, both in life and in my responsibilities to *Sideboard*, which in reality, are my responsibilities to the **Magic** community and you, the reader. The simple fact is, like me or hate me, you visit this site and visit these pages, providing the hits that keep the *Sideboard* in operation and me in a job. For that, I owe you.

Not too long ago, I started negotiations with the *Sideboard* when a change in payment policy demanded a reworking of my contract. I talked briefly to other sites and looked at the possibility of a change of lifestyle that would come with my getting a ‘real job’, but Thomas Pannell, my editor here at *Sideboard* (who you people didn’t even vote into the Invitational! At least Comer got the alternate slot!) stepped up a lot and I stepped down a little and we hammered out a working agreement that should keep me here for a while and a lot more constantly.

I don’t know if you’ve noticed an increase in the quantity of articles coming from these fingertips of late, but that’s part of the deal. I’m going to be writing a lot more from now on and doing so on a consistent schedule that will see Wise Words posted every Friday, set reviews coming out within a month of Prerelease day and a number of other tasks with both the binary and paper magazines taking up my time. My 30th birthday on February 20th brought with it some fears with regards to the direction my life was heading in and I guess that woke me up to the reality that living day-to-day just isn’t quite enough. This **Magic** beatnik went and got himself a new yuppie approach and while I haven’t entirely embraced corporate America, I’ve moved a step closer toward realizing their needs and mine.

So, with all of this going on, I was asked to complete the *Onslaught* Limited review, something I hadn’t planned on doing. Mostly, I wanted to forego the last five installments, because I’d taken so long to get to them and felt continuing to write them along tribal lines would be irresponsible. Doing so would be misleading with regards to how to draft the format and as a result, I felt like the review, which has received a lot of positive feedback over the years, should be up to a certain standard and that misleading the reader, regardless of intent, wouldn’t be acceptable, as educating erroneously would be worse than not doing so at all. That’s why I did the color rankings in Wise Words: I wanted to give you the alternate viewpoint that more accurately defined the format.

The result of using five weeks worth of columns up is that I’ve fallen behind in my reporting of PT goings on. Sure, there was a bit about Nick and the picture of Trey, but really, a big part of my enjoyment in writing Wise Words comes from my desire to promote the Pro Tour, its personalities and its goings on. Over the last few weeks, a lot’s been happening while I’ve been chatting about colors. This is my attempt to catch up.

### Buh-Bye Masters, Hello Paychecks

When Wizards of the Coast started up the Masters, there’d been talk of simply sending out paychecks to the game’s top players. The Masters was a lot of fun, sure, but it wasn’t practical for a lot of reasons:

1.  Logistical nightmare – The extra day and extra money (some of which has been reinvested into the end of year payout) created havoc for the WotC employees who have more than enough to do in organizing a Pro Tour weekend.
2.  Divided attention – Masters competitors were showing patterns of performing poorly in Pro Tours while in the Masters due to having their attention split between two formats. With part of the Pro Tour’s purpose being to promote stars who would in turn promote the game, this was counterproductive.
3.  Divided attention II – Wizards never wanted the Masters to diminish the Pro Tour’s stature, which it seemed to be doing
4.  Single Elimination - Was a ridiculous way to determine payouts of this caliber in a game that, while skill-based, does involve a luck element. Swiss would not be possible without increased costs and man hours for WotC employees in a way that just wasn’t realistic.

Now, the Masters has been replaced by end of payouts as announced at [http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=sideboard/news/20030226a](/en/articles/event-coverage/2003/02-26-master-series-prize-distribution-change), a move that, at least among pro players, has been praised as good for the game. There are a few who have been hurt by the announcement, namely those players whose recent performances had virtually assured them spots in Masters taking place next season, like Nicolai Herzog and players whose play of late suggests potential for greater earnings, like Kai Budde. Both Chicago finalists have gone on record as saying they believe the changes are good for allowing players to go about being professionals, knowing what’s coming at the end of the year instead of living one Masters at a time and saving WotC a little money and a lot of headache. Sorry for the lack of decklists folks, but this one’s for the good of the game.

### Suspended

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpla02/895.jpg)Former *Sideboard* writer Nick Eisel and recent suspendee Trey Van Cleave have been suspended for three and two years respectively. You probably know all about this by now. Nick’s punishment was handed down for the pre-meditated adding of cards to his GP-Boston sealed deck. Trey, who’d only been back from a suspension for bribery for a little over two months, was suspended in part for cheating by looking at draft neighbor Zvi Mowshowitz’s cards as immortalized in the Josh Bennett photo shown here a couple of weeks ago and before that in the Boston coverage on *Sideboard*.

The Eisel case has been surrounded by conspiracy theory, cultivated in part by Nick himself in a recent article at [www.starcitygames.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20030321000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.starcitygames.com&origin=sideboard_ww_20030321). Let me clear up any possible misunderstandings right now: in no way would it be in Wizards of the Coast’s best interests for Eisel to be caught. The credibility of punishing ‘one of their own’ is outweighed in ridiculous proportion by losing a strategy writer and promotional tool of Eisel’s caliber. Nick, known to many as the ***Magic** Online* Posterboy, was a new face representing a new kind of **Magic**, the online variety, which has helped tremendously in taking the game to the mainstream, something Wizards and Hasbro have both wanted to do for some time. I recommend checking out each angle with opened eyes.

As for Trey, on a personal note, I’m ecstatic. There have been many who have come out against the penalty, listing among the reasons the level of infraction (viewed as extremely minor by many), the questionable use of a digital camera to obtain evidence (and no, the image was not doctored) and the possibility that Trey’s eyes had merely darted in the general vicinity of Zvi’s cards but not necessarily at them.

### Look at the Picture!

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpbos03/947.jpg)This is a man who is cheating. His head is straight ahead, his eyes to the extreme left, and as a person who has met Trey, I can assure you he doesn’t suffer from an affliction of extreme twitches or the like. I’m very willing to give a player who has served their time their fair chance at redemption, but this guy has shown repeatedly since his return from the sidelines that he was just as willing to use sketchy and unsportsmanlike (to a ridiculous degree) behavior to his advantage in his attempts to win and his suspension prevents him from doing so. That means he can’t wrongfully deprive every person he leapfrogs in a tourney’s final standings of what they rightfully deserve, can’t take the individuals he’d face out of prize contention and can’t demean the game any more. This is a good one for the good guys. Screw impartiality.

Oh, on a side note, I predicted at the beginning of the year a previously suspended player would be suspended again. At this point I have to apologize to those who have been asking: I do not provide winning lottery numbers.

### Book Recommendation

Speaking of Hasbro, I just finished *Toy Wars*, a look at the history of Hasbro and its relationship with Mattel, including the latter’s attempted takeover in 1996. For me, it was kind of like reading about an adoptive grandparent and a loveable one at that. Nice to know where we’re coming from now and that it’s a genuinely good place relative to possibilities corporate America provides.

### Magic Online Goes Real Life

Announced at [http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic/magiconline/news031103](/en/articles/feature/2003/03-24-press-release), ***Magic** Online’s* presence is going to be felt in the paper community in a more direct way than ever before with the WotC decision to hold a World Championships qualifier online.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1b/92/1b9225bd145c521d4e1d6dfb733f3d8d25ed1673ea4606077adc915520427c9d.jpg)Is this a big deal? Absolutely. First off, it’s the first ever open qualifier for the World Championships, which until now have only been open to players with ridiculous ratings, enough Pro Tour points to be considered pro players and those fortunate souls who put forth that one superlative effort on Nationals weekend. From a corporate viewpoint, this is a promotional bonanza, offering the possibility of a ***Magic** Online* champion of sorts while showing the world a connection from what have obviously been two separate games in a lot of ways.

People seem worried about the integrity of World Championships and the potential for cheating in a world where players don’t have faces. Yes, Mrs. Kibler, Bob Maher Sr. or little Werner Budde (I doubt there’s actually a Werner Budde, but I’m over the Atlantic and need a third example) could qualify and you know what? It’s one slot. And they’ll get slaughtered. No offense guys.

Yes, a banned player could win. So they won’t get invited. Yes, a lot of little things could go wrong in the worst-case scenario, but think of the benefits! No needing to qualify at Regionals. No having to pay airfare and hotel just to play in the equivalent of a qualifier at Nationals. No more doing so just to get mana screwed out of the tournament. This doesn’t mean the live game is dead, only that the Online game is very much alive and if that needs to be celebrated by allowing for the possibility of one competitor in 300 getting crushed, I’ve got news for you: for every win, there’s a loss. If they don’t deserve to be there, it’ll show up in the standings. I can’t wait for the tournament. Anyone have a deck?

Anyways, I’m about four hours from Brussels and a second stopover before heading to Venice, where I’ll be arriving in the morning, which means I need to get some sleep if I’m going to be in any kind of shape for this Pro Tour. If I can find a way to recharge this battery, there’ll be another Wise Words before next Friday. Until then, have a good week.
