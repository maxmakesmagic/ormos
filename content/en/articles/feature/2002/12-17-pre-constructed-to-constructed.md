### Part 5: Incinerating Infestation

* * *

Alas, here we are in what will be the last article in the "Pre-constructed to Constructed" series. But be strong, your family would want it that way. And take heart in the news that you will be able to purge your hurtful feelings in a barrage of chaos and death. That's right, we're finishing up with Infestation, the red *Seventh EditionTM* theme deck.

I said [last time](/en/articles/feature/2002/12-03-pre-constructed-to-constructed) that Decay was my favorite theme deck, and that was true. Infestation does an equally good job of displaying the themes of red cards: spells that damage your opponent and creatures directly, "fast creatures" (that is, cheaper than normal for their power or toughness but at a cost, or else creatures that are costed properly but that have the haste ability to attack sooner than normal), spells **and** creatures that destroy your opponent's lands and artifacts, and (for reasons that R&D has sworn never to reveal) the ability to destroy Walls cheaply. What Infestation doesn't do as well as some of the other theme decks is to display the various themes of its color and still present a viable deck. Of course, that's why we're here.

The common gripe with Infestation is that it goes after your opponents' land, but not really enough to cripple them. You end up being a bit of a nuisance, but not enough of one to justify the number of cards in your deck that go after lands. So, for the first variation on Infestation let's just strip that stuff out, like so:

**Focus:** We pull the land destruction to focus more on the hair-pulling and eye-gouging aspects of red cards. The "Infestation" in this case becomes goblins, goblins, and more goblins. In fact, we're looking to benefit from the fact that you have mounds of goblins in your deck. Use your Goblin Matrons, at least once, to get an Embermage Goblin, and then run the chain of those out as quickly as you can.

Assuming you already have the Embermage Goblins off and running, the Matrons will often-times want to go and get another Matron, unless you feel you have enough Goblins out that it's time to go and get a Reckless One. Remember that once a Matron has come into play she has served a large part of her purpose; don't be afraid to cast Reckless Charge on her to force your opponent to take damage or trade a creature.

The Shocks, Lightning Blasts, and Spitting Earths are there to keep the ground free of blockers so that your goblins can keep going for the throat. Once your opponents get low enough on life, you can send the artillery directly at their noggins.

Note that the wall destruction has also been pulled from the deck. This is playing the incredibly good odds that your opponents aren't playing any walls in their decks. If they are, either shrug and say "good game" or stay patient and wait for a combination of your direct damage spells and/or multiple Embermage Goblins to seal the deal.

The other direction to go with Infestation isn't for everyone. Land destruction is a theme that seems to draw as much division amongst players as pure control. That is, people think it's great or they fume at the thought of it. While I happen to fall into the pro-fume camp, I'd be derelict in my duty if I didn't show you how this might go.

**Focus:** The thing you need to understand about land destruction if you're going to have any success at all is this: you aren't going to nail **all** of their land. It just isn't going to happen that often. Yes, occasionally it does, and you'll know you've accomplished complete land lockdown when half of your opponent's sentences are edited in the chat window. However, the real objective with land destruction is simply to slow your opponent down so that they aren't able to deploy their own cards the way they intend to. It's like blitzing in football--you don't get a sack all the time, but when you don't you're still hoping to disrupt the offense.

To that end, the ideal development of a game is to drop a threat or two in the first two turns, and then spend turns 3-5 (at least) casting spells that attack your opponent's lands. Shocks and Spitting Earths are for dealing with the early creatures that your opponent might get out. Meanwhile, the creatures you've gotten out are rushing to get as much damage as possible in before you finish them off with a last flash of direct damage.

**Sideboard:** The sideboard works much as before, although be forewarned that you're going to have serious trouble against a deck full of cheap threats of it's own. You're counting on disrupting your opponent's plans, but if they can survive on 1-3 lands then you're in for a long night. The Dwarven Blastminers give you recurring help against painlands and the like, but be careful not to bring them in against decks that feature the new "fetchlands" like Polluted Delta; you'll never nail one of those.
