This is his first time in Australia though, so after dinner he put on his super-slick polyester leisure suit and went on the prowl. His first stop was a jazz club in Chinatown, where he walked out with a smoking hot Chinese babe on each arm. Then they gained entrance to a dance club on Swanston Street, where Soh completely tore up the dance floor with his styling disco moves. Nobody does the hustle like Terry. John Travolta ain't got nothin' on him. At the end of the night, he had received so many messages from people looking to hook up that his cell phone battery died, so his alarm didn't go off this morning. Luckily his breakfast date knocked on his door at 9AM, or else he might have missed Day 2 entirely.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>And that, my friends, is why it's dangerous to be a baller.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="9"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, November 6: 11:39 a.m. - Round 10: Andrew Vance vs. James Zhang</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmelb05/r10_zhang.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>James Zhang</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>James Zhang and his <a>Psychatog</a> deck are still undefeated on the weekend, approaching the point where drawing in for the remaining rounds would be a real possibility if he's given opponents that can do the same. Andrew Vance is a 28-year-old public servant who is playing on of the many Boros decks lurking at the top tables. Tomoharu Saito has been grumping about the amount of <a>Kataki, War's Wage</a> laden decks at the top tables, a terrible card for his Affinity deck to deal with, but this time the red and white cards would have to overcome Dr. Teeth. This is a matchup so bad that last weekend Tsuyoshi Fujita actually sided out three lands while playing Antoine Ruel just to try and get lucky.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Savannah Lions</a> from Vance ate a <a>Force Spike</a> on their way to the grumper, but a second turn <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> resolved. Another red wizard Leaked his way to the graveyard as well, but his little buddy got in there for one and <a>Lightning Helix</a> to Zhang's dome put his life at fourteen. <a>Psychatog</a> hit the board on turn 4 for Zhang and immediately illustrated just why this matchup is so terrible for Boros players. Once Dr. Teeth hits the board, the whole concept of &quot;attacking&quot; becomes more of a a philosophical concept for the Red/White player rather than something that has any bearing on the real world. Vance's little Lavamancer tried and tried and tried to whittle down Zhang's life to a point where leftover burn could kill him and miraculously succeeded. Zhang simply couldn't find quite enough card drawing to finish Vance off with a pair of Togs, now did he pluck the necessary <a>Smother</a> to dispatch the Lavamancer before it contributed to his demise.</p>&#10;      <p>Vance 1 - Zhang 0</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmelb05/r10_vance.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Andrew Vance</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Zhang played first in game 2 and had the very Australian second turn play of <a>Boomerang</a>ing Vance's <a>Sacred Foundry</a> that he'd just paid three life to put into play untapped. Sadly, Vance had Isamaru in play on turn 1 and <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> on turn 2, putting a heap of pressure on the mana screwed Zhang, who was also getting poked in the eye each turn by his own <a>Cephalid Coliseum</a>. Being at four life by the time you put your fourth land on the board must be an unsettling experience. Zhang survived a couple more turns on the back of countermagic and some creature removal, but unless Zhang's luck completely flipped and Vance drew all lands from ther eon out, the outcome was hardly in doubt. One <a>Firebolt</a> to the dome and a <a>Molten Rain</a> later, and James Zhang had his first loss of the weekend, while Boros notched an improbable win from what is usually an awful matchup.</p>&#10;
