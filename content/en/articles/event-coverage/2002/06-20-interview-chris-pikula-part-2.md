**Wachter:** What exactly happened at The World Series of Poker? Everyone knows you had a good outing but there's been a lot of confusion from people who visited the website and were like "I didn't even see him listed, he's lying!"

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MI00/778.jpg)

**Pikula:** There's some confusion about The World Series of Poker. The World Series of Poker is a month and a half long series of Poker tournaments, where every couple of days there's a premiere tournament. There are about 25 of them every year, with different formats and different buy-in amounts. They're all considered prestigious tournaments, but the final event which is a $10,000 buy-in no limit Hold 'em tournament is what the general public thinks of when they think "World Series of Poker". The final event is considered the main event, but these other 25 tournaments before it are considered... those other tournaments are almost all bigger than any other tournaments there are. Of those 25 tournaments, there are probably 25 of the 40 biggest tournaments a year in Poker. I came in tenth... I made the final table at one of them, which is kind of like the equivalent of a Pro Tour or Grand Prix Top 8... somewhere between a Pro Tour Top 8 and a Grand Prix Top 8. Unfortunately the prize structure is much steeper than it is in **Magic**. First place was $96,000, and tenth place was $3,700, so it would be like at a Pro Tour if first place got $80,000, and eighth place got $2,000 or something. It's pretty steep.

**Wachter:** How would you explain the Poker-**Magic** connection?

**Pikula:** I don't think it's unusual. People who are good at games tend to be good at games. I think **Magic** players are able to do well at Poker just because they're smart people. The big difference between Poker and **Magic** is that in a **Magic** tournament, you want to get paired up against someone bad every round, because you want to win. But, when you're playing **Magic** normally, you're playing with your friends. And if you're good at **Magic**, you end up having a lot of friends who are good at **Magic**. Usually when you're doing a draft for fun, even if it's practice for a Pro Tour, you're doing it with good players. In Poker, it's very easy, and what you want to do is... all Poker is competitive. There's no Poker for fun. What you do is you end up going and trying to find the dumbest, most desperate people you can find because you want to take their money. Since **Magic** players are just smarter in general and have a pretty good understanding of games and decent math skills, they're very quickly able to learn how to play Poker well enough to beat average players.

**Wachter:** Over the past year or two the Americans haven't been doing as well in **Magic**, and many say that's because of Poker. 

**Pikula:** I don't agree. Not many of the top American players from before play Poker now. I think Americans aren't doing as well because the other countries have caught up. Also, because the United States has had **Magic** longer, a lot of the good players are just sick of it now, and they've moved on. **Magic** came along a little later to get big in the other countries, and three years from now those countries will get sick of it and some other country will get big. So I don't think it has anything to do with Poker.

**Wachter:** For those who don't know, explain what Straight Edge is and why you follow that lifestyle. 

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>A big part of Straight Edge is when everyone else is young and you're sixteen or seventeen or eighteen and everyone else is starting to drink and do drugs, you're trying to make people aware that you don't have to.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

**Pikula:** Straight Edge is a movement started by this band Minor Threat in the 80s as this hardcore punk type thing where you don't do drugs or drink. I don't call myself Straight Edge now because it's kind of like a thing where when you get older, it kind of loses its point. A big part of Straight Edge is when everyone else is young and you're sixteen or seventeen or eighteen and everyone else is starting to drink and do drugs, you're trying to make people aware that you don't have to. Like you don't have to go to college and get drunk every day. You don't have to get drunk at high school parties on weekends. You can still be a normal person and a cool guy and have friends without doing any of this. When you're younger that's a lot more important, but as you get older people tend to drink more responsibly, and you're not going to change people's opinions on it as much. I still don't drink or do drugs or smoke cigarettes or anything, but it's not as important to call myself Straight Edge. Like I don't wear Straight Edge t-shirts anymore. It's also led a lot of people to become vegetarian or vegan...

**Wachter:** That's my next question actually. Why are you a vegetarian?

**Pikula:** I'm vegetarian because I just don't really see the point of killing things when you don't have to. If I had a choice between eating a peanut butter and jelly sandwich for lunch or murdering an animal, and eating it, I'm clearly just going to make myself a peanut butter and jelly sandwich. I think it sucks that some people have to have the job of killing animals for food. It's horrible for the environment, it's not healthy for you, at least not in the amount of meat that people eat in the United States on average. A small amount of meat, sure it's not going to be unhealthy for you, but the majority of people United States eat way too much meat. It's terrible for the environment, and a huge, inefficient use of energy. Mostly, it's just that I don't want to kill things. I'm vegetarian and not vegan partly because... I do feel like I should maybe be vegan. It's somewhat hypocritical and I admit I'm a little weak on this, but it's really hard to be vegan when you travel as much as I do. Also, in a perfect world there's really not a use to being vegan, because there are companies now who are starting to produce dairy products without making the animals suffer. Like, you can have a dairy farm where the animals don't have lives that suck. You can have a chicken farm where you can get eggs without making the chickens' lives suck. It's just hard to do that on a large scale, so it may be a long time before we can move on to that point.

**Wachter:** You've been to a lot of Invitationals, and as someone who hasn't had consistent finishes in a while, do you think you still deserve to be in it? For example, last year Ben Rubin had two Masters Championships, and did not make the Invitational, whereas you, Dave Price and Gary Wise did make it. 

**Pikula:** I think almost by definition... in the All Star game in every major sport, basketball, hockey, I don't know if it's in football, and baseball, the fans vote for who they want to go to the All Star game, which is essentially what happens with the Invitational. In that sense, we deserve it just as much as... I mean Cal Ripken went to the All Star game every year after he was one of the worst third basemen in the American League. I'll admit right now that Ben Rubin's been a better **Magic** player than me for two years, but everyone else I'm sure will admit that I made the Invitational more entertaining for the people watching the Invitational and the people playing in the Invitational than everyone else around. I think I've been fairly important to **Magic** in a historic sense, just not in the current sense. If you want to reward... I mean they've changed the invite system to make better efforts to reward people who have had current success, and Ben fell just a little short probably because the Masters Series in my mind is not a particularly impressive feat. Like, you won a 32 person tournament, often which is half filled with people who didn't even prepare. I don't mean to make the Masters Series sound bad, but it's not nearly as impressive as wining a Pro Tour.

**Wachter:** On the topic of Invitationals, what made you decide to create Meddling Mage?

**Pikula:** The card I actually submitted was terrible, but that was partly because I wanted to submit a card, I wanted to submit a creature that a blue deck could get down on the table against a combo deck and just start an early clock that wouldn't destroy you that turn you cast it. I basically wanted to create a creature that when you get him into play, he's going to be able to stop them from doing something.

**Wachter:** Wasn't the original version something like you name the card, then sacrifice it to counter that spell?

**Pikula:** Well there were a lot of versions. Even the version that got submitted was just one of many variations which I was just kind of brainstorming to Mark (Rosewater). Mark was kind of like "Well just submit one and we'll fix it" and I was like "Fine, take this one." Part of the problem was I think what I wanted to submit was a Spiketail creature basically, like the Hatchling that you sacrifice to Mana Leak or whatever. Mark knew those were coming out and I didn't, so he was trying to lead me away from doing that because he was already making those cards. I never thought they'd make Meddling Mage as good as it was, so I never thought to submit something that good. 

**Wachter:** Are you happy with how it turned out?

**Pikula:** Oh, I'm really happy with how it turned out. Actually, it ended up that it's not that good. It was really good in the Invasion Block Constructed format, but that was mostly because there wasn't a huge variety of cards, so Meddling Mage got played a lot. It was also a big part of why I got invited to the Invitational I think, because it was right after a block of Pro Tour Qualifiers where a lot of people probably won spots on the Pro Tour with Meddling Mage. It has been good, it was really good at Worlds in Extended one year. I played it and made Top 32 at a Pro Tour this year, and I was playing for Top 8 at one point. It's a really good card, and I'm really happy with it. I wish it was better in Standard. 

**Wachter:** What is it like to have your own card and have kids ask you to sign it?

**Pikula:** Obviously it's really cool. Brian Kibler always says it's cooler to win the Invitational than it is to win a Pro Tour. I don't know if that's really true, but it's a lot closer than most people think. Playing with your own card is cool, and signing it for kids is a lot of fun.

**Wachter:** What was it like doing commentary at Pro Tours?

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MI00/861.jpg"><br><i>To say that Chris is a character would be something of an understatement.</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

**Pikula:** I really enjoyed it for the most part. It's a lot harder than most people think it is. You hear a lot of stuff from people when you make mistakes, but they don't realize that you're lacking certain information and you're trying to be entertaining at the same time. Sometimes you make a lot of mistakes and sometimes people give you a hard time, but it's really fun. The two commentaries where Dave (Price) was in the Top 8 at Pro Tour LA where he won and then U.S. Nationals were really fun to do.

**Wachter:** Because you were rooting him on?

**Pikula:** Right, and those were probably the best commentaries I did. 

**Wachter:** Most people still regard you as the most entertaining commentator ever. What do you think you did that others don't?

**Pikula:** I'm not sure really. I don't know; that's a tough one to answer. Some commentators, when they try to say something funny, it seems like they're so obviously... it doesn't feel genuine. Like they're going to ridiculous effort to be funny, and it's not like I ever did that. 

**Wachter:** So it's better to approach it as an impulsive, on-the-fly sort of thing?

**Pikula:** I feel like genuinely funny rather than funny because you're putting so much effort into being funny works better.

**Wachter:** Your commentary from the 1998 United States Nationals is immortalized because everyone has that tape. Do you find that people still come up to you and go...

**Pikula:** I actually signed one of the tapes! 

**Wachter:** What was it like being a "**Magic** icon" five years ago as opposed to now? Is it different in any ways?

**Pikula:** I was better five years ago, because five years ago I was still really good at **Magic**!

**Wachter:** Right, but I mean as far as being a "**Magic** celebrity", was it different back then?

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>Back then there was no Masters Series, and you couldn't measure people by money.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

**Pikula:** It was definitely different back then because back then... it all comes down to money now. Back then there was no Masters Series, and you couldn't measure people by money. It was just like, what people thought of you, and the people who were the **Magic** icons back then, everyone knew who they were, and everyone liked them. People weren't making $100,000. There weren't Masters Series where you'd just randomly make $25,000.

**Wachter:** So then did it make more sense back then because there was less money to be a nicer guy, so that you could get more respect? Whereas now, there's more money, so...

**Pikula:** No. It would never make sense to me to cheat.

**Wachter:** I don't mean you specifically. You're saying that the money wasn't as prevalent back then, so respect was more important?

**Pikula:** No... somehow it just felt different. I don't know. Back then it felt like it was somehow special because it was like "These are people who are Hall of Famers" or something. No one could tangibly say why they were one of the top players. Now, I feel like they're just this huge list of all these people who have won all this money, plus it used to be that there had only been like ten Pro Tours, and this many people had four Top 8s, this many people had three Top 8s. Now there's been so many tournaments that everyone has X good finishes. There's Kai (Budde) and Jon (Finkel) and everyone else is kind of... I don't feel there are people now who... it was very clear who the icons of **Magic** were then. Now it's like after Kai, I don't know who the icons of **Magic** are.

**Wachter:** Who were the icons back then, just for reference for people who may not be familiar?

**Pikula:** Me, Dave Price, Mark Justice, Brian Hacker, Finkel, Mike Long.

**Wachter:** Some of those guys are still active.

**Pikula:** Right. I'm sure I'm forgetting some. Brian Weissman, Matt Place, Olle Rade, Tommi Hovi, people like that. All the people who went to those first Invitationals. I guess it was just that there were less good players, and those good players, there was a larger gap between them and the next group of players. Now, there's just Kai who's ridiculous, and I don't know who the best ten players are.

**Wachter:** So the playing field is more even now?

**Pikula:** There's more good players, so the playing field is less stratified. 

**Wachter:** You have a reputation for being quite the storyteller at Pro Tours.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MI00/879.jpg"><br><i>Old nemeses.</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

**Pikula:** I am quite the storyteller, but I'm a very bad impromptu storyteller. Like people will come up to me and ask me to tell a good story, and I can't. But if something fires me up, I take hold of something and just run with it. I tell a lot of stories at events, but never under orders to tell a story.

**Wachter:** Any particular reason you enjoy telling stories?

**Pikula:** No, not really. I don't know, I guess I just have a good way of making things that happen commonly into sounding more funny.

**Wachter:** What exactly is your job?

**Pikula:** I'm a derivatives trader. I'm a specialist on the floor of the American Stock Exchange, I buy and sell financial derivative products, which are basically products that are derived from the values of one stock or newer stocks.

**Wachter:** A lot of people say it's impossible now to stay on the gravy train while maintaining a normal job. Is that true?

**Pikula:** It's pretty hard because of the number of tournaments that are not in the United States. It's always been hard for people who don't live in the U.S., but now there's so many tournaments not in the U.S. every year that it's hard to even have enough vacation time to go to every tournament. And if you don't go to every tournament, it's really hard to stay on the gravy train. Sometimes good players go a long time without a good finish, and the only way to stay on the gravy train without a good finish is to go to every Pro Tour.

**Wachter:** As someone who has a job and as a result hasn't been doing as well lately, is it frustrating that people who don't have regular jobs are able to stay on the Pro Tour while you have to struggle? Or is that just a sacrifice you have to make?

**Pikula:** I don't think it's frustrating. Every once in a while you're like "I wish I could play more **Magic**", but I'm not good enough at **Magic** to be better than... like if I did nothing but play **Magic** I don't know how good I'd be. Five years ago, when I was really good, it was because we were a lot better than everyone else. Everyone else hadn't been playing as long, or the game wasn't developed and there wasn't as much information out there. Now, even if I played every day, I don't know if I'd be one of the Top 20 players again. It's their choice, and I'm 27 now. When I was 22, I could play all the **Magic** I wanted, and there were other people with jobs who lost to me.

**Wachter:** So it's like a cycle.

**Pikula:** Right.

**Wachter:** Has your recent marriage interfered with **Magic** at all?

**Pikula:** Only a little bit. I wouldn't say "interfered" because I'm not as into **Magic** now. I don't feel like "Man, I'm married, I wish I could play more **Magic**!" 

**Wachter:** Why are you not as into **Magic** as you used to be? Is there some other factor involved besides your job, or is it just a matter of getting older and less interested?

**Pikula:** I would say that because I know that given my other commitments like my job, and I'm married, I won't be able to be a top **Magic** player again. I can still be good, I'm doing fine at Nationals right now. I can still be a good **Magic** player, but it'll be hard for me to be a great **Magic** player again, and that just... I've been playing for a long time and sure, it's still fun, but at one point I was a great **Magic** player and now I'm not. It's hard to play and lose when you used to never lose.

**Wachter:** So because you can't be on top the game loses some of its draw?

**Pikula:** Right.

**Wachter:** How has moving across the street from Neutral Ground helped?

**Pikula:** That has been a huge factor in why I still play the amount of **Magic** that I do. One of the good things about my job is that I don't work very long hours. I get home from work by five every day pretty much, while other people who have jobs come home at six or seven, eat dinner with their wife, and that's it. I can go home, eat dinner with my wife, be done by 6:30, and then go to Neutral Ground and game. So yeah, living half a block from Neutral Ground definitely makes gaming more of an option.

**Wachter:** How much do you test for Pro Tours now as opposed to the past? Is there anything you do differently?

**Pikula:** Yeah, now I don't test (laughs). The New Jersey kids say I'm a very good person to test with because I pay attention to the results, I'm not biased and I'm not stubborn, but I really don't test enough. 

**Wachter:** Are you washed up?

**Pikula:** I am semi-washed up. Yeah, I'm not as good as I used to be. A lot of people are better than me now, and I make mistakes, and I don't practice. Yeah, I'm not a great **Magic** player anymore. I'm still a good **Magic** player.

**Wachter:** Do you feel like you still have something to prove in **Magic**?

**Pikula:** A little bit. Only because there are so many new **Magic** players who never knew I was good! Like, they've never seen me be good at **Magic**! So I'd like to actually convince some of these people that at one point I was good at **Magic**.
