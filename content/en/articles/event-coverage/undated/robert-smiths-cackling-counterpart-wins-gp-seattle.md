## Grand Prix Seattle 2012

![](https://i.ormos.uk/82/47/8247dd6a69987202fc3d38f18d8397b5fb4f6993c0f38c66407c4d3da1fa1e39.jpg)

Early reports were mistaken, and it was not, in fact, the legendary front man of The Cure who Top 8'd Grand Prix Seattle. He may have entered the final draft eclipsed by the star power of Pro Tour Amsterdam Champion Paul Rietzl and reigning Player of the Year Owen Turtenwald, but quiet amateur Robert Smith acquitted himself like a pro and walked away a Champion.

Smith posted an 8-1 record during Day 1's sealed deck play, then followed it up with a 5-1 performance at draft. At a table marked by a wealth of powerful decks, Smith assembled a blue-black control deck with an awful lot going for it. The quarterfinals were a massacre, with all four matches ending in quick 2-0 results, and both Turtenwald and Rietzl fell in combat. Smith and his semifinal oppponent Jason Huang both earned qualifications for Pro Tour Avacyn Restored in Barcelona, but Smith's sights were set higher. After splitting the first two games, Huang gambled on a risky seven for the deciding game and paid the price.

Meanwhile in the other bracket, Californian semi-pro Iain Bartolomei was locked up with James Nguyen, whose red-black aggro deck was a favourite to win the event. Bartolomei's blue-white tempo build won the third game on the back of [Dearly Departed](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dearly+Departed) . He was forced into a third game against Smith, as well. Smith resolved a [Geistcatcher's Rig](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geistcatcher%27s+Rig) to kill [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) , and though it got sidelined by [Bonds of Faith](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bonds+of+Faith) , a backbreaking [Cackling Counterpart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cackling+Counterpart) made sure he'd have two more two-for-ones against Bartolomei's many fliers, and the trophy was his.

Congratulations to Robert Smith, Grand Prix Seattle Champion!

## Quarterfinal - Iain Bartolomei vs. Henry Romero

*by Steve Sadin* 

Iain Bartolomei is a longtime grinder who has five PTQ wins under his belt. But despite Bartolomei's proclivity to win qualifiers, he has been unable to come away with the grand prize in any of his three PTQ Top 8s this season.

But with a Top 8 berth here at Grand Prix Seattle-Tacoma, Bartolomei finds himself a mere match away from a coveted invitation to Pro Tour Avacyn Restored in Barcelona.

In order to earn that invitation, Iain's going to need to get past Henry Romero – a high school student who recently won his first PTQ (qualifying himself for Pro Tour Avacyn Restored in Barcelona).

While the up and coming Romero doesn't need to worry about advancing to the semifinals to earn an invitation, he would still love to celebrate his first PTQ win with his first Grand Prix win this weekend.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/dd/2b/dd2b86293909735766cfd0b37e95c1df0386f37001a9304702a22cfaa3a37ac8.jpg)

"How's your deck?" asked Bartolomei.

"Ehh, we'll find out. How about yours?" replied Romero.

"It's the best deck I've drafted all day."

### Game 1

Bartolomei won the roll, and Romero thought for a bit before keeping his opening hand.

It soon became clear why Romero had agonized over his keep, as Bartolomei was able to play a [Silverchase Fox](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverchase+Fox) , [Nephalia Seakite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Seakite) , and a [Silverclaw Griffin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverclaw+Griffin) all before Romero had played his first spell.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4b/04/4b0472e34586ad34535538b4050fd5b9b1659031cdfd88affcff473171a59e70.jpg)

*Iain Bartolomei*

[Dead Weight](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dead+Weight) , into [Morkrut Banshee](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Morkrut+Banshee) helped even the score a bit – but Bartolomei's [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) was more than enough for him to seal the deal against the spell-light Romero.

Iain Bartolomei 1 – Henry Romero 0

### Game 2

Romero got off to an early lead in game two with [Darkthicket Wolf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Darkthicket+Wolf) , [Markov Patrician](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Markov+Patrician) , and a [Dead Weight](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dead+Weight) to take out Bartolomei's first creature – a turn four [Moon Heron](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Moon+Heron) .

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4b/04/4b0472e34586ad34535538b4050fd5b9b1659031cdfd88affcff473171a59e70.jpg)

*Henry Romero*

But a [Silverclaw Griffin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverclaw+Griffin) , followed by [Dearly Departed](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dearly+Departed) allowed Bartolomei to stabilize on five life.

Romero had a [Victim of Night](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Victim+of+Night) sitting in his hand that would have allowed him to win the game if he ever drew a second [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) , but without the ability to cast his best removal spell, it was only a matter of time before Romero died to a [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) , and a [Feeling of Dread](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feeling+of+Dread) .

Iain Bartolomei 2 – Henry Romero 0

## Quarterfinal - Jason Huang vs. Paul Rietzl

*by Josh Bennett* 

Pro Tour Amsterdam champion Paul Rietzl was cautiously optimistic about his deck for the Top 8, calling it good but not great.

"I think if this had been on **Magic** Online, I would've drafted a 3-0 deck. Mono-red was out there, I just couldn't bring myself to abandon green early enough. It's different when it's a Top 8."

His opponent was Jason Huang, who moved with nervous excitement. He had put together a bit of blue-green beatdown.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e6/a5/e6a5c09a99785532b2e82f1fa1b93dd0527c9bf1f9a16c39fd97daa53b546351.jpg)

### Game 1

In defiance of their decklists, they both staggered off the starting blocks. Huang, on the play, didn't have anything to do until a turn-four [Grasp of Phantoms](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grasp+of+Phantoms) on Rietzl's turn-three [Lambholt Elder](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lambholt+Elder) . Rietzl replayed it without having a fourth land. Huang had a fifth, and a [Hollowhenge Beast](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hollowhenge+Beast) to go with it.

Rietzl passed his turn to flip over his Elder. Unfortunately for him, Huang had [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage) , flashing back [Grasp of Phantoms](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grasp+of+Phantoms) for a discount. Rietzl took five and offed the [Snap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snap) caster at end of turn with [Geistflame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geistflame) . He replayed his Elder, and passed again.

Another hit from the [Beast](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Beast) brought Rietzl to ten. Huang restocked with [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination) , and locked down the Elder with [Claustrophobia](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Claustrophobia) . Rietzl drew for his turn and stared in agony at his cards. Eventually, he spent his three mana on [Ashmouth Hound](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashmouth+Hound) and [Blazing Torch](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blazing+Torch) . Huang hit him to five, and [Battleground Geist](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Battleground+Geist) tightened the noose.

Finally, a fourth land for Rietzl. He played out [Crossway Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crossway+Vampire) and gave the Torch to his [Ashmouth Hound](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashmouth+Hound) . Huang didn't bother sending the [Beast](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Beast) , he just swung in the air for three, then cast [Stitched Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stitched+Drake) . Rietzl held back on his mana, but was dead to the incoming attack.

Huang 1 - Rietzl 0

![](https://i.ormos.uk/24/eb/24eb609cfbad0bdcd1d2afd817daa6a3a8c7c2e47cdadc3ca3cd8e1b0c9e61b3.jpg)

*Paul Rietzl*

### Game 2

"I think this match might be more important to me than it is to you. I still need to qualify for Barcelona."

"Well, it's pretty important for me too. I need the points to get to Platinum."

After 17 rounds of play, a field 1,160 has been cut down to just two: Iain Bartolomei, a California native who is piloting a Blue-White flier deck that features [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) , and [Dearly Departed](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dearly+Departed) -- and Robert Smith, a Canadian playing a controlling Blue-Black deck full of good removal spells.

## Top 5 Cards

### #1 - [Bloodline Keeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodline+Keeper)

![Bloodline Keeper](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Bloodline+Keeper)

If you asked someone what rares they wanted to open in *Innistrad* / *Dark Ascension* Sealed Deck, odds are [Bloodline Keeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodline+Keeper) would be on their list. Top 8'er Owen Turtenwald was the [Bloodline Keeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodline+Keeper) poster boy this weekend. Not only did he have it in his sealed pool, but he managed to steer his first draft on Day 2 in such a way that he was the recipient of a SIXTH PICK [Bloodline Keeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodline+Keeper) . I just want you to picture that for a minute. Everyone at the table knows which pack has the Keeper in it, because it is double-faced, and Turtenwald watches as it makes the long circuit around the table, finally ending up in his pile.

The ability of double-faced cards in general to signal to the table what colors you're drafting is something pros touched on again and again. Given the option, most would happily choose a less-powerful double-faced card. That small bit of public information has made *Innistrad* / *Dark Ascension* a much more cooperative draft format than its predecessors.

### #2 - [Stromkirk Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stromkirk+Captain) and the *Dark Ascension* Lords

![Stromkirk Captain](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Stromkirk+Captain)

In contrast to the relative safety offered by a double-faced pick, these cards are high-risk, high-reward first picks. They not only give your draft a plan, but the games where you draw them early are often devastating. Think about the no-win endgames offered by Direcraf Captain, where your army of zombies is killing the opponent no matter what. Or ask Luis Scott-Vargas how it feels to be on the wrong side of a double- [Immerwolf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Immerwolf) draw. Jon Finkel and Craig Wescoe both held up [Drogskol Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drogskol+Captain) as a card they'd like to hang their draft on.

Be sure to take a look at semifinalist James Nguyen's Top 8 deck. It's a thing of beauty.

### #3 - [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists)

![Dungeon Geists](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Dungeon+Geists)

The total package. The removal spell that kills your opponent. [Phantom Monster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Phantom+Monster) grew up and got mad, and the result is this dreamcrusher. In a format as fast as *Innistrad* / *Dark Ascension* Limited, games often come down to race situations, where cards like [Moment of Heroism](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Moment+of+Heroism) can really shine. The high tempo is the reason why [Silent Departure](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silent+Departure) is the premier blue common. But no card reverses a race like [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) .

Finalist Iain Bartolomei's deck is a streamlined tempo machine. [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) is the headliner, but so much of his deck is designed to dictate the pace of play. [Silent Departure](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silent+Departure) and [Feeling of Dread](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feeling+of+Dread) to keep the opponent off-pace. Two [Midnight Guard](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Midnight+Guard) s working overtime on offence and defence. [Nephalia Seakite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Seakite) to catch the opponent looking. Interestingly, he played eighteen land to make sure he'd be able to afford his high-end spells and expensive flashback costs.

### #4 - [Geistcatcher's Rig](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geistcatcher%27s+Rig)

![Geistcatcher's Rig](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Geistcatcher%27s+Rig)

Talking about six-drops in sealed, Owen Turtenwald said "You'll never cut a [Geistcatcher's Rig](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geistcatcher%27s+Rig) , because if you're winning, you're going to play against a lot of [Bloodline Keeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodline+Keeper) s." Luis Scott-Vargas echoed this, saying that in this format you need your six-drops to affect the board right away. But it's not just a sealed deck all-star.

Champion Robert Smith played just ten creatures in his deck, but he made sure they all counted. The Rig was particularly well-positioned against Bartolomei's air force. Not only a removal spell, but a big body that could fight through Bartolomei's smaller creatures. In the deciding game three of the finals, Bartolomei dropped [Dungeon Geists](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dungeon+Geists) hoping for an early advantage, but [Geistcatcher's Rig](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geistcatcher%27s+Rig) flipped the script on him, and was the beginning of the end.

### #5 - [Cackling Counterpart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cackling+Counterpart)

![Cackling Counterpart](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Cackling+Counterpart)

If Smith's [Geistcatcher's Rig](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geistcatcher%27s+Rig) was backbreaking in game 3, his [Cackling Counterpart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cackling+Counterpart) must've dissolved Bartolomei's skeleton completely. Bartolomei weathered the first volley as best he could, with flashed back [Saving Grasp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Saving+Grasp) for his flier and [Silent Departure](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silent+Departure) for the token, but a third 4/5 [Nekrataal](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nekrataal) is just bananas.

The fact that it's an instant dovetailed nicely with the rest of Smith's deck. Between [Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice) , [Forbidden Alchemy](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forbidden+Alchemy) , [Tragic Slip](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tragic+Slip) , [Nephalia Seakite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Seakite) and [Undying Evil](https://web.archive.org/web/20220927201211/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Undying+Evil) , Smith is happy to play a reactive game. There's so much value to be had, and Smith's toolbox keeps the enemy guessing. It just goes to show that *Innistrad*'s synergies run very deep.
