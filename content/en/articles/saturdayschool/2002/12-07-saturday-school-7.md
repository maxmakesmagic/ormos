* * *

The first few questions each week will now be labeled "Basic Questions," and will cover rules that most experienced players already understand. Fear not, for the tough questions follow.

Oh, if you're interested, Rune's favorite card is Mother of Runes, even though he says it doesn't really look like his mother.

### Basic Questions

* * *

**Q:** I attack with a **Nantuko Husk** and my friend blocks with a **Wirewood Savage** and gives it +1/+1 using **Vitality Charm**. In response I sacrifice the Husk to give itself +2/+2 before dying to kill both of them, but my friend said Nantuko Husk dies before getting the +2/+2. I say it kills both. Who's right?  
 --Roland Maio

**A:** Your friend is right. Since a creature needs to be in play to put combat damage assignments on the stack, it can't do this when you've sacrificed it. 

If a creature puts its damage assignments on the stack, and is sacrificed afterwards, it will still deal its damage as assigned. But if goes away before the damage is assigned, it doesn't get to assign any damage. You can assign two damage with the Husk and then sacrifice it to itself, but it will still only do two damage.

* * *

**Q:** Can I prevent the loss of life from a **Guiltfeeder** with a **Circle of Protection: Black**? Does that mean taking damage and losing life is the same thing?

**A:** Taking damage and losing life are not the same thing. Losing life is the end result of unprevented damage, but it can also be caused by other effects.

Circles of Protection can only prevent actual damage. Loss of life and life payments can't be prevented or altered by damage prevention (see "Damage" in the glossary of the [Comprehensive Rulebook](https://web.archive.org/web/20021207000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=/magic/comprules&origin=mtgcom_daily_rh49)).

So no, you cannot prevent Guiltfeeder's Ability with CoP: Black, since it is not dealing damage.

### *Onslaught* Questions

* * *

**Q:** I have a question about the card **Gratuitous Violence**. How does it work if I have more than one copy in play? Does the damage add or multiply, or are they not cumulative at all?  
 --Clinton Worle, Idaho Falls, ID

**A:** The damage multiplies. Gratuitous Violence has a replacement effect, which modifies the damage being dealt. You apply the effects in order, so the first one will double the damage, and the second one will double the doubled damage, for a total of four times the original damage.

* * *

**Q:** Let's say I have a 5/5 trampling **Kavu Titan** in play, plus two **Gratuitous Violence**. So if I hit you with my Titan, you'll take 20 damage. When I attack, you block with a 5/5 of your own. How much trample damage can I do to you?

**A:** Gratuitous Violence doubles the damage when it resolves, not when you assign it. You only assign the unmodified damage (5), and since this does not exceed the blocker's toughness (5), nothing tramples over. When the damage resolves, the damage is quadrupled from the Gratuitous Violences, and the blocker takes 20 damage, the defending player still takes none.

If the player had blocked with a 4-toughness creature, you could assign 4 damage to it, and 1 point could trample over. When damage resolves, each of these would be doubled twice, for a total of 16 damage to the blocker, and 4 to the defending player.

* * *

**Q:** I have **Words of Waste** in play and pay 6 to replace my next 6 draws with discard effects. Then I cast **Wheel of Fortune**. How would this play out? It seems that my opponents should both draw and discard simultaneously, but I couldn't find anything in the rules that explained how to do that.  
 --Jacob Orlove

**A:** The appropriate rule would be 413.2d which says in part that players make choices in order (active player first, then the non-active player), then all actions are carried out simultaneously. Since card drawing is done sequentially, players draw one card at a time, both at the same time. 

Your situation would go like this:

All players discard their entire hands at the same time. Stop. Then all the other players draw one card (and fail to discard). Stop. Then the other players each pick one card from their hands, and discards while drawing another. Stop. Repeat four more times. Stop. Then all players draws one card. The end result is that you will have one card left, and your opponents would have two (the last two cards drawn).

* * *

**Q:** I used **Artificial Evolution** on **Centaur Glade** to make a bunch of Elves. If I play **Parallel Evolution**, will the copied tokens be Elves, or does it copy them as Centaurs? I know **Clone** doesn't copy changes made by Artificial Evolution.

**A:** When you copy a permanent, you copy the "printed values" of that permanent. If it's a card, those are the values written on the card. If it's a token, they're the values specified by the effect that created the token.

Therefore, if a Centaur Glade creates an Elf token, it's creating a token whose printed values say Elf. Copying the token will give you another Elf token.

By contrast, if you had cast Artificial Evolution on the token itself, you wouldn't be changing its printed values - a copy of that token will ignore the effect of the Evolution.

* * *

**Q:** I play a **Battle Screech**. My opponent casts **Artificial Evolution** to change it from creating Bird tokens to creating anything else... say Lhurgoyf tokens. The spell resolves, I get two Lhurgoyf tokens and the Battle Screech goes to the graveyard. If I pay the flashback, will I get Lhurgoyf tokens? Or will I get Bird tokens?

**A:** You will get Bird tokens. When the first Battle Screech has finished resolving, it goes to the graveyard and forgets everything that was done to it, and when it's replayed with Flashback the Artificial Evolution effect won't be on it.

**Q:** I cast **Goblin Pyromancer**, then before the end of turn I play **Artificial Evolution** on the Goblin changing the second line of text to "At end of turn destroy all Beasts." After my turn, the Goblin Pyromancer stays around; do I get to check the second line of text every turn or just the turn Goblin Pyromancer came into play?

**A:** Artificial Evolution changes *all* instances of a creature type to another creature type, it doesn't change just one instance. The Pyromancer would also change its own creature type to Beast (though its name wouldn't change to Beast Pyromancer), and it would be destroyed at end of turn by its own effect. 

If it manages to survive the end of turn (by some sort of regeneration, or using an ability like **Imagecrafter**'s to make it "not a Beast," the ability would trigger again at the end of next turn, and each end of turn it is in play. It would need to be regenerated or have its creature type changed every time to stay continue to stay alive.

**Q:** On my turn I played **Armadillo Cloak** on my face-down **Whipcorder**. I attack a few times, but when it was my friend's turn, I really needed to tap his big creature. So I morphed the Whipcorderand used it. My friend said that my Armadillo Cloak goes to the graveyard. To get to my exact question: When you morph a creature, do enchantments and previous effects still stay on it?

**A:** Enchantments, previous effects, and counters still stay on when a creature is turned face up (or down). Turning a creature face up just changes what it looks like, it's still the same creature. The creature keeps all counters and enchantments, and all effects on it still apply. Modifiers to power and toughness are done using the printed power/toughness as initial value (they're the same in this case), but other morph creatures may have different power and toughness when they're turned face up.

**Q:** Regarding **Krosan Tusker**... Does the "draw a card" effect resolve before the "search for a land" effect, or vise versa? It makes sense that you would search for a land first, as the effect would be on the stack as a reaction to the cycling ability, much like "comes into play" effects.

**A:** The triggered ability (search for a land) goes to the stack after what triggers it (Cycling put on the stack). The stack resolves in reverse order, letting you search for a land before you draw the card.

**Q:** My **Butcher Orgg** says that I may divide its combat damage among the defending player and his or her creatures. If my opponent attacks and I block with the Orgg, is the "defending player" me? Could I choose to have the Orgg deal damage to my own creatures rather than the attacker?  
 --Rick Wagner, Ann Arbor, MI

**A:** When your opponent attacks you, you are the defending player, and you can choose to have the Butcher Orgg divide its damage between you and your creatures instead of assigning damage to the creature it blocks.

**Q:** I am totally confused by **Read the Runes**. Can you give a step-by-step explanation of how to play it?  
 --Robby Manginsay, Bulacan, Philippines

**A:** The mana cost of Read the Runes is ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif). So when you play it, you may choose any number for X - let's assume you choose 4. The cost of the spell is then ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif).

**Q:** Let's say I have a **Ruby Medallion** and 3 mountains in play and I play a **Lightning Dragon** for 3 mana. When it's my next upkeep, do I pay the echo cost at 3 or 4 mana? Does the casting cost change due to Ruby Medallion?

**A:** You have to pay 4 mana (![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)). Echo means you pay the mana cost in the next upkeep, and the Mana Cost is defined by the symbols in the top right corner of the card. The Medallions don't change the Mana Cost, they reduce the amount of mana you have to pay when you *play* a spell, they don't have any effect when you're not playing spells.

**Q:** In [Saturday School #5](/en/articles/feature/2002/11-09-saturday-school-3), you answered a question about **Spelljack**, but here's one clarification that is needed: if the spell you Jacked removes itself from the game when cast, does it remove itself to the same "zone" where it went when Spelljacked, enabling it to be recast? If I Spelljack my opponent's **Time Spiral**, can I then cast it over and over and over as it continually returns itself to the Spelljack "zone"?

**A:** Spelljack says you may play the spell "As long as the card is removed from the game". When you play the card, it stops being removed from the game, so that time has ended. You can no longer play it, even if the card would be removed again later.

**Q:** My opponent has in play: two **Elvish Champion**s and one **Titania's Chosen** with two +1/+1 counters and enchanted with **Rancor**. He attacks with the Chosen, a 7/5 trampler. I cast **Humble** targeting the Chosen. What is the power/toughness after resolution of Humble? We thought it would be 2/3 without trample (0/1 + bonuses from the Champions). Is that right or must we consider the Rancor also? Please enlighten us, I just love the surprise of Humble.  
 --Peter Geurts, Venlo, The Netherlands

**A:** When you calculate a creature's power/toughness, you do it in the following order: You start with the initial value (what's printed on the card, or what a copy effect makes it), then you apply all counters, then finally, you apply all other effects, in timestamp order (the order the effects started working). Since the Humble is the last effect, and it sets the power/toughness directly, it will disregard all other modifiers. The Titania's Chosen will be 0/1 with no abilities.

**Q:** **Belbe's Portal** is in play, with Dragon as the chosen creature type. I use Belbe's Portal during my upkeep to bring **Draco** into play. Draco says to pay the upkeep "at the beginning of your upkeep." Since I brought it into play during my upkeep, it is no longer the beginning of my upkeep, and I don't have to pay the upkeep cost, correct? Also, since Draco was under my control during my upkeep, it doesn't have "summoning sickness," correct?  
 --Brian Besl

**A:** Since you brought it into play after the beginning of the upkeep, the ability won't trigger that turn, and you won't have to pay. It will trigger in each of your later upkeeps if its still around, though. Draco won't be able to attack since you haven't controlled it since the beginning of the turn; the turn starts before the upkeep step, and you must have had control over the creature since the very start of the turn to attack with it.

**Q:** If I activate **Hermit Druid**'s ability at the beginning of my upkeep and put **Squee, Goblin Nabob** and **Ashen Ghoul** (with three other creatures on top of it) in my graveyard, (a) can I return Squee, Goblin Nabob to my hand? And (b) can I pay one black mana and put the Ghoul into play from my graveyard?  
 --Fermín

**A:** Squee's ability is triggered at the beginning of the upkeep, and since you're *in* the upkeep already, the ability won't go on the stack this turn. The Ashen Ghouls have activated abilities that can be activated any time in the upkeep, so you can still return them.

**Q:** If a player puts a creature under **Cold Storage** or **Safe Haven** and there are enchantments on the creature, what happens to the enchantments? I say they remain on but a fellow player believes they do not. What about counters or effects like counters?  
 --B. Young

**A:** Cold Storage and Safe Haven removes the creatures from the game. When cards leave play, all effects on them end, counters will be removed, and local enchantments won't have a legal permanent to enchant, so they're put in the graveyard. When the cards later return to play, they will be new cards, with no memory of their former lives.

The only exception to this is when cards have Phasing. Phasing has special rules that permanents leaving play keep enchantments, counters and effect when they come back.

**Q:** I enchant an opponent's creature with **Quicksilver Dagger**. Since I control the enchantment, does that mean that anytime he taps his creature to attack me that I can choose to deal him a damage and draw a card?  
 --Cody, Kirkland, WA

**A:** Quicksilver Dagger gives the ability to the creature. Only the controller of the creature can tap it to use the ability, and the "you" in the ability will be the controller of the creature. Tapping the creature is cost the player must pay to play the ability, the ability isn't used automatically every time the creature is tapped.

**Q:** My friend has a **Control Magic** on one of my creatures but I cast **Recoil** on the creature to return it to my hand. My question is, who has to discard a card?  
 --Daniel, Malaysia

**A:** The Recoil returns the permanent to its owner's hand, then *that player* discards a card. "That player" looks back on the player who gets the permanent returned, so you will have to discard a card.

**Q:** Could you please provide the outcome to the following situation? My opponent sacrificed his **Academy Rector** to his **High Market**, then removed the Rector from the game to activate the Rector's ability. I responded to the Rector's ability going on the stack by making my **Morphling** untargetable. When the Rector's ability resolved, my opponent pulled a **Confiscate** from his library and took control of my Morphling. My opponent said that because the enchantment comes directly into play, rather than being cast, it does not target the Morphling and immediately begins enchanting whatever permanent he chose. I think that when the Confiscate comes into play, it must target the permanent it will enchant. Who is right?  
 --Ben

**A:** Local enchantments only target when they're played (using the stack). When they come into play through other means (**Academy Rector**, **Replenish**), they're just put into play enchanting a legal permanent. Morphling can't be the target of spells or abilities when its ability is active, but the enchantment isn't a spell or ability when it's being put into play like this. It doesn't target when it's put into play by an effect, Academy Rector says that it's just put into play.

Read more about local enchantments in the [Comprehensive Rulebook](https://web.archive.org/web/20021207000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=/magic/comprules&origin=mtgcom_daily_rh49), rules 214.8c-i.

**Q:** I have **Opposition** and some Squirrels in play, plus a creature that I want to protect from my opponent's **Contested Cliffs**. After his untap step I tap a Squirrel to tap the Cliffs. In response he taps the Cliffs himself to activate it. In response I tap another Squirrel and target the Cliffs. Theoretically, my Squirrel-tap is on top of the stack and should resolve before he has a chance to tap the Cliffs himself, thus preventing his Beast from dealing damage to my creature. Does this work, or does his desire to tap the Cliffs himself take priority (a land "with the speed of an interrupt/mana source") over my effects on the stack?  
 --Josh Audet, New Orleans, Louisiana

**A:** When a player activates an ability or plays a spell, he or she must pay all costs when the spell or ability is put on the stack. A player can't be prevented from paying costs when the he or she has priority to play spells and abilities. Your opponent can respond to you trying to tap his permanent by tapping it himself.

**Q:** Before Sixth Edition rules, there was a specific rule that prevented you from using a card's activated abilities until you'd paid all of its upkeep costs. I cannot find it in the new rules--was it removed? Can I activate **Gurzigost** during my upkeep before I put two cards on the bottom of my library?  
 --Andrew Forsythe, Belfast, Northern Ireland

**A:** This rule was removed. There are no "upkeep costs" any more, all the former upkeep costs are now triggered abilities that trigger at the beginning of the upkeep. You can respond to these by using abilities of the permanents if you wish. It is legal to respond to the triggered ability by activating the Gurzigost so you have cards to put under your library.

**Q:** If I play a **Skullscorch** on someone with one or no cards in his hand, does he have to take the 4 damage since he can't discard two?

**A:** The wording "X happens unless the player does Y" on spells is the same as "The player may do Y. X happens if the player doesn't want to do Y or is unable to do Y".

If Skullscorch was worded "Skullscorch deals 4 damage to target player unless he or she discards two cards," the player would have the option of discarding two cards. If the player is unwilling or unable to do so, the player would take 4 damage.

Skullscorch has the reverse wording, giving the player the option of taking 4 damage. The player can always choose to not take this damage, and will then have to discard two cards. If the player doesn't have enough cards in his hand, he discards as many as he can, which may be zero.

**Q:** My girlfriend had a creature with the enchantment **Lure** on it, and her opponent had an **Avatar of Hope**. Lure forces all creatures able to block the enchanted creature to block it, but the Avatar can block any number of creatures. Is the Avatar allowed to block the Lured creature and all the other attacking creatures?  
 --Nathan

**A:** Yes, you can do this. Lure only requires the Avatar to block the Lured creature - but it doesn't say anything about what *else* it may block. You may declare other blocks however you wish, so long as they don't conflict with the block demanded by Lure.

**Q:** I have both an **Ensnaring Bridge** and **Caltrops** in play. I have no cards in hand. My opponent declares his **Birds of Paradise** as attacker, and then responds to the Caltrops' one damage with a **Giant Growth**. Is the Birds still a legal attacker? Does it survive the one damage?  
 --Morné Rankin, South Africa

**A:** Caltrops has a triggered ability that triggers on the Birds being declared as an attacker. The player can respond to the ability with Giant Growth, which will resolve first, and pump the Birds to 3/4. They will survive the one point of damage. The Birds are still attacking legally.

If the player lets the Caltrops resolve without playing the Giant Growth first, the Caltrops deals one damage when the ability resolves, and the Birds will be destroyed before the Giant Growth can be played.

**Q:** If I were to play the spell **Quiet Speculation**, could I use it to take cards like **Wonder** or **Brawn** and put them in my graveyard? They have that tombstone icon beside their names. Or does the card in fact have to say "Flashback X" directly on it?

**A:** Quiet Speculation only lets you search for cards which have the Flashback ability written on them. The tombstone icon means that the card does something in the graveyard. All cards with flashback have the tombstone icon but not all cards with the tombstone icon have flashback.

**Q:** If an opponent uses my **Scandalmonger**'s ability against me, does it count as a spell or ability controlled by the opponent? I ask this question because there are several cards like **Guerilla Tactics**, **Sand Golem**, **Mangara's Blessing**, **Spiritual Focus**, and **Dodecapod** that have an ability that reacts to a spell or ability from my opponent that makes me discard. So basically, do these cards combo with Scandalmonger?

**A:** The ability of Scandalmonger is controlled by the player who played it. Your opponent controls the ability even if you control the Scandalmonger. Yes, this is a combo.

**Q:** I have a **Paladin en-Vec** and Commander Eesha in play. I declare my attack. My opponent responds by playing **Jilt**. He targets the Commander Eesha with the bounce bit and pays the kicker to do 2 damage to the Paladin. I say he can't target the Paladin because the kicker is a red ability, but he says the spell is blue. What happens? Is the spell blue and the damage red?

**A:** A spell's color(s) are determined by the symbols in the mana cost (in the top right corner of the card). Kicker is an additional cost, and does not affect a spell's color. The Jilt spell is blue, even if you pay the kicker, and the Paladin is a legal target for it.

**Q:** In a desperate ploy to keep myself alive, I drop a **Glacial Chasm** (sacrificing a plains) to complement my **Soul Echo** (nine counters). Now, Glacial Chasm says that all damage dealt to me is reduced to zero. Is this "different" - technically speaking - from preventing it? In other words, if my brother **Fireball**s me, can he remove counters from the Soul Echo thereby killing me (Since that Glacial Chasm upkeep is so steep I was around -35 life at the time)? Or is "reduced to zero" a different mechanic than "prevent or redirect"?  
 --Jared in PA

**A:** Glacial Chasm's Oracle text says that the damage is prevented. 

When several replacement effects (damage prevention in this case) interact, the affected player chooses the order they apply in. A player would usually apply the Chasm's effect first so that the Soul Echo doesn't see any damage being dealt, and no counters are removed.

Glacial Chasm has a Cumulative Upkeep of paying 2 life. Cumulative Upkeep gives you the option to pay the cost, if you don't want to, or is unable to pay it, you have to sacrifice the permanent instead. If the upkeep of Glacial Chasm demands a higher life payment then you have life available, you can't make the payment, and have to sacrifice the Chasm instead.

The answer to your question: If the Chasm in play, it will keep you alive from your brother's Fireball, you don't have to remove any counters. The Glacial Chasm's upkeep is optional, you can always choose to not pay it, and stay alive for a while longer.
