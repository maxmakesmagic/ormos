Red. The color of fire, heat, destruction, and chaos. The shade of hot lava exploding from a volcano before gushing hastily across the surface of the Earth, scorching everything in its path. In our case, however, we’ll have to settle for something a little less intense: the red commons in *Legions*. 

When the dust had finally settled on straight *Onslaught* Limited, it was very clear that red was far and away the best color, possessing not only the best common Sparksmith, but also the depth and strength to surpass the other four colors almost as much as black did back in the days of *Urza’s Saga*. Enter *Legions*.

When we talk about the red commons in *Legions*, it all comes back to one thing: Skirk Marauder. Without this guy, the color would have experienced a complete collapse after its dominance in *Onslaught*. However, the Marauder gives red enough punch to get you through the third pack without too many injuries.

Despite the fact that he costs ![5 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/33/3c/333cbd82f68b5e306003b2955e517a94ffa7a3ea03087365c10afad540856dce.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) to end up with a 2/1 body and a Shock, he is actually very good. Providing a Nekrataal-like effect with the added chance to go to the dome is never a bad thing in Limited. Combine this with the fact that he is easily splashable due to the nature of morph and you have what I believe to be the best common in the set. Many will argue Skinthinner for this position, due to the fact that Dark Banishing has historically been better than Shock, but there are a few points which make me take the side of the Skirk. The main point is obvious: the one red mana in the casting cost makes the card far more accessible and consistent to use. The second important factor is the actual amount of mana required to flip it up. Skinthinner requires a hefty ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) in contrast to the more reasonable ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) on the Marauder. When you take into account the fact that, a lot of the time, the Marauder will do the exact same thing the ‘Thinner is doing, it seems much better to pay less mana. Certainly the ‘Thinner is better against green decks with larger beasts that cannot be taken down by two damage, but this is a format of 2/2’s, after all. After all of this, we still need to add in Skinthinner’s incredible interaction with Cruel Revival, that makes the choice for best common that much closer. It’s a toss up, but I like the splashability of the Marauder.

So with the best commons in both *Onslaught* and *Legions*, red should be the odds-on favorite for best color, right? Unfortunately, it’s not quite that simple. While red has the depth and power in *Onslaught*, it really drops off after the Marauder in *Legions*. And when I say drops off, I mean literally falls off a cliff.

The next card in the lineup is the Crested Craghorn. Not that this pseudo-removal spell is a great card or anything, but there is absolutely nothing else! The fact that it will always be a surprise is really the only thing it has going for it, as when someone passes the turn with ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) up, you can pretty much assure a Pinpoint Avalanche. This is nothing in comparison to the fact that Pinpoint can’t be killed by a lonely Sparksmith, cycled Solar Blast or a Lavamancer’s Skill. The last annoyance is its inability to kill creatures with tap effects like Timberwatch Elf, unless you can catch them before they go online. The card is still fine by all means, but nothing like the quality of the red commons in *Onslaught*.

The last dude worth noting before we get into the effect these cards will have on deck archetypes is the Skirk Outrider. Another plausible goblin, albeit with a completely different purpose than most. In a deck with a good number of beasts, he is effectively a 4/4 trampler for four mana, which is a very good thing. The one annoyance is the obvious risk of card disadvantage through killing the beast and blocking with a 3/3. Actually, having a beast in play is a lot harder than it sounds and after that, you have to keep it around. Despite all of this, when the overall power level is low, you take what you can get, and this card starts looking much better than it would in a regularly powered set. 

After those, we run out of cards that have any outstanding characteristics worth talking about, so I think it is beneficial to discuss the impact of the *Legions* pack on the archetypes containing red.

The deck that received the most benefit from the red commons is certainly red-green. Since the Marauder is a welcome addition to any red deck or also a welcome splash, it seems like a moot point to mention him in specific regarding any archetype, although there is the notable mention of Backslide in blue-red. However, red-green does gain a number of tools to add to its already impressive arsenal. Skirk Outrider is clearly the main reason for this, due to the overwhelming number of beasts present in the two colors. *Legions* only increases this number and makes the card that much more efficient. Also important is the Crested Craghorn, as it finds its best home here with the company of Wirewood Savage, Vitality Charm, and Snarling Undorak to watch its back. Finally, we have the more mediocre cards that can have an impact on the late game for the red-green mage. Cards like Bloodstoke Howler can be extremely good in a deck full of beasts if given the time to work properly. The same goes for Macetail Hystrodon and Shaleskin Plower, both of which are very mediocre, but find a comfy home here.

The archetype that lost the most from *Legions* is a little less apparent. Blue-red gains the likely suspects in Skirk Marauder and Crested Craghorn, but after that, most of the red commons just aren’t synergistic at all with the rest of the deck. Macetail Hystrodon can have a place as a late-game fatty, but the rest of the cards end up just being random morph filler. The loss of an *Onslaught* pack containing all of the powerful red cards, as well as the omnipotent Lavamancer’s Skill that is the signature of the archetype, is far too painful in comparison with the low-end cards we get in *Legions*. Blue, however, does offer a number of cards to help out in this category, but that’s another article. 

You can’t stay on top forever, and just as quickly as red rose to the top in *Onslaught*, it is starting to decline with *Legions*. With that, here is a standard common pick order based on the overall power level without regard to a specific decktype. These are all of the cards worth mentioning in the red common slot.

1.  Skirk Marauder
2.  Crested Craghorn
3.  Skirk Outrider
4.  Macetail Hystrodon
5.  Shaleskin Plower
6.  Flamewave Invoker
7.  Bloodstoke Howler (can move up if you have lots of beasts)
8.  Goblin Grappler
