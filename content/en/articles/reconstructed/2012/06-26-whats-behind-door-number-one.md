You approach an unmarked door.

  
![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/rc/rc201_cellar.jpg)

Cellar Door | Art by [Rob Alexander](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Rob+Alexander%22%5D)

What do you do?

[Turn Back](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Default.aspx) !  
Open it!

The door budges slightly. Your spine prickles as you hesitate. It's heavy and difficult to open, like it hasn't been opened in years—or perhaps was once sealed shut. A glow emanates from within.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/rc/rc201_door.jpg)

[Door of Destinies](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+of+Destinies) | Art by [Larry MacDougall](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Larry%20MacDougall%22%5D)

Do you finish pushing the door open?

[Close it](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Default.aspx)!  
Push it open!

You push with the brunt of your force. No effect.

Eager to get inside, you channel some mana to help blow this door apart. Suddenly, the door creaks forward, sliding open. In front of you is a cataclysmic whirlwind of colors, and madness, and thought, and the frayed ends of charred knowledge in between. For a split moment, you can see between realities; between worlds; between life and death itself.

And then... And then...

*Nothingness.*

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/rc/rc201_6pvksot0dk.jpg)

[Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness) | Art by [Svetlin Velinov](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Svetlin%20Velinov%22%5D)

*You lose! Good day, Sir!*

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/tcg/products/m13/q98x5z4ji9_en.jpg)  

Here's a card you haven't seen for a while. Last seen skittering around on Commander tables or in Adam Prosak's Cube draft decks, it's back in ***Magic** 2013* to teach a whole new class of players about what it means to push people through the door.

At the cost of [Progenitus](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Progenitus) plus a five-mana down payment to win the game, [Dooring](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness) someone is hardly the easiest route to victory—but it's certainly a fun one. I've had a penchant for alternate win conditions—although as [Aaron Forsythe](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/magazine/archive.aspx?author=aaron%20forsythe) wittily notes in the developer comments on this card, this is really more of an alternate lose condition—ever since I saw [Chance Encounter](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chance+Encounter) back in *Odyssey*. They're crazy and difficult to pull off. Getting them to work can be maddening.

But can we do it? Yes we can! It's time to hop into our void ship and go exploring.

Fortunately, I have a partner in my insanity. Reader Ross Drew sent in a five-color list that meshed well with the [door of doom](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness). Let's take a look:

The Battle Plan

First, an aside. There are a lot of ways you could start a deck like this. I suspect many would begin with four copies of [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan), but that's not the route I want to go down. Why?

I'm a man of principle.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/rc/rc201_0m94rvw5gt.jpg)

Magmaquake | Art by [Gabor Szikszai](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Gabor%20Szikszai%22%5D)

Look. If you're going to build a deck that wins with a wonky alternate win lose condition, you had better *use the card*. What kind of textbook lame, pointy-hat wizard are you if you summon a [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan) and then kill your opponent by attacking with it before the door is open?

Did Gandalf just teleport Frodo to Mount Doom? Did Dumbledore just tell Harry everything he needed to know right away? Was Bugs Bunny a natural at basketball before Michael Jordan entered the picture? Uncommitted Planeswalkers just end up as part of a joke on the side streets of Kephalai.

This means that, as a challenge to ourselves, this deck is going to rely on [Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness) for its singular route to victory. Go big, or go home. Does that mean I'm going to be taking a super-Johnny deck-building bent this week? Absolutely. Am I excited? Definitely!

Now that I've established *that* important principle, let's look at how we're going to make this work. Most importantly, the advantages of playing a deck like this.

The way I see this deck playing out is a five-color control deck. Accelerating into ![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/white.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/white.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/blue.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/blue.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/black.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/black.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/green.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/green.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/red.gif)![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/red.gif) is a fairly unrealistic goal, so the easiest way to ensure you get there is a long game. We're going to have access to all of the best control cards in the format. Of course, there will be *some* acceleration and fixing to help hit the right combination of ten mana—but the core of the deck will be controlling.

Additionally, since our kill condition is an artifact, we also don't need to be too concerned with creatures. In fact, the fewer the better—creatures will just enable our opponent's removal.

Let's start by running down the cards in the deck to take a look at what elements are worth keeping.

Card Breakdown

There's a lot to decide in a deck like this. Playing a deck that has every card in Standard at its fingertips can be a bit overwhelming for deck building. Fortunately, the deck we're starting from has several core elements to draw inspiration from.

**[Angelic Wall](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Wall)**

I would prefer to not play many creatures if I can avoid it, especially because I'm going to want board sweepers. I actually think [Angelic Wall](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Wall) is a very interesting option against beatdown decks—but with all five color at my disposal, I'm sure I can find a noncreature I'd rather have here.

**[Borderland Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Borderland+Ranger)**

Now this is a creature I can get behind! It searches up a land and blocks an attacker. I will say that I'm preferential to [Solemn Simulacrum](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Solemn+Simulacrum) in this slot because it puts the land directly onto the battlefield and picks up a card on the way out, but this is a good example of the kind of creature I would want to play.

**[Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel)**

Sorry Serra—all we need to win is [Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness)!

**[Lingering Souls](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lingering+Souls)**

While not a straightforward win condition—these tokens can do some nice blocking—I know that I'll definitely end up killing some opponents with Spirits. It's Door or bust—these souls will need to linger elsewhere.

**[Abundant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abundant+Growth)**

Now this is definitely a card I'm going to keep and play the full four copies of. It might look fairly innocuous, but it does everything I need. Getting all ten mana of the exact right colors is tricky, and [Abundant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abundant+Growth) goes a long way toward that by turning excess sources of a color into any color. It fixes all of the deck's colors early on. Additionally, it gives us another card in the process so the net cost to casting it is very little. This is exactly what I'm looking for!

**[Sphere of the Suns](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphere+of+the+Suns)**

I definitely would like at least one two-mana accelerator in my deck. While the colorless nature of Sphere is nice, running out of counters is a very big problem as the game goes on. I'm going to swap these for [Rampant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rampant+Growth) because, even though Growth requires green, the permanent source is crucial.

**[Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice) and [Desperate Ravings](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desperate+Ravings)**

Some two-mana card drawing is definitely on the list for this deck. However, the final version is unlikely to have eight slots just for two-mana card drawing. While normally I feel like [Desperate Ravings](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desperate+Ravings) is the superior card, some mix of these spells is likely optimal for two reasons.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/rc/rc201_think.jpg)

[Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice) | Art by [Anthony Francisco](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Anthony%20Francisco%22%5D)

First, mana considerations. This deck will likely have a higher quantity of blue cards than red cards, making the blue for [Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice)'s initial casting easier to find than red.

Second, the random discard is actually very relevant here. While normally the card discarded isn't something I'm worried about, I can't afford to discard my win conditions here. A deck like this wants very few win conditions clunking up its draws, and so I am only going to play two Doors in the final build. Discarding one of them might drag the game on for much longer than this deck can support—and discarding two is downright lethal. I'll likely have a split between the two in the final build.

**[Forbidden Alchemy](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forbidden+Alchemy)**

This is a nice card to have when you need to dig for a specific answer—like, say, a board sweeper or a Door to close out the game. However, putting a Door into your graveyard is dangerous, and most of the time I would rather have a [Think Twice](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Think+Twice) or [Desperate Ravings](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desperate+Ravings). **[Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak)**

Countermagic creates an interesting discussion for this deck. On one hand, I am definitely going to want access to countermagic in the long game to fight off my opponent's over-the-top spells and disruption on Door. On the other, I'm going to be tapping out early to accelerate and fix my mana.

The way to support both of these dueling needs? Playing a hard counter like [Dissipate](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dissipate). Then you have the counterspell you need without the early game restrictions of [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak).

**[Feeling of Dread](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feeling+of+Dread)**

[Feeling of Dread](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feeling+of+Dread) is great for buying time in the midgame to let a [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel) or swarm of Spirit tokens swing over for lethal. It's not so good at buying time to reach ten mana for [Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness) in the long game. There are better options for the new incarnation of this deck.

**[Dismember](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dismember)**

This is definitely a removal spell I can get behind. I'd probably want to mix it up with my other removal spells for variety, but this is a card I could see playing one or two of.

A Whole Lot of Nothing

There's plenty of room to fit in cards—but slots are going to fill in fast because there are just so many good options. In addition to some of the replacements mentioned above, here are some of the new cards I narrowed down to adding.

**[Day of Judgment](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Day+of+Judgment), [Terminus](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terminus), and Magmaquake**

The game is going to go long, and you can expect to face wave after wave of creatures. Whether [Huntmaster of the Fells](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Huntmaster+of+the+Fells) or [Geist of Saint Traft](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Geist+of+Saint+Traft), you're going to need to deal with them in turn. [Day of Judgment](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Day+of+Judgment) does a lot of important work in a control deck like this. A little diversity with a [Terminus](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terminus) gives you some added play against problematic creatures like Thrun and helps versus undying creatures as well.

Magmaquake is an extra sweeper that also helps out against Planeswalkers. Proactive, threatening, repeatable sources of advantage are problems for a deck like this, and you need to be able to pick off their Planeswalkers or fall in the process. Magmaquake helps out in that quest while also just blowing up all of your opponent's creatures at instant speed. Into the magma you go!

**[Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder)**

Consistency is paramount, especially in a five-color deck. [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder) helps do everything from fix your mana to set up [Terminus](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terminus), from finding [Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness) to finding removal. As soon as you have access to blue mana, [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder) starts doing great things for this deck.

**[Blue Sun's Zenith](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blue+Sun%27s+Zenith)**

I wanted one big card-draw spell I could cast late and lock up the game with. [Blue Sun's Zenith](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blue+Sun%27s+Zenith) was exactly that card. It draws you so many cards in the late game that it's almost like a virtual [Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness). [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder) can help you find it if necessary, and once you cast it for a bunch you should be in pretty good shape. The triple blue isn't easy for this deck but it is very achievable in the long game, which is when you want this card anyway.

There are so many ways you could take this deck that it's hard to say that this is any kind of "perfect build," but it's certainly the way I imagined a Door deck coming together. If nothing else, it's a fun place to start.

Since this is mostly a fun deck built with arbitrary constraints and it's hard to know what the metagame will look like after ***Magic** 2013* releases, predicting everything well enough for a sideboard is difficult.

If I were actually taking this deck to a tournament, though, the place I would start for a sideboard would be more pinpoint removal like [Pillar of Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pillar+of+Flame), [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat), and [Oblivion Ring](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oblivion+Ring); more [Dissipate](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dissipate)s; and a transformational creature plan—likely [Fettergeist](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fettergeist)—for if they have a way to deal with all of your Doors. It depends a lot on your local metagame, so tinker as necessary. And remember, with a deck like this—have fun!

Honorable Mentions

There were several cool five-color decks that were great submissions but didn't fit with [Door to Nothingness](https://web.archive.org/web/20120626000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Door+to+Nothingness) quite as well as this one. Take a look!
