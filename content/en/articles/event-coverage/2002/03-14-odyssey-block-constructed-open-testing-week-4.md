A month ago I posted my initial deck designs for *Odyssey* Block Constructed - a brand new and unexplored format that actually seems more interesting than many of the recent Constructed formats have been. I read over a thousand emails (wow, I am almost glad its drawing to an end, I am not used to such a flood of messages) and tried out dozens of decks. Four weeks later, I present to you the much evolved top decks I have at the moment.

I am assured by other Pros who are testing in teams that these decks are not particularly good. Still, I believe that while you will see better Constructed versions of these in Osaka, many similar decks will be played. When I get back from Osaka, I will write a final article in this series, covering the decks that actually did well in Osaka and comparing them to what is posted below.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fc/d3/fcd303e216c832a96e5657bf356203e657af86193465daa8f87aac71517049b4.jpg)One of the most interesting parts of this experiment for me was to see how much the email input would affect my decks. The impact was not tremendous. While there was any number of solid ideas in the mail, few of them would not have been arrived at by any dedicated testing group of 3-4 players. With notable exception of the Psychatog deck (only slightly modified from the version sent to me by Scott Seville) these versions were of my design. Many alternatives were proposed. For example, there are many proponents of Nimble Mongoose or Seton's Scout in the u-g deck. However, after much testing I still found my version to be either superior, or at least better-suited to my play style. 

I do not want to sound ungrateful to those who sent me ideas and deck lists - I reviewed them all carefully and found plenty of solid ideas - but my concept of creating an uber-deck by combining efforts of a large group of players never materialized.

Although I will not call my experiment a resounding success, it was also not a total failure. I learned a lot about how an average PTQ player thinks and builds his decks - and quite possibly got the motivation to playtest the format I would have lacked otherwise. Without further ado, here are the best 4 decks I have at the moment, in no particular order:

Probably the most fun deck to play of all these, monoblack has serious issues with Squirrel's Nest or Bearscape. I was forced to add Rancid Earth maindeck and further testing is needed to see if they don't wreck the deck's consistency too much. With Diabolic Tutor, there are quite a few possibilities for this deck and I will continue testing it in Osaka to see if it can handle the post-sideboard matches (one of its major weaknesses is lack of a strong sideboard).

Some of the things I learned about the deck: Breakthrough is far weaker than most players thought originally, Compulsion is great, Careful Study is very necessary, and you will need a way to win a mirror. Often it will come down to who draws more Aether Bursts at the right time, but a good way to break the creature stall, such as Thought Devourer, helps. It also flies over Nantuko Shade and squirrel tokens.

The Squirrel version I posted untested a few weeks ago has been working out extremely well. It was a bit top-heavy and could very rarely take advantage of Gurzigost (there are so many flashback cards, you rarely had enough cards in the graveyard to keep it around). I replaced them and one of the Beast Attacks with 2 Spellbane Centaurs and an extra Sylvan Might and the deck is running more consistently now.

Out went Peeks, in 2 Innocent Bloods and an Upheaval. I might replace Innocent Bloods with more creatures - Possessed Aven is a possibility.

Other ideas to try that I never got a chance to test thoroughly enough due to time constraints:

*   U-g control deck that takes advantage of powerful enchantments such as Persuasion and Squirrel Nest. This should have a very powerful sideboard that can deal with a lot of decks.
*   B-g beatdown. I tried the Braids version with Squirrel Nest and found it somewhat inconsistent, but never tried to just play very aggressive green creatures backed up by black removal in form of Edict, Butcher.
*   R-u Compulsion-Burn. I tested against it a bit and it seems viable, worth giving it a shot.
*   Zombie Infestation/Upheaval. The one combo deck I did not really get a chance to test. I played against it and wasn't too impressed, but my opponent was inexperienced and it is possible that a much more solid build exists.

Tune in to *Sideboard's* coverage of Pro Tour-Osaka. And wish me luck - with other pros having access to my deck lists as of three days before the Pro Tour, I am going to need it.
