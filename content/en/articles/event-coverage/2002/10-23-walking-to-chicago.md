A number of people have asked me if would be doing a “Walking to Chicago” column for this season of PTQ’s. The decision making process and constantly shifting constructed meta-game that made “Walking to Houston” so interesting (to me, anyway) are not present for a Limited PTQ season. I briefly considered proposing a column called “Limping to Chicago While Having the Snot Beat Out of Me by Some Little Kid and his Rare Dragon” but it didn’t really trip off the tongue.

I played in a PTQ in Harrisburg and managed to ride Kamahl and triple Wirewood Savage toward the Top 8. As soon as people recovered from their Shock at my Top 8 appearance they immediately wanted to know about the draft. 

You see, the draft format for the Top 8 of this round of qualifiers—and the Day 2 format for the relevant Grand Prix’s—is Rochester Draft. This is a format where as each pack is opened its contents are laid on the table face-up and each player makes his or her picks in full view of the other players. If you have never played/seen this format I will endeavor to illustrate using the cut to a Top 8 of a PTQ for reference. I will also provide a chart that shows all of the choices these eight very good players made during the Top 8 of last weekend’s PTQ at Neutral Ground in New York City.

After seven rounds of Sealed Deck play the standings were as follows:

One of the reasons that this Top 8 is so interesting to me is the quality of the players. Every single player has qualified and played in at least one Pro Tour in the past. I don’t think there was a Top 8 with this level of experience in our area during the whole Odyssey Block Constructed season.

Tony “The Shark” Tsai is a member of Team Deadguy and just competed at Pro Tour Boston with Invitationalists Chris Pikula and David Price. Tony has competed in numerous Pro Tours and has made multiple Grand Prix Top 8’s. On the rare occasions that failed to advance to a Grand Prix Day 2 he has also contributed coverage to this very site.

Seth Burn is one of the most well known writers on the net. He also has the dubious distinction of NOT going to more Pro Tours that he was invited to compete in than any other player in the history of the game.

Mark Swoyer has won numerous qualifiers and is one of the most consistent PTQ players in the Northeast. 

Pat Donovan has a demanding job in the television industry and does not get to play nearly as much Magic as he would like. The last time he was in a Top 8 he Secret Forced his way through a field of Necro Donate to qualify for Pro Tour: Lin Sivvi.

Jordan Berkowitz is a rising star. A former JSS powerhouse, Jordan seemingly qualifies at will. He won an OBC qualifier during the second week of the season and seemed confident that he would repeat that performance during this second weekend of *Onslaught* Limited.

Zev Gurwitz is the man behind Zevatog, the Standstill/bounce version of the Psychatog deck that dominated Standard for too long this year. After his Top 32 performance in Nice Zev is looking forward to another Limited Pro Tour.

Ilya Kreyer is a strong local player who is the inadvertent originator of much of Neutral Ground’s slang. 

Scott Landis is an excellent player from the Philadelphia area who discovered the combo that the Griffin Canyon/Animate Land/Unnatural Selection deck was based around.

Before the draft begins the top seeded player chooses which player will begin the draft. Tony Tsai elected to have his opponent, Scott Landis, open first. Because all the cards are laid out face up and you get to see each card that every player selects, many players like to let their opponent commit to a color strategy before they make their own color decisions.

The first player opens his pack and lays all of the cards on the table. Everyone is then given a brief period of time to review the cards. (Some said it was too brief considering that the cards have only been available for a couple of weeks! Players were craning their necks to read rare cards they had never seen before and at least one card was picked erroneously due to unfamiliarity with the card art.) After the review period the first player (Scott Landis) picks a card and then the player to his left (Mark Swoyer) picks second and so on until it comes to the eighth player (Seth Burn) who makes the eighth and ninth picks. Then the sequence of picks reverses until it comes back to the second player who makes the last pick. The player that opens the pack does not get second pick out of the pack. The second player then opens his pack and the process is repeated with the first player getting “the wheel” with the eighth and ninth picks.

Let’s take a look at how the first set of eight packs played out. The blue pick is the first pick out of the pack.
