Olivier's Corner's back!  
  
*At first I'd like to apologize to the readers for having announced my column would be back on last week. Unfortunately, I had underestimated the difficulty to write it in a foreign country without a laptop. Many things have happened since my departure from Amsterdam to Prague. I almost Top 8ed at the Pro Tour, then I went immediately to LA where my brother Antoine won the 2006 Invitational.* 

Being back home after a 20-hour trip, I can now look back on both these events. Prague was truly amazing. The city itself, the people I hanged out with, and the Pro Tour of course.  
 Arriving in Czech Republic, I aimed at making it to the top 8 and grab first place at the Race. Both came close, but none of them happened.

### Ready, Steady... Draft!

POD 1, TABLE 43  
  
 1 Saitou, Tomoharu  
 2 Joens, Klaus  
 3 Wolowiec, Przemyslaw  
 4 Levy, Raphael  
 5 Jurkovic, Robert  
 6 Ruel, Olivier  
 7 Fiori, Stefano  
 8 Bartolomei, Iain

My first pod was pretty decent with two other pros at the table, Tomoharu Saitou from Japan and my friend Raphaël Lévy from Sweden.  
 My only draft plan was to avoid playing white and green in the same deck as the Convoke cards are way less good now (tokens in only one pack out of three), and as drafting three colors including Selesnya means you can't have one guild in every pack.  
 My first picked was easy as I opened Primordial Sage. I'm not a huge fan of green guys but this one is just too good for being passed. Pick two was easy. Painful but easy, as Lighting Helix was by far the best card in the pack. White/Green/Red, I guess I'll just have to take lot of fixers and grab a fourth color from there.  
 In pack two, I first picked, not without a long thinking, Djinn Illuminatus over Steamcore Weird, as I didn't know yet whether I'd be passed Orzhov or Izzet cards. The answer came on third pick when I received Pillory of the Sleepless just after picking a not so good Dryad Sophisticate. Then I saw two Orzhov Signets and Silhana Starfletcher to fix the manabase. After two pack, my deck was WG splash red and black, but there were pretty good chances for the last pack to balance my four colors. I would need more and more fixers then.  
 It was painful when I decided to first pick Rakdos Carnarium over Rackdos Ickspitter; Painful, but worthy, being passed Wrecking Ball and two more Ickspitters. Then it was about it, except for three Eidolons (one W and two G) which were pretty good in my deck, particularly combined with my two rares.  
 My deck worked pretty well in round one and three VS Tomoharu Saitou and Stefano Fiori. Only Robert Jurkovic should have destroyed me in the second match, but his deck failed to give him the lands to do so. I would say my deck was worth 2-1 but I'm 3-0 luckily.

### Familiar Faces

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptpra06/terry.jpg) POD 2, TABLE 6  
  
 1 Yamaguchi, Satoshi 9  
 2 Taru, Genki 9  
 3 Soh, Terry 9  
 4 Ruel, Olivier 9  
 5 Matousek, Pavel 9  
 6 Tang, Mun Wye 9  
 7 Snepvangers, Bram 9  
 8 Mori, Katsuhiro 9

Pod two offered more familiar faces, among which 2005 Invitational champion Terry Soh and World Champion 2005 Katsuhiro Mori. This pod also gave me by far my best deck of the tournament.  
 I opened a fantastic first pack featuring Cerulean Sphinx, Galvanic Arc, Last gasp and Compulsive Research and I went for the rare. Pack two was pretty easy too as Terry passed me Keening Banshee. On pack three, I took Faith's Fetters as there were already no good blue or black cards left. Was Terry in these colors? Would one of my first picks stay in my sideboard? There were pretty good chances. My last notable card from pack one was a Vedalken Dismisser. On pack two, I passed Terry a fourth pick Savage Twister. I received almost nothing in pack two, but at least the signals were clear for pack three, which appeared to be a higway for Azorius cards as Terry was UGR. Azorius Guildmage, Seal of Doom and Court Hussar joined the team, and I ended up with a very solid WB deck splashing blue for only Sphinx, Hussar, Dismisser and Trial/Error. Splashing double blue was actually ok in my deck since I had three Signets and one bounceland.

I won my first round to Mori pretty easily as my deck gave me its best draws. Here's the link of the report from the [online converage](https://web.archive.org/web/20060518000000/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgevent/ptpra06/fm4) After one more win to Genki Taru, I had to face my first loss of the tournament, after Terry Soh's extremely solid UGR featuring Savage Twister and Burning Tree Shaman crushed me in two fast games.

### One of the best matches of Magic

POD 3, TABLE 2  
  
 1 Canali, Pierre 15  
 2 Williams, Anthony 15  
 3 Ruel, Olivier 15  
 4 Amir, Elisha 15  
 5 Nygaard, Nikolas 15  
 6 Smith, Don 15  
 7 Fiori, Stefano 15  
 8 Taru, Genki 15

I've played and won against Stefano and Taru in this tournament, Pierre Canali is better in constructed, so the player I fear the most is definitely Nikolas Nygaard.  
 The draft goes pretty well for me in the early picks as my first four choices are Compulsive Research, Moroii (which I took over Last Gasp, but I'm not sure about that one, the cards being in my opinion really close), Vedalken Dismisser and Golgari Signet. I also got passed the excellent Dimir Guildmage and Glimpse the Unthinkable for a very tough 5th pick. The Guildmage would be good no matter what, but I knew I would get two muddle the mixture back. If I could get two copies of Izzet Chronarch + Izzet Guildmage in Guildpact, Glimpse would be insane. I went for the risky choice and took Glimpse, and finished pack 1 with two copies of Muddle the Mixture.  
 I was pretty disappointed opening pack two as there was no card I truly wanted. I went for Pyromatics for it'd be good with Muddle and Chronarch if I could get it. I second picked the very expected Chronarch over Ogre Savant, but I got mostly crap from there except for Ogre Savant, a second Pyromatics and Izzet Signet.

Pack three almost didn’t help me; I ended up with a horrible curve and don’t even reach 23 playables. It’s going to be hard to do any better than 1-2 with this deck.

Round 7 feature matches are announced. I'm called on the bridge with Nygaard, for what will remain one of the best matches of Magic I've ever played. This is game three, I'm nearly drawing dead, as I've two turn to draw Chronarch or I'm dead. The situation is as follows.  
 ME: 8 life, 9 manas, Glimpse in hand  
 NIKO: the I life, 4 power I can't deal with (something like Dimir House Guard and Guardian of the Guildpact), many cards in hand, 20 cards in library.  
 I untap and draw my blue Eidolon. Helpless in defence, I think for two minutes and just play it to mill Nikolas for 3, then I turn it back to my hand playing the Glimpse. Do I have any one mana gold card so that I can play my Eidolon twice on next turn? Na, Riot Spikes is in my graveyard already. Whatever, he attacks, drops me to 4, plays another guy and passes. C'mon Chronarch! One time!  
 Nope, not Chronarch, just Compulsive Research... Wait! That might do it. Play Eidolon, sac it, Compulsive Research on Nygaard. Cards left in the deck? 0? Go!

That's how I finish day one with a very satisfying 6-1 record.

All my friends are going out, but I’d rather go to sleep to be in a good shape during day two.  

### Topdeck Mode Activated

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptpra06/fm8_canali.jpg) I start day two back to where I ended day one, in the feature match area, facing Pierre Canali this time. My game one draws are awful, but I manage to put Pierre down to six. I unfortunately die with 11 lands on the table and Pyromatics in hand. Pretty good draws give me game two, and game three starts really fast as I initiate the race. I can see I'm probably going to be a bit late on that race, but having Muddle + double Pyromatics in the deck, I decide to keep on racing to put in down in life as much as I can. I draw land, land, now I'm gonna be very short, land again, one more turn before I'm dead, three outs in the deck, Pierre's at 3 life and I draw... Pyromatics! Two games on top decks in a row, even if that one was about 50% chance when I decided to race.  
 On round nine, I'm facing Genki Taru again. Taru loses game one to a big mana flood, and decides to play two auras after mulliganing in game two to try and race me. At this point my hand is Savant, Repeal, Dismisser, so this is probably my easiest game of the week end.

Only two more drafts to go and I'm 8-1, after 3-0ing with a pretty bad deck. Only 3-2-1 for Top 8!  
 In part two, I'll go back to the last six rounds with a complete analysis from my final draft.
