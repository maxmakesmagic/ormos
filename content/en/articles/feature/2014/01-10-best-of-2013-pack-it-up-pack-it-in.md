I'd be remiss if I mentioned Mono-Blue Devotion as [one of the best decks of 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20140110000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/best-2013-mastering-blue-2014-01-06) but didn't mention its biggest rival—Mono-Black Devotion. Normally the "best" deck is easy to identify, but Mono-Blue and Mono-Black are not making it easy. 

![Pack Rat](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Pack+Rat)

[Grand Prix Louisville](https://web.archive.org/web/20140110000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/braun-duin-leader-pack-louisville) was the breakout tournament for Mono-Black Devotion (as it wasn't taken seriously, despite making [Top 8 at Pro Tour *Theros*](/en/articles/event-coverage/2013/10-12-top-8-decks-of-pro-tour-theros) the week before) and it was also the first career trophy for the Heisman-posing Brian Braun-Duin. That Top 8 also featured Brad Nelson and Todd Anderson, also playing the same deck as BBD, and cemented Mono-Black's place in the Standard metagame. 

Mono-Black Devotion has cheap removal, card advantage, threats, and lifegain—all the necessary tools for a midrange deck to succeed. Having a rock-solid mana base goes a long way also. Who is favored between Mono-Blue Devotion and Mono-Black Devotion is a hotly debated topic, but only one of those decks contains the mighty [Pack Rat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140110000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pack+Rat). 

Long thought to be a scourge of Draft and Sealed Deck only, [Pack Rat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140110000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pack+Rat) has proven that it's got what it takes in Constructed as well. Sometimes Mono-Black ignores the fact that it's a midrange deck and goes on the offensive with an army of Rats. Anyone expecting a long game might be in for a rude awakening.
