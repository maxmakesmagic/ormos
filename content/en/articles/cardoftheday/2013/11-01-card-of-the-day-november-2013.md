The blue sun in this card's name refers to one of the five &quot;suns&quot; of the plane of Mirrodin (which is now New Phyrexia, which still has the suns). In case you aren't familiar with it, the plane is orbited by five small satellites of pure mana (its suns), one for each of the five mana colors. These orbits are irregular, so it’s possible for any number of the suns, from zero to all five, to be overhead at any given place or time on the world. <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Obviously, all the myths and stories out of Ancient Greece are relatively old, but evidence suggests the myth of the gorgon was ancient even in <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period">Hellenistic times</a>. Reliable interpretations of the myth go back to the fifteenth century BCE, with outlier estimates pushing back <i>millennia</i>. In <b>Magic</b>, this Standard season has been <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20131101000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&amp;method=visual&amp;action=advanced&amp;subtype=+[%22Gorgon%22]&amp;format=+[%22Standard%22]">really good for Gorgon tribal</a>, but the subtype reaches back nearly to the beginning of the game, being <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20131101000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=1448">introduced in Legends</a>. <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Wednesday, November 13, 2013</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Colossus of Akros" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Colossus%20of%20Akros&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            <a>Colossus of Akros</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Theros</i>. As a creature without flying, our big Golem here is susceptible to being hit with an <a>Earthquake</a>. No matter how much mana gets pumped into that X spell, though, the Colossus's indestructible will keep it around. Why mention such a basic rules interaction? Well, surviving that huge <a>Earthquake</a> spell is a lot better than what happened to the Colossus's <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_of_Rhodes">real-world inspiration</a>, which <i>was</i> brought low by an earthquake in 226 BCE (fifty-six years after its completion). <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Tuesday, November 12, 2013</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Fleecemane Lion" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Fleecemane%20Lion&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            <a>Fleecemane Lion</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Theros</i>. As you might have guessed, yes, Fleecemane is indeed a top-down design based on the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemean_Lion">Nemean Lion</a>. Much of the fun of reading Multiverse developer notes comes from finding little gems of levity in them. In this case, the final note for <a>Fleecemane Lion</a> states &quot;Erik likes GW 3/3s. :)&quot; The Lion didn't start off as a 3/3 for two, though. Multiverse reveals that its casting cost (and monstrosity cost) were the last two aspects of the card finalized, as noted by aforementioned lead developer <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20131030175620/http://www.wizards.com/magic/magazine/archive.aspx?author=Erik%20Lauer">Erik Lauer</a>. <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Monday, November 11, 2013</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Shipbreaker Kraken" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Shipbreaker%20Kraken&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            <a>Shipbreaker Kraken</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Theros</i>. <b>Release the Kraken</b>... to lock down opponents' creatures! There are lots of ways to tap one or more creatures in <b>Magic</b> and prevent them from untapping, usually until their controllers' next untap step. <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20131101000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?action=advanced&amp;text=+[tap]+[untap]+[creature]%7C[don%27t]%7C[doesn%27t]">I mean, lots</a>. <a>Dungeon Geists</a> and <a>Tidebinder Mage</a> are the first creatures to lock down others like this when they enter the battlefield, but the Kraken is the first to have that ability affect multiple creatures. <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Friday, November 08, 2013</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Winged Coatl" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Winged%20Coatl&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            <a>Winged Coatl</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Commander</i> (2013 Edition). While it perhaps doesn't carry the same power as &quot;poison arrow&quot; in translation, the word &quot;coatl&quot; comes to us from the Nahuatl (one of the major Aztec languages) word for &quot;snake.&quot; The Aztec coatl didn't have wings (being an ordinary snake) like the Alara one does, but <i>Quetzal</i>coatl did! In 1976, the <b>Dungeons &amp; Dragons </b><i><i>Monster Manual</i></i> included the winged-snake version (spelt &quot;couatl&quot; there), which has been a part of coatl lore ever since. <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Thursday, November 07, 2013</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Nemesis of Mortals " src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Nemesis%20of%20Mortals%20&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            <a>Nemesis of Mortals</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Theros</i>. There are <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20131101000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&amp;method=visual&amp;action=advanced&amp;subtype=+[%22Snake%22]&amp;power=+%3E%3d[3]&amp;tough=+%3E%3d[3]">thirteen Snakes</a> with power and toughness 3+ in <b>Magic</b>, and three of them are from <i>Theros</i>. Of all Snakes, <a>Nemesis of Mortals</a> is the largest. The inspiration for this enormous serpent comes from <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladon_%28mythology%29">Ladon</a>, a myth from the Archaic Greek period (c. 800–480 BCE). The stories about Ladon conflict a bit, but the one thing they agree on is that Ladon coiled around a tree bearing golden apples that granted immortality, acting as a sleepless guardian. <p></p>&#10;      </div>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <b>Card of the Day - Wednesday, November 06, 2013</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td colspan="2">&#10;      <div>&#10;        <p>&#10;          </p><div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <a>&#10;                <img alt="Sakura-Tribe Elder" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Sakura-Tribe%20Elder&amp;options=">&#10;              </a>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </div>&#10;          <b>&#10;            <a>Sakura-Tribe Elder</a>&#10;          </b> – <i>Commander</i> (2013 Edition).
