The look of *Time Spiral*. Where to begin? *Time Spiral* was a very different block for **Magic** Creative from day one. Many of the normal conversations were completely irrelevant (is it crazy metal-world? Who runs the Izzet Guild?), and a whole new set of visual problems had to be tackled… and tackled well! We knew the bar was set awfully high after the tight, smart and successful world building executed in the *Ravnica* block. I’m going to be speaking between roles here. My main involvement in *Time Spiral* creative was not as Art Director, but as lead Concept Artist, working with Jeremy Cranford (my predecessor) and Brady Dommermuth to provide the visuals that would ultimately populate the style guide. I will do my best to give insight from the Art Direction and Concept Art standpoint as faithfully as I can.

It’s worth mentioning that you can listen to a pretty good preamble to all of this in [September’s podcast](/en/articles/podcast/2006/09-16-all-time-spiral-all-the-time). It’s worth the download just to hear me marble-mouth my way through the interview. Fun!

Here is what we knew about *Time Spiral*, or as it was known at the time, codename: Snap. We knew it was the long awaited return to Dominaria. We knew Dominaria had been wrecked a couple times over by the Mages’ War and by the Phyrexian Invasion. We knew the card file was broad, calling upon anything and anyone from any point in Dominaria’s representation on cards, and we knew that the card file was changing a lot! We knew there would be out-of-time ‘cameos’ on the cards, usually of personalities, but maybe even of places, and that these cameos had to be discernible visually within the setting.

The correct direction seemed to be the idea of ‘skins,’ a set of ‘rules’ and visuals that could be applied to whatever ended up in the card file. We could decide what denoted white or blue on this troubled Dominaria and apply that to whatever Legendary character or creature or familiar place to tie it into the setting (and to ignore these things for out-of-time characters and show them in their pristine condition to set them apart). For this article, I’m going to give you a quick run-through of what these ‘skins’ were for each of the colors of **Magic**. For each, you’ll notice that there are guidelines for the handling of locations, garb, creatures and gear.

Let’s start with the basics… the world in general. We quickly established tonal notes to set a mood for the set. No blue sky. No sun on the horizon. The hostile environment gets worse, more toxic, as you climb in elevation (this comes to bear especially in red). Figures are depersonalized, and conversely the land itself has taken on an anthropomorphic despair. We took big tonal cues from the work of Beksinski and other evocatively bleak but über-engaging artists. I also felt it important that we be true to the havoc wreaked by the Phyrexians and that we be ever-mindful of the legacy that conflict left on the face of the land.

On to specifics!
