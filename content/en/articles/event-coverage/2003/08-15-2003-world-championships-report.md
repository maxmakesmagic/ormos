Pro Tour-Mainz was the only PT-level event in Germany to date. That was almost six years ago. I qualified for it the hard way and was pretty excited about it. I was just some guy, with no real chance to win anything. I made it to the Top 64 and was very, very happy with that. Last week, things were a bit differently. The 2003 World Championships took place in Berlin, Germany, not much more then 250 miles away from my hometown. Today, I am not just some guy. I am sitting on top of the lifetime winnings list. I have won more than twice as many Pro Tours as any other player. I’ve been Pro Tour Player of the Year three times and going into the tournament, I was in a great position to take the title for the third year in a row. I was certainly one of the favorites for this tourney and I really, really wanted to do well. I mean, I want to do well in any tournament I play in, but this World Championships was different; the urge to win the whole thing was a lot stronger then in other tourneys. Winning this one in Germany would be the best accomplishment possible for me.

But, unfortunately, we live in the real world. When I woke up at home on Tuesday morning, I indeed won a trophy, but it was only the one for winning the draft portion of the event. I still was within one step of the Top 8 for most of the tournament and it came down to just one match, in the end. This is how the tourney went for me…

I started testing seriously two weeks before the event when several people moved into Dirk’s place to hang out, play soccer, do barbecues and game. Well, mostly to game actually. The well-known names present were Blume, Mello, Brucker, Ziegner and Larkin. There were about fifteen people that were at Dirk’s flat at some point and all of them helped a great deal. Props go to Dirk as well; not everyone is brave enough to host a big group of gamers. His kitchen actually barely survived the disaster, but as the mice living there with Dirk weren’t scared away, I assume that we weren’t all that bad.

The format that we playtested most was Standard. Almost everyone liked Mirari’s Wake right from the start and the normal builds of all other decks just couldn’t reliably beat the deck. We started with the version Reinhard Blech built for the European Championship with four Wakes, four Decrees and no Mirari. The deck turned out to be a machine against green-red and goblins. Those decks can hardly beat a turn four or five Mirari’s Wake followed up by a Decree of Justice. But the deck was quite obvious and we expected everyone else to have pretty much the same version. The next step was to build decks that were designed to beat Wake. I had a blue-green-black madness deck that splashed for Cabal Therapy and Zombie Infestation with two maindeck Upheavals. Wake still won about 40% and without Unsummon, the madness version couldn’t really keep up with goblins, green-red or other blue-green decks. Not viable. Next up was zombies. Therapy, Fiend, Braids, Mind Sludge and Graveborn Muse must be enough, right? Well, sometimes, yes. But then there were a lot of games where the Wake player just drew into a Wake or the black deck didn’t have the Therapy ready to make sure Wake doesn’t hit play. And with the enchantment in play, the deck could draw a ton of cards to just win right away. The black deck won slightly more than 50%, but the version couldn’t beat creatures any more. Not viable. Someone then built a madness deck with two Upheavals and four Mana Leaks. That still wasn’t good enough to win more then 45-50% and, at that point, we knew that we were going to run Wake. The bad news was that both Decree of Justice and Wake, the most powerful cards the deck has in general, are quite poor against blue-green madness. We expected everyone to have Quiet Speculation and a maindeck Ray of Revelation and tapping five lands to cast Wake just to get it destroyed is not exactly fun. With Wake being virtually never in play, the Decrees were obviously pretty small. And even if you had eight or more lands, there was still Wonder to leave the soldiers useless and Unsummon or Logic in case the Decree was used to summon angels. At that point, David started to play mirror matches. A lot of mirror matches. He found that again Wake and Decree are not all that great. Sure, if you untap with Wake and keep it in play for one or two turns, you definitely win. But early on, you can’t really protect it against Mana Leak and later your opponent should be able to Cunning Wish for Ray of Revelation and that kills two of your Wakes. The Decrees are good and win games sometimes, but in general your opponent is sitting there with a hand full of Moment’s Peaces and Wraths of God. And without real countermagic, you can’t really start to fight for his Wraths. One Mirari made it back into the maindeck as it proved to be very powerful in the late game and it was okay against blue-green as well as they usually had no way to deal with artifacts. Circular Logic and Rewind are both good as well, but they are pretty awful against anything else and while we expected quite a bit Wake, we also wanted to beat creature decks.

 b) Your opponent doesn’t draw lands

I made use of a) b) and c) plenty of times in that draft. Overall, the tourney was still pretty good for Germany. Three Germans made it to the Top 8, probably a new record if you don’t count Team PTs. I helped Daniel Zink and Peer Kroeger test their matchups a bit on Saturday. Peer knew that he had no real chance against Wake with his Reanimator, but Daniel looked alright.
