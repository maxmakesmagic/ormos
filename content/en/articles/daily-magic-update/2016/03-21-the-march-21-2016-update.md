The Daily *Magic* Update is a roundup of everything *Magic* you should know on March 21, 2016. Today's Update is brought to you by lots and lots of things to cover. This weekend, today, and [April 6](#april6) have given us quite a bit to talk about.

# Today's Previews

Every day during preview season, you can look to this section for any previews you might have missed, as we'll keep it updated with anything previewed somewhere other than DailyMTG.

1.  [Burn from Within](http://www.hareruyamtg.com/article/category/detail/2573)

A new graveyard-related mana rock. It's been a while since we've had a two-mana, non-creature artifact that produces mana. Could this be something special?

Mono-blue decks, we see you with this preview from PureMTGO.com. But you won't be seeing many creatures in play if you can commit to Islands with this beauty. Like, a real commitment. [If you like it then you better put a ring on it–level commitment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4m1EFMoRFvY).

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Grixis Control Takes Paris](https://web.archive.org/web/20160622071601/http://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/gppar16/top-8-decklists-2016-03-20)

With a Hall of Fame Pro (Ben Stark) standing in his way, Petr Sochůrek didn't blink. And why would he? He had three of the most powerful characters in Standard on his side—[Chandra, Flamecaller](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Flamecaller); [Jace, Vryn's Prodigy](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Vryn%27s+Prodigy); and [Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalitas%2C+Traitor+of+Ghet). This weekend, it seemed like the four of them—Petr included—could do little wrong, and the Grixis deck took down a strong Top 8 at [Grand Prix Paris](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-paris-2016).

Surprisingly, much of this deck is surviving the rotation. [Dig Through Time](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dig+Through+Time) is the biggest loss, though [Murderous Cut](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Murderous+Cut) and [Disdainful Stroke](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disdainful+Stroke) hurt as well. Losing the fetch lands is rough, but with only three colors, it's entirely possible the deck holds on to enough fixing to remain around. Cut and Stroke are likely at least a little replaceable too. It's [Dig Through Time](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dig+Through+Time), really, that will make or break whether this deck can survive come rotation. Is it replaceable? Can we find another card drawer? Is Chandra still amazing?

It's almost time to find out. But, in the meantime, congratulations to Petr Sochůrek for winning Grand Prix Paris.
