There are a handful of players the caliber of Paul Rietzl. The Pro Tour Hall of Fame elect, multiple Grand Prix and Pro Tour Top 8 player – winning Pro Tour Amsterdam in 2010 and Grand Prix San Jose (with his teammates Matt Sperling and David Williams) and Mexico City in 2012 – is undoubtedly one of the game's greats. With his career and successes spanning both Limited and Constructed formats, his understanding and approach to the game is insightful and entertaining.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpslc14/rietzl_d2.jpg)

*Pro Tour Hall of Fame elect and 13th-ranked Paul Rietzl is one of the game's greatest players.* 

  

He's also currently ranked 13th in the Top 25, as one might expect of the player who made it to the finals of a Pro Tour held the same weekend as a Grand Prix he almost made Top 8 of: Paris, 2012.

Rietzl's draft began with [Indulgent Tormentor](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Indulgent+Tormentor), [Siege Wurm](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Wurm), and [Cruel Sadist](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Sadist), and followed close to the black-green plan from there. Plenty of removal, including [Plummet](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plummet), [Crippling Blight](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crippling+Blight), two copies of [Flesh to Dust](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flesh+to+Dust), and three copies of [Stab Wound](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stab+Wound). That isn't the say Rietzl's draft was just elementary. 

"That was a little weird, wasn't it?" Rietzl asked when the interview began. What were some of the standout moments that Rietzl was looking back on?

"I wish I took that [Plummet](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plummet) over the second [Festergloom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Festergloom)," he said. "I already passed the [Siege Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Dragon) and I had to take a [Plummet](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plummet) over a second [Roaring Primadox](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Roaring+Primadox). One of the hardest picks was taking Ranger's [Guile](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Guile) over [Mind Rot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mind+Rot). I already had [Cruel Sadist](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Sadist), [Indulgent Tormentor](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Indulgent+Tormentor) – things I want to protect. [Mind Rot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mind+Rot) is a card I don't want to main deck, but Ranger's [Guile](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Guile) is a card I never want to main deck. [Mind Rot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mind+Rot) is a card you might play if Pack 3 doesn't go well, but in the two particular packs I had I was willing to take Ranger's [Guile](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Guile) over it. [Mind Rot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mind+Rot) loses some value in Draft over Sealed: People's decks are more focused and it's less about trying to grind out advantage over a longer game." 

The first pick in pack three turned out trick as well: [Necrogen Scudder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrogen+Scudder) versus [Netcaster Spider](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Netcaster+Spider). "It turns out that [Netcaster Spider](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Netcaster+Spider) would have been better than [Necrogen Scudder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrogen+Scudder) for my deck," Rietzl admitted. "It's not the case in most decks, but I looked to see if I could table a black card out of the pack and they were all terrible. But I'm not unhappy with a Scudder." Three [Stab Wound](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stab+Wound)s followed the [Necrogen Scudder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrogen+Scudder), setting up opportunities for Rietzl to be surprisingly aggressive with the right draw. 

"What I would have loved to have is a Rotfeaster Maggot," Rietzl said. "But if I could have gone back I'd still have taken the [Satyr Wayfinder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Satyr+Wayfinder) over it. Black-green has so many different five drops; with the way my deck played out with [Cruel Sadist](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Sadist) I could use another life gain card, and \[[Rotfeaster Maggot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rotfeaster+Maggot)\] happens to be the best one. But I'll have to just make do with three [Stab Wound](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stab+Wound)s." 

What's a card some may be surprised to find Rietzl likes in his deck? "Black Cat is an all-star in my deck," he said. His deck is "so slow. Cat helps with convoke, it blocks, and it's cheap."

So how did Rietzl feel about his deck overall? "I think from a high level my deck is great," he said. "I have a really good curve, and all my spells are the premiere removal spells – exactly what you want. I have rares. I made a few early picks in the draft that were designed to set me up for a high ceiling later on: Taking [Invasive Species](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Invasive+Species) over [Rotfeaster Maggot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rotfeaster+Maggot) to turn on [Satyr Wayfinder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Satyr+Wayfinder) and Necromancer's Assistant. You can get flooded on five drops pretty easily and thankfully I'm not." 

"My average card quality is pretty high," Rietzl said. "In my deck, the seventeenth through twenty-third cards," referring to the marginal picks like [Black Cat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Black+Cat) that typically fill out a Draft deck, "are really good in my deck. They support convoke and the fact that I can selectively kill with plentiful removal the threats that they can't stop, and use my worse cards to deals with the threats they can. And [Indulgent Tormentor](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Indulgent+Tormentor) is absolutely absurd: Every time you play it it's better than you expected." 

So what's really changed between our first sight of *Magic 2015* at the Pro Tour and now, the end of it's time in the spotlight? "The way it's changed the most is that people's evaluation has gotten better," Rietzl said. "It's hard to draft the good decks like green-white convoke, and green-black graveyard recursion. You have to focus on bread and butter *Magic*: Good curve, removal spells, and bombs, all of which my deck thankfully has." 

"I just like drafting creatures; I draft creatures over everything," he continued. "Like [Titanic Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Titanic+Growth) as a great card the last time they printed it but I think it's borderline unplayable. Here," in *Magic 2015* Draft, "I want to cast creatures every turn because of the convoke mechanic and that you can't really get punished by sweepers." The lack of cards like [Pyroclasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyroclasm) or [Day of Judgment](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Day+of+Judgment) mean creatures can count on sticking around in most games. 

"For that reason, Nissa's Expedition – an objectively bad *Magic* card in my opinion – becomes good because I play so many creatures," Rietzl said. "I don't mind splashing nearly as much as in other formats because Nissa's Expedition is fine, [Meteorite](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Meteorite) isn't bad, and you'll always play [Satyr Wayfinder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Satyr+Wayfinder): They don't make you fight too hard for the splash. If the fixing is almost good enough to make a two color deck it's pretty attractive to splash something like [Kird Chieftain](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Chieftain) or [Heat Ray](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Heat+Ray). In most Limited formats I'm the last person to want to splash but I don't mind here." 

So what is a kind of card that doesn't excite Rietzl? "I don't like cards like [Sign in Blood](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sign+in+Blood) here. I'd rather have a two-drop creature on turn two," he said. "It adds strain on your mana, isn't a creature, and there's never a turn I want to cast it. In this format, card advantage isn't as important and there aren't that many mana sinks: If you flood out it's pretty hard to win." 

Does Reitzl play favorites with archetypes and colors in drafting *Magic 2015*? "The green-black graveyard deck is the one I have the most fun trying to draft. It's what you almost saw in this one," Rietzl said. "I have a Necromancer's Assistant in the sideboard and [Invasive Species](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Invasive+Species) in the main. I never had the cards that made it come together, but that's why it's important to take the cards with the highest floor: You may not get the synergistic cards but you keep the best chance to 3-0 the draft. I had a draft on *Magic Online* the other day with six [Satyr Wayfinder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Satyr+Wayfinder)s, three [Gravedigger](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gravedigger)s, and a bunch of [Kird Chieftain](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Chieftain)-type creatures, and I could play it all. It was pretty great." 

"The more I play a format, the less I develop a preference for one deck or another," Rietzl continued. "Early on I'll have a preference, but now I've played with the cards so much that I'm basically comfortable drafting any deck. It really goes back to Drafting 101: Read the draft and what's open. In the middle of Pack 1 I could have taken the [Jorubai Murk Lurker](https://web.archive.org/web/20140907000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jorubai+Murk+Lurker) but I stayed the course, and I think I was rewarded for that." 

So where does *Magic 2015* fit in the pantheon of Limited formats? "It's actually a really good Draft format," Rietzl said. "In Sealed, the good cards are so good you need a plan for beating them, but in Limited the cards have more interactions and linear strategies – artifacts, convoke, graveyard – but there's other things you don't pick up on until you draft it enough. I found *Magic 2015* has more replayability for a Core Set than you'd expect. This format ended up developing better than expected."
