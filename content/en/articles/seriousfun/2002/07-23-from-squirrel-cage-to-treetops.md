Some of my favorite flavor text has nothing to do with Squirrels.

Jackalope Herd, coming in at a hefty 4/5 for four mana, taunts your opponent with the truism *"There's little more demeaning than getting your butt kicked by a bunch of bunnies."* What makes the ordeal even worse is how simple it should be to force those bunnies off the board: just make your opponent play a spell, *any* spell, and they bounce. For casual players, it has been easy to turn this to our advantage: Wizards of the Coast was kind enough to print Pandemonium in the same expansion as the Herd, and kitchen tables around the world got scratch marks from all the bunny paws jumping up and down on them. (Ah, Pandemonium. How many bad cards has that enchantment made look good? Timid Drake, Pangosaur, Viashino Sandscout . . . the list goes on and on.)

Death by bunnies has to be the most ghastly way to lose a **Magic** game. (There is, you know, a sort of poetic link between Kezzerdrix and Jackalope Herd that could form the basis for a deck . . . hmmm, perhaps another time.) But after bunnies, the most humiliating road to defeat must be lined with another small rodent: the squirrel.

![squirrels](https://i.ormos.uk/b1/f2/b1f2a74d0c287d08666db237d03242795e5c319f29ca75b5698f952566332fcf.jpg)

Squirrels in the grand scheme of **Magic** are like cooling rods in a nuclear reactor. They are in the game to make absolutely certain that no player gets so serious, so completely geeked out with the fantasy world of mystical mages or the mathematical world of topdeck probabilities, that he or she can't be brought back to Earth with four simple words:

*"Squirrels swing, lethal damage."* 

I mean, come on--you were bitten by a Squirrel and you died. I don't care what behemoth blew your arm off the previous turn or how unlucky you were to draw nothing but lands, or if you got a graduate degree from MIT, or whatever.

A Squirrel *bit* you. And you *died.* Go sit on the bench, man.

Naturally, this image fits the casual player perfectly. We don't mind dying at the paws of Squirrels. In fact, it kinda tickles. Nor do we mind the only slightly lesser humiliation of depending on a bushy-tailed army to get the job done. Hey, if it works, it works.

But exactly how *does* it work? Glad I pretended you asked. I hereby present the Top 5 Death-by-Squirrel strategies for use in your next group game. Some of them are incredibly deadly; some are barely so; others are just hoping against hope you'll topdeck Epic Struggle. (No struggle is truly epic without the participation of ten-ounce rodents.)

![Squirrel Cage](https://i.ormos.uk/b2/bd/b2bd20226fbd1adfb046b00a688f1b27a132a5f11b89a25eb07a61c1d777ec6f.jpg)

### 5\. The Squirrel Cage

"Squirrel cage" is an actual English-language metaphor for "an endless loop." To wit: Earthcraft, Liege of the Hollows, and Ashnod's Altar. There are different ways to do this, with different supporting cards. Explore your own variations if you're combo-inclined.

My favorite memory of a deck like this involves watching a guy playing Squirrels build up an arbitrarily large number of them--I believe he came up with the highly original number of 1,000,000. He looked at his opponent, who had a mere five permanents (four islands and an Altar of Dementia), and said, "So you concede, right?"

His opponent said, "I don't think so," and calmly played Reins of Power. "Steal 1,000,000 Squirrels. Sack 1,000,000 Squirrels to the Altar. Mill 1,000,000 of your cards."

Of course, Sandstorm, Pyroclasm, and Simoon are also highly satisfying answers to this problem, assuming you're not operating the midst of an Overrun. Speaking of which . . . .

### 4\. Supernaturally Large Squirrels

Small Squirrels are pretty funny. Really big Squirrels may be even funnier. While obvious choices include Overrun, there are trickier ways to give your fragile army the boost it needs. Consider a Vernal Equinox in play, six mana available, and a Nut Collector in your hand; or an active Goblin Welder that trades the Thran Weaponry in play for the Coat of Arms in your graveyard; or a bunch of Squirrel tokens led by a pair of Krosan Beasts and a Rites of Initiation in your hand.

Multiplayer tip: Large-scale solutions, from Prismatic Strands to Evacuation, are often available to blue and white, no matter how big your armies get, so you should defeat opponents playing black, green, and red first. And save any Creeping Molds or other utility you have until you're facing down that control player--you'll need them.

### 3\. The Chosen Rodent

One oft-overlooked possibility with a swarm of Squirrels is allowing a single unblocked Squirrel to go crazy (please note the pun I avoided right there. That's a free service, folks--no extra charge for skipping the obvious).

You can stay consistent in both color and artwork by choosing Might of Oaks. You make one incredibly vicious Squirrel using Hatred (best in team play because replenishing life will be an issue). Or you can punish a reckless red mage by playing Temper on one token in the midst of an Earthquake. This last option not only keeps the Squirrel large, but also gives you the chance to put *counters* on top of your *token*, which ain't nothin' to sneeze at.

### 2\. Fabulous Flying Squirrels

The fact that flying Squirrels actually exist doesn't making pelting your opponents with them any less fun. Wonder may be more spectacular when used with a Wurm token, but with a Squirrel token, the possibilities are a bit more, well, aesthetically pleasing.

When Squirrels fly (and it could be Wonder, Levitation, Balloon Peddler, Skyshaper, or whatever), you can do interesting things with them. You can push the tokens into formations with your fingers while you make airplane-buzzing sounds. (Everybody knows that Wurms don't have propellers, and therefore cannot make these sounds. They also lack submachine guns, which is critical to any airborne raid.)

You can also get a plastic tray or something else with a flat surface that can sit above the table. Then you can make your flying Squirrels jump off the platform as they gain flying. Don't forget to shriek *wheeeeeeeee* as your nimble rodents zip up to the platform, and then *aaaaaieeeee* as they come crashing down again. *Aaaaaieeeee* is a highly revered war cry among the *Tamiasciurus* genus. (Note to my friends at Wizards: Once the storyline has room for a Squirrel Legend--and any self-respecting fantasy storyline should, no?--then Scurryday would be a lovely joke name for those of us who are taxonomically inclined.)

### 1\. Conspiratorial Squirrels

Have you ever wanted to play a Crush of Squirrels? Do you wish you had a 6/6 flying Squirrel Legend that could give you *more* Squirrels, or force a Persecute, or banish a certain color from the board whenever it dealss damage? Do you suppose that Verdant Force really has a soft spot for Squirrels instead of Saprolings, and in fact may be a closet Squirrel itself?
