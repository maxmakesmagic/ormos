**Sideboard: How did you end up teaming with Neil and Gary?**

Maher: Well originally, Neil and I were planning on playing with Dave Williams. Dave and I have been friends since around ’97. All the kids approximately our age - Finkel, myself, Zila, Williams, the OMS brothers, Nate Clarke, Feming, all those guys, we’re all friends. We used to get together and just draft. We all knew each other and we all respected each other’s talent. We wanted to draft, because we all just played. So we used to do random teams and all got to know each other from there and our relationship just grew. 

Neil and Dave knew each other from the PTQ circuit. They were really close, and I got introduced to Neil at Barcelona. He started off quite well in Barcelona and Dave convinced me that Neil was a good player. That whole weekend, Neil was kind of like our point man for drafting. He’d go out and find drafts, and then we’d team up with him. The three of us have been tight ever since. But then Dave got banned and Neil had never even considered that Dave and I would play with him. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptbos02/987.jpg)I guess he in his own mind doesn’t see himself as good of a player as he is. He always thinks it’s a joke when people refer to him as the number one Limited player in the States. I honestly believe it to be true. He never thought of it as an option and Dave approached him one day after he had made Top 8 in San Diego, and said “You realize that if you wanted to, it might be a possibility when I get unbanned for you, me and Bob to play together.” Neil just had this stunned look on his face like “Really? You guys would play with me?” Dave thought Neil would blow it off because Dave had zero Pro Tour points and it would be really hard for us to qualify. He wasn’t sure Neil would want to back out on the friends he had been playing with. Neil was just thrilled that we really thought he was good and weren’t just drafting with him that weekend.

So we spent the whole season trying to qualify with Dave. We fell like two points short; Neil and I both had awful Worlds performances. It just came down to scrambling for a team, and we happened to have dinner with Gary Wise and he said he had been thinking about playing with a team, but if we’d be interested he thought that would be his best option. Neil and I talked about it and with me just coming off a suspension… Gary’s a good guy. He’s not someone you have to worry about having a bad reputation or anything like that. It just all worked out and both Gary and Neil could come out to Madison and playtest. Because, with Dave we all know each other and communicate well, which is really good for the team events, because you have to know what the other people are thinking. So, it just happened to work out where Gary took a week and a half off and spent it in Madison and we could all get to know each other’s games.

**Sideboard: All three of you guys have very different personalities. Does that interfere or help?**

Maher: The personalities Neil and I have aren’t as different as people would assume. If we’re around people we’re very comfortable with, we’re very similar. It’s just that Neil has somewhat of a stand-offish attitude when he’s around newer people. I have a general attitude of “I like everyone until I have a reason not to”. So, from the outside looking in, we probably look like three points on a triangle, but me, Neil and Dave are very close and very similar and we know each other well. Gary does have a very different outlook on things.

Neil and I both come at the game from a very pessimistic standpoint - we always feel that we’re losing and we can’t afford to make any mistakes. Gary always comes at it that he feels like he’s winning and he should be winning. Even when we get interviewed at the Team Pro Tour and get asked for predictions, Neil and I won’t give one because we don’t want to say we’re going to lose, but just coming out and saying we’re going to win feels cocky to us. It doesn’t feel like something we should be saying because it seems like we’re boasting. Gary always needs to make that prediction that he’s going to be winning.

**Sideboard: Gary actually pulled me aside away from the table during a post-draft interview at Boston and told me you guys would win just so you wouldn’t have to hear it.**

Maher: Yeah, and we do have very different personalities, but it was part of the reason we did as well as we did. Gary’s personality made sure Neil and I weren’t out half the night drafting. We were always very on top of things. Gary is a very organized person. He put us together to practice and even though his record in the tournament wasn’t stellar, it wasn’t poor either; he had a lot to do with our success there. 

**Sideboard: In what way?**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptbos02/794.jpg)Maher: Coming up with draft strategy, but even just… Gary was very good at keeping our heads in the game. Neil is very easily frustrated. He hates losing more than most anything in life. Gary was very good at talking to Neil and re-centering him, getting him back to where he needed to be. Gary was very good with me because he’s very good at communication. I can’t watch the whole draft at once, but during the draft we hadn’t actually decided on a strategy. During the draft, if it seemed like there was a close call, he kept deferring to me. Having Gary there really helped, because we were able to communicate any information we were trying to figure out very quickly. It was just something we clicked on. There were even things where we hadn’t worked out signals beforehand. We always knew that if there was something on the fly, we knew what other people were talking about. It just gave us a certain cohesion. It was a yin and yang where he just covered us on a lot of our weak points.

**Sideboard: Who led the drafts? Was it one person specifically?**

Maher: No one led the drafts. Gary realized while we were practicing that his card evaluation wasn’t quite where he thought it was. He basically deferred to us because as long as we’re all using the same card evaluation, we’ll have at least a cohesive strategy. If we’re concentrating on making sure that our opponents aren’t getting certain cards, then we can do that. It’s not like if he’s on the end, then a card is going to slip past him. When it was close calls, he usually deferred to Neil and I, but no one actually led the draft. There were different parts of the draft that everyone did. Especially matchups, like certain people just knew specific matchups better than other people. We moved our colors around a lot more than other teams. We were much more concerned with where our matchups went than where our colors went. So, a lot of the time we had to defer to each other. No one really led any of our drafts.

**Sideboard: Is the team going to stay together or will you guys team with Dave now?**

Maher: Neil, Dave and I are too close not to play together, but we also have a problem with the Team Masters coming up. If we were to finish in the finals, we’d qualify for the following Masters…

**Sideboard: So then you’d do the Masters with Gary and the Pro Tour with Dave?**

Maher: That’s what we were thinking, but this weekend as we were talking about it, we realized that if we qualified for the Masters with Gary and then played in the Pro Tour with Dave… when Gary goes to play in the Pro Tour with whoever he plays with, let’s say Gary makes the finals of that Pro Tour. All of a sudden, Gary has to decide whether he wants to play in the Masters with us, or his new Pro Tour team, and somebody gets left out of $3000. So, it brings a whole new level to it because Gary knew even before we played with him that Neil and I always planned on playing with Dave. Long term, it’s the three of us. We’re trying to work something out that if Neil and I play with Dave, and Gary with his new team, that we can make it work for everyone. But all of that hinges on whether we make the finals of the Masters or not. 

**Sideboard: Do you think you could have the same success with Dave that you did with Gary? You were saying that Gary provided certain things, so do you think that taking him out of the picture would be detrimental?**

Maher: It could. I don’t claim to know everything about the team format. That was my first successful performance in any kind of team thing basically. But, Neil and I also learned a lot just being around Gary. I don’t know. Gary certainly adds a dynamic to the team that’s truly intangible, but Neil and I also felt bad for Dave because he was happy with the team he played with at the Pro Tour, but Neil and I didn’t necessarily…

**Sideboard: Who did Dave play with again?**

Maher: Chris Benafel and Pat Chapin.

**Sideboard: And you ran interference for them in the grinder.**

Maher: Yeah, we actually had to find someone that Neil and I wouldn’t qualify with. We got into the grinder with a team that had 49 points, because if you had 50 points you couldn’t play.

**Sideboard: Who did you play with?**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptbos02/970.jpg)Maher: Nick Little. He’s a guy from Indiana. He’s a friend of mine. So we found someone who was willing to play that wouldn’t give us 50 points so we could run interference for Dave and we were really happy he got into the Pro Tour. But, Dave’s another highly competitive person like Neil and I and we really wanted him playing with people at the top of their game.

**Sideboard: Well, Benafel and Chapin isn’t a bad team.**

Maher: No it’s not a bad team at all, but it was one of those rogue teams where people just got thrown together. We felt bad for Dave when they didn’t perform well. So, it’s just one of those things where you know you have a cohesive unit and you’re not just putting together a mercenary team. I, Neil and Dave have never given it a shot. If we play in the Team Pro Tour and do awfully and Gary plays with a new team and does awfully, we might put it back together and just throw up our hands and say, “We don’t really understand what’s going on, but this works so why don’t we go back to it?”
