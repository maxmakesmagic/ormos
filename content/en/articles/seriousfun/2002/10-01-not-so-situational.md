### Cures and instincts that can serve you well

Even though *Onslaught* card previews are officially done, I wanted to take a moment to address one or two more cards I feel are particularly interesting in group play. There were many cards that won’t make it onto my “top ten” multiplayer list; but that doesn’t mean they’re not a beating in group play.

Take, for example, False Cure.

First impressions are often unfairly negative. False Cure will strike most players in one of two ways: either as a tournament metagame card with no purpose, since life gain is usually not a viable strategy; or (more accurately) as a multiplayer card that seems a bit situational – I mean, *what if no one’s playing life gain?*

Indeed. What if no one’s playing life gain? I mean, dead cards are deadly in any format, and as any strategist will tell you –

Hey. Wait a second. *Not playing life gain?! Why aren’t YOU playing life gain???*

### PHYSICIAN, HEAL THYSELF

Readers familiar with my work will recall that I am, at best, [ambivalent](/en/articles/seriousfun/2002/06-25-insert-bad-life-wordplay-here) about white life gain. But they’ll also remember that I do enjoy it when I can twist it into a shadow of its original self.

A leisurely scan of any card database will show you that not all life gain works the same way. Sometimes, the card only lets “you” gain the life. But many times – and in at least two spectacular cases – it tells you to target a player. Why on earth should that player always be *you?* Why should *you* have all the fun? Share the wealth, for heaven’s sake.

Start with Congregate. (Everyone else does.) While I’ve had good fun using Misdirection when other players cast this spell, my new favorite trick may be casting it myself, and then following it up with False Cure. With enough creatures on the board, six mana can do a heck of a lot of damage. As we build our deck below, we’ll look for ways to increase the number of creatures out there.

![Congregate, Swords to Plowshares, Armistice](https://i.ormos.uk/e4/71/e4714510e9ef3dc6a68d23a0efac59f3a1b083453bf9543ddf1b2058e4398630.jpg)Continue with Swords to Plowshares. I wouldn’t always add the False Cure – it’s typically enough just to get rid of the creature in question – but it would be a spectacular trick on a plump fattie.

End with Armistice. The deck I’m thinking of will likely empty its hand a bit too quickly, so replenishment is advisable. Add life gain for an opponent, and you have the ideal situation: a “political” card that we’re finally using the right way!

From here, the deck begins to build itself. The two central tricks – False Cure with life gain, and rebuilt card advantage where you can get it – nearly dictate the pattern of the deck. Syphon Mind will let us take advantage of another new *Onslaught* multiplayer gem, and token creatures are probably the best way for a white-black deck to get the most creature bang for the card buck. (Remember – those Congregates have to be good!) I wouldn’t recommend this deck for more than three opponents, but careful prodding with token creatures should combine with a fun False Cure trick to win plenty of times in a small crowd.

### MUST…ATTACK…CAN’T…STOP…

![Overwhelming Instinct](https://i.ormos.uk/e4/2f/e42fbb52e1f61bc01ad8a0f880c883a8b5cf9111566f58543b17a77c65caff00.jpg)A card that may appear slightly less situational, but will still turn many players off because of its built-in drawback, is Overwhelming Instinct.

The problem with Overwhelming Instinct is the same problem many cards in this set (such as the "tap five" Lords, Biorhythm, and Aggravated Assault) will have – for it to work the way you want, you absolutely depend on having creatures on the board.

The more I look at *Onslaught*, the more I believe that the correct metagame call is global sweepers – Mutilate or Wrath of God will simply blow away half of the kitchen table not because they’re killing off creatures, but because they’re wiping out entire deck themes. Overextension in multiplayer is very risky. Overwhelming Instinct (or insert other appropriate *Onslaught* card name here) demands that you extend further than normal. Therefore, Overwhelming Instinct in multiplayer is very risky. It begs your opponents to render it useless.

So you’ll need to answer two questions before using this card:

*   Can you find a good response to global sweepers such as Starstorm?
*   Is this enchantment’s strategy really worth protecting (i.e., is the extra card every combat phase really worth it)?

For the sake of argument, I’m going to suggest that the answer to both questions in group play is “yes.” The trick to using this card will be twofold: **haste**, which is a strong multiplayer mechanic because it throws off math and makes opponents uncertain of their payoff; and **recursion**, which increases the chances that you will maintain three creatures on the board at any given point in time.

Where you don’t have the rare (or *Legends* uncommon), just throw in any random card you have that generates lots of creatures (from Mogg Infestation to Rith, the Awakener) or recurs stuff you might need (from Restock to Reclaim). Adjust red (or white!) mana as necessary.

Overwhelming Instinct isn’t the path to victory in such a deck – not like False Cure is above. But it does give your deck a probable long-term advantage. Even having a single copy on the board as a “dead” card applies relentless pressure on the dwindling number of opponents you’ll face as the game goes on – they can’t burn your Squirrels, Bobcats, and Saprolings forever. Eventually, you’ll get to attack the way you want.

Two more quick ideas with Overwhelming Instinct: First, Aggravated Assault or other multiple combat phase cards gives you the chance to draw repeated cards. (And yes, while we’re talking about that card, it’s solid in group play. There are also “arbitrarily large” Aggravated Assault combos. You’ll have a wonderful sense of accomplishment if you discover them for yourself.) Second, “vigilant” creatures that don’t tap to attack – from Lightning Angel to Ardent Militia – give you more incentive to extend with your attacks a bit.
