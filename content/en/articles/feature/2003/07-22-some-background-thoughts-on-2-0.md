### Why We Did What We Did

* * *

Well, the [**full list of new features**](/en/articles/feature/2003/07-22-version-2-0-cometh) you can find in ***Magic** Online* Version 2.0 is up now and I'm sure some of you are curious about why we set things up the way we did. The purpose of this article is to answer as many of those questions in advance as possible. Here goes...

### The Trading Post

Everyone who does any trading within ***Magic** Online* seems to agree that the current trading rooms could work a lot better. The tricky thing we discovered when we looked into how exactly to improve them is that there are lots of different users who want the trading rooms to do lots of different things. Some people like to imagine themselves on the floor of the stock market, pouncing on offers as soon as they see a good opportunity to buy low or sell high. Others just want to take a leisurely browse through someone else's collection and see what leaps out at them. Still others want to turn their cards into tix with as little effort as possible.

We decided that we wanted to make trading fun for as many people as possible, so Version 2.0 contains three radically different systems for finding trading partners: 

1.  The **Market** consists of constantly scrolling text messages -- it's similar to the original trading system that we had way back when the game first launched, but it's been improved with an extra feature -- the pause/play button. We expect that the adrenaline junkies and the Wall Street wannabes will hang out here.  
  
2.  The **Bulletin Board** works pretty much the same way that the current **Trading Post** works, except that it's all merged into one room (since trying to split it up into different categories didn't actually work) and posts will "time out" after 2 hours.  
  
3.  The **Casual Trade** room is designed to be the emporium that so many people have asked for. When you walk in you'll see a bunch of avatars sitting at tables with their binders open on the table. If you want to look through someone's binder, you sit down at that table and browse. Special icons give you an idea before you sit down of how many rares a given person has flagged as tradable and also how active a trader he or she is.

Our hope with this three-room system is that each of you can decide what you like to do and then just do that. We believe there are enough of you who like to "play the trading game" in each of these ways that all three rooms will have the critical mass of people necessary to support them.

### The Newbie Experience

One of the biggest complaints we've gotten about ***Magic** Online* is that it isn't very friendly to new players. The product has gotten off to a great start, but we think it can get even bigger and better. One crucial piece of that is making sure that players who have never played a **Magic** game before can figure out what's going on. We're introducing a whole suite of changes in an effort to meet this goal: 

1.  There's a new tutorial that teaches you how to play **Magic** against a computer opponent. If you're reading this article then that probably means you're already playing ***Magic** Online* and you won't care about this, but we're really happy because we think the new tutorial is a vast improvement.  
  
2.  There's a **Tour** that should help new players find their way around the ***Magic** Online* community and several slide shows that explain various aspects of **Magic** and ***Magic** Online* in more detail. You'll also be able to get to the tour and slide shows directly from our website.  
  
3.  The Free Trial is a lot easier to find since it now has a button in the middle of the **Log On** screen instead of a tab up at the top of it. Of course, the new tutorials and the tour are available from the Free Trial server, too. (We're also looking at plans for a mentor program that will operate in the Free Trial area, but that's still down the road.)  
  
4.  This is the one that will actually affect most of you: We changed around the room locations a bit. Now there is a **New Player View** which has only a small subsection of the many ***Magic** Online* rooms visible. To get to the myriad mess of sometimes confusing and sometimes overwhelming possibilities (that regular players already understand and know how to use), just select **Expert Player View**. The flip side is that all the rooms geared toward new players disappear when you go to the **Expert Player View**.

### The 1800 Room

**Magic** is at its best when players are playing against opponents who are about as skilled as they are. Whether you're playing casual Constructed or Two-Headed Giant or 8-person Booster Drafts or the Monthly Championship, **Magic** is more fun when you can get a good game. With this understanding in mind, we've always wanted there to be a place where the game's top players could go and know that they would get to play a peer. Whether they're practicing for Pro Tours and Pro Tour Qualifiers or just playing for pride and ego, there should be a place where only high-rated players can get in.

With Version 2.0, that place will exist. We've created a whole new room -- the **1800 Room** -- that you can't get into unless your Composite rating is at least 1800. Inside that room there are places to play Constructed decks, places to do Team Booster Drafts, places to just hang out and chat, and (here comes the biggest change) there are 8-person tournament queues that pay out 9 packs for first place and 5 packs for second.

Limited tournaments will still cost the same 2 tickets as normal queues; they just give away more prizes. Think of it as our way of rewarding you for being among the best players in the game (or, if you prefer, think of it as a bribe to lure you out of the general population and into a room where you can all fight amongst yourselves and leave the more casual-minded drafters alone to have their own fun). Constructed 9-5 queues will cost 6 tickets (because if we gave away 14 packs for a 5-ticket entry fee then the prizes would be worth more than the entry fees).

### More 4-3-2-2 Queues

Several months ago we tried an experiment and introduced an *Onslaught* block Booster Draft queue where the prize schedule was 4-3-2-2 (instead of the usual 8 packs for first and 4 for second). Our goal was to create an environment where drafters would self-select into two groups: a more casual audience that mostly wanted to draft "just for fun" (but would be a lot happier if only they won a little something) and a more cutthroat audience that wanted the payout to be as top-heavy as possible.

Well the results of the experiment are in and the vast majority of ***Magic** Online* players prefer the 4-3-2-2 payout. Because of its popularity, we're expanding the 4-3-2-2 payout and when Version 2.0 goes live there will be a 4-3-2-2 queue for every format that we support with 8-person tournaments.

Even though players who prefer the 8-4 prize payout are in the minority, we still care about them and believe there should be a place for them to get together and play against each other. That place is the **1800 Room**. Version 2.0 is moving the 8-4 queues to the **1800 Room** and upping them to a 9-5 prize payout. So, if you're one of those players who likes a top-heavy prize schedule, get your Composite rating up to 1800. All the 8-person queues in the regular **Sanctioned Tournaments** room will pay out 4-3-2-2 but all the tournaments in the **1800 Room** will pay out 9-5.

### Trophy Room

Another cool feature of Version 2.0 is that each player has a personal trophy room now. If you right-click on players' names and select **Trophies** , you'll be able to see trophy icons representing all the tournaments they have won.

### Auto-matching In All Casual Play Rooms

Tired of having your avatar sit at an empty table while all the chat in the room consists of other players keep typing "open table"? Well, we thought that was lame too and this feature should fix that. As of Version 2.0, whenever you start a new game, you will be automatically paired as soon as someone else attempts to start a game using the same rules. (If you don't want this to happen, just type some text into the **Comment** field -- or choose **Buddies Only** -- and you won't be paired automatically.)

### *Eighth Edition* Cards

A few hours after Version 2.0 goes live, *Eighth Edition* cards go on sale (new card frames and all). When *Eighth Edition* goes on sale in the [***Magic** Online* Store](https://web.archive.org/web/20030722000000/http://magiconlinestore.wizards.com/) at 9 AM PDT Monday morning, *Seventh Edition* product won't be stocked at the store. That means no one will ever be able to buy online *Seventh Edition* product from Wizards again. If you're going to want any more of it, you might want to stock up now.

### How To Get 2.0

If you're already playing ***Magic** Online* then you don't need to do anything special -- the first time you log in after Sunday night, the game will automatically patch itself. However, if you've never installed ***Magic** Online* before, then you have a couple of options. You can download it (for free) by [**clicking here**](https://web.archive.org/web/20030722000000/http://wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic/magiconline/downloads) or you can buy the new Core Game with CD that goes on sale for $15 on Monday, July 28. The CD includes the ***Magic** Online* client, a rulebook, a game manual, and some paper cards. You'll still wind up downloading a sizable patch if you buy the CD (since it has a version of ***Magic** Online* that was up to date when we burned the CDs a couple months ago), but it will save you the massive download times associated with the full client and you'll get 81 cards to start your collection with.

That's all, folks.

So there's a rundown of some of the cool stuff that is being added to the game on Sunday and a peek into the thought processes behind the changes. It's a lot of stuff so there will be an [**extensive downtime**](/en/articles/feature/2003/07-22-version-2-0-cometh#downtime), but I definitely think it's all going to be worth it.
