### 

This is the final installment on my series analysing the cards of *Odyssey* for the purposes of Limited play. The entire series is posted [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### Artifacts

**Catalyst Stone**  
 This is the kind of card you really need to build your Constructed deck around, and in Limited you won't have the options to make it work. Of course, if both you and your opponent have four each of Firebolt and Elephant Ambush... A 13th - 15th pick. 

**Charmed Pendant**  
 I thought there might be hope for this card in the combining of threshold implementation and mana acceleration, but it simply costs too much to cast, as turn four is really when you want your best creatures hitting the table. A 10th - 12th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b9/68/b968ad012414ee087d4835506af7e0cf8c41f3cbeca8117ba8ed1300691b6bd1.jpg)**Eggs**  
 I generally won't run these unless I'm playing both colors, but if I'm short of cards and/or need threshold badly, I'll definitely run the one-color Egg. The best thing about these is that they come late. Don't force them, just grab one when there's othing else in the pack. They aren't good enough to work harder than that for them. a 7th - 9th pick.

**Junk Golem**  
 With the inclusion of *Torment*, Junk Golem comes with the additional bonus now of offering you a madness outlet, and it's a good thing; before *Torment* it was awful. Even now, you need to have a lot of madness and basically no way to utilize it, and even then your deck has to be pretty bad. Make it your goal to never cast this in Limited again if at all possible. An 11th - 13th pick.

**Limestone Golem**  
 Six mana is just too much to pay for a 3/4 creature, even if it does replace itself later. Much more viable in Sealed Deck then draft, draft is all about the early game and getting the jump on your opponent, and the Golem won't help you towards this goal one iota. I usually pass it in the hopes its eventual owner will find it staring back from their opening hand. An 9th - 11th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c9/dc/c9dcb993ee66b0c8b4218cbc455da20b9074edd96ad0639f8b7fe1c44ef7ebcb.jpg)**Millikin**  
 Nate Heiss is gonna love me for saying this: Millikin is the best artifact in *Odyssey* Block Limited. This is more a statement on the weakness of artifacts then the strength of Millikin, but this little guy is pretty good for a 0/1. Mana acceleration is a big deal on turn three, with more four casting cost spells around than anything else, and if Millikin ended there it would still be playable, but the milling of a card is huge, accelerating your deck to threshold, giving you free flashback cards and providing quick targets for Gravedigger, Scrivener and the like. If your deck needs it really badly, you can pass fourteen other cards in lieu. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Mirari**  
 The fight for second best comes down to Patchwork Gnomes and this potential monster. Mirari is obviously limited in its applications, but in the right deck can do obscene things, like duplicate your Ghastly Demise, then duplicate your Morgue Theft getting back Scrivener and Gravedigger, then duplicate two more Demises before duplicating the flashbacked Morgue Theft. Of course, this all requires a lot of mana and makes Mirari useless in the early game. Don't take it unless your deck is prepared to take full advantage, but if you're in the early stages, don't be afraid to take it and start drafting with it in mind. A 4th - 6th pick. 

**Otarian Juggernaut**  
 I'm not a fan of this card despite the easy mana, because when I pay four for a creature, I want more than a mere 2/3. The Juggernaut can definitely be a force in the late going, but it's so boring in the early game that I'd rather someone else have it. If the Juggernaut is good enough for your deck, you likely won't 3-0, but at least get some graveyard fillers to give yourself a chance. An 8th - 10th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/21/fb/21fb3700ff4bdaf2fb0b9e36fc3a899f5e9d23f06da49aa357b7520d0c2b36ed.jpg)**Patchwork Gnomes**  
 These guys got really good with the introduction of *Torment*, leaving them closer to Millikin than Mirari. A precious three-drop in an environment that lacks in that department, the Gnomes give you a threshold/madness outlet while providing a weapon that's useful on offense and defense. The introduction of Cabal Torturer to the environment hurts a lot, but the Gnomes will always be good enough for your deck. A 3rd- 5th pick. 

**Sandstone Deadfall**  
 When I first saw this card, I thought it was too slow to be effective, but people told me it was pretty good, filling your graveyard and such. I started playing it to see if those people were right, and unfortunately, they weren't. The Deadfall definitely has uses and can prove a solid choice for those archetypes that excel on holding the ground and slowing the game, but as a whole, it's a card you'll want to pick late or pass. The only targeted effect in the format that kills Iridescent Angel. An 8th - 10th pick.

**Steamclaw**  
 A better answer than those black cards like Coffin Purge and Tombfire to graveyard-reliant decks, the 'Claw is a very slow card for what it does, which causes a lot of problems for the controller. If you're confident that your deck can keep up with those aggressive green decks despite drawing this dead card, then it's a viable sideboard option. Otherwise, don't bring it in. An 11th - 13th pick.

### Gold

**Atogatog**  
 No. No, no, no. Not even if you have Crystal Quarry. For a reiteration, see the Crystal Quarry entry. A 15th pick.

**Decimate**  
 In a two-set format where one set has no artifacts and the other has four playable ones in some 350 cards, this card will almost never see use, unless your blue-black opponent has a bunch of enchantments and their Mirari is a must kill. I mean MUST kill. Mirari is never a must kill if your deck attacks. Decimate is red-green. Red-green attacks. A 13 - 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2c/27/2c274361b77f137fb80f624441c4e92b4c4b7cc47bf3f25ed270c9d2497be4d8.jpg)**Iridescent Angel**  
 With the *Torment* Nightmares rendering other rare bombs a little less effective, this 'little lady' maintains her strength, making her all the more impressive. The hardest creature to kill in the format, the Angel will generally live and let your opponent die, dealing twenty points in five turns. Worth the splash and the seven mana, the Angel is one of those rare cards that you'll seldom see, so when you do, take it. A 1st pick.

**Lithatog**  
 This is a really good card that unfortunately doesn't work very well in the best deck it's colors provide. Red-green's best variant is Squirrel beats, which doesn't focus on threshold and doesn't want to play 1/2 creatures on turn three. However, if the cards have insisted on your playing a slower red-green variant, this 'Tog will be more than useful, providing you threshold whenever you please and combining with Demoralize to finish your opponents off. A 4th - 6th pick, but it's a better card than that...

![](https://i.ormos.uk/84/87/8487e881bf8fab9ff32d8b6f3ab73b9921d096ea0053a6f5bcfacd600f04127d.jpg)**Mystic Enforcer**  
 ...And this is a better card than Lithatog. White and green are both operating at a disadvantage right now, but if you're playing either one, this guy will usually be worth splashing the other for, because frankly, it's one of the top ten cards in the set, especially now that everyone is playing black. You all know what it does, so I don't need to go there, just draft it high; it's a game winner. A 1st pick.

**Phantatog**  
 Madness made the Phantatog better, but it still isn't good enough to make most of my blue-white decks. Frankly, you don't want to discard too many cards (Strength of Isolation is white's only viable madness card) to this guy, and your enchantments are usually in play for a reason, so unless you have a bunch of Auramancers, it's going to be a 1/2 for a big majority of the time. An 8th - 10th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fc/d3/fcd303e216c832a96e5657bf356203e657af86193465daa8f87aac71517049b4.jpg)**Psychatog**  
 Now this is the stuff. The best color combo in the format lucked out here, getting the best of the Atogs, which happens to do exactly what blue-black needs it to. Quite often, that color combo's biggest problem is beatdown, and Psychatog nips that strategy in the bud, then becomes a juggernaut later on. Especially good with graveyard fillers like Cephalid Looter, careful that you don't draft too many graveyard reliant cards like Painbringer with it. There's only so much to go around. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Sarcatog**  
 Remember what I was saying about how few artifacts there are in this set? Well, that really hinders a creature whose other ability relies on a large graveyard in a color combo that often has none. Red-black has a lot of flashback and a lot of cards that are better than this one which require cards from the graveyard. In other words, this is a mediocre card in a bad situation. It's playable in dire straits, but more often than not you should just pass it for something else. An 8th - 10th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8c/79/8c790dcd10c74051fa2f0acabe8ab5ec8f131ad0fb459c58df3042fa8a656f1a.jpg)**Shadowmage Infiltrator**  
 Well, Jonny Magic got a little worse in *Torment* thanks to all kinds of black creatures running around the place, but that doesn't mean he isn't really strong. 'Finkel' can single-handedly win you a game and do so fast, coming out on turn three and more often than not attacking on turn four. If the Infiltrator gets through more than a couple of times, you can save yourself a lot of frustration by sideboarding rather than waiting for death. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Thaumatog**  
 A strong card that just never gets played. Thaumatog isn't quite strong enough to splash, but at the same time rests in colors that seldom get played together. The ability to sacrifice lands is a strong one, especially in a color combination that has many strong threshold abilities and few strong threshold activators, but until *Judgment* comes out, there just won't be enough white and green to build a deck most of the time. That means this Atog will just keep going around the table. Remember: if you do manage to draft white-green, you're almost certainly the only one doing so at the table. You might get this 9th or 10th in that situation, so don't pick it early. A 6th - 8th pick, but it has the power of 3rd - 5th.

**Vampiric Dragon**  
 Eight is a lot of mana to pay for anything, especially a creature in Faceless Butcher's environment, but this thing sure does pack a punch. Generally, when the Dragon hits the table, your opponent's guys have no choice but to scurry, scatter and eventually die, while the Dragon is busy flying overhead for massive amounts of damage. Great in a red-black control deck, the dragon shouldn't be picked over top-notch commons like Firebolt and Demise, but it doesn't go too far behind. A 3rd - 5th pick.

### Land

![](https://i.ormos.uk/51/1f/511fd121ddfcbd56874739f83622d2a8768af49f9576342cc567a637615412cd.jpg)**Barbarian Ring**  
 Hey, two damage is two damage, and while the Ring may deal more than that to you, the versatility of being able to target a creature will usually make it worth playing if threshold is a realistic possibility. How else is your red-black deck going to kill Mystic Crusader unless... a 6th - 8th pick.

**Cabal Pit**  
 ...you have Cabal Pit! I think I'd rather have the Ring more often then not, but the two are very similarly powered. Careful picking it over some other playable though: black often has a lot of trouble getting to threshold, and you may be creating an excuse for yourself to take damage. A 6th - 8th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/54/45/54452d901ceae610146a6145387e21d31c890ab0d6e8b67a2927611f578bf754.jpg)**Centaur Garden**  
 This one's easy. If your deck seems capable of achieving threshold consistently, the Garden will be very good for you, providing mana early and a useful ability later on. If you aren't playing blue or Millikin though, it's unlikely the latter will occur. Green doesn't worry about single points of damage much, but be realistic: you don't want to play a card whose only function is to hurt you. A 6th - 8th pick.

**Cephalid Coliseum**  
 Funny thing, in blue, if you have threshold, as often as not it means you have other filter mechanisms like Think Tank and Compulsion that got you to threshold in the first place, and as a result of that I try not to play the Coliseum if I think I don't need it. If, however you have means of getting to threshold other than those filters, the Coliseum is main deck worthy. Especially good with Plagiarize. An 7th - 9th pick if you want to play it.

**Crystal Quarry**  
 DO NOT USE THIS AS AN EXCUSE TO PLAY ATOGATOG. A 15th pick.

**Deserted Temple**  
 Got three Squirrel Nests? Do I have the card for you: it's another forest. Don't play the Temple, the colorless mana is too much of a drawback. A 13th - 15th pick.

**Filter Lands**  
 Once you've drawn each of your two land types, these are going to be worse than either one, but until that moment they're invaluable. Very good for splashes, as long as you're playing both colors the land produces, it will be good enough to play. An 9th - 11th pick.

**Nomad Stadium**  
 A member of R&D told me they were trying to make it worse but couldn't figure out how. The only thing I can think of is including it in a deck instead of leaving it in the 'board. A 15th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3e/91/3e91593214e27a85a375c5ecb54bd1e92f8332f8cbc142c5b0a8c8e322acc8be.jpg)**Sac Lands**  
 The sac lands, that being the lands which come into play tapped and allow for their sacrifice to produce one colored mana of your choosing, are generally pretty good, offering your mana base stability and your graveyard a potential occupant, but they aren't always appropriate. If your two-color deck has little in the way of threshold, the negatives associated with coming into play tapped can outweigh the positives. I never play these things in my red-green decks, where I hope to start consistently, but they're usually good enough elsewhere. An 9th - 11th pick.

**Tarnished Citadel**  
 Ken Ho made this work in Block, and for that I give him kudos, but I'd like to see him do something with it in Limited. I know I can't. A 13th - 15th pick.

### Final Comments

*"The trick to playing this deck well is to know when you should keep Braids around and when let it go. This is achieved by practicing."*

The above is a quote from Alex Shvartsman's recent Regionals article here on Sideboard.com, and while he didn't intend it to be, Alex's quote is one of the most profound statements I've ever read on Limited play. Each year I write these analyses (I wrote these late *Odyssey* installments because so many people had been asking for them), and yet I see people making the same old mistakes for the same reasons: they don't put what they read into practice enough to truly understand what they're doing in order to succeed.

I think I'm a pretty good drafter and am pretty good at analysing what works and explaining it, but in the end, I can't sit down at the table for the reader. These articles are a good guideline, but in the end, only through practice and practice and more practice will you truly learn to outdraft the other people at the table. Once you do though, it'll be worth it: it's like playing Extended against Standard.

Do what you have to get games in. Download **Magic** Online or organize live drafts at home. While using actual boosters is preferable, if you need to, create packs out of your collected sets if that's what it will take to keep you going. Whatever it takes, make sure you approach the table with the intent being to learn from your mistakes and successes. That's how you learn what works in any format. One last time, I'm sorry it took me so long to get these last few installments up, but I hope they'll help you on your way to a better understanding of the *Odyssey* Limited format. Good luck, and if your packs don't contain bombs, I hope you'll outdraft the lucky ones and beat them anyway.
