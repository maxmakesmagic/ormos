Usually, when I set to writing an article, be it on a card, format, team or what have you, things occur very naturally. You take your topic, research it a bit, put your thoughts on the topic on the page, do a little organizing, put everything in its appropriate context, be it strategic, historic or political and use the spellchecker to clean up the mess.

For two days now, I've been staring at a blank page, trying to muster the words for this preview, but they haven't been forthcoming. Maybe it's because Naturalize isn't glitzy like Fact or Fiction, a powerful, potential game winner like Grinning Demon, or even quirky like Dwarven Blastminer. Nope, Naturalize, in and of itself, is actually pretty boring. Strong, sure, but primarily boring. Until that historical context comes through.

The problem with putting that historical spin on Naturalize though is that there are two very balanced ancestral branches that could be accredited with this descendant. On one hand, there's the 'green-has-enchantment-kill-and-green-has-artifact-kill-and-now-they've-been-combined' approach, on the other, there's the 'there-have-been-many-cards-that-kill-enchantments-and-artifacts-and-they're-all-white'. Neither seems to complete the admittedly considerable task of trying to figure out just where Naturalize falls. The reason for this is simple: Naturalize is more than the sum of those parts.

As you look over those fake spoilers out there and eventually look at the real ones up close, you're going to see a lot of 'roided up powerhouses, but none of those cards will be played as seriously, as often, or as long as this one. This boring, cheap and \*yawn\* effective common is going to be available to everyone from Kai Budde to Timmy, Power Gamer, and they're all going to have to sheepishly admit that this card is one to play. They'll sit with it in hand until the appropriate target makes itself known, and then that target will be nothing but a memory and a story. Card trades for card, 'nuff said. How droll.

If you don't believe me, take a look at the formats you'll find yourself playing in this day and age and think about how Naturalize would affect them. In Limited, the most comparable card, Disenchant, has always been playable in any format it has found its way into, taking out the Cursed Scrolls, Crooked Scales and Predator, Flagships that have plagued us. I remember watching Darwin Kastle draft in the Top 8 of London, reacting in surprise when I saw him pick Disenchant over Phyrexian Ghoul as a third pick. Afterwards, I went and asked him about the selection, and his logic was as obvious as my opinions usually are: "Disenchant seems to always destroy a power card. I think that makes it a power card". The days of broken Limited artifacts have been gone for two years now, and *Onslaught*, while offering more in this department than *Invasion* and *Odyssey*, isn't much different, but the utility, efficiency and sheer strength of the card mean that it'll be a solid sixth-seventh pick. Mediocre but playable. Unexciting, but solid.

What would Naturalize have done in *Odyssey* Block Constructed? It would have made decks like Confinement and Mirari's Wake virtually unplayable for one thing. It would have taken out Catalyst Stones in blue-green mirror matches, blue-black Infestations if you desired, Elephant Guides once blockers had been declared, Divine Sacraments that kept your Howling Gales from doing their job, and so on, and so on. Naturalize likely wouldn't have made any main decks in our present environment, but it would have made sideboards, and more importantly created an entirely new environment because of the effect its presence had on the other decks in the field.

In Standard, this would have been a mainstay in Wonder Dog sideboards. Ensnaring Bridge? Check. Worship? Check. Every problem card not starting with 'F' and ending with 'lametongue Kavu' blue-green saw was a problem because blue-green had no effective way of dealing with both artifacts and enchantments in one little package, meaning there wasn't enough room in sideboards for both. With Naturalize, there would be. Throw in the wrench in Squirrel-Opposition plans, the pathetic death of Hunting Grounds and the general rearranging of every sideboard in the format to accommodate the resulting changes and you have a profound effect...

... and that brings us to the new Extended. Without the dual lands, the option of Disenchant in the sideboard is only available to those players who are actually playing white. Naturalize makes it so that a lot of decks (New Oath, Stompy, Trinity Green, the Rock... shall we say anything with green in it?) that couldn't run Disenchant can now run 'Disenchant'. 'Can', by the way, means 'will', and by that same notion, you 'can' play Naturalize in Type 1 too.

So there you have it. Sad to say, every time you build a deck, draft a card or even bother to think about Constructed environments of all shapes and sizes, there's a new power card to contend with, and it isn't a dragon or a card drawer or cool new counter. It's a simple reactive card, hardly creative by R&D's standards ("we could make a blue Lightning Bolt... oh wait! I've got it!) aside from the fabulous color scheme (mere window dressing, right?), but plain and simple, it's the best artifact/enchantment solution green has ever had, by a long, long way. Of course, if mana isn't a consideration, you could always Desert Twister. If you want mass enchantment destruction, you could play with Tranquil Grove. You can cast your Orangutans for utility and your Crumbles and Emerald Charms for a little more mana efficiency, but in the end, if you want to be as competitive as possible, Naturalize is the way of the future. Go boring or go home.
