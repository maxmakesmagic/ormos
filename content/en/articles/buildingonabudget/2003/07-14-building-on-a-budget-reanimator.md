### What Falls Down, Must Come Up

* * *

***Magic** Online* is a funny place...I think it might be an economists dream (or nightmare) to study. *(There's at least a PhD in it for someone. -- Dan)* I tend to be pretty good at keeping track of card prices, but sometimes ***Magic** Online* can take twists with prices I would never expect -- hence this article. I wanted to do a Reanimator article a while back, but the card prices were staying too high. Now that they have come down a bit, this deck (which is chocked full of goodies) is finally possible for under 30 tickets, which I hold to be my maximum for any deck I write about in this column.

<table><tbody><tr><td><p>Reanimator has been a staple in the <b>Magic </b>metagame from the beginning -- since the days of Alpha, <a>Animate Dead</a> was lurking around in somebody's deck. When <a>Dance of the Dead</a> was printed in the <i>Ice Age </i>set, Reanimator started picking up steam. Eventually (through a few incarnations) it turned into the deck we know today. In general, the Reanimation spells available today are much worse than before (compare <a>Zombify</a> or <a>Stitch Together</a> to Animate Dead). However, the creatures coming back from the graveyard are far, far better (compare any creature in a previous Reanimator deck to <a>Arcanis</a>, <a>Phantom Nishoba</a>, or <a>Visara</a>).</p>&#10;<p>The thing I always liked about Reanimator was how simple it was, yet it had the ability to crush opponents with the sleek effectiveness of just putting a creature out there and saying: &quot;Go ahead. While the deck concept is rather simple, you need to know certain things while playing it -- mainly which creature to get in any situation. In this regard it's much like the old Rec-Sur deck. When you cast Buried Alive, you have to know what you're playing against and what's best to dig up. Anger is always right, but the other two depend on the situation. Here are some guidelines: 

*   Arcanis is what you get if you aren't in a dire situation. Drawing cards is like candy in this deck.  
  

*   Phantom Nishoba is great if you're low on life or are playing against a super-aggressive deck like Red-Green or Goblins.  
  

*   Visara is even better in the above situation if you have plenty of life.  
  

*   Symbiotic Wurm is great if you think your opponent has Edict or Wrath.  
  

*   Petradon is wonderful against Control and land-light opponents.

In general, the game plays out by you casting an early Sickening Dreams or Last Rites and discarding some manner of fatness. When turn four rolls around, you bring said fatness back into play to smash said opponent. Got it? Mix in Buried Alive for flavor.

The trick to winning with this deck is to not overcommit while not taking it too slow. There's a fine line between when you hold back and when you start swinging with Visara and start ignoring your opponent's creatures. Seeing that line is the hard part. The ultra-fatties aren't born to block, so fight with them!

### Tips On Playing the Deck

<table><tbody><tr><td><ul><li> If you have <a>Anger</a> in the graveyard, you may be better served by not casting <a>Doomed Necromancer</a> on turn three. Instead, wait until turn four when you can cast and activate it (with haste). This is especially important if the Necromancer is the only reanimation spell in your hand. </li><br><br><li> Stay at a high life total. If you're at 10, that's too low. Get the <a>Nishoba</a> as a rule of thumb in that situation. </li><br><br><li> Don't be afraid to tap <a>Arcanis</a> during your turn. Sometimes you need him to block, but three more cards will probably say otherwise. </li><br><br><li> Don't worry about discarding excess lands to <a>Dreams</a> or <a>Rites</a> -- you only need four to function. </li><br><br><li> Don't forget that you <b>need</b> a mountain for <a>Anger</a> to work. This is the only reason the pricey <a>Bloodstained Mire</a>s are in the deck. </li>&#10;</ul>&#10;</td>&#10;<td><table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td><td></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td><td><a><img alt="Phantom Nishoba" src="https://i.ormos.uk/78/b1/78b127d0629a5c3ad87ac0e5d3224b468e5dbefb368ad20897822dc63ddc69d8.jpg"></a></td><td></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

### Sideboarding

**Silent Specter**  
Silent Specter with haste is a good way to start the day against control decks.

**Guiltfeeder**  
Guiltfeeder is another anti-control card -- you can actually cast this one, too! Great versus Tog and Wake.

**Ancestor's Chosen**  
Ancestor's Chosen is a life-gaining machine that's good to board in versus the same decks Phantom Nishoba is good against. One of the two cards will win you the game.

**Undead Gladiator**  
Undead Gladiator is another anti-control card, but it's also nice because it is one of the few cards that has great synergy with Buried Alive without any help.

**Engineered Plague**  
 Yah, you know what to do with this one already.

**Withered Wretch**  
Withered Wretch is just plain good against much of the field (not to mention against this deck). Use it to your advantage!

### Adding More Money To The Deck

<table><tbody><tr><td><p>Alternative versions of the deck exist that have more of a green mana base; using eight fetchlands and <a>Genesis</a>, creating a toolbox effect that is less dependent on the reanimation spells. This allows you to reuse creatures with <a>Buried Alive</a> and Genesis, making the deck far better versus Control. <a>Entomb</a> is another card worth considering in the deck, especially a green version because it lets you dump a <a>Riftstone Portal</a> into the graveyard.</p>&#10;<p>If you decide to play the green version, never underestimate the power of <a>Birds of Paradise</a>.
