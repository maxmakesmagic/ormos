### Part 1: Building a Better Bomber

* * *

In this first article, I'll address some basic strategy for building better decks and give an example of improvements based on the Bomber theme deck. In the coming weeks, I'll concentrate on giving more decklists and analyze individual cards more.

Let's get going with some rules of **Magic** deckbuilding and a little theory.

### Magic Numbers: 4, 24, 60

*   You cannot have more than 4 copies of a card in the deck (with the exception of basic lands). Don't worry about this rule too much; ***Magic** Online* won't let you put more than 4 copies of a card in your Constructed deck.
*   Each deck must have a minimum of 60 cards. You can have more (there's no upper limit), but it's good to keep as close to 60 as possible so your best cards are not diluted into a sea of less good ones, and so your deck's focus can be exploited to its fullest (I'll explain below what I mean by focus). *Seventh Edition* theme decks are only 40 cards, so your first task will be to make the minimum size.
*   A 60-card deck has, on average, 24 lands in it. It gives reasonable probabilities of drawing land early, when you need it the most: 86% of having at least two lands in your opening hand, 59% of having at least three.

### Constructed Formats

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b1/fd/b1fd3d68d549472caf17c704be31fc72d9b52ef92a44d24510399a6abcd52157.jpg)***Magic** Online* currently supports sanctioned Standard, *InvasionTM* Block Constructed, and *OdysseyTM* Block Constructed tournaments. Standard, the most popular Constructed format tournament, currently comprises all the sets available on ***Magic** Online.* When building a deck for a Block Constructed tournament, you're limited to cards from the available sets in the block. For example, in *Odyssey* Block Constructed, you're limited to *Odyssey,**TormentTM,* and *JudgmentTM* cards. To verify that your deck is valid in each format, click **Stats** in the **Deck Editor**, then click the **Format** tab.

### Deck Focus

Every deck needs a focus. Focus means how you're planning to win with the deck. For example, the Bomber deck's focus is "Stall the ground with walls and fly over for the win, or attack from a distance with Prodigal Sorcerer. In any case, try to outdraw your opponent."

### Matchup-Specific Strategies

If you try to make your deck as versatile as possible, it probably won't play the same against the multitude of possible decks in the environment. To reflect the diversity, your deck's focus should be adaptable so that you can adjust it after you've found out what's in your opponent's deck.

For example, in a Bomber versus Bomber matchup, your opponent will probably have as many flyers as you do. The matchup comes down to card drawing and alternative damage solutions. Once you realize that, you can adapt your strategy and make better choices in order to win the game.

### Sideboard

**Magic** tournaments allow the use of 15 additional cards that you keep aside from your deck. These cards can be used in games two and three of a match. After the first and second games of a match, you can swap cards in your deck on a one-for-one basis with cards in your sideboard. This helps you tune your deck in order to defeat your current opponent. The next time you begin a match, your deck will be returned to its original configuration.

I'll provide a sideboard with each decklist.

Now that you know some of the basics, it's time for a few decklists. For each of them, I will provide a focus and some matchup strategies. For this week's issue, I modified the Bomber deck (*Seventh Edition* blue theme deck).

**Focus:** Use counter-magic to protect a Thieving Magpie, play Unsummons to get past flying defenses, and outdraw your opponent. If that plan doesn't work, sit back behind your own defense and try to fly over with Air Elemental or Mahamoti Djinn.

**Comments:**  
Hibernation helps survive assaults from green decks; Baleful Stare makes sure you outdraw red decks. Memory Lapse is very useful if your opponent is also using counter-magic as it helps you win the counter battles that will likely occur. Replace Wall of Air with Treasure Trove if your opponent does not have a very aggressive deck.

**Matchup-Specific Strategies**  
 Against a defensive deck, drawing more cards is your goal. Key cards: Merfolk Looter and Thieving Magpie. Protect them at all costs and discard your walls and Remove Souls.  
 Against a fast, creature-based deck, surviving the early game is the most important thing. Key cards: Force Spike, Remove Soul, and Wall of Air. Once you've stabilized the situation, a big flyer will do the job.

Further Improvements If you are getting familiar with expert-level sets, don't hesitate to replace some of the less good *Seventh Edition* cards with better *Odyssey* or *Invasion* block cards. 

*   Unsummon can be replaced with Repulse*(Invasion)*.
*   Inspiration can be replaced with Fact or Fiction*(Invasion),*Deep Analysis*(Torment)* or Concentrate*(Odyssey)*.
*   Remove Soul can be replaced with Exclude*(Invasion)* or Circular Logic*(Torment)*.

Keep in mind that cards from *Invasion* block sets won't be legal in Standard tournaments once *OnslaughtTM* cards [rotate in](https://web.archive.org/web/20020918000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=/default.asp?x=mtgcom/daily/rb36&origin=magic_magiconline_articlemb091802). However, *Invasion* block cards will be playable in the new Extended format.
