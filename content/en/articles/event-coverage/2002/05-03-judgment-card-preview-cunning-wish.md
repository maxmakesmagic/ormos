![](https://i.ormos.uk/46/a2/46a2f3b43bcd7acfcb036b04e918059cf2d15f26541ea7c13a60fc74af2cb529.jpg)For those of you who don't remember Ring of Ma'ruf, I can only cringe at the gray hairs that must be creeping onto my head; for the rest, I ask you to remember how enchanted people were with the idea of grabbing *any card* from outside the game. No matter that the Ring cost ten mana, between casting cost and activation, and made you skip your draw step (not to mention that it had to be used during your draw step), it was a mechanic in and of itself. The possibilities were endless, people even brought in *Illuminati* cards. However, as we all know, the Ring wasn't any good. It would take a fine card, indeed (perhaps something so good that it was only allowed in a game with different rules), to make the Ring's investment worthwhile. Instead, I think people were envisioning, at some level, a card like Cunning Wish. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b2/a5/b2a57a434eedc1f8327146a2ee5cafe25dc912e1dcfb59106a23e50620914394.jpg)Make no mistake, this card is the real deal. It used to be that we had to include "silver bullet" hate cards in our main decks if we wanted to tutor for them. Consider the way Jon Finkel used to spend 2 life, a draw step and the possibility of drawing a dead card, to get his Perish in game 1. Now, for those of you who want to get a Slay before sideboarding, you can forgo the agony of having a potential dead card lingering in your deck, as well as the obvious discomfort that arrives after forfeiting a draw step and 2 life points. The cost? A few more mana, and some flexibility, but there are plenty of instants to get. 

Of course, when a card doesn't do anything on its own, requiring you to pay mana just to trade it in for a card that you can use, it had better be getting you a pretty busty card. Which instants meet this criterion?

Slay, as I mentioned, is a pretty fine card to find, allowing you to spend six mana, in increments and at instant speed, to bury a green creature.

Aura Blast, another card that is rarely worth having in your main deck, is going to be nice for all of the expensive and powerful enchantments in Standard. There are many incarnations of green-blue-white decks that just can't afford to have main deck enchantment kill, but will have serious problems with cards like Phyrexian Arena, Yawgmoth's Agenda and Opposition. Now they effectively have four cards that can find a very efficient answer, plugging a whole without risking drawing dead cards in matchups where the opponent has no enchantments.

Are you as sick as I am of seeing Life Burst in Standard decks? A Samite Minstration in the sideboard looks like it would be a nice card to get when facing the heat of those mindless red-green decks (My pride is fragile enough without the prospect of losing to a deck called "Frog in a Blender").

In a long game where you have more than enough mana, but not enough tools to stop your opponent? Ever played with a Fervent Denial?

Perhaps you are being attacked for the finishing blow, maybe a Vengeful Dreams is what you need.

Speaking of blows, what about that Dismantling Blow that didn't quite make into your main deck?

You could, of course, also tutor for good cards, meaning that you might leave a Fact or Fiction or a Repulse in your sideboard - a sad thought, I know.

While Cunning Wish is obviously a bit clunky at ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif), especially since you probably want to have Fact or Fictions in your deck as well, its flexibility makes it a real consideration for any blue control deck. This mechanic means all sorts of hate cards will be flying at you game 1. As usual, the depth of options and opportunities to surprise your opponent is always a good thing, making Cunning Wish both powerful and skill intensive. I, for one, will do my best to enjoy it.
