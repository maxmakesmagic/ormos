In my last article series for the Sideboard, I attempted to bring you a chronicle of my efforts to qualify for Pro Tour-Houston. Although I did not qualify, my journey brought me to Houston anyway (let's not even talk about Chicago), where I provided coverage for the Sideboard during the Pro Tour weekend (I will be doing the same in Chicago). I had the opportunity to see the best players in the world as they attempted to Break Open the brand new Extended format. I was excited about trying to qualify for Venice and reviving the series for the current round of Constructed qualifiers. 

I began preparing for the season by talking with my regular deck collaborators and occasional teammates, Mike Flores and Jon Becker. As usual, Mike was confident that the format was a solved puzzle for him and there was not much room for lively discussion about Extended unless you consider “BDM! Let me tell why that is awful…” to be lively discussion. As usual, Jon and I discussed rogue strategies and how Mike Flores was probably wrong. 

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>All of the relevant deck lists from Houston were assembled and cobbled together as best we could to form a deck library.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I wasn't getting anywhere and I needed to do some actual playtesting. Jon lives in Philly and Mike does not leave the house much since getting married. I don't really enjoy playing online that much, so that was another snag. Prior to PT-Houston, Seth Burn and I had discussed the need to get some organized playtesting off the ground among Neutral Ground regulars. Seth took the initiative and organized a mailing list and actually set fixed days of the week for us to game. The group included its fair share of writers seeking grist for their mills: myself, Seth, Zev Gurwitz, Sean McKeown, and Adam Rubens. We also incorporated a number of Neutral Ground regulars looking to take their game up a notch: Mike Short, Aaron Moshiashwili, Rich Fein and Don Lim. Finally, we looked to some of the better younger players in the area such as NY State Champion Jeremy Cash and Matt Boccio. I'm sure I've forgotten a handful of players and I apologize if I did.

All of the relevant deck lists from Houston were assembled and cobbled together as best we could to form a deck library. We kept careful track of our playtesting results. The only deck I had any real success with was a variant of Reanimator. I decimated Adam Rubens time and time again with the deck while he wielded Justin Gary's Oath deck. I seriously considered Reanimator as my week one choice. After GP-Reims (it was pointed out by Jon Becker that this is an anagram for GP-Miser) we went into overdrive, making sure we could either beat red or understood how to play with it in all of the relevant match-ups.

The first PTQs in our area were looming the same weekend that the first winter storm of the season was Threatening to hit the east coast. Plans to travel to several hours Boston or Roanoke were revised to stay local and play in a $150 Extended tournament at Neutral Ground. I had been doing all of my testing with playing cards and deck maps and did not have anything assembled with the tournament only a few days away. I set myself to the task of organizing my collection and identifying the holes in each of the decks I was considering that I would need to fill via trade or purchase.

I used to own Neutral Ground and always had access to the store's inventory when building decks. After I was done with the deck, the cards would return to inventory and I would move on to another design. One of the startling realities of no longer owning a store is preparing for a Constructed season. I had no problem during Block because I play in so many Limited events and pick-up drafts, but Extended would prove to be a real challenge. I was finding that I was coming up two or three copies shy of almost every card I needed for most of the decks I wanted to play. 

I began to joke about playing my OBC u-g madness from last season. 

Then I started joking about playing Waterfront Bouncer in it.

Then I found out that I owned four Waterfront Bouncers.
