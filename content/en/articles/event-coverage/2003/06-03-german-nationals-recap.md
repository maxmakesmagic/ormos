![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPAMS01/975.jpg)German Nationals has come and gone and Dirk Baberowski was the last man standing. He met Thomas Neumaier of Bavaria in the finals. He was 8-0 with his Reanimator up to this point. They split the first four games and it came down to the final one. Thomas had to mulligan down to five going first. But his deck was kind. His hand consisted was Barren Moor, swamp, mountain, Burning Wish and Doomed Necromancer. Best five-card hand possible? Probably not, but close at least. Dirk, on the other hand, kept a powerful hand, but it had only one forest. Thomas did draw a fourth land on his fourth turn, but it was a Barren Moor and instead of abusing Visara right away, all he could do was to cast a Necromancer and pass the turn. Dirk didn't have the needed second land right away, as well, but he was doing fine anyway. Visara finally came into play on the fifth turn and Thomas took one of Dirk's creatures down right away. Neumaier summoned a second Doomed Necromancer but missed the land to activate it. Being the pro that he is, Dirk topdecked on his turn and lived the casual players dream - he slammed Coat of Arms on the table, tapping a Llanowar Elves and two Seekers of Skybreak and in came a Wirewood Herald and three Birchlore Rangers. Thomas blocked the Herald but the three Birchlore Rangers on steroids dealt 21 damage. Game and championship to everyone's favorite multiplayer deck! Not quite what I expected going into the tournament, but you have to admit it is both stylish and fun.

I don't think I need to introduce Dirk any more. He won the last two Team Pro Tours and PT-Chicago in 1998. This is his first Nationals Top 8, but after the Swiss was finished, his win wasn't exactly unexpected, as everyone agreed that he was the best player and had a very favorable matchup in the quarterfinals with Maxim Barkmann's blue-green-white madness deck. Flying Roar of the Wurm tokens don't do very much against Wellwisher with Wirewood Lodge after all. Maxim had a shot with his maindeck Worships, but they never showed up and the Elves marched to victory in three fast games.

The runner-up Thomas Neumaier, on the other hand, is rather unknown. He played at PT-Tokyo but lost the last round of Day 1 when he was playing for Day 2. His brother Michael finished in the Top 8 of the Nationals two years ago and both Christoph Lippert and David Brucker said that Thomas is actually the better player of the brothers. He proved it this year, although it was a lot closer then most people thought it would be after he 6-0'ed the first day with a Reanimator deck. He went 0-3 in his first draft but came back to win his second pod. He edged out Daniel Graefensteiner, yours truly and Janosch Kuehn on tiebreakers, claimed the 8th seat and never looked back, beating first seat and third time Top 8'er Jan Brinkmann's monoblack control in five games even though Jan had two Withered Wretches and three Ensnaring Bridges in the sideboard. He went on to win the mirror match against Peer Kroeger in the Semis and met Dirk in the Finals, where his run in Standard finally found a dead end.

The third National Team member is a long time player from Dortmund: Falk Bernhardt. Falk played a Psychatog version that was running a lot of bounce with Aether Burst and Chain of Vapor main deck. He played another Psychatog deck to a Top 8 finish last year, but lost in the quarterfinals against the eventual winner in a mirror match. Things were different this time. Falk went 5-1 in Standard and a 4-1-1 record in draft secured the Top 8 finish. He was down two games to zero in the quarterfinals against Holger Althues with black control but came back to take the match 3-2. Dirk Baberowski gave him a sound loss in the Semis when Bernhardt won only the game where he drew both of his Engineered Plagues and his single Hibernation, but the green horde walked over him in the other three games. The match in the loser-bracket for the third slot in the National Team was not too difficult though. His opponent was Peer Kroeger, who happened to run a Reanimator deck and he didn't really like his chances against seven bounce spells in the main deck along with Duresses and a Haunting Echoes in Falk's sideboard. Bernhardt took that one 3-0, to no one's surprise.

Peer was very happy with his performance anyway. He won German Nationals way back in 1996 and reached the Top 8 of two Pro Tours - Mainz and Dallas. He hasn't played as much recently but he is living in Berlin right now. And who would want to miss a World Championship in his hometown? Certainly not Peer. He traveled to Nationals to qualify and he did just that. His Reanimator deck took him to a 5-1 finish after a loss in the last round to Jan Brinkmann's Wretches. He won the first draft pod and the one win he needed from the second draft was a formality. Bernd Brendemuehl was waiting for him in the Quarterfinals. Bernd came to Nationals unqualified. He went 6-0 in a Standard grinder with Sligh and went 5-1 on Day 1 with the same deck, losing to Thomas Neumaier's Reanimator. Peer was playing a very similar deck and it seemed to be Bernd's Nemesis for the weekend as Peer defeated him 3-0 to advance to the Semis where he lost the mirror against Neumaier.

That means that the National Team for Worlds in Berlin is Dirk Baberowski, Thomas Neumaier and Falk Bernhardt with Peer Kroeger being the alternate. Falk mentioned that he might not be able to attend as he might have exams during Worlds but I have known Bernhardt for several years and he's been playing tournament Magic all this time. I somehow can't believe that he is going to skip the first World Championships he qualified for, especially as it is in Germany. Dirk also happens to be one of the best team players in the world right now and as both Thomas and Falk are pretty good on their own, I believe Germany has a decent shot to claim the team title for the second year in a row. At least I will be there and cheer them on.
