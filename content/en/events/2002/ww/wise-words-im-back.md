### 

*The following was written Wednesday, August 14th. Blackout and laptop complications delayed its submission until the following Monday.* 

*Sorry for the delay, Gary*

More times than I can count during the week of Worlds, people asked what’s happened to Wise Words. For three weeks now, I’ve been lax in my *Sideboard* duties, owing to a number of factors from lethargy to the departure of my long time boss and friend Jeff Donais to having taken a step back from **Magic** over the last couple of months. The two Pro Tour gap between Masters and end of year pay out-inclusive PTs has given my sub-conscious the opportunity to take a break from what has otherwise been an eight-year dedication to **Magic**, and you know what? I think the fact people have become accustomed to the column, which was once so sporadic, making its scheduled appearance every week but one for the last five months, means that I’ve reformed my erratic ways. I’m comfortable with the break.

That said, one result of my departure was a well-earned abysmal showing at Worlds, at least in the main tournament, where I played two decks I hadn’t practiced with as if I hadn’t practised with them. By round fourteen, I was out of the money, embarrassed by my inadequacies and very happy to depart from the standings. My Worlds was done, the drop box checked and frankly, I was happy to be away from the rigors. 

Now on my way to Amsterdam with Josh Rider before flying home tomorrow, it’s time for my life to undergo reform. It’s time to start up Wise Words again, start taking Magic seriously for the first time in months, time to finally start work on the book I promised so long ago, get the *Scourge* Limited review posted and stop using the many disruptions my life has endured over the last ten months as an excuse to remain disrupted. For those of you who like the column, I apologise for the empty forays to Sideboard.com, for those of you who rejoiced in my absence, I hope you enjoyed my time away, but I’m back now and I’m not going away for the foreseeable future. A lot of information has piled on my cerebellum over the weeks, and its time to purge through the best outlet I have at my disposal. Here’s hoping the result will make for a good read.

So many things happened at Worlds that to expand on most of them would lead to the shut down of the world wide web due to lack of space, so point form seems appropriate. Have a look:

*   Daniel Zink, a former German Champion and Euros Top 8 finisher, is a very solid player who, unlike many of the German pros, doesn’t deify Phoenix Foundation, and to that end, should he decide to dedicate himself more fully now, the opportunity for him to work with Germany’s more established stars in a capacity other than fetching coffee and taking dictation may be available to him. Daniel’s now looking for teammates for Boston. Anyone need a 32 pointer?
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/450.jpg)Zink’s victory, if the Top 8 roster is any indication, may in part be due to home field advantage. He was joined by Wolfgang Eder and Peer Kroeger, who was very good at **Magic** way back… during the Crusades. Kroeger’s Top 8 was his third, the others being Dallas and Mainz\*. Peer qualified for Worlds by playing in German Regionals, then coming fourth at Nationals, both of which he did only because the opportunity to play a home field World Championships was too good to pass on.
*   Zink and Kroeger were both part of a German colony that took place at Dirk Baberowski’s home in Salskitter, including every German pro but Dirk himself, who was busy playing football and hanging out at work. At one point during the two-week ordeal, Marco Blume left the house with a good feeling about the team’s Wake deck, which Zink eventually played to victory, only to return to find the decklist mangled in ways he knew to be wrong. Marco asked one unnamed German what had happened to the list. The reply? “It’s ok. I’ve spoken to Kai. It is correct.” Marco, who bows to no man, left the corpse in his trunk and restored the list to respectability. Marco, Dirk and Patrick Mello are all seriously considering retirement after Boston.
*   While Zink is a good kid, the Top 8 finisher I’d hoped would win the whole thing was Jin Okamoto. Generally regarded as Japan’s best player (a title both Itaru Ishida and Tsuyoshi Fujita could both arguably claim) Jin was long overdue for a Top 8, and a Japanese World Champion would have been great for the game. Be that as it may, he became the first Japanese player to Top 8 at World Championships, finally getting the big check every player needs to maintain the Pro Tour lifestyle. Sadly, Jin’s pro points still fall short of Masashi Ooiso’s, meaning there likely won’t be an Okamoto Djinn Invitational card this year.
*   The coolest deck of the tournament, with apologies to Bill Jensen’s 1.X Battle of Wits deck, was undoubtedly the Japanese goblin deck, featuring the “Most powerful goblin ever printed (Itaru Ishida, 2003)”, Goblin Recruiter and its many little friends. Randy Buehler credits Mons Johnson with having created the deck, but until the addition of Goblin Warchief, it was just never viable in tournament play.
*   In round one, I was sitting next to Jeroen Remie when his opponent, playing goblins, cast Sulphuric Vortex against his Wake deck. Jeroen let it resolve, untapped, put six mana in his pool and played Transcendence for the lock. With that kind of start, how could he not Top 8?
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/555.jpg)Needing two draws to make Top 8, Carlos Romao opted to draw with Remie despite a good match up with Mind’s Desire against Jeroen’s Rock deck. In the final round though, he was paired against Tuomo Niemenen, who despite having locked away a Top 8 spot opted to play as a win would ensure a Quarterfinal match up with Wake against Gabe Walls’s Slide and the first seed, which would pay him more should he lose the Quarters or Semis. A day later, Carlos needed Brazil to win its final match in order to ensure himself a place at this year’s Invitational, and they were paired against – of course — Finland, who had already iced a Top 2 berth and included Niemenen. This time, Tuomo forced the big man to play because of friends on other teams who could make more money with Brazil’s demise. Of course, the Finns defeated Brazil, leaving the former champ’s Invitational future in doubt. I can’t wait until Romao and Tuomo get paired again. Finland eventually lost the final, which of course left Tomi Walamies in second place.
*   Meanwhile, Walls was busy hate drafting Justin Gary in the final team draft of the Swiss with the U.S. already ensured of a Top 2 slot. The result? Justin had the team’s best deck, a green-white cleric mess, and Malaysia managed the win despite Justin’s individual triumph. Gabe played five colors and fifty-seven cards, while Gary eventually managed to get the “I was the champ the first time the U.S. failed to win Worlds” monkey off his back with his victory in the team competition. It looks like Walls will remain with YMG, while teaming with former CMU-TOGIT member Jordan Berkowitz and fantasy favorite Samwise Gamgee for Boston.
*   Of course, it would be irresponsible for me to not point out YMG destroying CMU-TOGIT in the main event, with two players in Top 8 to our none. However, when it came to the PTQs, the competition wasn’t close, with all three PTQs this weekend either being taken or split by CMU-TOGIT members. The lack of enthusiasm shown by the Evil Empire (as YMG shall now be known), a.k.a. America’s team (The wearing the team shirts on National teams day… shameless) in those tournaments was apparent, with Craig Krempals and Anand Khare taking the two individual PTQs while Mike Turian, Eugene Harvey and I split the final of the team PTQ. Once again, YMG just didn’t come to play.
*   Josh Wagener, who worked with YMG for the event but is unlikely to stay on, came into the week with a certain stigma about him due to his present legal problems, but it seemed to be universally agreed upon that he’s a pretty good kid. Don’t believe the hype.
*   Wagener, while not finding a permanent team, did find partners for Boston in what may be the most random pairing of all time. He’ll be working with the always-hyper-and-loud-enough-to-make-even-me-look-quiet Mark Zajdner of Toronto and Peter Myrvig, the quiet Danish giant who made Top 8 at Houston this year. It is unknown how the team will fare after round four, when most experts are predicting Zajdner will have been killed by his teammates.
*   Perhaps the coolest dining experience in the world is to be had in Berlin at a restaurant called Unsicht-Bar Berlin. For just sixty Euros, you can be treated to fine dining in utter blackness. The room has absolutely no light and you can’t see your hand in front of your face. If you’re going to go, I recommend taking Antonio DeRosa with you, as comments like “Uta (our waitress), I like it when you touch my hand” and dirty, knowing laughter at the question “Can I do anything else for you?” emitting from the dark makes one quickly realize he’s a dirty old man at the age of 22.
*   In Boston news, Mike Turian and I have elected to drop Eugene “Eu-Genius” Harvey fro our team in favor of Russian player Eugenius Idzikovsky, who finished ahead of the former U.S. Champ in the standings despite Harvey’s 5-1 run on Day 3.
*   One thing that was evident at Worlds was the rigors time has had of late on the Pro Tour. No Skaff Elias. No Jeff Donais. No Dave Price or Mike Long. It’s a whole new world.
*   Ben Stark, Peter Szigeti and Brian Hegstad were to team at Boston assuming one of them went to Worlds to collect the two pro points they needed to reach fifty. None of the three attended, and they’re now presumably hunting for Boston alternatives.
*   Spain’s Carlos Barrado was disqualified from the tournament for stacking his deck, a fact about which I was especially pleased as I’d played and lost to him a couple of rounds earlier.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds03/a938.jpg)Alex Shvartsman was said to be searching valiantly for a sense of humor throughout the weekend but failed to find one. I approached a number of German undertakers on the subject and each one had the same reply: “That guy just doesn’t get it”. Alex, whose integrity in commenting on events he has nothing to do with and somehow managing to get himself in the story is beyond reproach, recently accused me of a lack of any such thing with my obviously dead serious reporting on goings on in Detroit. Hey Alex, do us all a favor: get a clue. The rivalry is now officially on. Thanks for the fuel.
*   Dave Humpherys has now made four Top 8s and finished fourth in each of them.
*   Kai won the Player of the Year race. In other news, the sun rose, the Berlin Wall came down eleven years ago and I have a big mouth.
*   Matt Place made his first appearance on the Tour in more than three years, even starting 3-0 before losing to Josh Rider. Place roomed with Brian Weissman, Mark Chalice and Bertrand Lestree. George Baxter was rumoured to be making the trip, but instead travelled to Brazil to spend time with John Lennon, Elvis Presley and Tupac Shakur.
*   Jeff Cunningham, who recently carried the Week in Review banner for the *Sideboard*, has retired from his post, signing a four-book deal with Bloomsbury Publishing. The British publishing giant issued a statement explaining the signing:

    > “We figured Jeff was writing fantasy anyways and it was time to start paying him to do so.”

    Alex Shvartsman will be resuming his old post for the interim, and assures his readers there will be nothing funny whatsoever to be found in those pages.

That’s about it for now. Have a good week.

\* To show you how long ago those occurred, one was my first PT, the other my first money finish.
