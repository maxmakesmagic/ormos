This document will define a Match History Appeal and the aspects of a player's match history that may be appealed. Please read this document in its entirety if you are considering filing an appeal.

This policy becomes effective April 16, 2001. Any portion of this policy may be amended, changed, or corrected by the DCITM players' organization without prior notice. It is important that you have the most current version of this document, located at [http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dci/appeals](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/12-29-dci-match-history-appeals), prior to filing an appeal.

### Section A: Definitions

A player's match history includes the dates and locations of all sanctioned events a player has participated in, the name of each opponent played, the results of each match, and the number of points won or lost from each match. A player has a separate match history for each game and ratings format he or she has participated in (for example, **Magic: The Gathering**® Limited or **Pokemon**®; Constructed).

A Match History Appeal is a formal request to correct a possible error in a player's match history.

### Section B: Match History Appeal Process

The following are guidelines for filing a Match History Appeal.

**Review of a Player's Match History** 

A player should review his or her match history to check for any possible errors. To review your match history, please go to [http://webapp.wizards.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20090603130745/http://webapp.wizards.com/)

Note: Personal ratings may not be visible while ratings are being run or when other portions of the DCI website are being updated. If you have recurring difficulties viewing your match history on the DCI website, see *[Section D](#sectiond): Resources and Contact Information for Tournament-Related Issues*.

**Review of the Match History Appeals Policy by the Player** 

A player is expected to be familiar with this policy to avoid filing an appeal that does not have a valid basis. Valid bases for appeals can be found in *[Section C](#sectionc): Criteria for Filing a Match History Appeal*.

**Submission of Match History Appeals** 

Until May 1, 2001, players may submit their appeals in writing via fax, mail, or email. After this date, players may submit appeals via fax, mail, or Web form - **emails will not be accepted**. There is no fee for filing an appeal. The fax number and mailing address for submitting appeals are listed on the Match History Appeal form. The DCI appeals homepage is located at [http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dci/appeals](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/12-29-dci-match-history-appeals); links to the online appeals form and the PDF version of the Match History Appeal form are located on this page.

A player may appeal more than one aspect of an event. For example, if in one tournament there appears to be an error in the type of event reported and in the K-value of the event, both suspected errors may be included in one appeal. 

If there is a perceived error in two or more events, a player must file an appeal for each of the affected events.

**Filing Appeals Related to Grand Prix Byes or Pro TourTM Invitations** 

To file an appeal that is related to a bye to a Grand Prix or an invitation to a Pro Tour or higher-level event, use the following steps:

1.  Review the Appeals section of the current [DCI Invitation Policy](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/welcome-to-the-dci-document-center).
2.  If an appeal for a bye or invitation can be resolved through the Match History Appeals process, then follow that procedure, making sure that faxed or mailed appeals clearly indicate that they are bye- or invitation-related. The Web form for Match History Appeals has both bye and invitation check boxes; these types of appeals will be reviewed on a priority basis.
3.  If a bye- or invitation-related appeal cannot be resolved through the Match History Appeals process, the appellant should follow the directions in the Appeals section of the current DCI Tournament Policy. All relevant information includes, but is not limited to, the player's complete name and address, DCI number, and any other information necessary to provide a quick response.

**Initial Review** 

After receiving an appeal, DCI staff will conduct an initial review to confirm that there is a valid basis for the appeal. We will send a reply that indicates the appeal has been initially:

*   **Accepted:** The appeal is found to have a valid basis and sufficient information has been provided. The appeal will continue to the fact-finding stage.
*   **Returned:** The appeal may have a valid basis but is being returned due to insufficient information. The appellant may refile the appeal if the missing information is provided.
*   **Rejected**: A valid basis for the appeal is not found, and the appeal may not be refiled. The reason(s) for the rejection will be provided to the appellant.

**Fact Finding** 

The DCI Appeals Board will research the available facts connected to the appeal. This process includes, but is not limited to, comparing the information supplied by the appellant with the tournament reports the DCI receives from the tournament organizer. The tournament report as submitted by the tournament organizer is the official record of the event. When a discrepancy exists between the tournament report and what the appellant lists on his or her appeal, the DCI may contact the tournament organizer to review the discrepancy. The DCI Appeals Board will always assume the organizer is correct, and will only change tournament results if the organizer supplies the DCI with information contrary to the original report.

The appellant may be contacted during this process if it is discovered that more information is needed. An appeal with a significant amount of factual errors may be denied without further review.

**Approval/Denial/Referral** 

Once sufficient information has been gathered and reviewed, the DCI Appeals Board will approve, deny, or refer the appeal. This decision and the reason(s) behind the decision will be sent to the appellant by email or mail. 

Any of the following may occur if the appeal is approved:

*   **Corrections:** Corrections to the match history will occur within one week of approval.
*   **Incremental corrections:** If multiple aspects of an event are being appealed, individual aspects to the match history may be corrected prior to the final disposition of the entire appeal. The appellant will be notified of the dispositions of each aspect at the conclusion of the entire appeal.
*   **Recalculation:** Corrections to a player's rating and ranking will occur during the next ratings run subsequent to the match history correction. In the case of a bye- or invitation-related appeal, a rating recalculation that takes into account the corrections made to the match history may be made to determine if a bye or invitation is to be awarded.
*   **Invalidation of event:** Occasionally, it is found that appealed events have uncorrectable errors that necessitate event invalidation. This removes the event completely from the all of the participants' match histories.

An appeal may be referred for either or both of the following reasons:

*   If it is found that an appellant has knowingly provided incorrect information for the purpose of unfairly changing his or her match history or obtaining byes or invitations, the Appeals Board may refer the appeal to the DCI policy manager for investigation and possible action against the appellant.
*   If during fact finding, the Appeals Board uncovers possible violations of DCI policy by other players, judges, or tournament organizers, the appeal may be referred to the DCI policy manager for possible action against those involved. In these cases, corrections to the appellant's match history may not take place until after the policy administrator's investigation has concluded.

### Section C: Criteria for Filing a Match History Appeal

**Valid Bases for Filing a Match History Appeal** 

*   **Participated-not listed:** An event has been received and processed by the DCI, but it is not listed in a player's match history.
*   **Participated-additional rounds listed:** An event is correctly listed in a player's match history, but the listing shows rounds that the player did not play in.
*   **Participated-missing rounds:** An event is correctly listed in a player's match history, but the listing does not show rounds that the player played in.
*   **Listed-did not participate:** An event is listed in a player's match history, but the player did not participate in the listed event.
*   **Incorrect opponents reported:** A match history lists an opponent that the player did not play.
*   **Incorrect match results reported:** A match history incorrectly lists the results of a match.
*   **Incorrect tournament type reported:** An event was processed with an incorrect tournament type. (For example, a Limited event is listed as a Constructed event.)
*   **Incorrect game type reported:** An event was processed with an incorrect game type (For example, a **Magic: The Gathering** event is listed as a **Pokemon** event.)
*   **Incorrect K-value reported:** An event was processed using an incorrect K-value.
*   **Incorrect rating result:** A player believes he or she did not receive/lose the correct number of points from a match.
*   **Incorrect canceled status:** A player participated in an event that was reported as canceled.

#### Limitations and Exclusions for Filing a Match History Appeal

*   Denied appeals are final and may not be refiled.
*   Errors in events that occurred more than six months ago may not be appealed.
*   Errors in events that have been invalidated may not be appealed.
*   Errors in non-sanctioned events may not be appealed.
*   Errors in formats and games that are no longer sanctioned by the DCI (for example, **BattleTech**® and **ARC System**TM games) may not be appealed.
*   A player may not appeal an event in which results have not been received or were received with errors and returned to the tournament organizer. (See *[Section D](#sectiond): Resource and Contact Information for Tournament-Related Issues* to find out how to file a missing tournament inquiry.)
*   Rulings made by tournament judges may be appealed to the head judge during an event. The head judge's decisions are final and may not be appealed to the DCI. Serious concerns regarding judge rulings that may warrant investigation should be directed to the DCI judge manager at Andy.Heckt@wizards.com. These concerns, however, will not result in the alteration of tournament results.

Appeals that lack sufficient information to begin preliminary fact finding will not be considered received until that information is provided. This is important for bye- and invitation-related appeals, as they must be received within the time frame specified by the DCI Tournament Policy.

Unusual circumstances may arise that are not covered by the previous lists. If you have any questions about whether a particular aspect of an event may be appealed, see *[Section D](#sectiond): Resource and Contact Information for Tournament-Related Issues* for a list of contacts from which you can request clarification on a possible appeal.

### Section D: Resources and Contact Information for Tournament-Related Issues

This section is intended to assist players in finding the appropriate contact for DCI-sanctioned tournament-related issues that are not covered in Part 1 or 2 of this policy. Please obtain the most current version of this document for the most accurate contact information.

**Determining the Status of a Tournament** 

Go to [http://webapp.wizards.com/tournaments.aspx](https://web.archive.org/web/20090603130745/http://webapp.wizards.com/tournaments.aspx) and search for the event in question. The status code should be one of the following:

*   PND (pending): Event has not yet occurred.
*   CNC (canceled): Event was reported canceled.
*   NOT (not received): Event results have not yet been received but are not yet considered late.
*   RTN (returned): Event results were submitted but were returned to the tournament organizer.
*   RCD (received): Event results have been received but not yet processed.
*   PRO (processed): Event results have been received and processed.
*   DLQ (delinquent): Event results have not been received and are late.
*   INV (invalidated): This event has been invalidated by the DCI.

Match History Appeals may be filed only for events that have status codes of PRO (processed) or CNC (canceled).

**Requesting Information on Tournament Results That Have Not Been Submitted** 

If you have questions about the status of event results that are delinquent, it is usually better to check with the tournament organizer for event information-including results submission-before contacting the DCI. If you have not received a satisfactory response from a tournament organizer regarding an event results submission, and its status is DLQ, you may submit a missing tournament inquiry with the DCI.

**Submitting a Missing Tournament Inquiry** 

Send an email with the following information to john.grant@wizards.com with "Missing Tournament Inquiry" in the subject line of the email:

*   Player's *complete* name and address and DCI number
*   The date of the event
*   *Complete* event location information (name of store, city, state, and so on)

**As it often takes two to three weeks to process event results, please do not file a Missing Tournament Inquiry sooner than six weeks after an event has occurred.** Missing Tournament Inquiries that are not submitted according to these instructions may not receive a response. 

If this policy does not address a question or concern you may have that is related to your match histories, or if you have questions or suggestions regarding this policy, please send an email to john.grant@wizards.com with "Match History Appeals Inquiry" in the subject line. Be sure to include your *complete* name and address and DCI number (if you have one) with any correspondence.

**If You Have Difficulty Viewing Your Online Match History** 

Currently, only players enrolled in the **Magic**® Player Rewards program members can view their match history online. If you are enrolled in **Magic**® Player Rewards, and you are having difficulty logging into the DCI Personal Information Center, please use this link: [http://wizards.custhelp.com/](http://wizards.custhelp.com/)
