### Comments On The Community

* * *

I'm a fairly new **Magic** player (getting close to an intermediate level) but an ancient cyber geek (remember BBSs? I do) and the idea of playing **Magic** online is bloody cool.

Since starting with ***Magic** Online,* I've played games with people who made snide comments all the way through, others who seemed to find new ways to make animal noises, and a few who just didn't say a word the entire match. Some have tried to rip me off and others have just viewed my newness as a chance to beat me down to beef up their stats.

Don't get me wrong. I've met some nice people who were fun to play against and offered helpful advice. However, one real jerk can ruin a whole day of good games.

It looks to me like the ***Magic** Online* community is trying to figure itself out. Like every other online group, it's made up of a diverse group of people from a wide spectrum of economic, educational, and social backgrounds. Some of us are writers by profession, others are typing with two fingers. We've got teens who've been online since preschool and grandparents who just got a computer last year.

Here are some observations I've made that might help make the ***Magic** Online* community a bit friendlier place to play.

### In-Game Chat

Unlike physical **Magic** games, you can't bluff/intimidate/amuse your opponent to the same degree, so mellow out with the chat. For example, I'm still slow in thinking through my options each turn, and combat math is still a pain to compute. So, if you're chatting with me during my turn, chances are, I'm ignoring you completely. Although it's still distracting to have it flowing by at the bottom of the screen, it's not enough to throw me off my game.

Obviously, this is more important in timed matches versus untimed casual games. However, you may find yourself chatting with someone who doesn't speak your language, so don't get upset if your opponent isn't very talkative.

Oh, and although it's cute when you type "rar" and attack, it gets old really fast.

### Shouting

Don't SHOUT when chatting. This has been something stated over and over since the early days of BBS's and email. Take of your CAPS LOCK please!

### Acronyms

Keep the acronyms to a minimum, or at least stick to just a few--not everyone uses them, for one, and sometimes they're just silly.

### Dealing With Players

If you've got an opponent who's just acting like a jerk, then shake it off. Play your best game and learn from how your opponent plays and ignore the idiocy. Then, before the game's over, right-click the player's name and add him or her to your **Blocked players** list.

On the other hand, if you play someone who is friendly, fun, or just generally helpful, add him or her to your **Buddy list**. After a while you'll have your own mini-community of players who you know won't ruin your day by acting like a brat.

### Smack Talk

Remember that what you think you're saying may not be what the other person is hearing. And if you're playing someone you know it's a lot different than playing against a stranger. This is especially true online, where all those wonderful visual and auditory cues are gone.

When I play **Magic** with a friend, we may insult each other all the time. But if Joe Schmoe calls me a foul creature of the night for playing my Upheaval or stealing his Spiritmonger, then I'm pretty darn well offended. In a physical game, I can see my opponent's face and hear the laugh. With someone I've played a lot, I'll know how serious that person is. Online against someone I don't know, I have no idea whether my opponent is trying to be funny or not.

One way around this is to use emoticons (those smiley/frowny faces). While overusing them may cause your opponent to say foul words to you, they're invaluable when all you've got is text communication. Other than the ones you can get in ***Magic** Online,* you can use grimaces and funky-dos as well. For example: &gt;:-&gt; (devilish), %-) (loopy), :-\[ (vampiric), :-7 (wry), :-@ (screaming, a personal favorite), :-P (nyahhhh!), :-/ (skeptical), etc. These are from a great collection at [http://www.charm.net/~kmarsh/smiley.html](https://web.archive.org/web/20021226000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.charm.net/~kmarsh/smiley.html&origin=magic_magiconline_articlelm122602).

Related to this is that writing is a skill and not everyone does it well. So sometimes you think you're being crystal clear but the person you're conversing with thinks you're being vague, maybe even contradictory.

### Scams

Don't fall for scams and report players you think are trying to pull a fast one on you.

**Magic** is one of the most complicated, interesting games in existence; people who play **Magic** are darn smart. Unfortunately, sometimes these smart people use their knowledge to take advantage of less experienced players. Being a newbie, I realize it's tempting to take someone's word when he or she offers to help with a problem. After years dealing with online groups, however, certain things send red flags up in my head.

Why people still fall for this is beyond me, but I'll say it again. When someone asks you for your password, credit card number, or coupon code: DON'T GIVE IT TO THEM.

In my experience, companies and other people who run online services never ask for your password because of security issues. And so Wizards has said, repeatedly, that no one from Wizards will ever ask for your password.

If you get an email supposedly from Wizards of the Coast, check to see if they are writing from a name@wizards.com address--and I don't mean just looking at the address listed. Check the full headers of the email and see if the address really is from Wizards of the Coast. Then, contact the ***Magic** Online* Community Support Group (magicconduct@wizards.com) with the email information and tell them this person is running a scam.

Two final pieces of advice, from "The Notebooks of Lazarus Long" by Robert Heinlein: "Never argue with an idiot. Other people may not be able to tell the difference." And my personal favorite: "Never try to have the last word. You might get it."
