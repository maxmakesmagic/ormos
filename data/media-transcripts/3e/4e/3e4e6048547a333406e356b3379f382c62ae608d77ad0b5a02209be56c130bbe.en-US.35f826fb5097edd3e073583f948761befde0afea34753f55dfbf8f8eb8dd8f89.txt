Meltdown, Pernicious Deed, Energy Flux Affinity. Couldn't possibly win an Extended, could it? Well, don't tell that to PT Columbus champion Pierre Canali, who piloted his hybrid Affinity deck to A8. No record on day one, the number one seed going into day three and a Pro Tour Championship over Shuhei Nakamura in his very first PT event. Really. Has anyone's love for a deck been returned in greater measure than of Affinity and Pierre, despite a number of unorthodox plays, can always smash through Jeff Sharons throwback blue-green Madness deck 3 to 1 before defeating narrowly multiple Pro Tour top 8 participant Olivier Ruel and his Goblin deck three to two overcoming Sparksmith, normally a big problem for Affinity with his tech dot engineered plagues in the sideboard awaiting economy in the finals. Will Shuhei Nakamura piloting red deck wins? Nakamura was thought to be a big underdog early and 15 year old American Gadiel slavered and his reanimator deck. Nakamura played perfectly and took advantage of Sleaford early mana difficulties and pulled out a three one victory. You know we're coming a second turn Akroma Angel of Wrath. In the semis, Knockmore faced off against Englishmen Nick W, perhaps the most prepared player in the tournament with a plate blue white scepter chant deck. Despite next ability to imprint fire Ice or Orim's chant on Ice Crown Scepter and use it every turn even and even multiple exalted angels for Nick in the final game, the tiny red men and again extremely tight play led Nakamura into the finals in a perfect three O sweep. Going into the finals, the consensus among the commentators and the crowd was that the matchup favored Nakamura, particularly in game one before Kanali was able to bring his chills in from the sideboard. So one must have forgotten to tell the ever smiling Kanali whoever they are supposed to lose. Game one and you do the best possible opening hand with turn 1/8 of Isle, followed up by Double Disciple, Double Ravager over the next three turns to quickly close out the opening frame. In Game 2, Kanali was in for an unpleasant surprise. Nakamori emptied his hand on turn 4, playing in Snaring Bridge. With no way in his deck to remove the bridge, Cavalli would have to win through a Ravager Disciple kill. Cavalli's deck again proved its worth, providing him with a steady flow of artifacts and Thoughtcast to let him pull off the combo before Nakamura could find 16 points of burn. In Game 3, Nakamura's draw looked lightning fast with a number of 1 drops and a lot of action Kanali kept in hand, which was relatively slow and uninspiring except for one important detail, the ability to cast a second turn Chill. The Chill slowed Nakamura's drawdown so drastically the Canali was able to steadily build up his board, the multiple Ravagers and Mirror Enforcers all allowing him to make one massive attack, overwhelming Knockwurst, Jackal Pups and Lava Mancers bringing Kanali the Pro Tour Columbus 2005 Extended Format Championship. Overall, it's been a fantastic weekend. Have extended format play with eight different decks in the top 8 extends never been more fun.
