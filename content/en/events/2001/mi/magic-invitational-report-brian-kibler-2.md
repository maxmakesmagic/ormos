### 

There has perhaps been no tournament in the history of **Magic** for which writing a play-by-play report would have been more pointless than for this year's Invitational. **Magic** Online provides players around the world the ability to replay every game from the entire event, if they so choose - although I can certainly think of other ways I'd rather spend my weekends. This is both a blessing and a curse. The blessing is that it provides a powerful resource for players looking to improve their play by analyzing that of the pros. The curse is that everyone can see every play you made wrong - or even every play that even LOOKED wrong - as well as the fact that reports from the event might not be nearly as interesting, as everyone can just say "watch it yourself". Sad to say, this report is going to fall into the latter category, as I'm swamped with end-of-semester work at school, so I don't have the time or energy to go into an in-depth analysis of all of the games I played.

Of course, that doesn't mean I'm just going to skimp out on you all completely. Sure, I can say "watch the replays" to explain how my matches went, but the games themselves are only a small part of the **Magic** Invitational. The experience this year wasn't quite as grandiose as last year in Cape Town, which was also my first Invitational, as well as my first time in Africa - but it was certainly something to remember, even if there was no cheetah petting or praying that Matt Vienneau would fall into shark infested waters involved.

My experience for this year's Invitational began not long after Worlds, when I was still residing at Eric Froelich's house for a day or so before making my way down to rejoin the "real world" after spending a month in Mini Colony seclusion. This particular night at the Froehlich house was rather miserable, as both Rubin and Hegstad were already gone - Ben taking his laptop with him - leaving me to the fate of watching EFro play **Magic** Online incessantly. One can only take so much backseat-driver gaming before it starts to become something like the game shows Renton talks about in Trainspotting.

One redeeming quality of that night, though, was an instant message EFro got from Thomas Pannell - one that I was very much not expecting. Apparently, for some brief window in time, my Composite rating had surged above Anton Jonsson's by a whopping three points and, as a result, I was the #1 ranked player in the world and received an automatic slot in the Invitational. A lot of people had been assuring me that I was very likely to get voted in, but even so it was nice to know for sure that I'd be playing in Seattle without sweating out the final tally. Thanks to everyone who said they would've voted for me - and keep me in mind for next year's ballot. The way things are going so far this season, it looks like I'll need it.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/53/12/5312ffae830f21e9a4afa3c7718c5f67063035a467ec4b780ce5e346a88b46fc.jpg)The next big step toward the Invitational came when I got access to the **Magic** Online test server. Because Onslaught's official **Magic** Online release was not until November, the invitees received temporary test accounts to give us a chance to play with the new cards. Unfortunately, it was a rarity for enough of us to be on at once to do any actual drafts - or even practice Two-Headed Giant - so most of my time on the test server was spent playing Sealed Deck or Standard. Some of the invitees let our friends use the test accounts when we weren't online, so it wasn't uncommon to go on and find Huey on one account playing against Dan OMS on another. As a matter of fact, I don't think Finkel ever went on his own account during the test period, and JonFinkel\_INV was always logged on. Quite the secret agent, that Jonny Magic. 

Step three was traveling to the Invitational itself. I failed to really look at the itinerary WotC sent me and just said it was okay when I looked at the dates, but really ought to have tried to get myself booked on Delta. I'm a super-uber-extra-special frequent flier with them, and it's certainly a plus that I happen to live right at their hub, so I can pretty much get a direct flight anywhere. Not so for this trip, however, as a layover in Chicago (where I meet up with everyone's favorite Keeper of the Beasts, Brian Davis) means I don't get into Seattle until around midnight on Wednesday. A quick cab to the hotel reveals that most everyone else is already there and drafting, and seeing as we have to be up early the next day for the tour, I head to sleep at a reasonable hour. Don't expect to see that line in this report ever again.

Thursday is tour day, which pretty much equates to "play **Magic** Online and wander around WotC headquarters all morning, and wander around in Seattle without **Magic** Online to play all afternoon". The tour of WotC was pretty interesting, and I was especially impressed by the game library, which is a big room with more copies of gaming materials than most tech schools. The second part of the "tour" consisted in the WotC staff driving all of us to Seattle and basically saying "do whatever you want for the next five hours, then come to dinner". A large contingent of the Invitees headed off to explore the city's sights, whereas Humphreys, Price, Finkel and I decided to explore the city's Irish pubs, with the less parched Pikula, Pannell, and Josh Bennett not far behind. 

Five hours full of Red Bull, Guinness, war stories, and harassment of Dave Price later, we made our way to the restaurant for dinner. We were told the meal was on WotC, but they're only paying for one alcoholic beverage per person - reminiscent of erstwhile reporter Matt Vienneau's exploits from the year previous, I'm sure. To make up for my inability to drink on the company dime, I order the most extravagant drink on the menu, along with a filet. There's nothing quite like free steak and alcohol - except, of course, free sushi, so I make sure to get a plateful of nigiri while I'm at it. If you haven't had Unagi - freshwater eel sushi - you're missing out.

You're also missing out if you haven't had a chance to go out with Tomi Walamies and Eivind Nitter. These two Scandinavians are absolute riots. After dinner, Finkel, Price, and I return to our previous haunt with these two clowns in tow, along with Scott Larabee to boot. Most of the conversation centers around our fellow patrons in the bar, and how they rank on the all-important binary scale. If you don't get it, just think harder. Eivind lives in some crazy fantasy world where almost every girl he sees is a zero, it seems, although when two gorgeous women passed our table at one point, the Norwegian rock star had to admit "even I am not invincible." This entire time, I would like to point out, Walamies was wearing a shirt that said "If you see da cops, warn-a-brotha" with the Warner Brothers logo. Just picture it. Yeah, I couldn't help but laugh either.

After a while, Larabee decides to head back to Renton, but only Tomi is ready to call it a night. The rest of us hail a cab and tell the driver to take us to the university district, or - and I quote - "somewhere we can find college girls". We end up at yet another Irish pub, but our cab driver did not disappoint. We spend the next couple hours listening to a cover band over constantly re-filling rounds of drinks. It was almost like I'd never left Atlanta, except my company consisted of the most bitter man alive, a one-time **Magic** legend, and a Scandinavian who owns pants that cost more than my entire wardrobe instead of my fraternity brothers. No points for guessing who's who. I managed to convince Finkel to get up and dance toward the end of the night, but Price and Nitter stoically refused. Some people. Closing time put us in a cab back to Renton, and I crawl into bed in time to get a few hours of fitful sleep before it's time to game.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b6/39/b63907223958da33a6e43b5afedd1da90f3ba89a47169e611d79112d05b05046.jpg)The first day of play is a fairly disappointing one. I manage a 2-1 record in Two-Headed Giant, which is a very fun format, complete with team trash talk, high fives, and victory laps, but post a less impressive 1-2 in Online Extended. My g-r-b beatdown deck's plan against g-u decks - Smother and Terminate - just didn't cut it against the two I played, as Phantom Centaur and Mystic Enforcer ended up doing me in. Despite my shortcomings in the tournament itself, I had a blast chatting it up with all the people who were online watching the matches and rooting for their favorite players. Particularly amusing was Jon Finkel's "unmasking" of Kai Budde's secret screen name, prompting literally dozens of people to message Kai within seconds.

That night Brian Hegstad - who lives in the area - drove up to hang out. No one else seemed to want to go out that night, so Brian and I went to a party at his friend's house before heading to an Alice in Wonderland themed rave in the industrial district. As many of you have likely read in his profile in a recent *Sideboard* magazine, Brian is openly gay, and the party we ended up dropping by at was apparently in the "gay district", more or less, of Seattle. We didn't stay long, but in the time we were there Brian got a number of questions about whether I was "his man" whenever I was out of the room. He told them no, I wasn't, but apparently didn't have the heart to tell them that I was straight. 

Next on our list of destinations was "Down the Rabbit Hole", which ended up being a blast. The venue was fairly small, but well set up, with all kinds of cool decorations and such, including a video game console. I'm not really sure what game it was that we played, but it was some sort of Goldeneye-esque game in which you play some manner of rodent armed to the teeth. I had a lot of fun both dancing and blowing people up as a squirrel packing a flamethrower, and before we knew it, it was something like five am. A quick ride back to Renton and I manage to get at least a few minutes of sleep before I have to get up to play.

Day 2 went much better for me than Day 1. Last year, I figured out the Auction format fairly quickly, and ended up taking one of the best decks for an extremely cheap bid. This year, people had a better idea of the bidding structure, but I was reluctant to bid extremely aggressively early on and ended up with a fairly mediocre deck because of it. I don't really like the Auction very much since so many of the decks are so bad or bid so low that games are pretty much decided by mana draws. I didn't play a single game in the entire format this year that wasn't decided almost entirely by mana, and that doesn't make for a particularly interesting format. *Onslaught* draft was much more straightforward, with the only real situation of note coming when Diego Ostrovich misclicked on a Mistform Dreamer in front of me midway through pack one. The amusing thing about sitting in the same room as the people you're drafting with on **Magic** Online is that you can communicate all you want - Diego literally yelled over to me "it was a misclick!" when he heard me vocalizing my annoyance. 

At the end of Day 2, I was in the running for the finals with a 3-0 performance on Sunday, but since our Standard decks were due at the beginning of the day, I didn't really have anything I could do. Finkel was in the same boat, so what else is there to do but go out and party all night again? I managed to nap for about an hour before Hegstad, Finkel, and I took off for another rave, where we met up with Jesse Wilke, a fellow gamer from the Seattle area. Amusingly enough, this party was at the same venue as the one the night before, though it wasn't quite as elaborate a production - no decorations or video game console this time. Despite the sparse surroundings, I once again had a great time, dancing until my eyes stung from sweat. I actually had one girl come up to me and say "Man, I love your (stuff) - you've inspired me", which is especially funny because I really have no idea what I'm doing - I just move how I feel like moving. The others figured she was probably just trying to hit on me. What a bunch of haters. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/14/a9/14a9d7289b929601ae2d83ba33023ad7c04cb4233c73e8f2eb9fb0c4b5795561.jpg)We stayed pretty much until the place shut down, which meant that once again I could measure the amount of sleep I got in minutes. Despite my weariness, I manage to pilot my Burning Wake deck to a 2-1 record, losing a match to Carlos in which I double mulliganed and don't draw a third land until he already has Braids in play. My first game of the day set the tone for me, when I manage to Firecat Blitz Ishida for something like 150 points of damage. My goal became to deal as much damage in one turn as I could - a goal which I managed to realize during the gunslinging portion of the event. In a game against a player whose name I sadly can't remember - and for some reason the gunslinging rooms on **Magic** Online no longer seem to be available - I attacked for something like 150,000 points of damage. I could've done more - in fact, arbitrarily large number, had I made a few simple changes to the deck - but I found that **Magic** Online has a token limit. After Mirari'ing Burning Wish and Parallel Evolution (doubling Crush of Wurms tokens) a few too many times, along with fetching four Coats of Arms with Golden Wish, I had 192 806/806 creatures. Unfortunately, I never got to see the final outcome of my attack, as WotC headquarters closed long before the damage finished counting down. 

That night the partying was much less intense, with Rosewater hosting a laid back gathering at his house. I managed to get some sleep before my flight the next morning, and crashed immediately upon returning to Atlanta. Unsurprisingly, I came down with an unforgiving illness not long after I got back, the echoes of which I'm feeling even now. Of course, it can't have helped that I went to a DJ Tiesto show the Thursday after I got back, which was the first day I actually got out of bed for any extended period of time. Perhaps I ought to settle down and not go out and push myself so much at events.

Nah. 

Thanks for reading - I hope you enjoyed this compilation of random babble. Soon enough I'll crawl out from under my piles of things to do and manage to get myself writing again, so stay tuned to *Sideboard.com*. Oh, and one last thing - the quote of the event, courtesy of Dave Humphreys while sitting at the pub on tour day watching people walk by: "I'm a biologist, and that was no woman".
