It's party time.

The native dancers, the eighteen rounds of Swiss, the WotC speeches, the greatest title of them all, they're all less than a week away. World Championships is here.

This is the biggie. Ask any pro worth their mettle and they'll tell you that winning World Championships is the final frontier for any career. There's more money, more prestige and more glamour in this tournament than any other, and the lot of us are traveling thousands of miles to the furthest reaches of the planet to try and get a piece of the pie.

Granted, this may not be the blow out that the Olympics were two years ago, but more than fifty countries are being represented and most of the world's best will be in attendance. You'll have your usual grudge matches, feature matches and 0-5 matches, but this time, there's more at stake.

There are a lot of stories to watch for this tournament. Here's a few of them:

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a719.jpg)Return of the Great One - After six months of down time, Bob Maher will be back at the table trying to do his Worlds 2000 performance one better. Maher was suspended for his part in a ratings scheme gone awry, but he's done his time and has been chewing at his leash thanks to the impatience bred by his inactivity. 

Mini-Colony - Check out those names: Budde, Rubin, Mello, Froelich, Kibler, Lippert, Klauser, Hegstad... there's a lot of firepower there. I've talked to team members who have indicated they're pleased with their Constructed progress, but now is the time to prove the hype was worth it. The original Colony managed to take Osaka despite some admittedly mediocre numbers, so there are two questions that the new team needs to answer: Can anything short of a win be called a success? And, has the hype been worth it? 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fe/bb/febb942d5480d4296e06d4da3620656461341f2f5e012da2e24c3b5daf3322e3.jpg)**Magic** Online Watch - With the release of **Magic** Online and the August 1 release of *Judgment* on that program, a number of the Tour's finest have been upgrading their play time to ridiculous proportions, and the questions about how much the program will help the pro player may start being answered. Finkel, Thoren, Benafel, Jonsson, Mowshowitz, Kibler, Jensen, Linde, Froelich, Tannenbaum, Weigersma...the list is a long one which, regardless of its result, could help define **Magic** Online's role in the community for years to come.

The Dutch - If I were Dutch and wanted the three strongest players in the nation representing me at World Championships, Noah Boeken, Kamiel Cornelissen and Tom Van de Logt would definitely be in my top four, and arguments could be made for each of them being in the top three. Van de Logt is the defending World Champion, Boeken owns a European Championship belt and a Masters win, while Kamiel is that guy who made two Pro Tour finals in a row last year, his first full season on the Tour. These three monsters are the Dutch National Team and have to be considered the favorites heading into the Team competition, and the watch for potential usurpers is on. More on this later.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images%5CGPFLO00%5C176.jpg)The Kai - There is only one Kai Budde, and he's now three years removed from his lone World Championship. How far back does his memory reach? Kai's done pretty much everything, so now its time to start doubling up. He has to be called the favorite for this tourney, with his play skill buoyed by a brand new team of expert deckbuilders. Kai will be trying to duplicate Jon Finkel's 2000 performance, when Johnny Magic won both the singles and team divisions, but while the German team is solid, Mark Zeigner and Felix Schneiders aren't Chris Benafel and Aaron Forsythe. It's a tough hill to climb, but Kai's is the biggest walking stick.

Top 5 predictions - These are the five players who are most likely to win World Championships in my opinion:

1) Kai Budde - I've written this stuff so many times you all must have it memorized. On to the others...

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a713.jpg)2) Anton Jonsson - This guy is as hot as anyone in the world right now, and that includes the Juggernaut. Anton hasn't managed to win the big one yet, but this year he's made Top 8's in Limited and Constructed, won a National Championship and made Top 8 at European Championships. He has a great team full of powerhouse deckbuilders around him, and he's been online twelve hours a day or so getting ready for this tournament. This could be the breakthrough.

3) Olivier Ruel - Olivier's had a very strong season, with the pinnacle coming at Osaka where he came within a game of the championship. Olivier may not appear as serious as other PT denizens, but that's because he loves to game, and the strong showings and routine Masters invites aren't a fluke. One other factor here: for the last three years, the last-place finisher in the Invitational has come back to win a Pro Tour in that season. When he took that 2-0 lead over Ken Ho in the Osaka final, everyone figured number four was in the bag, but it wasn't to be. Sydney is his last chance to take advantage of 'the curse'. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a831.jpg)4) Rob Dougherty - Time and again, Rob's name has decorated PT Top 8 lists in both Limited and Constructed, and this could be the time for the big one. As if Your Move Games wasn't a strong enough team before, Zvi Mowshowitz' addition seems certain to improve things in deck building despite the lack of room for it, and when you're the owner of one the world's biggest gaming stores, you're going to get your work in. Rob is diligent, building a lot of the decks his team eventually plays and has put up numbers in Constructed over the last two years that few could bother to compare with. With fifteen of the twenty-one rounds using the sixty-card minimum, this could be Rob's time.

5) Jens Thoren - I really wanted to keep this list to one player from each team, but Jens, Eric Foehlich and Ben Rubin proved too much for the rest of the planet. In the end, Jens makes the grade because of three things: he's made a Constructed Top 8 this year, he's made a Limited Top 8 this year and the rest of Team Punisher speaks about him in hushed whispers as if he was the Second Coming. He's also looking to avenge his showing a year ago, when he dropped before Day 2 in order to protect his Limited rating for Masters purposes. With five million pro points, he doesn't need to worry about his rating any more.

And the teams...

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bb/8a/bb8a9584bfcfae97b0794a86cf620e2c98259358a95bbdb94c1b89cbea3c3e14.gif)1) The Netherlands - I said it before, I'll say it again, there's just a whole lot of power here and the rest of the world will have to step up if they want to beat these guys. The key to their strength is the lack of a weak link.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a7/d9/a7d9383c807864b03906a97090b260137c7b7dd92f6ee2b213b965cdbfc9fba6.gif)2) Germany - How can you argue with Kai Budde? He's taking up the captain's chair for this one with two capable lieutenants, one of which, Felix "Flix" Schneiders, has quietly become one of the world's best deck builders, as evidenced by his convincing Kai to play Trix in New Orleans and at last year's Worlds and his manufacturing of the Trix-Oath hybrid. Flix and Mark Ziegner may have difficulty with their Dutch opponents, but they have as close an auto win in seat one as you can muster. When two capable players need to win one match between then per round, things are looking pretty rosy

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d3/1b/d31b0e64e8ddae7d33c8967b2f1c4ea0dc200d1ac78056a9a20cdbb6e1bb32e2.gif)3) The United States of America - That's right folks, I don't see these guys even making the final. Team U.S.A. shocked the world a year ago after being written off, but they were the major beneficiaries of a system weighted towards the Team Rochester that doesn't exist anymore. Captain Eugene Harvey could be in a better position than anyone in this competition to capitalize on the Team Rochester format operating out of that format's hotbed, Pittburgh, but the inexperience of Andrew Ranks and Eric Franz, playing in their first World Championships, could be an issue. Some people would put them lower, but I think you have to respect anyone who makes it through the U.S. Nationals gauntlet.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3f/73/3f7306681ee618b27197b2f0adec063e39324bd00f5c5496855c3acfba0e95c3.gif)4) Sweden - I mentioned before how well National Champion Anton Jonsson has been playing of late, but add on fellow Punisher Mattias Kettil and you have a strong team. Kettil and Jonsson don't live close to one another, so they won't have the geographical advantage of practicing together, but strength is just that and these guys have it. Mattias Wåglin, a virtual unknown, rounds out the team, and his play may ultimately decide whether these guys finish higher or lower in the standings.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/38/b9/38b9acfcb7124249113d18481d302b1982da1723bac18a75a3a2c4d3affb4aaa.gif)5) France - Christophe Haim has had a terrific half year since he and Franck Canu moved in together, with a Top 8 finish at Osaka, a finals appearance at European Championships and a third-place finish in French Nationals, and he'll be joined by two other players with strong Pro Tour finishes this year: Florent LeFranc and National Champion Sylvain Lauriol, who also made Top 8 in Japan. Talent won't be an issue here, but cooperation could be, as some French players have suggested that Lauriol may not be the most cooperative teammate, as evidenced by his working alone for Osaka. 

Matches I'd like to watch:

1) Kai Budde vs. Cole Swannack - One has won five Pro Tours and an Invitational, the other is the next best thing to home town favorite. You probably haven't heard of Cole, but he's the twelve year old who came out of nowhere to win New Zealand's National Championships. It's possible that youth could prove an advantage, with the accompanying naivte potentially protecting him from the understanding that he's facing the same fate as most of Kai's opponents.

2) Bob Maher vs. Ed Fear - They're good friends, both back from suspension and both fun to watch play **Magic**. Bob wants a triumphant return, and this would make for a great story in that saga. Fear is one of the Tour's great showmen. This would be one to remember.

3) Finland vs. Sweden - Nothing too complex here: In seat one, we have Tomi Walamies vs. Anton Jonsson. Friends, teammates, National Champions, these two could face off with a lot on the line in the one match that can provide something bigger than team alliances, even if its only for an hour. Did I mention they're both great players? 

4) U.S.A. vs. Germany final - Kai has more than once made juicy comments about the United States' community and its players, and the possibility of his being in position to end their reign of dominance would be too juicy to pass up. The U.S. has won this event for five years running. 

5) Chris Benafel vs. Gary Wise - Oh come on, you know you'd all love it.

Before I close things up for this week, I wanted to make a quick comment about the last Wise Words. If you missed it, I wrote a cheery piece about **Magic** Online because I love it. It wasn't solicited or even suggested I write it by the *Sideboard* and I wrote it of my own accord. A number of cynics have gone on record suggesting that that installment was blatant advertising for a program that, frankly, every reader of this column already knew about and also suggested that my writing that article was actually my selling out to the corporate world. To those people I say this: I've written about this game for five years now in as honest a fashion as I know how. I've done so at some cost to myself, alienating myself from some players on the Tour, but have done so out of a respect for the game and a need to be truthful that I don't really want to explain. I'm a little insulted by the allegations those cynics levied against me, and would hope my record as a writer would stand for itself. To the rest of you, I promise this: I've never told anything but the truth as I see it in my writings and will continue to do so. All I ask in return is a little more faith and a little less conspiracy theory. Thanks for listening. 

Have a good week.
