**Magic: The Gathering®** *Online*, to put it simply, is one of if not the best thing to happen to this amazing card game in quite some time. That’s a big statement considering innovative and entertaining occurrences seem to be a daily trademark of my favorite hobby. I know many of you out there already agree with me though. If you don’t, then let me explain why you should.

**Magic: The Gathering** *Online* is more than just an online game. It is an incredible selection of tools and raw materials. Also, the **Magic** *Online* community is more than just a massive group of people playing in an online game. It is a marketplace of masterful craftsmen. With those two significant aspects combined, the choice of what to do with **Magic** *Online* lies in your capable hands. 

At one extreme, you can select to never pick up a single tool and just simply use the materials lying about in some crude manner. Play a game here, play a game there, never once really putting things together to benefit or enhance your game play level. Sure, you may get to play some preconstructed deck you purchased a few times over several months, but there is so much more you could do… even with that one single preconstructed deck. At the other end of the spectrum, you can keep all your tools in top working order, replacing old ones as time passes by. You can make sure these tools find their way into the hands of the most gifted tradesmen you’ve encountered, constructing your computer gaming sessions into a wondrous and powerful creation of experience mixed with foresight. Here’s the thing that might throw you though. Either direction is fine. After all, it’s your choice for entertainment, so how can you really navigate wrong along one path instead of the other? 

Me? I tend to exist somewhere in the middle of both roads. I think most of us do. Chances are, you play more than once or twice a month, maybe even logging in as often as several times a week. At the same time, chances are you don’t leave the game on 24-7, playing during work, hoping your boss doesn’t walk up behind you in your cubicle asking about the memo regarding your TPS Reports. And I’m guessing that you want to play and have some fun whenever free time inserts itself into your schedule, but at the same time you want to improve your overall game and make sure your time spent doesn’t feel wasted. 

I think most people can figure out how to have a good time and enjoy themselves playing this game online. I know that guy I played last week who smacked me around with his Tooth and Nail/Composite Golem/Door to Nothingness/Clock of Omens Mirrodin Block Constructed deck definitely knows how to have a good time. If you do need some help though, let me make two great suggestions:

1.  Get involved in games in the Casual Play/Casual Decks area. Some of the most enjoyable sessions I have had to date come from games in this room. In fact, one of my current favorite obsessions is casual Prismatic games. What could be more enjoyable than a 250+ card deck… I mean really?
2.  Read *[Into the Aether](/en/articles/intotheaether/2005/03-15-intotheaether-versus-the-evil-chicken)* by Jay Moldenhauer-Salazar every Tuesday. Heck, read his past articles while you’re at it. Not only will you surely gain some helpful insight into the **Magic** *Online* experience, but you might finally realize what exactly most players mean by, “Let’s play for fun.”

Improving your overall game… now this is where the whole tools and craftsmen discussion is going to come full circle. This is the lure that hooked me onto playing with digital cards versus cardboard ones. Basically, **Magic** *Online* offers you three materials that are far superior to their physical counterparts.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_mprnews_18b_pic1_en.jpg)

First, there is the Deck Editor. The ease with which you can assemble and retain your decks cannot be elaborated upon enough. I make all my Standard and Block deck designs with **Magic** *Online*, and once the Extended rotation is synchronous between on- and off-line, I will use it for that as well. Even if I don’t have all the cards in my online collection for a particular deck, putting together the deck list is still a breeze. Just make sure you uncheck the My Cards option to see all the cards available. And use the filters. They are your friends. Trust me, once you start doing this, you will never want to make decks any other way. I just wish WotC would put all cards ever made available online just for this one purpose. I don’t need them to play with online; just give me the pictures so I can construct ALL my decks this way. Little piece of advice though, if you make as many decks as I do—burn the files to CD or save them on a Flash Drive. Recoverability is also your friend.

The second tool lies in playing the decks you construct online. Find out what does and doesn’t work in your latest creation. Use the program to put together already established deck archetypes and find others to play against. I promise you will have no problem finding them in the Casual Play area. Just hang out in the Constructed Games/Tournament Practice room and you’ll find them in no time. In here, you can learn the matchups you need to know for tournament-level **Magic**. You can also find matchups you haven’t heard of before. Watch others play the archetype decks as well. They may understand the decks better than you do. Ask them questions. You’ll find that most of the community will gladly answer your questions. Heck, find a playtest group, whether it is a Clan you join specifically for that purpose or a bunch of individuals you add to your Buddy List. This is fantastic for those of us in areas where we are lucky to get more than three or four folks together in the flesh to do such things. In fact, this is one of the few ways I have available to me to practice for real-life Constructed tournaments. **Magic** *Online* ends the excuse of those who claim they can’t find anyone to practice against.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_mprnews_18b_pic2_en.jpg)

The third tool, and my personal favorite, is online Limited games. Between online Sanctioned 8-Player Booster Drafts and online Leagues, I don’t think there has been any other instrument that has increased the skill level of players worldwide more. Some might argue that strategy articles are the driving reason for this, but I think imbedded experience is far more potent than temporary knowledge. **Magic** articles have been around for far more years than the online game. I’ve been playing through both of these periods of this game’s evolution. I am here to tell you that the skill level of players at local events and PTQs (at least in regards to limited events) has gone way up in the last two years. If this is not due to **Magic** *Online* Limited games, then I sure would like someone to let me in on the secret origin of this phenomenon. This is one of the main reasons that the vast majority of my time online is spent playing in Limited games. Booster Drafting is great practice, but Leagues are an incredible bang for you buck. Leagues allow you to hone your Sealed Deck strategies and play as often as you like, and they give you the chance to win a substantial number of free booster packs. Nothing is better than advancing your game play, having loads of fun, and walking away with 27 booster packs to boot. 

Sure there are many other tools that **Magic** *Online* offers. Some of these may be more important to others than I have given them credit for. I’ve only touched the surface of what **Magic** *Online* empowers you with. There’s trading and redeeming card sets. There’s the fact that the game handles all the rules without error (minus the occasional software bug). The visual rules element that is so elegantly integrated into **Magic** *Online* alone can sharpen your grasp of the game immensely. There’s Release Events and Premiere Events. There’s the Magic Invitational Coverage. I simply don’t have enough time or space to go in depth about all of them. Besides… that’s Jay’s gig.
