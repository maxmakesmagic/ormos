### Grand Prix-Tampa

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/86/97/86979151680a0bae615acd124aaf8b37b858e015e9b0d46d480e6f64e5e86f0a.jpg"><br><i>The people's champion?</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

Sol Malka has long been a fixture at Pro Tour events, but did not enjoy much success. He is perhaps best known for designing 'The Rock' b-g archetype which he played for years in Standard and then Extended, and which Pustilnik used it to win Grand Prix-Las Vegas. Finally, Malka had his day. He won GP-Tampa, having drafted an incredibly fast b-r deck (what was that Mindslicer doing in the sideboard though?) with three Barbarian Outcasts. He defeated Jeff Cunningham in the finals.

Cunningham recently broke out with a Top 8 finish in San Diego, and has continued his streak in Florida. Perhaps his practice over at The Magic Colony has been of help. The Colony placed four people in the top 12, with Fung, Ho and Benafel placing 10th through 12th in a row. Colony affiliate Brian Kibler had a good weekend, placing 6th. It was also the second Top 8 for local hero Antonino De Rosa, who dominated the Swiss rounds of the tournament.

"5-Color Magic" creator Kurtis Hahn set a record he wasn't proud of - getting kicked out of the tournament before it even started. Hahn was involved in a draft at the tournament site the night before, and got into a loud argument with a staff member who asked him to leave. In the morning, he found out that the abovementioned staff member was tournament organizer John Shannon, who refunded Hahn's entry fee and showed him to the door. Hahn spent the day helping out with *Sideboard* coverage.

Notably absent from the tournament were Dave Humpherys (whose workload meant he barely got the time off to go to Osaka) and any member of Team Godzilla. Mowshowitz, Comer, and Johns have not skipped a U.S. Grand Prix in the recent months that I can think of, but all were missing this time. Fluke? We are going to have to wait until Grand Prix-Milwaukee to find out. Congratulations to Noah Weil, who celebrated his birthday on Sunday of GP Tampa.

### Grand Prix-Antwerp

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/ae/8f/ae8fca572977169f220146ec72a12a4aaa66fcc58297c4af164fb292aa8ba29a.jpg"><br><i>Now beating pros in their dreams, Kai Budde</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

What can I possibly say about Kai Budde that has not been said already? Budde attended all four European Grand Prix held this season. His finishes were 1st, 2nd, 1st, and 1st - most recent one being in Antwerp. Budde navigated his deck to a perfect 6-0 games finish. As a matter of fact, every match in this Top 8 was won 2-0, making for an interesting bit of trivia. Budde was joined in the Top 8 by his teammate Dirk Baberowksi as well as an ex-teammate Mattias Jorstedt.

Antwerp is home to Wizards of the Coast's European office. I bet the organizers were overjoyed to run an event where they could travel back to their own homes for the night.

Belgian players surprised us by putting up one of the weakest performances ever by the home country at a Grand Prix. Only five players made it to Day 2 despite the home-turf advantage in numbers, and only one finished in Top 16. Johan Kennes finished ninth overall, winning the top Amateur prize and an invitation to PT-Nice. 

Among those at the top of the Player of the Year race, Farid Meraghni and Jens Thoren both edged up a point by finishing in Top 32, and Gabriel Nassif picked up 2 points in Top 16. With his Top 8 finish, Baberowski edged out teammate Marco Blume for 5th place in the race. Blume made Day 2, but finished out of the money.

No American players traveled to Antwerp. This is the first Grand Prix opportunity I skipped since September, surprising many people. Truth is, after a month of intensive traveling, I just needed a bit of extra rest. Sadly, I may end up skipping Grand Prix-Barcelona as well - it happens to take place during spring break and it is very difficult to find inexpensive airfare at that time - but I will keep looking.

### Magic Trivia

Last week's question:

You have no cards in your hand and no cards in your graveyard. You have any number of cards left in your deck. You have 11 islands and 11 mountains in play. You are at 1 life. Your opponent is at 20 life, and has just cast Urza's Rage targeting you. With the Rage on the stack, your opponent has no cards in hand or graveyard, and 8 islands untapped. You have one nonland permanent in play. What permanent could you have in play that would allow you to win the game in this situation?

Answer:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/75/70/7570a88a496a16d1194524dbf7a437924d2b05255008db5f6a9d69bffa88ff1b.jpg)

Wild Research. Respond to Rage by activating Research to look for Fiery Temper. As it is the only card in your hand, it will be discarded at random, allowing you to pay its madness cost. Repeat this 3 more times, with 3 more Fiery Tempers. This requires 12 mana to do. With the remaining 10, search for 2 Violent Eruptions, one at a time, and pay their madness costs. Before the Rage resolves your opponent has taken 20 damage

New Question (Courtesy of Scott Wills):

It's your main phase in game three of an *Odyssey-Odyssey-Torment* draft. You have Cephalid Broker and 4 tapped islands in play, and one island in hand. Your opponent is on two life with loads of creatures to attack and block with. You activate the Cephalid Broker and are able to kill your opponent during your main phase. What two cards did you draw?

### Play of the Week

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/28/d7/28d76f20a7e78a5b6194494999d68d8aeb09cbdc18de08935d0e18d2e3f6adb2.jpg"><br><i>Better lucky than good.</i>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

It pays to be lucky. Gerard Fabiano was playing in Top 8 of a PTQ in Tampa and ended up with a Crazed Firecat in his hand. Things were looking grim as he was only a turn away from casting this creature. His opponent already had several creatures in play that were taking small bites out of his life total. To make matters worse, an opponent who played first, laid his seventh mana and summoned his own Crazed Firecat. He lost the coin flip, but a 4/4 creature added nicely to his forces anyway. Gerard tapped out to summon his own Firecat... and won EIGHT count them EIGHT flips in a row! A 12/12 creature helped him make a comeback and eventually win the game.

### Bad Play of the Week

It's never a good thing when I am the protagonist of my own "Bad Plays" column - but this one certainly belongs in here. By far, the worst match of **Magic** I played in ages. I can only hope that 2.5 hours of sleep I got the night before were to blame. 

It was round 8 (round 1 of Day 2) at Grand Prix-Tampa and I was paired up against Adam Levy. I drafted an interesting green-white deck that had enough sac lands and mana fixers that I was able to splash Ambassador Laquatus and Breakthrough. Little did I know that I was about to screw up with both of these cards in the same match!

In the first game it was the end of my opponent's turn. I sacrificed an Abandoned Outpost - my only source of blue mana - and tapped five lands, leaving a pair of plains untapped to help protect an Aven Flock, and cast Breakthrough. Only to be reminded by my opponent that Breakthrough is a sorcery. Yes, a card I cast countless times in testing *Odyssey* Block Constructed. I was able to use the one white mana I had to pump Aven Flock and then burned for five. Not only did I not get to draw cards, but I wasted my previous turn saving up the mana for Breakthrough, and on top of that sacrificed my only source of blue mana! I never ended up casting Breakthrough that game, but was able to win with what was on the table.

**Sorcery**  
*Sorcery is a card type. The active player can play sorcery spells only during his or her main phase when the stack is empty. A sorcery spell is put into its owner's graveyard as part of its resolution. See rule 408.1d.*

In the second game, things seemed to be going very well. I got Ambassaror Laquatus into play and tricked my opponent into blocking a Springing Tiger with three 1/1 Squirrel tokens. I then used the Ambassor to mill myself and make the Tiger 5/5 - so far, so good. On the next turn with the board cleared, I sent both my Tiger and my Ambassador Laquatus in! And then my opponent cast his Beast Attack. Out of the graveyard. I already dealt with the first Beast Attack token several turns earlier, and completely forgot about it sitting in the graveyard. Poor Ambassador Laquatus met an untimely demise, and I went on to lose the game I thought I had in the bag. I did win the match by taking game 3, but it hardly made up for the stories of my awful mistakes that already spread throughout the room.
