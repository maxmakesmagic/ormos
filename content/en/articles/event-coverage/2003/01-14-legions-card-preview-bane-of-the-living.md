First of all, I need to apologize for a missing Extended article. In part two of that series, I mentioned at the end that I would write one more. I actually intended to do so but a combination of time problems and a very quickly fluctuating Extended metagame prevented me from writing that one. Grand Prix-Reims was the last event for me in this season and, other then a few local league matches with Rock, I haven’t played Extended since then. And, as the format changed too rapidly, I felt that I couldn’t really write anything that had substance without putting a lot of testing in and I just didn’t have the time to do that.

And now back to the real topic. 

This preview introduces a beating of a Limited card: Bane of the Living. Dave Price already covered Skirk Marauder, a little goblin that can do so much when you unmorph him. This ability, ‘morph trigger’, is a new mechanic that is going to be introduced in *Legions*. Aaron Forsythe covered the rules regarding this ability [here](/en/articles/feature/2003/01-08-morph-trigger-rules-primer). While Skirk Marauder is going to be good in Limited and will definitely see play in Standard Sligh decks, Bane of the Living is going to rule supreme in Limited. Let’s take a closer look at this insect first:

For ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) you get a 4/3 body. That’s a pretty good deal to start with. *Onslaught*’s Gustcloak Sentinel would be a very good creature even without its ability. There just aren’t many Hill Giants around in this block. I doubt very much that Bane of the Living will be played unmorphed very often, though. The morph trigger that this guy has built in pushes the card over the edge:

Morph ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) - When Bane of the Living is turned face up, all creatures get -X/-X until end of turn.

I don’t think I have to explain what this means. Bane of the Living is a walking Wrath of God that can be used at instant speed! If you invest ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif), Krosan Tusker and Slipstream Eel are the only two commons in *Onslaught* that survive this effect. Countermagic and damage prevention can’t help your opponent’s creatures. Regeneration you say? No sir, that won’t do it either.  
 The red instant Starstorm is one of the most powerful Limited cards in *Onslaught* block and there are a lot of gamers, including Ken Krouner, who claim it is actually the best card in *Onslaught*. But, compared with Bane of the Living, it will have to be happy with a second-place finish. Sure, killing all creatures in play in one turn costs ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif)![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) while Starstorm is only ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif). But Starstorm deals damage, which means there are quite a few things that can save your opponent’s day. Comparing the insect with mass removal is wrong, to some extent, anyway. The best use for this beating of a creature is to activate the morph trigger for two as long as the board permits that.

Imagine this scenario, which happens all the time in *Onslaught* Limited:

You are playing second and your opponent opens the game with a turn three morph. You play Bane of the Living face down on your turn. Your opponent attacks and proceeds to cast a second morph. You untap, play a fourth land and unmorph the insect paying ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif). The result? You swing for two, pass the turn with a 4/3 creature on the table while your opponent is left with nothing. Does this situation sound familiar to you? It surely should if you played a lot of *Invasion* Limited. There was a creature in *Planeshift* that had a similar effect: Flametongue Kavu.

In this case, the difference between Bane of the Living and Flametongue Kavu is that your opponent’s second morph would probably trade with your precious Kavu while, with Bane of the Living, your opponent will either have to spend another removal spell or a creature with at least power 3 to deal with the insect. In this scenario you are up two or three cards on your opponent, depending how he or she deals with the Bane of the Living. While having the potential for an even more impressive effect on the board, Bane of the Living obviously has the disadvantage of being less effective against creatures with a toughness of three and higher. To be truly amazing, you really want to keep the 4/3 creature in play, which won’t work if you want to kill most of the green beasts out there. The good news is that *Onslaught* Limited is a format filled with 2/2 creatures. That the set *Legions* contains only creatures and not a single spell certainly makes mass removal effects even more powerful.

Bane of the Living will have a much lower impact on Constructed though. Black is not really the color where extra mass removal is needed. Mutilate is very well-suited for that job. Also, as I stated earlier, the total cost for destroying all creatures goes up to ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif)![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) if you want to cast and use Bane of the Living right away. And for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) you can cast Visara. Against a black deck, your opponent shouldn’t have all that many creatures on the table anyway and in most cases, I would rather cast the gorgon legend then the insect, as having an active Visara against a creature-based deck is game over. Cleaning the whole table is a good thing, but a Constructed deck can easily recover from that, while recovering from a Terminate-effect each turn for the rest of the game has proven to be difficult. There is one huge thing that favors Bane of the Living when compared with Mutilate though: it is not a dead card when you play against a creatureless deck. Swinging for two a turn is totally acceptable if you disrupted your opponent’s game with cards like Duress and Mind Sludge. And you can always morph him. 

All this changes when it comes to Block Constructed. Black doesn’t have a reset button and Bane of the Living fits right in. There is a German team league that uses Block Constructed as a format and I got in a few games of *Onslaught* Block Constructed through that. There is a pretty good green-black or green-black-red beast deck going around that abuses Oversold Cemetery. This card, in combination with Bane of the Living and three other guys in your graveyard, totally owns any creature-based strategy. Krosan Tusker and Explosive Vegetation provide the necessairy mana boost to get going, but once you reach eight or nine lands in play to cast and activate Bane of the Living each turn, it is time for your opponent to either get rid of the Cemetery really fast or scoop up his cards. Right now, the dominant deck in Block is the cycling deck based around Astral Slide and Lightning Rift, which is also becoming more and more popular in Standard. Bane of the Living doesn’t really get you anywhere in that matchup. The creatures of choice are Exalted Angel and Jareth, Leonine Titan and killing those with the insect’s ability is not an easy task. Jareth is in those decks to win the mirror, in case you wondered what he is doing there.

To summarize, I predict that Bane of the Living won’t see all that much Constructed play, but you can be sure that it will appear in quite a few Prerelease-winning decks. It is almost impossible to play around it, but if you have two creatures with toughness 1 or 2 in play while your opponent has just one morph and two swamps along with two other lands avaible, I suggest not casting another creature on your turn. Otherwise the insect might morph and eat you and your puny creatures for lunch …

Enjoy the Prerelease and wish me luck in the Chicago Masters, where I will very likely fail to advance to round 2 for the millionth time.
