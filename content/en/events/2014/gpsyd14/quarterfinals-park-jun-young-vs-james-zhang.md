In the distant past, James Zhang was a force to be reckoned with in the Australian *Magic* scene, winning GP Melbourne in 2005, and running it back at GP Sydney in 2006. Park Jun Young on the other hand, arrived with a top 8 at GP Kitakyushu in 2007, before finally clocking up a second top 8 in Kuala Lumpur and a win in Minneapolis earlier this year. 

Park's White/Black deck developed early but Zhang come back over the top with a [Garruk, Apex Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garruk%2C+Apex+Predator) he was fortunate to receive fifth pick in pack two. Park rebuilt with a [Divine Favor](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divine+Favor) on a [Rotfeaster Maggot](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rotfeaster+Maggot), and Zhang was unable to muster anything to stop it. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpsyd14/T8_Zhang.jpg)

*James Zhang is hoping to win his second GP Sydney in a decade.* 

  

**Park 1 – Zhang 0** 

Zhang trumped Park's early stream of 1/1's with a turn three [Necrogen Scudder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrogen+Scudder), a [Carrion Crow](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Carrion+Crow), and a [Juggernaut](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Juggernaut). Park took down the [Juggernaut](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Juggernaut) with a [Necrobite](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrobite), and eventually dealt with the Scudder with a [Flesh to Dust](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flesh+to+Dust), but it was too little too late, and Zhang evened up the match at one apiece on the back of his early offence. 

**Park 1 – Zhang 1** 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpsyd14/T8_Park.jpg)

*Park Jun Young is looking to stop James Zhang repeating his victory from 2006.* 

  

It was Park's turn to dominate the board in the early game with a [Zof Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zof+Shade) and a [Shadowcloak Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shadowcloak+Vampire), forcing Zhang to trade half his team and a [Ranger's Guile](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ranger%27s+Guile) with the Shade. [Garruk, Apex Predator](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garruk%2C+Apex+Predator) eventually came down to pull Zhang back into the game, but Park finished the Planeswalker with his Vampire before it could get out of control. [Plummet](https://web.archive.org/web/20140824000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plummet) took down the Vampire, and Park was out of gas. Zhang deployed a series of small but effective beaters to close out the match. 

**James Zhang defeats Park Jun Young 2-1**
