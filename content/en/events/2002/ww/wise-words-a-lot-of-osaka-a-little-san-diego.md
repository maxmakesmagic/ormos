With my oft-noted 37 day writing hiatus coming at the heels of Pro Tour-San Diego, I have two PTs worth of little observations, notes and anecdotes to get out of my head, and as per usual, I'm going to use Wise Words as the medium for that task. Pretty informal style going on this week, but with so much to fit in to a limited amount of space, a man's gotta do what a man's gotta do, and that means no flowering up the facts. Enjoy.

*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/993.jpg)Osaka seemed to be missing a number of top PT personalities. Despite his high ranking in the Player of the Year race, Constructed powerhouse Tomi Walamies didn't make the trip, choosing instead to prioritize his scholarly pursuits for one PT stop. Also absent was Masters competitor Ben Klauser, also favoring school, not to mention San Diego winner Farid Meraghni, Gab Tsang, Scott Johns, Tommi Hovi and Sigurd Eskeland, whose fall from the gravy train has shockingly coincided with his concentrating on his job and his relationship. The big question for me, though, is where was Satoshi Nakamura? Japan's most identifiable player was nowhere to be found all weekend.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTTOK01/780.jpg)Speaking of notable Japanese players, Tsuyoshi Fujita, Japan's first and only Top 8 finisher, almost became the second, as he sat at or near the top of the standings for most of the tournament before eventually falling to 14th place. Fujita wasn't the only Japanese player to show up though, as Tsuyoshi Ikeda, a long time PT participant, sat right next to Fujita more than once at the Feature Match tables, coming 13th. Panzer Hunters, the Japanese team that finished second in the Masters a year ago, came within one game of taking it a step further before a great comeback by misters Budde and Baberowski.
*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/ce/2a/ce2a59958f7bad333a46a784ef3e8f4c90a28fb0dc7c830697715246dd421af9.jpg)Phoenix Foundation actually came very close to not playing together at Boston this year. If the team hadn't won the final of the Masters, they wouldn't have been ensured of a slot at next year's event, and as a result, they would have wanted to preserve their rating. Beating the Hunters means that their rating shouldn't be relevant in the foreseeable future, meaning that the team should show up in Boston intact and in full force. Kai's Sunday streak continued in Osaka, now moving to seventeen matches.
*   Patrick Mello's disappointing finish in Osaka ended an impressive streak in which he'd made money at every pro event for a year. Over that time, he's also made a couple of GP Top 8s, made Top 4 at PT-Barcelona and seen Masters action, making for a pretty good streak.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/921.jpg)One thing Pat did do right was to choose to play the black deck in Osaka. At one point, some two-thirds of the top twenty were playing black, and despite Ken Ho's win with a decidedly anti-black version of the second best deck in the tournament, green-blue, it was obvious to observers that this would be the deck to beat before the addition of *Judgment* to *Odyssey* block. I'll talk about the black deck more in next week's Wise Words.
*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/c1/c7/c1c7141f625aeb7546ec54888fad23f2b25a551bc06a97bb27f3e5d503bea914.jpg)Alex Shvartsman played black in Osaka, but his version appeared to be lacking, running outmoded cards like Carrion Wurm in the main. As far as the big creatures went, YMG seemed to be the only ones who'd figured out that Stalking Bloodsucker might be the best of the black fatties, with its evasion ability proving very important against blue-green decks, likely making it better than Laquatas's Champion.
*   Ken Ho's Pro Tour victory was the first for an American since Zvi Mowshowtz's win in Tokyo a year ago. The best quote I heard all weekend came from Mike Turian during our Feature Match. When someone commented that the Americans hadn't won an event in a long time, Mike said 'Yeah, most of them are playing spades right now'.

    There were some players worthy of a special mention in Osaka's Top 8. Jens Thoren's semifinal finish made him one of the few players with consecutive Top 4 finishes, firmly establishing him as one of the game's powerhouses. Olivier Ruel, the French clown prince of **Magic**, finally made his first Top 8, while Ho's win turned what looked to be a Colony disappointment into a major success. Osyp Lebedowicz has now captured two Top 32s and a Top 8 in his three PTs and may be teaming up with Eugene Harvey and Mike Turian for PT-Boston. For me, though, the story of the Top 8 was Rob Dougherty. Rob's last three Constructed PTs have seen him make two Top 8s and lose the final round of the Swiss to teammate Dave Humphreys to miss a third. Most impressive of all though is the fact that in all three he and some of his teammates played decks that he designed. Dougherty has quietly turned himself into one of the top ten PT players in the world.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/814.jpg)YMG was decidedly the best team in the tournament, but not because of the way they finished. After Day 1, the team checked the *Sideboard* every half hour until the Day 2 decklists were posted, allowing them to know what would be sideboarded against them, a huge advantage. They also compiled a list of what everyone in the top half of the field was playing part-way through Day 1, and the advantages were obvious. I'd be surprised if more PT teams didn't adopt this more mature, professional approach to the Pro game in the near future.
*   As for the true winners of this tournament, it was definitely the French, with four players in the Top 8 and another, Wilfried Ranque in tenth place. Coming on the heels of Farid Meraghni's victory in San Diego, the French, often the bridemaids in the past, have really begun to flourish this year, now right up there with the United States, Germany and the Netherlands. The French played mostly black or blue-green decks
*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/73/67/7367c00b11e76616aed0455ab16002b4fff2107723e7d99b7f9d9ac2dba4da42.jpg)Without Walamies in attendance, team Punisher still had a strong showing from Jens Thoren. Half the team played monoblack, though they didn't figure out the crucial Braids sideboarding strategy for the mirror match, while the other half of the team played a surprisingly aggressive white-green deck, with Tireless Tribe and Wild Mongrel providing a discard outlet for Roar of the Wurm, Arrogant Wurm, Basking Rootwalla and Strength of Isolation. It's pretty hard for the black deck to stop a 7/8 pro black guy surrounded by one-casting cost dorks that soak up Edicts and Innocent Bloods.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/magic_colony.jpg)Magic Colony showed up with Brian Kibler's blue-black-red Psychatog deck and the deck Ho won the PT with, but with Kibler's disappointing record on Day 2, I think the group would admit the experiment did not go as well as they'd hoped the first time around. Word is though that there's going to be a second go at the Colony with a newer, even stronger roster, and I've heard the names Clegg, Turian and Gab Tsang being thrown around. Could be a big problem for the rest of the world.
*   For Godzilla, the Euro-American team including Phoenix Foundation, Zvi Mowshowitz and Hampton Court Palace, Osaka was a real disappointment, placing no members in the Top 8, but I still believe we had as good a black deck as anyone in the tournament. The team split three ways, playing black, blue-green Breakthrough and blue-green Compulsion.
*   Kai Budde extended his PT non-winner streak to two in a row in Osaka. Fortunately, he was able to soften the blow with a twenty thousand dollar check from the Masters.
*   Jon Finkel's 1-3 performance in Osaka left his qualification for Worlds in doubt.
*   Randy Buehler and I have had our differences, so when I did the commentary with him, things were a little strained at first. Fortunately, the name Ryan Fuller came up, and that was all the common ground we needed.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/743.jpg)Brian Hegstad and other members of the former ABU have joined up with CCGhouse.com, while AlphaBetaUnlimited.com has now started sponsoring Ben Rubin, Dan Clegg and Terry Tsang.
*   I'm going to start writing for Hobby Japan in addition to the *Sideboard*. It's kind of weird knowing that the stuff I write here will be translated into Japanese, but at the same time, I'm pretty pleased about doing something that people feel is worth translating.

There were a couple of noteworthy things that happened in San Diego I wanted to mention here:

*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/770.jpg)Going into my Feature Match with Noah Boeken, I watched Ryan Fuller head over to Noah and say 'You know what to do', insinuating that the former European champ should take the same tack Ryan did in New York, trying to rile me up and force a mistake by saying outlandish things, and while I was ready to field the insults, I decided peace was worth a try. I told Noah 'I'm willing to just play **Magic** if you are', and Boeken shook my hand and we played a good match. It was a pleasant surprise that came for a potentially unpleasant situation.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/750.jpg)One other pleasant surprise in San Diego was Donnie Gallitz's Top 4 finish. Donnie, who'd been attending Pro Tours since Dallas, had finally resigned himself to a fate without a Top 8 finish, and to that end, he promised his other half, Rose, that San Diego would be his final appearance. Despite only knowing half the rare cards in *Odyssey*, Gallitz rode a combination of attitude, perseverance and good humor to the Top 8 draft before being stopped by Farid Meraghni in the semifinals. Donnie was so dominated by the Frenchman that he took it upon himself to set fire to Farid's Spellbane Centaur while still on stage.
     After the tournament, the speculation was that with the potential of the Masters calling and newfound success still fresh in his mind, Donnie might betray his promise and make a return to the Pro Tour wars, but if his performance in Osaka is any indication, the former Tongo Nation member has stayed true to his word. He was a no-show.
*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/WORLDS2000/images/123.jpg)On my last Rochester draft table, 'Baby Huey' Billy Jensen made what I feel was one of the best draft picks I've ever seen made. Billy is generally recognized as one of the top drafters in the world, so it should come as no surprise that it was he who made the pick that I didn't even have the foresight to think about his making.
     Billy was sitting in seat seven drafting blue-white, with me proving to be the only other white drafter on the table when he opened his second booster pack, the tenth of the draft. Billy opened a particularly strong pack for him, with the best white common, Mystic Zealot, as well as the best white rare creature, Wayward Angel. After the twenty second review period, despite the Angel being the better card, he chose the Zealot.

With Kibler drafting blue-black behind Joost, Baby Huey's logic was that to take the stronger card, Wayward Angel, would be an invitation to Kibler to foray into white, eventually weakening Billy's deck. With two Zealots already in his arsenal, Jensen knew he'd be able to stop the Angel, and while he'd be deprived of the use of the Angel himself, the 3/5 flying Zealot (eventually) was nothing to sneer at either. That meant that while the Angel was the better card, the Zealot would be the better card for him in this particular draft, as passing the double white Angel would protect him from having potential splashers taking his white cards in the third booster.
     The result of Billy's pick became obvious pretty quickly. I got the Angel sixth, and the two of us went a combined 5-1, with Billy's loss to me having nothing to do with the Angel, which he dealt with very easily. His knowledge of draft showed him that his deck would be better if he kept the white path clear going into the third pack, and he was right. Just a little more proof that you have to know more to be a good drafter than which cards are better than which: you also have to know how to control the draft.

That's about it for now. Thanks to everyone who sent me congratulatory emails this week, and save those emails on the black deck for now. Like I said, I'll take an up close look at the version I played in the next Wise Words, including suggestions for changes I'd make if I were to play it again. Obviously that won't leave you ready to play post-*Judgment* Block, but it should send you in the right direction. Have a good one.
