# Top 25

1.  Everything Changes

The Pro Tour always shakes things up, but this one absolutely routed the Top 25. There are a ton of little stories all over, so let's look at the whole thing.

Starting at the very top, we have a new No. 1! Player of the Year Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa needed exactly a 1st-place finish to win Player of the Year and take over the top spot, and he did exactly that, pushing Márcio Carvalho down to No. 2 for the first time in basically forever.

While PVDDR won the largest prize, the largest bump went to Samuel Pardee, who jumped from No. 21 to No. 4 with his 2nd-place finish. Pardee has been a perennial Top 25er, but this is easily his highest rating since we started keeping track of that kind of thing.

Four other players entered the rankings from somewhere off the screen thanks to strong or even Top 8 finishes. Sam Black is the highest of these, and he finished in the Semifinals, achieving his goal of reaching Platinum for the year. The strong performances of Donald Smith, Javier Dominguez, and Christian Calcano also jumped them into the ranks of the elite—though Smith and Calcano have been here before.

Just two other players on the rankings had positive jumps: quarterfinalist Seth Manfield and Draft Master Martin Jůza.

That means four players dropped off the rankings. Brian Bruan-Duin, last year's World Champion, didn't quite make the cut. Neither did Matt Nass, Kentaro Yamamoto, nor Oliver Tiu. They're not far off the pace, however, so don't expect any of them to be gone too long.

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Mono-Black Zombies](/en/events/2017/standard-ptq-2017-07-30/standard-ptq)

If you want to be beating Ramunap Red, the undead might just be your best bet.

Think about it—they get on the board just as fast as the red deck. Thanks to [Liliana's Mastery](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Liliana%27s+Mastery) and [Lord of the Accursed](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lord+of+the+Accursed), they get bigger than the red creatures. Zombies are often able to flood the board, making cards like [Ahn-Crop Crasher](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ahn-Crop+Crasher) and [Earthshaker Khenra](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Earthshaker+Khenra) less effective. And Kalitas's life gain can help the deck recover from any precarious total.

And then there's this:

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Grasp+of+Darkness)](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grasp+of+Darkness)

Ramunap Red's trump card for basically everything is [Hazoret the Fervent](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hazoret+the+Fervent)—but even Hazoret can't escape the Grasp.

We saw this trend emerge immediately as the Pro Tour shook out, with the [*Magic Online* Standard PTQ](/en/events/2017/standard-ptq-2017-07-30/standard-ptq) not just won by Zombies (congrats Ryuumei!), but dominated by black decks the same way the Pro Tour Top 8 was held by red—six of the Top 8 contained some number of [Grasp of Darkness](https://web.archive.org/web/20170802000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grasp+of+Darkness).

So say hello to darkness your old friend, sleeve up some Zombies, and crush that alliterative red deck.
