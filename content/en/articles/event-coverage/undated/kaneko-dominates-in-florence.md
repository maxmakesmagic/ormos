Masami Kaneko is the Grand Prix Florence 2007 Champion!

After two long days in beautiful Firenze, Italy, Masami Kaneko can claim yet another Grand Prix Trophy for Japan. Not the predicted blue-black Teachings deck that dominated the swiss, but the blue-green aggro deck was what it took to win this title. And thus the *Time Spiral* Block season ends. Join us next month for some Limited action with the new Lorwyn set!

The <a>Void</a> for two cleared the board, leaving his opponent with two Goblin tokens and a <a>Uktabi Drake</a> and <a>Summoner's Pact</a> in his hand.</p>&#10;      <p>The next attack brought Rasmus down to three, and Marco added <a>Thornweald Archer</a> to the board (he did not have a second green mana in play to pay for the upkeep of Drake or Pact). Another Consideration did not give Rasmus a <a>Void</a> or <a>Damnation</a>; only some rather awkward answers. He played <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a> (for one, only a <a>Swamp</a> in play, no Urborg) on <a>Thornweald Archer</a> and had to play <a>Pact of Negation</a> on <a>Uktabi Drake</a> to stay alive. The <a>Shadowmage Infiltrator</a> he could muster on his next turn could stop the Goblin tokens, but <a>Summoner's Pact</a> for <a>Uktabi Drake</a> still did the last two damage.</p>&#10;      <p>Marco 1 - Rasmus 0</p>&#10;      <p>Marco put in almost his entire sideboard, replacing chaff like <a>Thornweald Archer</a> (if your opponent does not run <a>Tarmogoyf</a>, it is not that good) with superior creatures like <a>Heartwood Storyteller</a> and <a>Greater Gargadon</a>. Rasmus added some cheap removal spells to his deck, instead of late game cards like <a>Detritivore</a>, <a>Haunting Hymn</a>, and <a>Pull from Eternity</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 2</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Rasmus played first. Once again, he had to take out some dice for his <a>Molten Slagheap</a>s and <a>Coalition Relic</a>. Marco suspended <a>Greater Gargadon</a> and made a 1/2 Kobold with the help of <a>Llanowar Reborn</a> plus <a>Kher Keep</a> and by stacking dices on top of each other. Rasmus, under not much pressure, started chaining <a>Mystical Teachings</a> into each other, and searched <a>Spell Burst</a> along the way.</p>&#10;      <p>Marco was stuck on three lands and any spell he would play would be countered by <a>Spell Burst</a> with buyback, so he just make Kobold tokens and was probably plotting a big turn ahead with the <a>Haze of Rage</a> in his hand and his suspended Gargadon. Rasmus would not fall for that, though, and cleared a couple Kobold tokens with <a>Damnation</a> before a storm could get out of hand (and also ensuring the 1-power token would not get him lower than 16 life). Marco, unable to draw lands, then had to start discarding, while Rasmus drew more cards with <a>Foresee</a> and <a>Careful Consideration</a>, charged up some lands, and frankly didn't do much either. But he didn't have to; he had his opponent <a>Spell Burst</a> locked and just had to watch out for a big <a>Haze of Rage</a> turn.</p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/a4/3a/a43a39875b2f1ce13309a0235d9fab600c5e376d784e28e2fb42179c9b3d604f.jpg">&#10;      <p>And eventually that time came. With the Gargadon at one counter along with four 0/1 Kobolds and five lands in play, Marco went for it. He started with <a>Summoner's Pact</a> and got <a>Uktabi Drake</a>. He played it and Rasmus let it resolve, knowing that countering it would only give more <a>Haze of Rage</a> copies. Marco then played <a>Haze of Rage</a> (+1/+0 times three) and it resolved as well. With his last two mana, Marco then cast another <a>Haze of Rage</a> and Rasmus went into the think tank. He did the math and eventually allowed all copies to resolve. Marco then declined to put in his Gargadon prior to his attack, smelling a <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a> from Rasmus (who had Urborg in play, and clearly wanted to do something else with his mana than <a>Spell Burst</a>), and just attacked with his four tokens and the Drake. Rasmus played Tendrils on the flyer, and Marco sacrificed it to his Gargadon so that Rasmus would not gain life. But that forced him to remove the last counter and play the Gargadon (it was countered by <a>Pact of Negation</a>). Now the coast was clear for Rasmus to flashback <a>Mystical Teachings</a> for another <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a>, which ensured a massive life swing. A <a>Slaughter Pact</a> also killed a token so that Rasmus was still at a healthy 13 life after the attack.</p>&#10;      <p>Marco had gone pretty much all-in on that turn, but the game was not over yet. He still had some goodies left in hand. While Rasmus started attacking with <a>Shadowmage Infiltrator</a>s, Marco mustered another big turn with <a>Summoner's Pact</a>, two <a>Uktabi Drake</a>s, and a <a>Haze of Rage</a>. Rasmus had to buyback and waste his <a>Spell Burst</a> (he couldn't buyback it the second time) on two <a>Haze of Rage</a> copies to stay alive at three life. Then Rasmus started making <a>Urza's Factory</a> tokens, and <a>Void</a>ed the remains of his opponent's hand, and it looked like Oots would win this game after all. But Marco topdecked <a>Stormbind</a>, and Rasmus did not have a counter in hand left and Big Oots was already too low on life to race <a>Stormbind</a> with <a>Urza's Factory</a>. While most players may then have just recklessly activated <a>Stormbind</a> twice to deal the final points of damage, Marco patiently kept his cool and did not do that. He was not slow-rolling; he was playing around Rasmus' only out: <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a> on an <a>Urza's Factory</a> token. Marco showed why he made Top 8 by always keeping two mana and a card in hand while he was activating <a>Stormbind</a> on Rasmus' life total, so that if Rasmus would play Tendrils on a token, he could respond by shooting it to negate the life gain. Two turns later, the game was over.</p>&#10;      <p>A well-deserved big round of applause ensured for Italy's hometown hero when Rasmus extended the hand. </p>&#10;      <p>Marco 2 - Rasmus 0</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="11">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Sept. 9: 7:45 p.m. - Semifinals: Marco Cammillucci vs. Andre Coimbra</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Frank Karsten</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Grinning Ignus" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f3/6a/f36ab737a1f7559a86c7cd4dfd79e5514b1056ab29e20cd25a494265c9201eb5.jpg">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>This match is a battle between two red-green decks, but apart from the colors they play, the decks are completely different. Andre Coimbra from Portugal is playing a red-green combo build of his own making, featuring <a>Grinning Ignus</a> as an engine for his <a>Wild Pair</a> and <a>Dragonstorm</a>s. Marco Cammillucci from Italy is playing a red-green aggro deck, featuring <a>Summoner's Pact</a> and <a>Haze of Rage</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Marco: You have a really strange deck.<br> Andre: You too!</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 1</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Andre got to play first and had a good draw. <a>Wall of Roots</a> and <a>Coalition Relic</a> accelerated him into a turn 4 <a>Wild Pair</a>. Marco, in the meantime, applied the beats with <a>Thornweald Archer</a> and <a>Kavu Predator</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Andre then cast <a>Radha, Heir to Keld</a> and searched <a>Grinning Ignus</a> out of his deck with <a>Wild Pair</a>. Ignus returned and was recast for <a>Avalanche Riders</a>, and then again for another <a>Avalanche Riders</a> to destroy all of Marco's red mana. The next turn all hell went loose. Another <a>Wild Pair</a> came down and Ignus flipped up and down to get two more <a>Avalanche Riders</a> and a couple backup Ignusses or Igni, or whatever the plural may be. The next turn Ignus plus <a>Wild Pair</a> searched for <a>Primal Forcemage</a> and then Andre could start getting <a>Bogardan Hellkite</a>s (as the Ignus was 5/5 due to Forcemage). Marco, unable to keep up with that madness, scooped up his cards.</p>&#10;      <p>Andre 1 - Marco 0</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 2</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/0f/ae/0fae77cabb3d861b9ef715c8479f6044c25fc1829ca5d0fc2a1e29af9d5322bd.jpg">&#10;      <p>Marco started off with a mulligan, while Andre kept his opening seven. Marco played <a>Llanowar Reborn</a>s on his first, second, and third turn, and used them to make a 5/4 <a>Uktabi Drake</a> on turn 3. He paid the echo and another Drake came down on turn 4 (with <a>Grove of the Burnwillows</a>), taking Andre down to nine life. Andre made multiple <a>Coalition Relic</a>s in the meantime, aiming for a turn 5 <a>Bogardan Hellkite</a>. But it would come too late. Marco made a third <a>Uktabi Drake</a>, and attacked for exactly enough damage. Wow…amazing. Just Drakes and graft counters is all it took for a turn 5 kill.</p>&#10;      <p>Andre 1 - Marco 1</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 3</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Andre started off with an opening hand containing two lands and two <a>Search for Tomorrow</a> amongst other cards. Together with <a>Grinning Ignus</a>, this gave him a turn 4 <a>Wild Pair</a>. Marco suspended a <a>Greater Gargadon</a> on turn 1, made <a>Kavu Predator</a> on turn 2, and <a>Uktabi Drake</a> on turn 3. He could not pay the echo with his two <a>Mountain</a>s and <a>Grove of the Burnwillows</a>, and instead played <a>Fatal Frenzy</a> on his <a>Kavu Predator</a> to deal lots of damage. Using <a>Wild Pair</a>, he searched a second Ignus (security against potential burn) and two <a>Avalanche Riders</a> out of his deck (in order to prevent Marco from potentially unsuspending <a>Greater Gargadon</a> and playing <a>Fatal Frenzy</a> on it next turn). Another <a>Avalanche Riders</a> took out a land, and then a <a>Dragonstorm</a> fetched multiple <a>Bogardan Hellkite</a>s. Marco got his Gargadon in play, but Andre chumped it with <a>Avalanche Riders</a> and attacked for the kill with his Dragons.</p>&#10;      <p>Andre 2 - Marco 1</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="12">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Sept. 9: 8:08 p.m. - Semifinals: Mido Kagawa vs. Masami Kaneko</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Sebastian Abresch</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Things are off for the semi finals, Mido Kagawa from Italy with his blue-black control machine vs. Masami Kaneko from Japan, playing a green-blue creation featuring many control beating spells like <a>Mystic Snake</a> and <a>Delay</a>. We will see which one will be ahead. </p>&#10;      <p>Both players shuffle excessively, Kaneko wins the die roll and chooses to play, naturally. Both keep their opening seven. </p>&#10;      <p>Kaneko starts fast with <a>Looter il-Kor</a> on his second turn, aiming to get some card quality advantage over Kagawa. Sweeper and Looter beat him to 15, suspended <a>Riftwing Cloudskate</a> announces some bouncing action. </p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Mystic Snake" src="https://i.ormos.uk/ce/8b/ce8b6d6f70bb5ad698fb0d417e1d3f7a0af3849f35f85a140942bc2dbdbd44f7.jpg">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Of course, Kagawa has the <a>Damnation</a> for Kaneko's two guys, who draws a card off <a>Horizon Canopy</a> and has to ship his turn without playing any beater. Kagawa casts his second Consideration and wants to counter Cloudskate with <a>Delay</a>, which is itself countered by a <a>Mystic Snake</a>. Grafted Riftwing beats for 3. </p>&#10;      <p>After drawing endless cards, Kagawa of course has a second <a>Damnation</a> for the guys, a hardcast <a>Riftwing Cloudskate</a>s from Kaneko meets <a>Cancel</a>. The control player then tries to win the game with <a>Triskelavus</a>, which gets destroyed by <a>Pongify</a>, but not without releasing one Triskelavite. Kaneko draws his next card, again finding no pressure, and goes for Game 2.</p>&#10;      <p>Kagawa 1 - 0 Kaneko</p>&#10;      <p>Kaneko keeps, Kagawa also. Again, there's the <a>Looter il-Kor</a> on turn two going for damage and card advantage, soon joined by a morph. Kagawa just plays <a>Coalition Relic</a> and passes. His Consideration goes uncountered. Mystery morphed creature and Looter beat Kagawa to 13, who wants to play a morph himself in the next turn. It meets <a>Mystic Snake</a> and shows itself as - surprise - <a>Vesuvan Shapeshifter</a>. A tapped out Kaneko can't counter <a>Slaughter Pact</a>, destroying his freshly cast Snake. </p>&#10;      <p>Pact is paid and turn is shipped without no play, an end of turn <a>Venser, Shaper Savant</a> from Kaneko meets himself and dies after bouncing Relic. Nevertheless, beats from morph continue and Kaneko casts a second one, leaving only <a>Island</a> open. <a>Void</a> clears the board off of zero casting cost critters, Kaneko recharges with double <a>Looter il-Kor</a>, which eat a <a>Damnation</a>. No play for Kaneko after that, bad sign…</p>&#10;      <p>Really? Kaneko casts Teferi end of turn, but it gets countered by <a>Spell Burst</a> - with buyback! Things are looking cruel for the Japanese. He tries a morphed <a>Brine Elemental</a>, which picks <a>Spell Burst</a> from Kagawa's hand, this time without buyback. The black-blue control mage casts <a>Triskelavus</a>, Kaneko kills it with <a>Psionic Blast</a>, leaving one lonely Triskelavite on the table. It beats Kaneko down to 15 with Kagawa stabilizing at 8 life now… but Masami is also able to force a morph with <a>Delay</a> countering <a>Spell Burst</a> No. 2. <a>Void</a> kills morph (Shapeshifter) and reveals <a>Mystic Snake</a> and <a>Psionic Blast</a>, which is quite OK for Kaneko. Kagawa's <a>Damnation</a> get's countered by <a>Mystic Snake</a> and that's where he scoops up his cards.</p>&#10;      <p>Kagawa 1 - 1 Kaneko</p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/9f/dc/9fdc8b73ae053316b6eb3dec0db88191704086378db6fd8e1e36de3d5eea253f.jpg">&#10;      <p>Again, both players keep their starting hands, Kaneko has the first real action by suspending <a>Riftwing Cloudskate</a> on turn two, adding Looter a turn later. <a>Strangling Soot</a> takes care of this, Kaneko tries it with a <a>Tarmogoyf</a>, being 3/4 and then 4/5 off graft. <a>Void</a> meets <a>Delay</a>, Cloudskate bounces Kagawa's storage land. This leaves Kagawa without the possibility to cast <a>Damnation</a> (only one black mana), if he doesn't draw Urborg. Indeed he does not and takes heavy beats to 3 life. On his last possible turn, his <a>Damnation</a> meets <a>Mystic Snake</a> - and that's enough for Masami Kaneko to advance to the finals of Grand Prix Florence 2007!</p>&#10;      <p>Kagawa 1 - 2 Kaneko</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="13">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Sept. 9: 9:03 p.m. - Finals: Andre Coimbra vs. Masami Kaneko</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Frank Karsten</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/a6/f1/a6f12d14c4e4f52d2c82a14278f3db86e351cb22819985c4cc1d87083ae9c753.jpg">&#10;            <i>Masami Kaneko</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>And we are down to just two players here in sunny Firenze: Andre Coimbra from Portugal (playing a red/green combo deck with <a>Grinning Ignus</a> to power out <a>Dragonstorm</a> and <a>Wild Pair</a>) and Masami Kaneko from Japan (playing a blue/green aggro deck with all the usual suspects like <a>Tarmogoyf</a> and <a>Mystic Snake</a>).
