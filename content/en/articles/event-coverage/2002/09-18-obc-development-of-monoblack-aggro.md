Much has been written on OBC decks. Curiously, one of the more interesting and certainly powerful archetypes has not been well covered. When 'OBC monoblack' is uttered, one immediately has visions of Mind Sludges and Mutilates. However, there is an altogether different archetype in the format - monoblack aggro. My first experience with this archetype came in early August.

When testing for Worlds, it soon became apparent that something like 90% of the field was going to play monoblack control or a blue-green variant. I was searching for a "silver bullet" - a deck that might defeat both of these archetypes. An aggressive monoblack deck could potentially be that solution.

It is apparent that Braids is one of the more powerful weapons against monoblack control. The Mesmeric Fiend/Rancid Earth/Braids draw is exactly what a monoblack deck itself hopes for after sideboarding, and that draw virtually guarantees victory. There is another incredibly powerful weapon in the matchup - Ichorid. Monoblack control has a very difficult time dealing with haste creatures, as all of its removal spells are sorceries. Although Ichorid is not a viable sideboard option in the mirror for this creature-light archetype, it certainly works when you combine it with a variety of fast creatures.

The second part of the puzzle would be defeating blue-green decks. Faceless Butcher is a powerful card in the matchup, but it is not enough. In comes Slithery Stalker - as there are very few blue creatures in u-g builds, it is practically as good as a Butcher, and it costs less mana. In effect, the deck is shaping up to be pre-sideboarded against both of its most likely match-ups. Here is what the initial version looked like:

4 Mesmeric Fiend  
 4 Nantuko Shade  
 2 Crypt Creeper  
 4 Slithery Stalker  
 2 Organ Grinder  
 4 Faceless Butcher  
 4 Braids, Cabal Minion  
 3 Ichorid  
 1 Laquatus's Champion

4 Chainer's Edict  
 4 Rancid Earth

24 Swamp

Laquatus's Champion quickly proved too slow for the deck and was replaced by a third Crypt Creeper or Organ Grinder. Organ Grinder is certainly the "techiest" card of this deck, but I feel it definitely belongs. After an initial creature rush, Grinder can help finish off an opponent who was able to stop your attackers, and it can swing for three in the early game, trading with better creatures or dealing fast damage.

In testing, the deck was performing well vs. monoblack, but having trouble with some of the blue-green builds - especially the ones that could reach five mana to cast Grizzly Fate. Justin Gary felt that we should attack their ability to obtain threshold in addition to attacking their mana base. His build was significantly more aggressive. Although I did not keep precise notes, it looked approximately like this:

4 Zombie Cannibal  
 4 Crypt Creeper  
 4 Nantuko Shade  
 4 Mesmeric Fiend  
 3 Slithery Stalker  
 3 Faceless Butcher  
 4 Braids, Cabal Minion  
 2 Ichorid

4 Chainer's Edict  
 4 Rancid Earth

24 Swamp

Significantly faster, this deck could overrun the slower blue-green versions while denying them threshold and removing key cards from their graveyard whenever Zombie Cannibal could come in unblocked.

As we tested more and our versions of the prime decks improved, the win percentage of monoblack aggro declined. It could certainly auto-win a number of games on the strength of the Rancid Earth/Braids combo, or by simply overrunning an opponent. However, it simply did not feature cards as powerful as the other archetypes - which meant that an opponent who managed to survive the initial onslaught could almost always make a comeback and win the game. In the end, none of the team played this deck at Worlds. Some of us used a blue-green QuietFate variant, others an aggressive u-g Standstill (most notably Dave Humphreys who managed to sweep the day 6-0 with it) and others yet monoblack control. I still had a soft spot for this deck however, and was hoping to see something similar to it appear on the tournament scene. I was not disappointed.

Two weeks after Worlds, monoblack aggro finally emerged as a viable archetype at Grand Prix-London. Designed by Johnny Chapman and incomprehensively named "Pirates", the British version of monoblack aggro won the GP Trial on Thursday and managed one Top 8 finish in hands of Craig Stevenson. The archetype was briefly covered [here](/en/events/2002/gplon02/deck-analysis-pirates) by Mark Wraith.

And so the most recent version of the deck is very similar (though admittedly much better tuned) to what was tested for Worlds. Millikin is very interesting in the deck - although you hate casting a creature that cannot attack in such a deck, it does work miracles with Ichorid in addition to allowing for a third turn Braids. Zombie Infestation is another interesting addition to the deck. As you never actually need more than four mana, it converts additional lands into Zombies, and it gives nightmares to monoblack, which has no instant way of removing the tokens, thus ensuring that every two cards pitched will deal at least two points of damage.

Despite the obvious advantages of Zombie Infestation, I would still much rather have four Rancid Earths maindeck. The synergy with Braids is tremendous, allowing the deck to auto-win 10-20% of the time on the strength of a draw that features both cards. Even drawn by itself, it is disruptive enough in the format where most decks either need a ton of mana or play a very precarious mana base. Williams's solution was to sideboard three Rancid Earths. Mine would have been to cut one Millikin and three Infestations for them.

Screams of the Damned may seem to make little sense in a deck with so many one toughness creatures, but it does the crowd control job against Squirrel Nest, Bearscape, or Grizzly Fate-powered decks and can also act as a finisher once an opponent is at a low life total. 

Finally, Buried Alive is a very interesting sideboard choice against monoblack. You can go get several Ichorids and, unless an opponent has enough mana and time to cast Haunting Echoes, win the game off of them alone. 

With several weeks remaining in the OBC season, monoblack aggro should remain a viable archetype and should certainly be a part of your testing gauntlet, even if you choose to play another deck at your PTQ.
