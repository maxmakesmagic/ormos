*When a new set comes out, R&D will often bring decks to events to show off the potential of the new cards. These aren't our best FFL decks per se, but instead a glimpse into what the set has to offer. Often these decks are intentionally un-streamlined, both to keep opponents guessing and to make the deck more fun to play for 10 straight hours!*

*Aaron Forsythe played in the Champion Challenge at the Houston, TX Prerelease:*

This deck left players with two impressions: one, [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20091009000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) was as sick as they imagined; and two, Allies were way sicker than they imagined. I was repeatedly producing [Oran-Rief Survivalist](https://web.archive.org/web/20091009000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Survivalist)s that could trade with [Woolly Thoctar](https://web.archive.org/web/20091009000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Woolly+Thoctar)s and [Umara Raptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20091009000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Umara+Raptor)s that could attack past [Baneslayer Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20091009000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baneslayer+Angel)s, often while drawing tons of cards, making tons of Wolves, or wiping out people's hands. And [Eldrazi Monument](https://web.archive.org/web/20091009000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eldrazi+Monument) was just the haymaker I hoped it would be, winning several otherwise unwinnable games.
