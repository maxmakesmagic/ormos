We've featured recent **Magic** advertising materials here on **magicthegathering.com** a number of times, including both [video](/en/articles/arcana/2007/11-26-playmagic-com-videos) and [images](/en/articles/askwizards/2006/04-03-ask-wizards-april-2006#0412). But **Magic** also had some memorable advertising prior to the existence of the site that we've never featured systematically, much of which hasn't even been available online... until now. These fun ads from **Magic**'s past are available here and uploaded to YouTube for your viewing (and linking) pleasure. Enjoy!

To accompany the release of *Portal* in 1997, Wizards produced a series of "lifestyle" ads representing **Magic** as—well, as exactly what it is: a unique and exciting mental competition. Note that because these two ads were intended to drive potential new players toward the new starter-level **Magic** product, all of the cards and packaging featured in them comes from *Portal*.

### Competition

In 1998 came a new series of ads that was less concerned with broad appeal than with making **Magic** look like fun, particularly for established gamers. It was our ad agency at the time that suggested framing these ads as "behind the scenes" looks at an over-the-top, fantasy version of **Magic** R&D. The ad agency had free reign to select the cards that would be featured in the ads based on their visual appeal, but they did choose cards—such as Rhox, Orgg, and Kezzerdrix—that have at least niche popularity with established **Magic** players. Once again, these ads feature the current **Magic** starter product at the time they were made—in this case, *Portal Second Age*.

### Bob from Accounting

Fun fact: In the "Bob from Accounting" ad—previously featured [here](/en/articles/arcana/2004/12-02-orgg-vs-bob-from-accounting-2) as a slightly less convenient 6.7 MB zipped file—the player on the right in the test game is played by Brian Selden, the 1998 **Magic** World Champion.

Several of these ads were quite popular among **Magic** players at the time, and two of the characters were enduring enough to warrant their own cards in the never-completed *Unglued 2*. By the time *Unhinged* was released, it had been so long since these ads aired that the jokes no longer seemed timely enough, and the cards were shelved. Art, however, had already been commissioned, serving as a sort of monument to these iconic (and, in the case of Poodle Boy, downright creepy) characters:
