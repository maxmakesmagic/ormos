I guess he just likes to play card games with the biggest stack in front of him. He was paired with TBS in the first round and you just knew that the winner of the match would be going the distance. Ben's aggro blue-black could not handle the tutoring power of the 260-plus card stack. </p>&#10;      <p>Eric did emerge from the tournament with an invite but he would be playing short-stacked since he could not scrape together a third copy of the deck. At 4:30 a.m., he was just far too bleary to possibly make the translation from Standard to Extended and instead was handed a deck by one of his many friends in attendance - Balancing Tings. Patrick played - sit down now, I don't want to surprise anyone - a mono-red deck that was designed to punish the mana-hungry control decks from Champs. It plays more like a black-blue aggro list than a traditional affinity build and it is more resilient against artifact hate that other builds. </p>&#10;      <p>Gabe led off with <a>Duress</a> and laughed at the <a>Cabal Therapy</a> in Eugene's hand. Everyone was looking for the necessary five wins in order to advance, although technically speaking they needed 15 points so it was possible to go undefeated with four wins and three draws. Frank Karsten and Kai Ikeda presumably ID'd in the last round to achieve their undefeated 15-point records.</p>&#10;      <p>Gabriel Nassif, Masashi Oiso, Adam Chambers, Craig Krempels, Carlos Romao, Tomohiro Kaji, Jose Barbero, Murray Evans, Pierre Canali, Paul Rietzl, and Dave Williams were just a few of the former Pro Tour winners, multiple Pro Tour Top 8ers, Player Card personalities, National Champions, World Champions and WSOP finalists who were scrabbling for their Day Two lives in Round 8 against less illustrious -- but no doubt up-and-coming -- competition. Nassif, Oiso, Chambers, Krempels, Romao, Kaji, Barbero, and Williams were all successful in their efforts. </p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Orim's Chant" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Orim%27s%20Chant&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>One of the more interesting bubble matches was between Akira Asahara and Neil Reeves. Asahara was playing Balancing Tings as designed by Masashi Oiso. Reeves had beaten Oiso earlier in day with his <a></a><a>Dredge</a>-a-Tog list, largely on a read of an Oiso bluff. Oiso had tried to <a></a><a>Orim's Chant</a> Reeves in order to bait a counterspell. Reeves felt that Oiso had &quot;the stone cold nothing&quot; and he saved his hard counter for later in the match.</p>&#10;      <p>It turned out that Asahara and Reeves were in exactly this scenario at the beginning of this season in Columbus, when Asahara pulled an Akroma out of his Goblin deck to send Neil home. That was not to be the case this time as Reeves took a grueling Game 1 from the Tings player. In the second game he put Asahara on the same bluff that he read from Oiso earlier in the day and let the Japanese player cast <a></a><a>Orim's Chant</a>. Asahara did not have the combo but he did <a>Burning Wish</a> for <a>Cranial Extraction</a> and took out both of Neil's Togs that were sitting in his hand. </p>&#10;      <p>No matter for Neil. He could still win by beating down with a <a>Genesis</a>. It did not come down to that, though. A defensive <a>Upheaval</a> and a well-timed <a>Mana Short</a> meant that Asahara did not have enough time to win the second game. Neil got his revenge from Columbus 1-0. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="11"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Friday, October 28: 10:03 pm - Not On The Bubble But Worthy of Mention…</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Ted Knutson</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Brain Freeze" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Brain%20Freeze&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>One play in Round 8 that took place outside the feature match pit really deserved mention as one of the cooler plays of the day. Chris McDaniel (otherwise known as Star Wars Kid) was battling Ryo Ogura at table 12. The Desire gave him two lands, <a>Fact or Fiction</a>, <a>Gifts Ungiven</a>, and <a>Early Harvest</a>. The card-drawing spells finally delivered a needed <a>Heartbeat of Spring</a>, which let Chris cast the <a>Early Harvest</a> and then a <a>Cunning Wish</a> for <a>Brain Freeze</a>, flipping 33 cards into Ogura's graveyard. McDaniel had another Freeze in hand, but when he flipped through Ogura's graveyard, he noticed an unexpected complication to his plan in <a>Krosan Reclamation</a>.
