*"More than one mage was driven insane by the sound of the [Millstone](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Millstone) relentlessly grinding away."*

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/images/daily/cardart_ANT_Millstone.jpg)

[Millstone](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=1012) | Art by [Kaja Foglio](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&artist=%5B%22Kaja+Foglio%22%5D)

Recently, Michael Majors took home the trophy at [Grand Prix San Diego 2015](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-san-diego-2015) with a deck that relied on milling an opponent to death—that is, removing all the cards from an opponent's library. Majors's weapon of choice? [Sphinx's Tutelage](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Tutelage), a powerful little uncommon enchantment.

By running a flurry of draw spells and 27(!) lands, the deck proved surprisingly consistent, digging for a copy of [Sphinx's Tutelage](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Tutelage) and ruthlessly grinding opponents out—sometimes in dramatically quick fashion.

In *Magic*'s history, mill decks have always occupied a special place in players' minds. Most players either love them (yours truly) or hate them (people who like "having fun" and "casting their spells"). Today, we take a look at a few mill decks from the past, in homage of Majors's victory.

# ORIGINAL MILLSTONE

At the very first Pro Tour in 1996, not one by *two* decks packing the original [Millstone](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Millstone) made it to the Top 8, with [Michael Loconto taking home the top prize](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Events.aspx?x=mtgevent/ptny96/results)!

# DROWNYARD: THE NEW MILLSTONE

What's better than a two-mana win condition? How about a zero-mana win condition? At Pro Tour *Gatecrash* in 2013, [Ben Stark made Top 8](/en/articles/event-coverage/2013/02-16-top-8-decks-of-pro-tour-gatecrash) with an Esper Control deck that killed people with [Nephalia Drownyard](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Drownyard)! . . . Okay, sure, sometimes he killed them the boring way by attacking with [Restoration Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Restoration+Angel), [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage), or even [Augur of Bolas](https://web.archive.org/web/20150819000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Augur+of+Bolas), but most of the time Stark controlled the board and won by "killing people with lands."
