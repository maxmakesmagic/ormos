### 

This is not a story about power gaming. As you all know, the Invitational is a place for good sportsmanship, not to mention that **Magic** Online makes rules lawyering kind of difficult.

So, about me and the Invitational. My first experience with this fine tournament was when I read through *Duelist* back in the Stone Age. I think it was 1996. There were pictures of these 16 guys, all dedicated pros like Leon Lindbäck and Thomas Andersson and some text about how they would soon fight it out for no prize money. I was troubled by the fact that a magazine I bought while trying to become a better player would waste two pages on something like this. Later I learned, though, about the prestige of this event. The idea of playing for no money was simply less thrilling back when Pro Tour was just starting to develop.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/mi02/985.jpg)

The next six years were quite uneventful for me, so I’ll skip them. Somewhere along the way I became a better player, learning from my mistakes. As I started getting mail concerning my tournament reports, and saw a deck referred to as Walamies.dec, I realized that I might have enough name recognition to qualify for Invitational through the vote. Why didn’t I write anything for five months before the vote, then? This was partly due to genuine laziness, and partly because I failed to accomplish much after Nationals. It would feel stupid to write just to remind people of my existence. We writer people have these moral dilemmas about whether it is justified to do something.

However, be it right or not, I will show you an excerpt from a letter I once got:

*u r one great guy  
 and the rest of your tourney results are good  
 u look kinda cute from your photos  
 hehe  
 with your hat*

I have to admit it was fairly cool to notice that I finished 6th in the vote, despite my absence. I have done quite poorly in votes before, for example finishing last when running for student council in junior high. I was very excited when I found out that Zvi had job issues and Kai had school commitments (yeah right). I managed to get some time off school and downloaded the Wizards **Magic** Online server in order to do some testing. It turned out, though, that playing against Wizards employees might not be the ultimate test of a deck. Not too many Invitationalists were usually online on the server, so the most we managed to do was 4 man drafts. I did get some test games in with the Standard Bird deck, though. It was smashing both Wizards and Beasts.

Off I went. At the Copenhagen airport, where I was switching planes, I ran into a nice 60-year-old lady. She asked me what gate the Seattle flight left from, and I told her that it is the one that says “Seattle”. Out of gratitude, she decided to let me in on a little secret: “Europe is terrible. Except Denmark. In Denmark, people are nice and things are right.” It is information gathering like this that makes traveling around the world so insightful.

After arriving in Seattle, Alan Comer was there to pick me up. We went to the dinner where other Invitationalists were waiting for me. Rosewater immediately informed me that even though they were paying for this, I could only order one alcoholic drink due to “certain events” in previous years. Silently cursing Matt Viennau in my mind, I proceeded to chill with the other pros (always wanted to say that).

So, on to the tournament. I had been a bit nervous about playing in this tournament, but all that excitement turned into boredom as I heard the pairings for the first round. Misters Price and Pikula received quite a beating from Jens and me. David spent all his mana to activate Machinist (that dealt like 27 damage), and Chris was simply manascrewed, so soon we had total control of the board.

I would like to take a moment to congratulate Chris on his eventual finish.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/15/27/152727be11cc3c95a4adc390f271bf545aa322d71c558c752bfd464ceabcbd3e.jpg)

The second round was a bit tougher. It seemed that Jens and Chris had better decks for the matchups, but after gaining approximately 50 life points with my Righteous Cause, we pulled it out.

Things went worse with Chris. Jens decided that it was a good idea to cast those stupid black multiplayer cards, so after a Buzzard and Syphon Mind, we were seriously down on cards. When I got attacked for lethal damage, I forgot to concede. This would have allowed Chris to block with his guys, totally slaughtering Dave’s team. This was made even more humiliating by the fact that I was the first to point out this trick to other competitors, in round one.

About the format: The multiplayer cards seem stupid. I feel like we won round one with Syphon Mind, round two with the Cause and lost round three to Syphon Mind. Other than that, it was refreshing to play multiplayer, of all things, in a big event.

Next up was Extended. The night before leaving for Seattle, Nicolai Herzog told me good things on IRC about the b-g deck he played at Worlds. I got a decklist, and copied it almost card for card.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/mi02/886.jpg)

Round four was versus Olivier Ruel. How he escapes from the mental institution to travel to events troubles me to no end. He was playing Battle of Wits, and I had no idea how this matchup (or any else, for that matter) was supposed to go. Hence I just decided to make him discard all of his cards in game one and Braids lock him in the next one, as he was naturally mana screwed twice.

Next one was Kibler and his g-r beats splashing for black removal. My fondest memory of this person is when I beat him at 2001 Pro Tour-Los Angeles with Armadillo Cloak and then got him to sign it. He was so frustrated about the loss that he didn’t notice when I stepped in line after a woman getting her Riths signed. 

In game one, I totally humiliated him with Therapy-Fiend, Deed and multiple Centaurs. He decided to beat me up the next two games though. I could go on a rant about how I lost my best matchup, but that is kind of old.

The last round of the day was versus my favorite Russian. Alex Shvartsman is with blue-green. I would complain more about others playing u-g, had I actually created or even tuned the deck I was running. Game one, I Deed and Echoes his stuff, but he beat me with a lone guy as I ran out of resources. I found it appropriate to take games two and three with assorted total control elements.

Thoughts on the format: It was interesting to see u-g threshold do better than madness. If someone is planning to run b-g, I suggest Jens’s version. It has a better late game due to less dead draws of discard spells, and not running Wishes enables it to have a sideboard. I would try some Therapies in it, main or sideboard, considering the 7 flashback guys and 3 Squirrel Nests. Therapy is, after all, the new must-play card.

Ah, finally some sleep. Jens snores like an old man, I have heard, but I always sleep so tight that pretty much nothing aside from an alarm clock wakes me up. I suppose we would be the perfect couple.

The next morning during breakfast, everyone is trying to find out others’ opinions about the auction decks, and form consensus about how much to bid. I trade notes with some guys, and Jon has some trouble understanding them.

*Is “sucks” worse than “h8” or “awful”?*

The bidding itself was relatively cool. Da Hump got the Fact-Flametongue deck way too cheap as others were still figuring out how much to bid. Alex, on the other hand, went all-out on one of the worst decks. He will probably provide us with some reasoning in his own report. I didn’t do so hot either, going to 5 cards with a deck that has expensive guys. I did this because I didn’t recognize the 4 and 5 mana Gustcloaks by name (I still don’t). The deck had some serious mising capabilities though, as it could drop fast guys and Anthem when many others are starting at 12 or something life.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/21/f1/21f13577e08dfabe36fd570de5c856a0b3774e0041f5772454e5377bcc26861c.jpg)

Round seven, I was paired against Gary Wise. He got the Looter deck at the same price than I did, 5 cards 20 life. Due to eight Looters, his deck was more stable. I immediately realized this, and decided to draw the perfect cards. If you want to see some truly humorous topdecking, watch my game one versus him. Game two is cool also, although there were some turns when I didn’t get the optimal card. Gary was upset with the result and decided to put me in a headlock.

Next round I played Jon Finkel with Battle of Wits. I was originally going to bash all my opponents, but I have to give Jon props for playing Battle in every format possible. Our games were of the uninteresting kind, as I got the screw game one and he drew practically nothing going the next two games. 

The thing with playing totally cosmic decks versus each other is that the games are rarely even. One could blame the Block and Standard formats for being boring, since they pit the same decks versus each other all the time, but the games do get fairly intense at times. I am not saying that people should stop playing Highlander Thrull versus T1 Poison; I am merely suggesting that the enemies of “normal” formats should maybe look at the subject from a different angle.

Round nine was against Carlos, who for some reason was not playing Psychatogs. Coming into this tournament, my main goal was to get paired against two South Americans. His Shivan Zombies were quite good against me, as I drew practically no Lavamancers (whine). He played them turn 2 every game, so I only won the one where I drew my lone Wrath.

Carlos was quite nice, though. Congratulations to him for winning Worlds.

For those keeping score: I am at the moment 6-3, going 2-1 in every format.

Once again, some thoughts about the format. The auction process looked skill intensive, and the guys with past experience seemed to get better deals. I already expressed my views about putting two decks from totally different worlds against each other.

The next format was Rochester Draft. I start going green and Nitter on my left in seat one was less than pleased. Hump on my right decided to play along and took two Tuskers from me. Considering the fact that I took Executioner over Swat, this draft might not be the best learning experience. The draft went okay for me, and I figured I’d go 2-1.

The first victim was Ishida. His monowhite soldier deck splashing blue for Crown of Ascension looked like it was in the wrong format. Aware of this, Itaru went on a frantic trashtalking spree in an attempt to get me off my game. That was of no use, though, as my Dirge of Dread did him in. The highlight of game two was me playing Dirge at the worst possible moment, as it was the only turn during which he could afford to cast Tethers, but he failed to have the card in hand.

Round eleven was versus Nitter. Looking at these games from **Magic** Online, one does not get an accurate representation of events that went on. Eivind played extremely slowly when he was losing games one and three, since he was whining and cursing at the computer in the other room. Brian Davis came to this room during game three since he just couldn’t stand Nitter’s antics anymore. I kept drawing my lone Wellwisher versus his g-w deck, hence the negative attitude. The fact that he drew many lands didn’t speed things up. I spent quite a lot of time during this match walking around and watching others playing.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/7e/8c/7e8c3232208ff831970530dcc5354f223701975890c0faef7d45cac350835d3f.jpg"><br><i> Also good.</i></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I was feeling kind of comfortable at 8-3, but then Gabriel decided to totally demolish me. The last time I was humiliated this badly by a French person was when I actually tried to communicate with a waitress in Paris using the English language. How did I dare? Dragon Roost in game one and Starstorm in the next taught me to behave.

This format was probably the most skill demanding, since Rochester has long been praised by pros and so on. However, what is up with all the rares in this format? I have a theory: the majority of the target market does not play Limited, but likes big rare guys. I know I did when I started out. Morph is a nice mechanic, although a bit random at times. The best thing about *Onslaught* Limited in my opinion is that having both morph and cycling reduces chance of not being able to cast spells. If the next two sets have as much morph and cycling, but less power rares, this might be quite a fine Limited format by end of the season.

I’ll talk a bit about the coverage of this event. I felt that *Sideboard* did quite a good job at capturing the feeling of the Invitational, and the pictures were funnier than PT ones. Only negative thing that comes to mind is them only having one Feature Match per round. Considering that they fly 16 people from around the world, I figured that they would want to report more about their matches. And concerning coverage elsewhere on the net… let’s just say that rumors regarding my neckless-ness are greatly exaggerated. 

I felt that **Magic** Online was a great forum for this kind of an event, and would not be surprised if the next Invitational is played in an exotic location but with computers. This is considering that it does not bring with it too many logistical problems, though. The ability to archive each game is a huge improvement over previous years. People have different ways of playing **Magic**, and the intuition that we have gotten from experience dominates our playing style more than purely rational thinking. It is claimed that the Japanese play in a way different from others, and now we have 30+ games of empirical evidence to look at. Go ahead and check Itaru’s games. I know I will.

So, back to the tournament. As I knew who my last two opponents were, I tried to scout their decks. That wasn’t too useful though, as we had already submitted deck lists Saturday morning.

Round 13 was versus Diego. There is probably something nice that can be said about his u-w control. All-stars like Ancestral Tribute combined with the powerful permission of Discombobulate do have the tendency to leave an opponent speechless. I ran over Diego in game one since he didn’t get double white, and the next one I won because he did not know about the Oppositions in my deck.

The next to last round was against Brian Davis. He and his u-g deck didn’t do much attacking this match, and Brian is more known as a big-time blocker. This matchup was quite easy in OBC, and me having Oppositions does not make it any worse.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fc/d3/fcd303e216c832a96e5657bf356203e657af86193465daa8f87aac71517049b4.jpg)

At this point I was 10-4, and with some calculations I noticed that I am already in. I had one more match to go, against Humpherys sporting Tog. Dave, mostly known for his wild youth, has recently slowed down a bit. It shows in his play style. In game one, I forget an Analysis in my graveyard and lose because of that. I then lose game three to unnecessarily holding a land in my hand. Not bad.

The new Standard seems nice. The fact that basic u-g did badly while funky decks went 2-1 or 3-0 is obviously refreshing. About the Bird deck I played: I would cut a Sacrament and something to fit 2 Logics main. The Wraths in the board were versus Sparksmith. Another possible, but more limited, answer is Stupefying Touch. You could also go with Circle of Protection: Red and hose the entire deck, I suppose. A second cycling land would have been nice, since they make Wayfarers better. I contemplated playing Exalted Angel main or sideboard, but decided against it. Glory was nice in the board, but I still think that Opposition is the more versatile card to play main.

Jens won his last round, so I was playing him in the finals. I immediately called Justin Gary and asked him to analyze this situation, and he promptly said: “It was Punisher against the world, and the world didn’t stand a chance”. 

About Jens… like most players, I have some ideas about how to make this game a better one. None of them include having a picture of Jens on a card, though.

We discuss the matchups, and notice that all of them are fairly even. The first one was Extended. Game one, I didn’t draw much, so he just beat me up. Game two was the one where I pulled off some more highlights: I cast Plague on Nightmares, so his Fiend gets to take away my Champion for good when he was at low life. Then I proceeded to attack with one Centaur too many, so he could win by Deeding away my Birds and attacking.

This was not optimal. I supposed I would have to get my act together a bit more if I wanted my own card. The second matchup went a lot better, mostly due to his inability to draw a single Wrath in twenty-something cards. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d6/3e/d63e4a554c1a91dd0cda0cde4fb759407dcc3cc5b7302f1a2c607f1bde5cc294.jpg)

The third match was pure slaughter. Jens kept putting down Visara to finish me off. Well, it’s not the first time I’ve lost to a topdecked five-power flier in the finals while getting opponent down to one life.

So, it was over. I felt a bit weird at this point. Obviously it rarely feels good to end the tournament with a loss. On the other hand, I can’t expect to win an event that I do only a few hours of preparation for. Jens actually did more preparation this time, since he had been playing his online Extended deck quite a lot in former Standard.

I apologize for being able to do only a little gunslinging, but Rosewater took me on a tour of Wizards after the finals. I arrived quite late on Thursday and hence missed it then.

After some slinging, we had a party at Rosewaters’. It was quite psychedelic, and hard to describe. Hopefully someone will manage to recap the events there.

I think that’s enough sarcasm for one article. I will now tell you a bit about my fall from grace, even though it is not Invitational related. As you may or may not know, I am unqualified for Pro Tour-Chicago. This is due to going 1-4 in San Diego, skipping Osaka, going 2-4 in Nice, Top 48 at Worlds, 3-3 at Boston and 4-3 at Houston. Things are not helped by the fact that I have not played a GP in 18 months. Why did I do so awful outside Masters? There are a number of reasons, but to make a long story short: I just don’t playtest enough anymore. There are legendary stories about people who can just hibernate for a year and then do well in a PT, like Svend Geertsen. I am most definitely not one of them. The only time I have done well without practice in the format was with the Bird deck at Worlds. Normally, I need countless hours of testing to understand the format. The reasons for me not testing are numerous, but school is not the least important of them. It just felt easier to go and try to mise in the Masters, than to actually work for an event.

So, I got what I deserved. My success a year ago was preceded by one and a half years of serious playtesting, so it is only fitting that I scrub out due to laziness.
