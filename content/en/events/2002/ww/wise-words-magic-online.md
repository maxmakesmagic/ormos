Hello. My Name is Gary, and I'm a **Magic** Online-oholic. This is my first confession.

It all started around seven and a half years ago, when a friend introduced me to a card game called **Magic: the Gathering**. It was simple to learn, impossible to master, had the strategy elements I liked from chess and the collectability of baseball cards and I really liked it. Within a couple weeks of learning the game, I was playing every night, attacking with Shivans and Serras, and when I wasn't playing, I was trading, getting cards I needed, cards I didn't need that I could trade for cards I needed and a bit of a rush from the clash of wills that was the trade.

Within half a year, I'd transmogrified my collection from lead to gold. I still had the Shivans and Serra's, but they were at the back of a binder filled with Moxes, Juzams and Abysses. I had every card I could possibly need for any tournament, and when that happened, the incentive to trade was gone, and I pretty much stopped, instead using my **Magic** time to play more and more. I played so much in fact that I eventually became a professional player, but with each tournament I played and each year that passed, I moved further and further from the innocence that kept my eyes wide in that first half year. Sure, there were new incentives: win lots of money, gain a little prestige among those few that cared, but those only went to harden my resolve, carry me from innocence. In short, I went from being **Magic's** equivalent of rosy-cheeked kid to crotchety old man. Nothing surprised me any more, and I thought I could never go back.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2f/c6/2fc68ef737da4ba9fdbfcde4aae5a6bee6c858e4cf2e5dc53e0ec1df72cc1ddd.jpg)Then, about a year ago, I was contacted about helping to playtest a new kind of **Magic**. Electronic **Magic**. Wizards of the Coast (the company that makes the game) had designed the game to play on the internet. At first I wasn't all that interested. I mean, there was already an online **Magic** program (Apprentice) that I used all the time, and there weren't that many people using this new **Magic** Online thing (the name of Wizards' new program) and besides, the download was massive. One by one though, my friends online disappeared from IRC as they discovered that WotC's product was pretty cool, and I finally relented.

Through the beta test, I drafted. Draft is a format where you usually need to buy packs of cards to play, but during the test, everything was free, so I drafted, and drafted until I had a collection of over 22000 cards (at 45 cards per draft, by the way, that comes out to around 500 drafts. I probably should have come to this meeting a long time ago), but it still wasn't anything special. The program was outstanding, safe from the cheating problems of real life tournaments and with an intellect that understood the rules as well as any judge in the world, but with no money involved, there was nothing being put on the line, no adrenaline rush. It was just a playtesting tool.

Then, about a month ago, **Magic** Online was finally released to the public. Each beta tester got a free draft to play with, but for some of us, it didn't end there. If you won that initial draft, you got 8 new packs. If you came second, you got four new packs, and combined with the values of individual cards and your ability to sell them, if you won enough and traded enough, you could keep playing forever. I won that draft, opening a card, Call of the Herd, that I was able to trade for a bunch of packs, and then I won a couple more, and I was on my way.

Unfortunately, not everything was sunshine and lollipops. My internet connection is not the best and it cost me a couple of drafts, and I got so enthused about drafting rares that I could sell for more drafts that I lost a few drafts through that as well. Suddenly, I had no event tickets (you need those to compete) and no packs (you need those too) and a bunch of single cards that were useless, right?

How little you know.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fe/bb/febb942d5480d4296e06d4da3620656461341f2f5e012da2e24c3b5daf3322e3.jpg)With nothing else to do in the program without my credit card, I went to the Trading Post, an online chat room where people went to trade cards for other cards, tickets and packs. There were hundreds of people there from every **Magic**-playing nation in the world, all screaming for the stuff they needed and hocking what it was they had, all towards the goal of playing a little more, and it dawned on me. **Magic** Online wasn't just a series of games of **Magic**: it was one big game, the goal of which was to survive as long as you could on what you had. When you have no more, you bust out the Visa or Mastercard, but while you had something, anything, there was a chance you wouldn't have to, and while you may have thought what you had wasn't great, with that huge and that diverse a market, if you had it, someone would want it.

I started trading and trading more. Soon, I wasn't playing that much because I was so focused on building my collection. It grew quickly, to the point where it had some 200 playable rare cards just through making good trades, usually in answer to people's requests in the Trading Post. I discovered other rooms, called casual, where players who didn't want to be hassled by requests for tickets or boosters could go, cards4tix, where players who didn't want to be hassled by traders could go, and auction, where there were singles being auctioned off 24/7. It was twenty-four hours, with denizens of the twenty-four time zones. It just never stopped.

I think about the cards I need to trade for as I try to sleep. I wake up, turn my computer on and log in to see what's going on. Morning, noon and night I am on **Magic** Online, just trading my way to a strong collection, when I was told that the Sealed tourneys were a good way to win a lot of packs at once. I entered two, won once, conceded the final of the other and now I'm **Magic** Online rich. I don't think I can ever stop... (breaks down crying)

Ok, enough of the confession. If you haven't tried it, **Magic** Online is unbelievable. If you're reading this, you have a computer and an internet connection and no excuses. If you don't have a credit card or a parent who is willing to bust one out, you can still go on, advertise that you have no cards, find a benefactor who will give a few commons and start trading up, and while that could be an excruciatingly slow process, you'll meet people from all over the world with whom you have something in common in the process.

**Magic** Online doesn't quite replace **Magic** in real life. It doesn't have the same social aspects, and there's something to the feel of cards in your hands, but it definitely compliments it well, but that's for me. The following is a conversation I had during a match a couple of days ago, while getting my posterior handed to me by an unknown opponent:

\[me\] what's your rating in real life? (on **Magic** Online you have a rating for **Magic** Online events)  
 \[them\] I don't play in real life

![](https://i.ormos.uk/23/16/23167148f29623d64521022a665314825402665594976b4f8df8b477393f2f8c.jpg)**Magic** Online's bringing players out of the woodwork. There aren't as many kids because the act of giving out your credit card number is a lot more difficult for some than handing the kid a twenty to go play **Magic** for the day, but they've been replaced. Replaced by professional family men who don't have the time for tournaments, but who can take care of the baby while doing a draft online. Replaced by women, no longer daunted by the real life (a term used to suggest non-internet goings on by internet denizens) competitive stupidity presented to them by testosterone-filled morons. **Magic** Online is a place where people can go while in bathrobes and pajamas, get a glass of milk from the fridge between rounds and watch TV while doing it. It's convenient and it's fun and it's amazing.

I've met a lot of interesting people and heard a lot of interesting stories on **Magic** Online while under the guise of my beta test nickname. I met Matthew Wang, a player I played at my first Pro Tour in Dallas (that's six years ago folks, Gary said, running a finger along his yard-long whiskers), who came back to the game because of **Magic** Online. I've met women who never felt a part of the competitive real life environment who are completely comfortable with logging in. I've met little kids who don't know what draft is, just hoping to get a few commons so they actually have sixty cards with which to build a Constructed deck to use in the casual play room, and fifty-year old men who don't like the obnoxious punk-aspects of live tournament interaction. Players are coming back to the game after half a decade, playing their first tournament thanks to living in remote areas that don't come with such **Magic**-frills. As a guy with a vested interest in the game and a confessed-love for it as a whole, it's really been an amazing thing to see. And this is only the beginning.

**Magic** Online's going to grow. The hundreds are going to become thousands as word spreads from man to woman to child. The kids without credit cards will get credit cards, the adults withholding from their kids will relent as the word spreads. We're still in the first month of the program's being, and already it's becoming apparent that it provides a lot of the tournament scene-good, while not fraught with a lot of the tournament scene-bad, and its going to introduce an entire new generation of players to the game we all know to be so great, through internet magazines and internet culture. Our little community won't be little anymore, and if that means we have to share, hey, that's all right, there's enough for everyone.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/57/c1/57c1cbb3eba5081650261e8479585c7b8afd61c2a2063d07f9551c3f91844cec.jpg)I can't help but think that **Magic** Online's going to change a lot of things. The face of the Pro Tour should be dominated by **Magic** Onlineites. Day in and day out I'm seeing nicknames like TGO (Bob Maher), Benafel (Chris), Huey (Jensen), AntonJ (Jonsson) Undertaker (Zvi Mowshowitz), Kibler (Brian)...Gab Tsang, Jeff Fung, Eric Froelich, Matt Linde, Alan Comer...these guys are putting in ten hours a day playing **Magic**, and its going to start showing in their performances. It's simple math: those who put in the work get the results. It could well be that soon you'll need a computer to stay competitive on the Pro Tour.

More importantly, **Magic** Online may lead to **Magic's** wider acceptance and integration into popular culture. This game's going on ten years now, and I still get asked 'is that like D&D?' Now, people might ask 'is that like Quake?' but the conflicting things they're hearing will at least make the ears perk up if nothing else. Fortunately, I think there will be something else... being inquisitive is a part of being human, and when people get that kind of conflicting information, they actually inquire further. Stranger things have happened. 

Now, I've written 1900 some-odd words of glowing praise for this program, but I'm me and you're you, and while most of the **Magic** Online denizens will concur with my sentiments, in the end, the only one who can convince you I'm right is you. Go to [www.mtgonline.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20060107044710/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic/magiconline/downloadlinks), download the program and try it for yourself. Sure, you'll never see your family again and your friends will talk about you like you're still hanging out with them, but other than that, I think it'll be a really good thing for you. In the end, all you need is a computer and a deck. 

Have a good week.
