Double-faced cards have an interesting impact on drafting. At the Pro Tour, after each pack is opened, all the players at a draft table hold up their double-faced cards and get a chance to look around and see what's been opened. This can have a big impact on how players draft.

At the start of the second draft, Pro Tour Hall of Famer Raphael Levy glanced around the table at the double-faced cards that had been opened, and saw a bunch of the usual suspects: the [Tormented Pariah](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tormented+Pariah)s and [Grizzled Outcasts](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grizzled+Outcasts) that seem to populate almost every draft but rarely have that big of an impact. But there was one double-faced card that really stood out to Levy: the [Daybreak Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daybreak+Ranger) that was opened directly to his right by Jun'ya Iyanaga.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Daybreak%20Ranger)](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daybreak%20Ranger)

When Levy looked through his opening pack in his second draft, he found an [Avacynian Priest](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Avacynian+Priest), a [Burning Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Vengeance), a [Voiceless Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Voiceless+Spirit), a [Nephalia Drownyard](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Drownyard), and an unimpressive five-drop in almost every color.

There's certainly a small subsection of players who are not only willing but actively excited to first-pick a [Burning Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Vengeance), because they love drafting around the card. Levy just isn't one of those players...

However, that [Daybreak Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daybreak+Ranger) in Iyanaga's pack forced Levy to examine his options in a very different light than he would normally.

Most of the time players will shy away from a card that requires you to build your entire deck around it (such as [Burning Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Vengeance)) until they've gotten a ways into the draft. If a player feels confident that the pieces necessary to make the deck work will come together, or if there isn't anything else of note in the pack, then [Burning Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Vengeance) can be a very worthwhile pick.

Knowing that Iyanaga was (almost certainly) going to take the [Daybreak Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daybreak+Ranger) – Levy didn't want to take a green card, because then he would have to fight with Iyanaga over green.

![](http://archive.wizards.com/mtg/images/daily/events/worlds11/levy.jpg)

*Raphael Levy: a man with a plan.* 

But the [Daybreak Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daybreak+Ranger) did more than keep Levy out of green; it also kept him out of white. When I talked to Levy after the draft, he explained to me that he figured that Iyanaga would go green-white. There weren't enough exciting Werewolves in the first set of packs, Levy explained, so it was very unlikely that Iyanaga would try to draft red-green Werewolves. That meant that the [Voiceless Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Voiceless+Spirit) would also be a dangerous pick.

Consequently, the French Hall of Famer realized that he would be able to draft a blue-based mill-yourself deck without any interference from Iyanaga. That's why he took the [Burning Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20111118000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning+Vengeance), and ultimately put together a white-red-blue mill-yourself deck.
