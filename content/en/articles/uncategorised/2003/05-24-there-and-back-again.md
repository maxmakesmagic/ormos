### Josh Talks Briefly About His E3 Experience

* * *

![](http://wizards.com/global/images/magic_magiconline_articlejb052303_pic1_en.jpg)

In the electronic gaming industry there are a lot of giants. New giants rise up from time to time to battle with the older, more experienced ones to prove their worth and that they have the right to be in the ancients' territory. Sometimes the older giants win and sometimes they lose. With this in mind I asked myself what Wizards of the Coast was doing at a show like E3. Why are we, practically halflings in the electronic entertainment industry, showing up in the giants' territory?

I'll tell you why: Because like David, we are packing a mighty stone in this here sling and we might just topple ourselves some Goliaths.

My friends and coworkers tried to prepare me for my trip to E3 -- to step into the ancients' territory. I had never been before, but I had heard glorious tales of battles, conquests, and defeats. My friends told me about booth babes, gigantic booths where the giants dwelled, the amount of noise, and all the free stuff available in the giants' kingdom.

And my friends weren't exaggerating. The realm of giants is majestic to behold. The first feeling I had (as a gamer) walking into that hall was pure giddiness. I was laughing out loud (and a little maniacally) at the sheer quantity of gaming goodness packed into the LA Convention Center. All this was about two hours before complete and total sensory overload set in. Then, I needed a Pepsi and a quiet place to sit as though my life depended on it.

As I walked between the walls of booths the size of small cities, I wondered how Wizards of the Coast would fit in. Eventually came to Wizards of the Coast's humble, but gorgeous booth - our beautiful "hobbit-hole". I was greeted by a nice, open view, and big, beautiful pictures straight from the latest card sets of Magic: The Gathering. Inside the booth were four nicely laid out computer kiosks for demos and the entire Wizards staff decked out special Magic Online shirts.

![](http://wizards.com/global/images/magic_magiconline_articlejb052303_pic2_en.jpg)It seemed that a big attention grabber was the massive poster adorning the back wall of our booth. With every card from *Odyssey* block, *Onslaught* block, and the *Seventh Edition* set, it was an impressive sight for folks wandering through. As it turns out, most of the people that I spoke with came into the booth because of that wall. "Oh, I used to play **Magic**," they'd say. I'd grin and drop the ***Magic** Online* stone into my sling. Once I began showing visitors the game, I had their undivided attention for 20 to 30 minutes, minimum.

It seemed like everyone was interested in ***Magic** Online.* We had visitors in the form of convention attendees, the press, vendors, and even other exhibitors! People wanted to come by and see what Wizards was up to. We found that a lot of them were **Magic** players back when the game came out but haven't done much since then. Another large portion of our visitors knew all about ***Magic** Online* and wanted to see what we were offering in 2.0. On day two, we had people lining up to receive the E3-exclusive box-topper: Two-Headed Dragon. Sam Wood was also on hand to give autographs.

It was a fantastic show for ***Magic** Online.* We returned to a venue where most people wouldn't expect us and drew a large number of the crowd to ***Magic** Online.* Nearly everyone who saw the game and took the time to hear about it went away excited and ready to at least give it a try. We stood up tall in a hall of giants and walked away with our heads held high.

E3, we'll see you next year! Take that, Goliath.
