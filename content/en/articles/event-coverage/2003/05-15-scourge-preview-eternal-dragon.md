Pro Tour-Venice served as a showcase for two things to come out of *Onslaught* block: the return of the popular cycling mechanic and the effectiveness of huge monsters in tournament play. Lightning Rift and Astral Slide faced down Silvos, Akroma, and Rorix – the latter finding its place even in the “weenie” deck of the format, goblins. In the end, cycling took the day, as Osyp Lebedowicz took the championship.

Those looking for an end to the effectiveness of the Rift/Slide deck may find respite in *Scourge*– but they won’t be finding it this preview. Eternal Dragon embodies the newest incarnations of both cycling and beastiness. At 5/5 flying for seven mana, this dragon is hardly the biggest on the block – after all, Darwin Kastle showed that much mana can produce a 17/17 monstrosity courtesy of Kilnmouth Dragon – but the power of this card lies not in its raw power, but in its resilience.

Eternal Dragon – as per its name – just won’t stay dead. Akroma’s Vengeance? Sure - he'll be back for more. Starstorm? Nothing but a stumbling block. Cruel Revival? Skinthinner? You’ll have to find something else to keep this dragon down. For ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif), Eternal Dragon’s controller can return it from the graveyard to his hand, ensuring a never-ending stream of dragonly goodness, despite your opponent’s best efforts to contain him. It’s like something out of the children’s song “The Cat Came Back”.

This Hammer of Bogardan dragon would be effective as a finisher even if it were only the dragon that wouldn’t die. But Eternal Dragon is more than that – it is a mana fixer and card advantage machine. *Scourge* introduces the landcycling mechanic, a takeoff on cycling that allows a player to search his or her library for a particular land type rather than drawing a card. Eternal Dragon is by far the most impressive of these, not only for its statistics, but also for the synergy between the mechanic and its recursion ability.

With the reduction in the power level of card drawing across the board – we won’t be seeing another Fact or Fiction any time soon – the ability to recur Eternal Dragon continually and cycle it for a plains is on par with a modern day Whispers of the Muse. With an overall mana cost of seven to return the dragon and cycle it, a comparison to the six mana buyback spell is not unwarranted. While cycling Eternal Dragon can only net land rather than action cards, the mana fixing in the early game and deck thinning effect late can certainly outweigh that drawback.

Perhaps most important to those looking forward to this year’s Block Constructed Qualifier season is the fact that land cycling counts as a cycling effect. Pro Tour-Venice may have served as home to the most triggered effects in Pro Tour history, with Lightning Rift and Astral Slide showing up at nearly every table. Eternal Dragon gives these decks a powerful weapon, serving as an inexpensive cycler to activate Rift and Slide, a mana fixer, a card advantage engine, and an effective finisher all in one.

Eternal Dragon is most likely to have the biggest impact on the mirror match, where games come down to a war of attrition. With Akroma’s Vengeances flying with abandon, one can rarely rely on either enchantments or creatures to win the game single-handedly. Eternal Dragon fuels Lightning Rifts in the early game while ensuring constant land drops – of utmost importance in the mirror – and once Vengeances start clearing the board, he comes back to serve double or even triple duty as a creature.

At 5/5 flying, Eternal Dragon is big enough to get into a fight with Exalted Angel and win or brawl with Rorix and take the pit fighter down with him. Akroma is still out of his league, but being able to square off with the Angel of Wrath is a bit much to ask of a creature that has so much going for it already. In short, Eternal Dragon is not only a powerful utility card, but also a dominant creature once it hits the board – which it can do again and again and again.

With Upheaval still in the format, it’s unlikely that the mana-hungry Eternal Dragon will have a huge impact on the Standard environment, but it’ll certainly be a card to keep an eye on. Once the *Odyssey* block rotates out of the format, Standard will lose both Upheaval and Wild Mongrel, the two most defining cards in the environment, and will start to look a lot more like *Onslaught* Block Constructed. When it does – Eternal Dragon will be waiting.
