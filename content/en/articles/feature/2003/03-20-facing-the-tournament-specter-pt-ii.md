### A Constructed Event Primer

* * *

So, you've spent time developing and modifying your deck, you've pondered the metagame and adjusted your sideboard, and you've tested against the best decks out there. What's the next step? Playing in the tournament, itself.

You may've done everything you can to get your deck ready for the tournament scene, but you also need to prepare yourself. And, while a ***Magic** Online* tournament doesn't have some of the ambiance of a physical tournament, much of what you need to be ready for is the same.

### To Sleep, Perchance To Topdeck

If you've read many tournament reports, then I'm sure you've come across more than one "got two hours of sleep and went top 8" report. While some Pro Tour players can pull this off, I don't recommend trying it for yourself. First, experienced Pro Tour players get used to the long grind of big tournaments. Second, you may be okay through the first rounds and may even get a second wind, but you'll eventually start making avoidable mistakes that will cost you games.

So, the night before a big tournament, try to get a good night's rest. This is especially true if you've had many late nights preparing for the tournament. You'll perform better and enjoy the experience more if you're not dragging and groggy on the day of the event.

### Trash The Talk

Anyone who's spent serious time in tournaments and even the **Constructed** rooms on ***Magic** Online* knows that trash talk happens. Many players consider it part of the psycological game that goes along with turning cards sideways. My advice: don't do it, and ignore it when your opponent does it to you.

Pschological advantage it may be, because nothing can ruin a good tournament like your opponent making rude remarks during a game. This isn't a football field where getting riled up makes you play better, and I'm pretty sure top-level chess players aren't usually found commenting on their opponents' improbable ancestries during a match. Do us all a favor and keep it civil.

Unfortunately, even if you're not engaging in trash talk, you'll come up against opponents who will. The good part is that ***Magic** Online* has the perfect answer: block them. If your opponent insists on being rude, don't hesitate to right-click his or her name and select **Block user**. You won't have to deal with that person's comments and can get on with the game. If you let it get to you, you stand a good chance of making mistakes you otherwise would've avoided.

### Are We There Yet?

If you've never participated in a large Swiss round event before, you need to know that this is going to take some time. Each round lasts at least 45 minutes and the next round won't start until all the previous round's games are up. In a large event like the March 22 tournament, there may well be ten full rounds of play before the Top 8 playoff.

Speaking of sitting, take every opportunity to get out of your seat for a good stretch. In physical **Magic** tournaments, you at least have some time before next round's pairings go up to move about. At the very least you have to pick up your stuff and find your seat for the next round. So, if you finish early, go AFK for a while and step away from your monitor.

Lastly, you have the unfortunate ability with ***Magic** Online* to be somewhere accessible to significant others, co-workers, children, and various others who might not understand that you're occupied. Make sure you've discharged any obligations that might cause interruptions at critical moments, and let people know that you are going to be occupied well ahead of time. This is one of those times where you'll definitely find out if you've been paying enough attention to your significant other in the past few months.

### Time Keeps On Tickin'

As a casual player, you may've never played timed games. Before you enter your first event, you better get used to it.

Play timed games while you're testing the deck you want to play, or even if you're still deciding which deck you want. You may discover that, while you have a couple decks that win fairly consistently, one may take much more time to finish off your opponent. The only way to know if the difference is critical or not is by playing games where you see how close your clock gets.

You'll also learn where you can remove stops and yields to save yourself **OK**-clicking during your games. In that, I mean you'll know when you *need* to have a chance to play cards and when you don't need to do anything unless your opponent does. That way, if time gets tight during the tournament, you'll know exactly what to clear and what to leave alone without having to think about it.

### Enjoy Yourself, Dangit!

The best overall piece of advice is to go into a tournament for the fun of it. That way, if you're not doing well, at least you're getting a lot of game-time in. And, if you *are* doing well, it's just icing on the cake for you.

Going into a tournament stressed out is guaranteed to lead to a bad day (unless you happen to thrive on stress, in which case have fun doing that). You'll be nervous, slip up, then berate yourself for making silly mistakes. You're playing **Magic** at home with a chance to earn a prize or boost your ratings--kick back and enjoy it a bit.

### Conclusion

Once you've got your deck prepped, spend some time getting yourself prepped, too. Get some rest, set up your space, take that special person out for dinner the night before in appreciation for him or her leaving you alone for a day (it just might work). Once you're playing, take it easy and don't let other people press your buttons.

Again, the [free tournament on March 22](/en/articles/feature/2003/03-12-another-tournament-announcement) is a great chance to give the tournament scene a try. Jump in, play some games, have some fun. And remember, in the end, it's only a game.
