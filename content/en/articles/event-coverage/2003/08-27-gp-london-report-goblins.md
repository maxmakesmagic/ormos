If you are planning to attend a Block Constructed PTQ in the next couple of weeks, I have a hint for you:

Your deck should at least include 10 basic plains or mountains, or both. I don't fancy your chances otherwise.

I realized that pretty fast during the limited testing I did for Grand Prix-London on ***Magic** Online*. I am not a huge fan of the white-red cycling deck, even though it obviously is very good. When I played some games with Gabriel Nassif, we learned that decks without mountains or plains aren't really competitive. I mean, Gabriel surely tried everything. The highlight was a blue-green Mind's Desire/Brain Freeze deck with such bombs as Elvish Pioneer and Sprouting Vines. The problem was that a turn 4 Silver Knight was usually a clock that this deck couldn't race at all. He had various black decks that also failed to win too much against the red deck. During all those games, I decided that goblins was just the best deck in the format. There are a couple of reasons, but the one that stands on top of them all is that the deck just abuses the most powerful cards in the whole environment: Clickslither, Siege-Gang Commander, Rorix Bladewing and Goblin Warchief.

Obviously white-red is running quite powerful stuff as well. Lightning Rift, Silver Knight, Wing Shards, Exalted Angel and Starstorm are all very good. But if you think about it, the only reason those cards are that powerful in block is that you have to be able to beat the goblin deck. Other then the Angel, all those cards would be quite unexciting if there weren't the little red men running rampant in the format. With so much hate for the red deck, the red-white decks did quite well at GP-Detroit over a month ago with Bob Maher and Eugene Harvey playing almost a mirror-match in the finals. But things changed since then and other good decks appeared. Monowhite control and a white-blue deck showed up and both of these have quite good matchups against white-red but don't like to play against monored at all. Both decks really need a turn 2 Silver Knight to have a chance at all and the monowhite deck has to follow it up with a Dragon Scales. But those two decks caused the white-red decks to change. Gempalm Incinerators left the maindeck and most people don't even play them in the sideboard these days. They were replaced with slow cards that win the mirror match, like Akroma, Angel of Wrath and Decree of Annihilation. And I know that those don't help against one deck – goblins.

Suddenly the matchup against the white-red decks was easily winnable. They had tons of bad cards in the maindeck and the red deck could board in both Sulfuric Vortex and Stabilizer, which are better than the cards that the white-red deck boards in, as those decks devote a lot of sideboard space to the mirror match and against other white decks. All of this made me run the following deck at GP-London:

The South American squad with Jose Barbero and Diego Ostrovich came up with an almost identical maindeck. They apparently were afraid to play the third Rorix because of the legendary problem. Jose had one Menacing Ogre and Diego one Carbonize instead, but I am fairly sure that the third dragon is the correct way to go. You really want to have one of them avaible as soon as you reach six mana. I was quite happy with my deck and looked forward to the tourney. This is how GP-London went for me:

Kibler wasn't really the opponent I wanted to start the tourney with, but my draws were good and he didn't see a Silver Knight in either game so I just attacked with some random creatures and won. The first game against Olivier was quite funny. He parised his opening hand going first. When he drew his first six cards, the top card of his deck was for some reason face up. Olivier argued a bit with the Head Judge, but he was forced to Paris once again. My hand was pretty good and I expected to get an easy win. Not that fast, sir. Olivier cast five spells this game. Turn 5 Exalted Angel with the help of a Temple of the False God, turn 6 Akroma's Vengeance, turn 7 Exalted Angel, turn 8 Akroma's Vengeance and turn 9 Akroma. Boo!

My next opponent won one game with a Silver Knight followed up by yet another Akroma while I couldn't draw Siege-Gang or Sharpshooter but the other games weren't that close as he got poor mana draws. When I played Reinhard in the last round, it looked like there was no way he could make it to the second day as his tiebreakers weren't good enough and he was friendly enough to concede. He was playing blue-white anyway and when we played for fun, the matchup was definitely in my favor.

The games against Pierre were quite frustrating. He naturally had turn 2 Silver Knight in all three and in the final game he was on seven life and I was holding a Searing Flesh. Now it came down to drawing skills. He had to draw what I later learned was his single Exalted Angel in his deck and attack with that before I drew my seventh land. I am sure the math is slightly in my favor here but sometimes something like 1% is good enough unfortunately. My next two matchups were nice at least. Someone forgot to tell Alex Shvartsman and Ed Fear that Bad Form is indeed about as good as its name suggests. Ed also boarded the land destruction in but I had a problem with drawing too many lands the whole tournament and that, fortunately, didn't stop in that game. Elie just didn't draw enough Silver Knights to put up a fight and I just walked over him. Against Geoffrey I finally saw turn 3 Goblin Warchief, turn 4 Siege-Gang Commander. Unfortunately, that didn't happen on my side of the board. In game 2, he went Prospector, Prospector, Warchief, Siege-Gang, even though I hadn't played a single spell yet and he knew that I had some amount of Starstorms in my sideboard. Well, sometimes playing ‘clueless' isn't a bad thing and I just scooped my cards in disgust. Florent took the draw in the last round because he thought he had a bad matchup I guess. At that time I figured he was playing white-red like Nassif and Tennenbaum and I was a bit surprised as his tiebreakers weren't all that good and there was like a 20% chance that he might not make Top 32 with a draw if the wrong people won/lost. Well, that was what happened and he ended up 34th while I barely finished in the money in 31st place.

Not everyone was as lucky as myself though. John Larkin played the same deck I ran and finished in the Top 32 as well but it was quite frustrating for him as he was 10-1 after round 11 and lost his last three matches. Now you must be wondering why Jose and Diego made Top 8 while John and I only Top 32, with an almost identical deck. Well, there was one difference. The South Americans played with a sideboard, while John and I played with 15 almost useless cards. I played a ton of games online against Mark Zajdner and his white-red-deck and I kept beating him after sideboarding with Sulfuric Vortex and Stabilizer. But then I had a brilliant idea just before the tourney started: if people play a deck like Maher won Detroit with, you don't even need Vortex. You just board in bigger creatures and that leaves Lightning Rift totally useless. And in that case, you don't even need Stabilizer any more and there is room for more mirror match cards. 4 Shock

With that card you only need to deal a few damage and then sit back until they do something and then you hit them with Rorix or Clickslither and that should be enough. I really have to talk with Bob at GP-Atlanta; he needs to either write articles on his decks or win more tourneys, but the way it is, you can't really rely on people copying him and that is pretty embarassing, isn't it?

Anyway, the white-red decks that I played were running Astral Slide and four copies of Exalted Angel and the combination of those two cards makes it pretty impossible to win with Menacing Ogres and Thoughtbound Primocs if you don't have access to either Sulfuric Vortex or Stabilizer. I actually would've won the matches against Olivier and Kung-Chieh if I had one of those cards into play in games 2 or 3 but the way it was, they just hit me with Exalted Angel with Astral Slide in play while I had useless Menacing Ogres and the one Searing Flesh in hand in all of those games. At least I can't really blame my performance on bad draws or something else, I am pretty sure that John or I or even both of us would've made Top 8 if we just had a proper sideboard. Diego:
