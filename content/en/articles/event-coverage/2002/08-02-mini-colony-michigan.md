It's been a while since the last report from here at the Mini-Colony, and there's good reason for that - for about a week, we haven't BEEN at the Mini-Colony. Yes, once again my fellow Colonists and I packed up and headed elsewhere in our pursuit of gaming. This time it was not the gamer utopia of Origins that was our destination, but rather Liz Lempicki's house right outside Detroit, Michigan.

Why, you might ask? To practice draft. The World Championships, for those of you who are unaware, is unique among premiere **Magic** events in that it features three distinct formats. Day 1 is Standard, Day 2 is draft (booster or Rochester, alternating each year - this year is booster), and Day 3 is Block Constructed (or Extended, alternating in similar fashion). A week or so after we'd returned from Origins, EFro proposed that we ought to make another excursion to Michigan, as Chris Benafel was planning to visit as well, and we could practice draft with him and Liz, as well as with Pat Chapin and Eric Taylor, both of whom happen to live nearby.

This plan was not without controversy. In fact, it was steeped in it. One might wonder why we'd uproot ourselves from our base of operations in Virginia to go to Michigan - some ten hours away - just to draft. Even if we wanted to practice with other pro players, there had to be somewhere closer we could go, didn't there? Unfortunately, the two magic "hubs" nearby - the Maryland apartment that is home to William "Baby Huey" Jensen, Brock Parker, and Dan Clegg, and features Matt Linde as an almost constant fixture - and Mike Long's "The End Games" store in Charlottesville, Virginia - were both ruled out as possibilities due to conflicts between members of our group and these sets of potential draft partners. 

People at the upper echelons of any competitive activity tend to have very strong personalities, and pro Magic is no exception. I remember someone joking not long ago that the sheer force of ego within the Mini-Colony would blow the roof off the Froehlich home, and if our arguments about the Michigan trip were any indication, I'm certainly glad words don't carry that much kinetic energy. It's not that our discussions were particularly heated - thankfully all of us are relatively even-tempered - but rather that we are all generally quite opinionated when it comes to things in which we have vested interest. When disagreement arose over going to Michigan, then, the lines in the sand were drawn.

My opinion on the matter was that the entire trip seemed like a bad idea. We had only recently returned from Origins, which had taken a large bite out of our available testing time, and had just started to establish a discernable pattern of testing regularly. Our sleep schedules remained askew, and Magic Online addiction continued to run rampant, but we had started seriously building a gauntlet and testing important decks and cards for both formats. I contended that abruptly leaving once again would take another big chunk out of our ability to test for the Constructed formats, as not only would we lose the time while we were away, but we'd also have difficulty re-establishing an effective rhythm quickly when we returned.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPDEN01/154fm11benafel.jpg)This issue, in turn, raised a host of other problems. EFro suggested that rather than just stop testing Constructed while we were there (to avoid leaking information) that we should include Benafel in our testing group, at least for the period that we were staying with him. This prompted immediate disagreement from both Hegstad and me, as Chris's history as a playtester - as he freely admits - isn't the most stellar. During the original Colony experiment, Hegstad said, Chris's contributions to testing were largely confined to trying to get everyone to draft instead of actually working on Block decks. He also has close ties to many other groups of players, including former teammate Noah Boeken, who is notorious for leaking deck information, and both Brian and I were very concerned that including Chris in our testing might lead to a loss of information security. I was not at all interested in a repeat of Worlds 2000, at which my testing group played Tinker and had other competitors coming up to us with evil grins and asking to borrow Pulverizes. The fact that Chris was one of these players didn't help his case any. Of course, if that repeat included two of us meeting in the finals anyway, I suppose I couldn't much complain.

In one camp, then, there was Ben and EFro, who wanted to go, and in the other myself and Hegstad, who thought the trip was a bad idea. In the end, however, the yeas had it, as EFro managed to get Benafel to promise not to reveal any of the information he might learn about our decks in our Constructed testing (with the realization that doing so could do nothing but hurt him in the long run), Hegstad had a change of heart, and I - despite my continued misgivings - agreed to go along with the trip if everyone else was so gung-ho about it. Michigan it was.

I, of course, slept nearly the entire length of our ten hour drive, a feat which was made much easier by the nest I created in the back seat of Efro's van with my alpaca blanket and pillows. For anyone who is unaware, the alpaca is a relative of the llama from Peru, and its hair is perhaps one of the softest and most comfortable substances I've ever had the pleasure of touching. I have two blankets and three pillows made of the stuff, and I've had more than one person ask me to buy them one and bring it to an event for them after they felt the ones I bring with me to tournaments. I fully intend on covering my entire room with alpaca the next time I come into a large amount of money. No, I'm not kidding. It's THAT soft.

Alpaca plugging aside, we arrive in Michigan Thursday night and head straight to Eric Taylor's apartment for his weekly **Magic** and food night. EDT is an amazing host, packing his apartment full of gamers and feeding them all home-cooked food to boot. This week's fare was macaroni and cheese and strawberry pie, and it alone was worth the trip. We did an eight man draft while we chowed down, and my double Mongrel, double Narcissism g-b deck managed to lose in the first round to EFro's u-g double Breakthrough/Persuasion/Grizzly Fate monstrosity, which ended up taking down the draft. Liz and Chris arrived sometime during all of this, as Chris's flight had been delayed for several hours, and after the draft ended, we head back to Liz's to get some sleep.

The rest of the week is largely a blur. EDT and Pat Chapin call and offer to come over to Liz's to draft with us, but in the end Chapin ends up coming alone. Unfortunately, this left us with only seven to draft, and between Liz having to go to school during the day, the rest of us sleeping at odd, non-synchronous hours, and far too much **Magic** Online being played while we ARE awake, we don't manage to get many useful drafts in over the course of two days that Pat stayed. We do, however, get a visit from the police, who showed up because of noise complaints about the party Liz's brother was having at the time, and after Pat's repeated offers to show them our **Magic** cards to prove that we really weren't doing anything illegal, we all pass our breathalyzers tests and return to our gaming. An amusing night, to be sure.

We did learn a number of useful things about draft, the most important of which was the power of monored. In a four man draft including Rubin, Benafel, Hegstad, and myself, I ended up with a deck that so bad I could hardly put together twenty-three playable cards in two colors. Rubin, on the other hand, was the only person among the four of us to be drafting blue OR black, and had a deck featuring Sengir Vampire, Grotesque Hybrid, and Hydromorph Gull all out of the same *Torment* pack he opened. I decided that rather than play any of my five or six mediocre white cards, I'd just play all twenty red cards I had - including two Firecat Blitzes (but sadly no Rites of Initiation) - and twenty mountains. My deck contained such hits as Dwarven Grunt and Soulgorger Orgg, but I took Rubin down in two quick games, including one in which I slowplayed my Orgg until he'd used his Morbid Hunger to gain some life and killed him with it the following turn. What does u-b do about a 6/6 trampling Orgg? Die, that's what. Even while I was celebrating my victory, Liz beat him once again with my deck, Firecat Blitzing all of her land away for a non-lethal attack and still pulling out the win. I beat Hegstad's g-w deck in the next round, and sadly lost to Benafel in the finals, but the lesson was clear. Monored Soulgorger Orgg is the archetype to beat in *Ody/Tor/Jud* draft.

In all seriousness, however, I do feel like I got a somewhat better feel for some of the cards in the *Odyssey* block, albeit not as much as I might've hoped for spending a week of my life off in Michigan for the purpose of drafting. We returned to Eric's house exhausted, and - at least in my case - relieved to be back. After a day or so spent hibernating to catch up on lost sleep, we returned to work. My fears about having trouble getting back into a testing groove were assuaged, and I've probably played several hundred games since we got back. I've quit playing **Magic** Online entirely in an attempt to devote more time to testing, but unfortunately Hegstad became addicted while we were in Michigan, and EFro seems to have dedicated himself to becoming a **Magic** Online Millionaire, playing and trading during a large portion of his waking hours. It's a hard habit to kick, to be sure, but hopefully we can manage to get along well despite the huge suck it has become on our testing time. 

This is likely one of the last Colony updates, as Worlds is quickly approaching. I'll try to get one more in before we actually leave for Australia and one final last minute report from down under, assuming I can find an internet connection in the land of kangaroos, and then, of course, a follow up tournament report with my final thoughts on my World Championships and Mini-Colony experiences.
