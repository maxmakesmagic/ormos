Friday, April 19th, 2002 marked an interesting day in **Magic** history. For some members of our community, it will go down as the day the spirit of the game died, for some it will mark the passing through of one of our most memorable writers and for some it will only serve as marker for the first time they heard of John Rizzo.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPDET01/9.jpg"><br><i>One of the few surviving pictures of the wily and elusive John F. Rizzo.</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

Rizzo was as popular as any writer in our community. His 10,000-word rants full of tangents, incomplete sentences and lost thoughts were easy to relate to in a way that provided wide appeal, as evidenced by his win in last year's War of the Internet Writers at [CCGPrime.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20020424000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://CCGPrime.com&origin=sideboard_ww_20020424), a contest he's likely to take again this year.

In an angry, expletive (usually) deleted-filled letter to the Ferrett (his editor), Rizzo, a volatile, fascinating and amusing Star City columnist for the last two years explained why it was he hadn't written for three weeks after a prolonged period of remarkable consistency: He'd quit **Magic**.

Along with the Ferrett's address, which bemoaned the departure of his most popular writer while at the same time agreeing with Rizzo's sentiments, there was a collection of essays from Star City's top talents singing Rizzo's praises.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Rizzo's letter went on to explain that he was sick of listening to **Magic** players openly moan about this and that before doing absolutely nothing to create change. That, among other reasons was why he was leaving the game, and perhaps more importantly his metaphorical pen, in the dustbin, never to be picked up again.

It's been a busy five days at Star City. Along with the Ferrett's address, which bemoaned the departure of his most popular writer while at the same time agreeing with Rizzo's sentiments, there was a collection of essays from Star City's top talents singing Rizzo's praises. In most of those works, it was apparent that Rizzo was good as gone even before the announcement for these guys. If the lack of articles for three weeks wasn't saying it, the man's silence on the Star City mailing list must have been deafening.

On Monday, a different kind of article made an appearance. *Why Rizzo was Useless Compared to Kai or Finkel*, an angry diatribe about the stupidity of martyrdom by *Sideboard* writer Toby Wachter, really stirred the pot. Toby's article suggested that the Buddes and Finkels of the game will always be more important to the game than the Rizzos and Wakefields (Jamie Wakefield, in case you haven't heard of him, was one of the finer writers about all things Magical until a few years ago when a different game piqued his interest and **Magic** got put on the back burner. Rizzo has been compared to Jamie as a newer, tougher everyman of the game). He also made the point that the canonization of these writers was wrong. The backlash was intense.

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>John Rizzo is a guy who loved Magic.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Half a dozen writers sent in anti-Wachter diatribes asking the question 'how dare ye bring to question the greatness that is Rizzo?' as if he'd made a personal attack on each and every one of them, but while the tone of Toby's article may have been overly severe (hey, the man breathes pro wrestling, did you expect quiet and tasteful?) it said some things I think needed to be said.

*\[Note: In an article on StarCityGames.com today, Toby explained that he never intended the email he wrote to be published - but he stands by his points. - Ed.\]*

John Rizzo is a guy who loved **Magic**. He was by his own admission not very good, taking Rogue decks to PTQs and going 3-4 or 2-5 with them. He gave bad advice, calling that particular spade a spade, and had a lot of fun doing it while making it fun for those who read him in the meantime. He was honest, tough on the issues and made the reader think about things within the game aside from the game itself. Rizzo helped to flesh out what **Magic** has become. We aren't players of a game any more: we're members of a worldwide community.

Now here's the problem I have with the idolization of Rizzo and Wakefield: These are two guys who loved the game. They took it and played it their way, waving off the Aaron Forsythes of the world and their sound advice in favor of maintaining their personal flavor. They poured their games, their hearts and their very lives onto the page, putting it on display for the world to see, and they shared of themselves in a way that helped the **Magic** community. 

Now, I don't think John Rizzo entered into this endeavor to make himself the idol of thousands, and I think he'd let out some kind of loud snort if it were ever suggested to him he should be one, because all he ever really was or wanted to be was just another gamer. Sure, he rose above through his words, but the thing that made those words so great was the fact we could all relate to them in some way, shape or form because there was a little bit of Rizzo in all of us. There was a little bit of all of us in Rizzo too (The joke he'd make here would be absolutely revolting), but in the end, there is one sad truth that will link John and Jamie in my mind that will be their lasting legacy:

They quit.

For their love of the game, **Magic** became so defined in their lives that they felt that they had to eventually cut it off at the throat and never touch those cards again, because **Magic** and their participation in the community had become so intermingled that in their minds they couldn't stop writing and just go on enjoying the game. I think we all forget sometimes that **Magic** really is just a game at its heart, even if so many of us have turned it into a lifestyle.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/1c/e11cfddd4ee9e7b2a693c960817624088b3d52a2234c0e083945024c8df6e1f6.jpg)

I find it ironic that it's this ultimate betrayal that has made Jamie larger than life and that seemingly has sent John down that same path towards Magic: the Sainthood, when the reality is that they should never have quit the game. Yes, maybe it was time for them each to put down their pens and stop playing in PTQs because they each had lost that edge that made the adrenaline rush of playing for a Top 8 as good or better than jumping off a cliff with a bungee cord tied to your ankles, but they didn't have to up and quit. Three months from now, I'd love nothing better than to know that Rizzo is letting the kids use his beloved Nightscape Familiars as chew toys as he tries to figure out yet another sixty-card combination of awfulness without sharing it with anyone, because that would show me that his heart is still that of a gamer.

Are Kai and Jon bigger or more important than John or Jamie? It's a tough comparison. The players give us something to aspire to while the writers give us something to learn from. There is some crossover here: Jon and Kai have both done some writing in their time, while Jamie made a couple of Pro Tour appearances and John sure tried to. I think the answer to this question is no, but Rizzo and Wakefield don't win this one either.

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>The Magic community as it is today has gotten to where it is thanks to a lot of contributions from a lot of people, and a balance between competition and community spirit.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Whether you're a public persona in this community or that thirteen year old gamer who wrote one grammatically dreadful Friday Night **Magic** report, you're doing your part to contribute to this good thing we have here, and if you've done what your commitment allows to make a positive contribution, you deserve the same kind of recognition Rizzo got last Friday. We each have our different reasons for playing the game or for caring about it. I play because I like being good at something and enjoy the thrill of victory, I write because its an enjoyable way to make a living, a good way to beat out my frustrations and hopefully helps to lay the groundwork that will make this good thing we have even better, and I mean no offense when I say this, but I don't do it for you. Neither did John, Jon, Kai or Jamie.

The **Magic** community as it is today has gotten to where it is thanks to a lot of contributions from a lot of people, and a balance between competition and community spirit. While Toby's article wasn't an entirely fair one in sight of the contributions Jamie and John made, I feel like he was right in that there are hundreds, if not thousands to whom that recognition should be spread out if recognition really needs to be given, and a lot of those people are still here. They're still playing in their basements, writing on their beat up, six year old computers, confusing their parents with talk of mana screw and just loving the game for the game it is and the support system it's become.

The Ferrett's article Friday summed up Rizzo's thoughts by screaming to the masses to get off their collective posterior and write. Make that contribution, not because you want the recognition, but because you love the game. It's a good way to express yourself and to put something to electric paper. It will provide fuel for others who will then give back to you tenfold by doing the same. Sure, it's time-consuming, and you may not be a gifted writer, but in the end, the relevant people won't care about your deficiencies as a writer or your inability to translate thought to paragraph. They'll only care there's another link to click on.

I'm not saying I won't remember Rizzo fondly. We traded emails sporadically over the last two years and shook hands once in Detroit and I'll value the little interaction I shared with the man. He wrote some of the most amusing and introspective articles this internet community has seen and for that he will and should be remembered, but now he's gone, and you're still here. He shouldn't be your hero or your role model. Be that to yourself.

Goodbye John. We'll miss you, but life and **Magic** will definitely go on.
