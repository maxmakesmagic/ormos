When a players first set out to learn **Magic**, they're often told to make at least a third of each deck lands. But a green stompy deck from the game's history was famous for ignoring that canard in favor of more spells. That deck? The aptly named Ten-Land Green. It played just 10 [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest)s and yet still managed to cast its spells aggressively. How? By using a few "free" lands in the form of [Land Grant](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Land+Grant) and [Elvish Spirit Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elvish+Spirit+Guide) as well as playing a number of free spells. [Vine Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vine+Dryad) can be cast with no mana, as can [Bounty of the Hunt](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bounty+of+the+Hunt). [Quirion Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Quirion+Ranger) can help [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20100730000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest)s work overtime, and a host of other creatures pressure the opponent to prevent them from taking advantage quickly. Neat!
