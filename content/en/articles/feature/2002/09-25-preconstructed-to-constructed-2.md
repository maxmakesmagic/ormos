The Way Wild deck introduces the forces of green magic. Green is a color of fast mana, big creatures, and creature enchantments. It also features powerful creature-enhancing sorceries and instants. The focus of green decks is usually to overwhelm your opponent with a horde of small and large creatures, but there are a lot of other possibilities for building green decks.

Green decks usually belong to the "aggro" category. This lingo term describes decks that try to win aggressively, by playing their cards instead of reacting to their opponent's plays. Most green creatures fit well in aggro decks, as they usually cost less mana and are larger than those in other colors.

**Focus:** Play lots of elves, enchant them with Blanchwood Armor or pump them with Giant Growth, Might of Oaks, and Elvish Champions to win the damage race. Creeping Molds are versatile enough to deal with a wide range of problems such as Circle of Protection: Green, Ensnaring Bridge, and can also be used on a land to slow your opponent down.

**Weaknesses:** The deck has absolutely no way of removing an opposing creature (except during combat, or by using Creeping Mold on the occasional artifact creature). It's also vulnerable to mass creature removal.

**Matchup-Specific Strategies**  
 Against another creature deck, the game comes down to a race. You should always be playing all your creatures and attacking as much as possible. Keep an eye on your opponent's life total and see if you can finish him or her with a surprise Might of Oaks.

Against more defensive decks, you should play a little differently. If you fear that your opponent might play Mutilate, Earthquake, or Wrath of God, keep a few creatures in your hand and lead the attack with the first two or three elves you draw. Once your opponent clears the board, play more elves and keep the pressure up.

There are different ways to play some of the cards. Blanchwood Armor is better played when your opponent is tapped out and cannot bounce or destroy your creature in response. Giant Growth is not meant to be played as an extra three points of damage early, but as a way to remove blockers or to surprise your opponent and finish him or her off. Keep Giant Growths in your hand otherwise.

Variants on green are infinite: blue-green decks featuring Wonder, Quiet Speculation, and Grizzly Fate are currently dominating *Odyssey* Block Constructed tournaments. The only limit to deckbuilding is your imagination. I encourage you to explore the different **Magic** websites and look at the tournament-winning decklists. You might not have the time to trade for the cards these decks use or you may just not like the deck's theme, but it will give you an idea of what cards are considered Constructed-worthy.
