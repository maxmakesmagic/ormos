*Khans of Tarkir* brought us one of the most acclaimed Standard formats in recent memory. In a quest to better understand what mattered and why in this Standard environment, I sat down with some talented planeswalkers and asked, "What card defines the current Standard format?" 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpden15/stark.jpg)

 ***Ben Stark:** "[Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) is probably the most powerful card in Standard. Cards like [Dig Through Time](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dig+Through+Time) and [Treasure Cruise](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasure+Cruise) are better in formats like Modern, but [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino)'s power adjusted for its casting cost is unmatched in Standard."* 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpden15/mackl.jpg)

 ***Valentin Mackl:** "[Temple of Malady](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temple+of+Malady) is played in a huge portion of the tier one decks. The card fixes mana exactly how we need it to with cards like Sadisi, Brood Tyrant and [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) defining the third or fourth turn of most competitive games. The format would likely be very different if it weren't for [Temple of Malady](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temple+of+Malady)."* 

  
  

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpden15/sutcliffe.JPG)

 ***Marshall Sutcliffe:** "[Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino)! A player is doing themselves a huge disservice when they make a deck choice without taking [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) into consideration. It might be the actual best card in the format, and it's so good against so many decks that the ability to beat a turn three or four [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) can be a litmus test for whether or not a deck is playable in this format."* 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpden15/manfield.JPG)

 ***Seth Manfield:** "[Whip of Erebos](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Whip+of+Erebos). Without [Whip of Erebos](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Whip+of+Erebos), the graveyard synergies that are central to a big portion of the tier one decks wouldn't exist. The whip gives us one of the most powerful lategame engines in the format while simultaneously helping us survive as one of the only playable cards with lifelink in the format. It's also extremely important to constellation subthemes that get value when we cast our whip or activate it to return an enchantment creature."* 

  
  

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpden15/pardee.JPG)

 ***Sam Pardee:** "[Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino). You're either playing [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) or you have a very good plan to beat it."*
