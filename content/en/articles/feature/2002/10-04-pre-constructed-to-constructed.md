Looking at the color white, there are usually two main directions you can go: cheap weenie creatures or all-out defense.

For the first variation on Armada, I designed a white weenie deck. This deck type gets it's name from the horde of not-so-big creatures it puts out on the board. As you can see from the list, evasion and other abilities are important for the creatures. The only vanilla creature in the deck is Knight Errant.

**Matchup-Specific Strategies**  
 Against offensive decks, your key cards are flying creatures, Pacifism, and Prismatic Strands. Keep the other creatures for defense. Against defensive decks, you need to draw more creatures than they can counter or destroy.

Another, more defensive way to use white creatures is to use Clerics. The *Odyssey* block offers a wide selection of powerful Clerics that work well together.

**Focus:** This deck plays a lot differently than the first variant. You shouldn't commit your resources to inflict early damage. Keep an eye on Cleric decks. They will become more powerful as the *OnslaughtTM* set brings in Tribal cards (cards that depend on having creatures of a certain type).

White is known for its defensive capabilities. However, it's quite hard to build a white-only defensive deck. White cannot effectively draw extra cards without the help of artifacts, and has no real way of dealing with mass destruction spells that do no damage (Obliterate, Wrath of God). To solve that problem, white players often add another color to their deck. The usual choice is blue for the counterspells and card drawing.

A quite competitive white-blue deck that I played earlier this year was a creatureless Millstone deck based on another of white's themes: life gain. (Credit for the original deck goes to Shane Sams.)

**Focus:** Stay alive. No matter what your opponent plays, your only goal in the first twenty turns is to stay alive and accumulate life points. Once at a safe total, start playing and protecting Millstones and try running your opponent out of cards. 

As you can see, blue was added to the deck only to provide some countermagic power and a few specific multicolored spells (Reviving Vapors, Teferi's Moat). The deck's core is the white life-gain spells. 

One of the funniest aspects of this deck is that it was a lot of fun to compete against the dominant Psychatog decks. (If you're not familiar with the Psychotog deck, it's a blue-black strategy that defends itself until it can get a 20/21 Psychatog through your defenses.) Even with all the cards in the graveyard and seven in hand, a Psychatog player couldn't normally deal more than 33 to 35 points of damage in one turn. If I got to that life total or higher with Comeback Cats, there was very little chance that I could lose. 

Although this deck features more rares than the others, many strategies can be adopted to mimic the same effect. For example, you could use more copies of Ancestral Tribute along with commons like Mental Note and Careful Study. You could substitute Chastise or Kirtar's Desire for Wrath of God. It's also possible to build the deck without Millstones and instead rely on Test of Endurance to win.
