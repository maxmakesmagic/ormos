The Mono-Black vs Mono-Blue matchup is one that is being hashed out at a thousand FNM's across the world every week. Both decks pack powerful punches but have a different strategy to take the game. Mono-Blue is, historically unusually, the aggressor in this matchup, hoping to punch through with efficient creatures and utilise the powerful God Thassa and her Bident to overwhelm the Black player with relentless card advantage. 

Mono-Black has some flexibility in its game plan, but generally aims to keep the Blue deck's devotion count to a reasonable level before going 'over the top' with its hugely powerful creatures. An active discard and disruption suite for Black keeps things interesting, but if that fails to materialise, as happened in this match, Mono-Blue can threaten to outpace the Black deck's slower pace.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/events/2014/gpbei14/r12_jin.jpg)

*Jin tries to puzzle his way through an early Thassa.* 

  

**Game 1** 

Shimizu opened the game with a [Judge's Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Judge%27s+Familiar) and a [Frostburn Weird](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frostburn+Weird). On his attack, Jin had a [Devour Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Devour+Flesh) to keep things from getting out of hand. Shimizu summoned a Thassa and followed with her Bident, crashing in with the powerful blue God and the Weird. 

Jin tried a [Desecration Demon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desecration+Demon), but Shimizu had a [Detention Sphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Detention+Sphere) to knock it out of the way and take the first game in a classic demonstration of Mono-Blue's capacity to just take games from the unprepared or the underperforming. 

**Naoki Shimizu 1 - Xin Jin 0** 

**Game 2** 

The second game started off in a slightly slower fashion. Shimizu had the first play with a lonesome Thassa, Jin upping the ante with a [Desecration Demon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desecration+Demon). Shimizu had a [Detention Sphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Detention+Sphere) for it, upping his devotion and taking the Demon out of contention. Shimizu followed up with a Bident and a [Frostburn Weird](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frostburn+Weird), the latter being promptly [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade)d. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/events/2014/gpbei14/r12_shimizu.jpg)

*Shimizu keeps the pressure on.* 

  

Shimizu had a [Tidebinder Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tidebinder+Mage) to reactivate Thassa, and started crashing in with his God and some attendant [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140821000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s. Jin played a tight defensive game, but Shimizu simply out-drew him over the following turns to take the match. 

**Naoki Shimizu 2 - Xin Jin 0**
