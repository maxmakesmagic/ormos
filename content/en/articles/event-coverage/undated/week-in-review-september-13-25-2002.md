### Magic Invitational

![](https://i.ormos.uk/63/9d/639d641230b9245f414e0724dc0c4b1bdff5ed0323a0d6f0b187dfdb329f929c.jpg) It was a slow couple of weeks, with no major **Magic** events taking place anywhere in the world for me to write about. Instead, I would like to share my thoughts about the upcoming **Magic** Invitational, as well as reintroduce the cast of characters that are about to compete for the chance to make their own **Magic** card.

A year ago, we thought that the Invitational would take place in Rio de Janeiro this winter. You can hardly blame me for not being nearly as excited about flying to Seattle instead - though the fact that we are going to be playing on **Magic** Online goes a long way to soothe the pain. Not that I prefer **Magic** Online to playing with physical cards, but it is incredibly convenient to be able to playtest with proxies and show up without several pounds of cards I might add to my sideboard as a last-minute decision. Also, the fact that players around the world will be able to actually watch the Invitational games rather than read someone's interpretation of them in Feature Match coverage is incredible. A major downside to doing this on **Magic** Online is that there are simply not very many cards programmed in. There can be no Type 1 and many other wackier formats are impossible. The formats that were selected seem bland - fine for a normal tournament but not as fun as I've come to expect from the Invitational itself. 

The greatest detraction from this year's Invitational is no fault of the organizers. It is the absence of the game's best player. Due to school commitments, Kai Budde will not be attending. Considering how he has dominated the tournament scene over the last couple of years, the Invitational is hardly imaginable without him and it is disappointing that he could not find the time to come. Another staple missing from this year's event will be Zvi Mowshowitz - recently hired for a 'real world' job, Mowshowitz could not take the time off, especially not if he hopes to be able to get time off for Pro Tour events. Here is a quick run-down on the players that will be attending.

**1 - 2002 World Champion - Carlos Romao (Brazil)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds02/516.jpg)Although 2002 Worlds served as the first introduction to Romao for most of the **Magic** community, he has been well known in South America for a long time now. Having finished fifth in Grand Prix-Sao Paulo in 1999, he improved on the record at GP-Rio 2000, beating me in the finals. Romao solidified his tournament record with a fourth place finish at the 2000 Latin American Championships. Romao's presence at the event is a sign that South American **Magic** community has matured well enough to produce true pro players, able to compete in the international arena.

  

**2 - Highest Ranked DCI member - Brian Kibler (USA)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTSD02/773.jpg)Kibler's first breakthrough came years ago at Grand Prix-Toronto, where he defeated a field of North American pros and amateurs alike to take first place. There was a long lull in his career as Kibler took time away from **Magic** to pursue other things, but he returned with a vengeance about two years ago and has been a staple figure on the pro scene ever since. He placed third at Pro Tour-Chicago and earned three more Grand Prix Top 8 finishes since. Kibler had a number of strong Pro Tour finishes since, including a Top 16 at Worlds - which allowed him to earn a high enough ranking to qualify for the Invitational without having to rely on the player ballot - where he would have been one of the favorites anyway. Despite a series of strong tournament accomplishments, Kibler remains best-known as a deck builder. With Mowshowitz out of the picture, he may be the player to watch in Constructed portions of the Invitational.

  

**3 - Highest Ranked Player, Pro Points - Jens Thoren (Sweden)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTOSA02/743.jpg)With Kai locking up the Player of the Year race early, Thoren and I competed for the second place, and he deservedly won. Fourth place at PT-Osaka and second place in PT-San Diego provided most of the points that launched him into the spotlight. Every year, one or several new top-tier pros emerge on the scene. Thoren is undoubtedly the best "new" player this year (he's had a pair of Grand Prix Top 8 finishes prior to this season, including 2nd place in Prague, but it is safe to say this was his breakout year).

  

**4 - 2nd Highest Ranked Player, Pro Points - Alex Shvartsman (USA)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphei02/a954.jpg)My claim to fame in **Magic** is that I own the Grand Prix circuit. I've got 18 GP Top 8's (few others broke 10) and countless Top 16 finishes. This year my PT record was also very solid - I placed 3rd in NY as part of team Illuminati and only missed one Day 2 this entire season. If Kibler is best known as a deck builder, I am best known as a writer, having contributed at one time to every major **Magic** web site and magazine from The Dojo to *Duelist* and InQuest. Do not look for me to finish well at the Invitational (I opened a store just a few months ago and have literally zero time to playtest), but I can promise a good tournament report afterward.

  

**5 - 3rd Highest Ranked Player, Pro Points - Olivier Ruel (France)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a990.jpg)Like me, Olivier is a Grand Prix fanatic, traveling almost as extensively to events. He has 9 GP Top 8's to his name and like me, he earned his first PT Top 8 this year, finishing second in Osaka. Ruel had a poor record at last year's Invitational where he and his brother Antoine were tied for last place - so he has nowhere to go but up!

  

**6 - 4th Highest Ranked Player, Pro Points - Dave Humpherys (USA)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a704.jpg)"The Hump" is one of the most experienced players at this year's Invitational. A winner of PT-Washington D.C. as part of team Your Move Games, his pro career kicked off back in 1998 where he placed fourth at PT-NY. This season, Humpherys started off by placing fourth at PT-New Orleans and finished off fourth at Worlds - a well rounded season to be sure. Humpherys is also the champion of GP-Minneapolis and Columbus, and has made Top 8's in St. Louis and Pittsburgh. There is something that works for him in the Midwest I suppose. Humpherys is traveling the world at the moment and will be back in Boston just in time for the Pro Tour, then it is on to the west coast and a new job for him. Will he have time to test for the Invitational or can his raw talent see him through?

  

**7 - 5th Highest Ranked Player, Pro Points - Gabriel Nassif (France)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptny01/a762.jpg)The first anyone heard of Nassif was in the very first Grand Prix of last season, where he lost to Kai Budde in the finals. His team lost to Kai in the finals of the very next event - Pro Tour-New York. Although he did not earn any more Top 8s since then, he did put up a string of solid finishes that ended up carrying him into the Invitational. Nassif is among a group of very good players to have emerged from France this year - Tenenbaum, Haim, Meraghni, Lauriol are all likely to remain a strong force on the Tour this year.

  

**8 - Top Ranked North American Player, Pro Points - Brian Davis (USA)**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/99/c3/99c339f1f8630ef311f4aac98536fd603f68eb7de55560247d2f92e51a25edbd.jpg)The youngest player at this Invitational, Davis broke through at Pro Tour-Chicago, where he made the finals but lost to Bob Maher. This year, Davis earned his second PT Top 8, finishing fourth at PT-Nice and he has numerous strong GP and PT finishes - enough to out-do other American pros for this Invitational spot. 

  

**9 - Top Ranked Latin American Player, Pro Points - Diego Ostrovich (Argentina)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/LAC01/798.jpg)Although not as well-known as Carlos Romao, Ostrovich made Top 8 at GP-Buenos Aires and played his way to the finals of the 2001 Latin American Championships before entering everyone's radar as one of this year's Worlds Top 8 competitors. Romao, Ostrovich, and a number of other Latin American players worked together for Worlds. Their testing obviously paid off. Will they capitalize on this success and keep the team together to prepare for Boston and Houston or was this a one-shot deal? It is pretty likely that the two will work together for the Invitational regardless.

  

**10 - Top Ranked European Player, Pro Points - Eivind Nitter (Norway)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPGOT01/954.jpg)Last year's European Champion, Nitter earned most of the points he needed to get this invite winning Pro Tour-Nice. Interestingly, he and Romao are the only Pro Tour winners of the season who will actually attend the Invitational. Budde isn't going, and Ken Ho and Farid Meraghi fell a few points short of earning invites despite their wins.

  

**11 - Top Ranked APAC Player, Pro Points - Itaru Ishida (Japan)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPkob01/834.jpg)Ishida is still chasing a Pro Tour Top 8. He made his way to the Invitational on the strength of a string of top GP finishes, including a win of the largest GP ever in Kobe. APAC players are steadily gaining ground on the pro scene with more countries earning Top 8 finishes (as Sim Han How has for Malaysia in Worlds) - but APAC pros did not post as impressive a result as they have last year with Fujita's second-place finish at PT-Tokyo. There is always the current season.

  

**12 - Highest Ranked Player, Player's Ballot - Jon Finkel (USA)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/864.jpg)Finkel has long enjoyed the undisputed title of best player in the world, until Kai came along - and even then it was some time until the German Juggernaut took over that position in most players' minds. Many still say that Finkel is at least as talented as Kai, if not more. The major difference is that Kai cares more about the game. He prepares for events and **Magic** is a major part of his life. Not so for Jon, who has been moving further and further away from the game - which is the major reason why this has been the worst season of his career, with Finkel falling off the Pro Tour and earning only 15 points throughout the season. It came as no surprise to anyone that Finkel was voted in. If he were to quit the game entirely, he would probably still make it into the next several Invitationals on the strength of his past accomplishments, not to mention Shadowmage Infiltrator and "Finkel vs. Garfield" Deckmaster box earning him recognition with the casual players. Finkel also happens to be very active on **Magic** Online and may turn out to be one of the more prepared players at this event - at least where the software interface is concerned.

  

**13 - Third Highest Ranked Player, Player Ballot - Gary Wise (UK)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTSD02/764.jpg)This is also not much of a surprise. In addition to posting solid tournament finishes through the season, Wise has continued to write for the *Sideboard*, remaining well-positioned in the public's eye. 

  

**14 - Fourth Highest Ranked Player, Player's Ballot - Dave Price (USA)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pttok01/897.jpg)Price continues to reap the benefits of his fame as the King of Beatdown. Although often forced to re-quality for the Pro Tour, he has turned it to his advantage when it comes to the Invitational, as the average PTQ player is more readily willing to identify with him than an average gravy-trainer. 

  

**15 - Substitute - Chris Pikula (USA)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptnor01/a740.jpg)Kai's invitation goes to the fifth player on the Ballot, who was Chris Pikula. Away from **Magic** to concentrate on his job, this stock broker is still extremely well-known in **Magic** community, half for his sharp tongue and half for Meddling Mage - the card he got to design for winning a previous Invitational. Pikula has not played in many premier events this season, but he's won an Invitational before so it is certainly not impossible that he might do so again.

  

**16 - Substitute - Tomi Walamies (Finland)**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/993.jpg)Pro Tour-New Orleans finalist, Walamies' sixth place on the player ballot was largely helped by his deck design. Walamies is credited with the "Punisher" OBC white-blue deck, as well as "Walamies.dec" from PT-New Orleans. Name recognition is king when it comes to the player ballot, and it allowed the Scandinavian player to earn the pass-down slot once Zvi Mowshowitz declined his.

  

### Coming Up: Pro Tour-Boston, GP-Hamburg, Onslaught Prerelease

![](https://i.ormos.uk/53/12/5312ffae830f21e9a4afa3c7718c5f67063035a467ec4b780ce5e346a88b46fc.jpg)Pro players will meet in Boston this weekend to compete in the fourth annual team Pro Tour. As this is the first Pro Tour of the season and there is no Masters held at it, players should have had all the time in the world to practice the format. Of course, not all the better teams will get past the sealed deck portion of the event. This is the first Pro Tour stop in Boston, which I and other New Yorkers can hardly approve of - the Northeast Pro Tour has been held in New York every time previously. Our loss is Massachusetts residents' gain and we shall see how the move affects spectator attendance. 

The last *Odyssey* Block Constructed Grand Prix is going to be held in Hamburg at the same time. The lack of pros will make the event far less exciting, but may offer a chance for up and coming players to have a slightly easier shot at qualifying for Houston. Finally, the rest of the world will get to sample a brand new set over the weekend. *Onslaught's* official release date is October 7 - a week after the Prerelease.

### Magic Trivia

Last week's question: How is it possible to kill your opponent on turn 1 in OBC?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/13/d0/13d0564b23c6acb90095a52c5fb9d2b34864d82e25db7c60a257918159d5a31a.jpg)Your hand must consist of Mountain, Rites of Initiation, four Basking Rootwallas and Anger. Play Mountain and cast the Rites, discarding Rootwallas and Anger. By the time Rites resolve, you will have four 5/1 haste creatures in play, ready to finish off the opponent.

### Play of the Week

Courtesy of Jeff Cunningham

(This is probably the most powerful play of the week I've posted since the initiation of this column)

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/e6/29/e6297b989bf04ba30c22ca4da7f6d9db6229476f446f3c60fc8e9da621eb066d.jpg"><br><i>For want of a swamp...</i></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

"I've been playing **Magic** for 8 years, and what happened to me this morning on **Magic** Online is probably the craziest play I've seen in that time. I was playing against a low-rated player, in the second game of the match after winning the first, and he had double mulliganed. I was playing a black-red deck that splashed green for Roar of the Wurm, and he was playing a rather sub-par green-blue deck. 

Although his draw was good for a double mulligan (Aquamoeba, Skywing Aven), by the seventh turn he had only 4 islands in play, with 0 cards in hand, at 4 life, against my Roar token enchanted with Shade's Form, two Barbarian Outcasts, and a swamp with freshly cast Caustic Tar on it. He untapped and drew... Churning Eddy. He killed my Wurm token, dropped my Shade's Form, bounced my swamp, killed both Outcasts, AND deactivated my Tainted Peak. He went on to win the game, and the match."

### Bad Play of the Week

Courtesy of Dave Meddish

"I guess you could call this the Battle of the Internet Scrub Writers. It the finals of a Friday night draft, I had a creature-light/removal-heavy b-r deck, while across from me, my opponent, David Caldwell, was had a b-u-r deck filled with insane bombs like Shadowmage Infiltrator, Guiltfeeder and Savage Firecat.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5a/31/5a31ff50609891f83753e29eb06e9d03b535e170e723e92ac29123dfe38d0859.jpg)I had gotten in a few points of damage in game one when Caldwell dropped an Ember Beast and Savage Firecat on consecutive turns, with me only having a Dusk Imp for defense. I do hold a Mutilate, however, and I'm trying to get up to seven swamps or find another removal spell. 

I survive one hit from the Firecat, but I'm now at seven life with eight lands in play (six swamps), holding a Mutilate and Patriarch's Desire. If I can get both through, I can pull the match out. So I start by playing Mutilate - and Caldwell taps out to Syncopate it, thusly creating a 0/0 Firecat. 

Suffice it to say, I went on to win the game and the match. And Caldwell earned a great deal of razzing for that one. But anyone who gets an Infiltrator can't be too unhappy."
