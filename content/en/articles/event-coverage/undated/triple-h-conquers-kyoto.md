## Grand Prix Kyoto 2013

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4d/35/4d359d2ef0ec1acdbc31204b410598e00dc0be30fd8cf1d48ab31ca16ad66720.jpg)

Congratulations to Rich Hoaen, Mike Hron, and Alexander Hayne, winners of Grand Prix Kyoto 2013!

The three foreigners stormed into Kyoto on a whim. After a long break from the game and armed with a weekend off work, Pro Tour Geneva Champion, Mike Hron, paired up with limited master, Rich Hoaen, to play in Kyoto. They needed a third. Recalling his experience in the finals of Grand Prix Montreal 2011, Rich Hoaen asked Alexander Hayne if he would like to join Mike Hron for Kyoto. Hayne obliged, and, after a tense finals match against Makahito Mihara, Kentarou Yamamoto, and Jun'ichiro Bandou, the three became Grand Prix Champions.

The last Team Limited Grand Prix in Japan was in Osaka over eight years ago. While many sets have come out in the interim, Japanese players came out of the woodwork to try their hands at *Theros* Sealed this weekend. Due to the skill required in *Theros* Team Limited, super-teams such as Martin Juza, Shuhei Nakamura, and Yuuya Watanabe, Akira Asahara, Shu Komuro, and Masaya Kitayama, and Shouta Yasooka, Katsuhiro Mori, and Tomoharu Saito were seen at the top of the standings throughout the weekend. Between absurdly powerful mono-color devotion decks, blazingly fast red-white decks, and masterfully controlling blue decks, 578 teams became 1 as we crowned the foreign champions.

As the sun sets on the autumn foliage a new page has been turned on Team **Magic**. Congratulations once again to Rich Hoaen, Mike Hron, and Alexander Hayne!

## Semifinals - Hron/Hayne/Hoaen vs. Yasooka/Mori/Saito

*by Nate Price*

 

As soon as the Top 4 was announced, this became the talk of the event site. The two biggest teams in the Top 4 were poised for a Semifinals matchup that no one was going to want to miss. On one side, three of the best Japanese players the world has ever seen in Tomoharu Saito, Shouta Yasooka, and Katsuhiro Mori. On the other side, the foreign invasion force of Rich Hoaen, Alexander Hayne, and Mike Hron. Combined, the two teams have over fifty Grand Prix Top 8s, thirteen Pro Tour Top 8s, and five Pro Tour wins. This was set to be an epic match, and it certainly didn't disappoint.

It was very curious to see the two teams' decks after their draft. Somehow, the teams ended up splitting the colors such that Hayne, Hron, and Hoaen had no blue or green drafters, while Saito, Yasooka, and Mori had no black drafters. Such an extreme skew is rare to see, even in a six-man draft like this. In the end, both teams appeared quite happy with their decks.

### Alexander Hayne (Black/Red) vs. Shouta Yasooka (Green/White/Blue)

Hayne and Yasooka were the first match to finish, though it took a full three games to get a result. Yasooka was the first up to bat, landing an early [Fleecemane Lion](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fleecemane+Lion), which he proceeded to turn into a monster on the fourth turn. Normally, this would signal an aggressive posture, but Hayne's deck had come out swinging, as well. Between [Erebos's Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Erebos%27s+Emissary), [Ill-Tempered Cyclops](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ill-Tempered+Cyclops), and [Felhide Minotaur](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Felhide+Minotaur), Hayne had quite an army, forcing Yasooka's Lion to play defensive duties. The role of offense was relegated to a [Staunch-Hearted Warrior](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Staunch-Hearted+Warrior) onto which Yasooka bestowed a [Thassa's Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thassa%27s+Emissary). The 7/7 got in for one hit before being cut down to size by a full defensive effort from Hayne, who lost his own monstrous Cyclops in the process.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/32/7f/327fd66df7bef7eddc57ba992fd1053b10a7d371250502c62b24a226c9d1ba1a.jpg)  
*Alexander Hayne*

With one 7/7 removed, Hayne was disheartened to see Yasooka tap eight mana to make a 7/7 [Mistcutter Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistcutter+Hydra) to replace it. He had to take the hit before he was able to untap and kill it with [Sip of Hemlock](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sip+of+Hemlock). These two massive swings ended up being too much, as it let the Lion begin to attack. Hayne's defensive efforts proved futile, as Yasooka added an [Agent of Horizons](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Agent+of+Horizons) and [Wavecrash Triton](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wavecrash+Triton) on his next turn to seal the way to victory.

As exciting as the first game had been, the second was anticlimactic to the same degree. Hayne got rolling with an [Arena Athlete](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arena+Athlete) wearing some big boy pants. Yasooka tried to set up defenses with [Pheres-Band Centaurs](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pheres-Band+Centaurs) and [Mistcutter Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistcutter+Hydra), but they both died to [Sip of Hemlock](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sip+of+Hemlock). When Hayne used a third Sip to remove a [Wavecrash Triton](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wavecrash+Triton), Yasooka just laughed and picked up his cards.

"Ok," he said nodding.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d2/da/d2dad900304b128b520f89702121dab56c928ed9b5805b0f2c5d7c78f15af0a0.jpg)  
*Shouta Yasooka*

That laughter died quickly in the final game of the match, as he found himself behind once again. This time, an [Insatiable Harpy](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insatiable+Harpy) picked up a [Dragon Mantle](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Mantle) and began to drastically change both players' life totals. Yasooka looked to be getting back into the swing of things with a [Fabled Hero](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fabled+Hero) with [Feral Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Invocation), but a [Sip of Hemlock](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sip+of+Hemlock) sent it to an early grave. The same held true for a [Nimbus Naiad](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nimbus+Naiad)-wearing [Wavecrash Triton](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wavecrash+Triton) that Yasooka tried to use for defense. With his gas depleted and no answer for Hayne's troops, Yasooka conceded and turned to lend aid to his teammates.

### Mike Hron (Black/Red) vs. Katsuhiro Mori (Blue/Red)

This match was not as quick as the one between Hayne and Yasooka, but it was certainly less interesting, mostly because both decks were so insanely defensive. Mori's deck featured two copies of [Akroan Horse](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Akroan+Horse) to spew out a neverending stream of defenders. Hron's deck, meanwhile, was a heavy black deck that tended towards longer games with his Gray Merchants of Asphodel.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/12/ee/12ee4da5441dd2375c86bc690e70aecde44a83cab9b40cd9b759602b392e4d40.jpg)  
*Mike Hron*

In the first game, things played out just like this, with Mori finding both of his Horses, while Gray Merchant drained a large chunk of life for Hron once the board cluttered with tokens. With the ground stall looming, Hron peeled a [Mogis's Marauder](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mogis%27s+Marauder), allowing him to swing with his team and knock Mori down to 4. He followed up with an [Insatiable Harpy](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insatiable+Harpy), and Mori couldn't find an answer before the flier finished him off.

In the second game, the [Insatiable Harpy](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insatiable+Harpy) and Gray Merchant once again set up the lifegain express, matched by a more aggressive board from Mori. ## Finals - Hron/Hayne/Hoaen vs. Mihara/Yamamoto/Bandou

It all came down to this. It has been a rare occurrence for a non-Japanese player to take home a Japanese Grand Prix, and an even rarer one still for it to be a team event. The last time that happened was all the way back in 2001, when Chris Benafel and Team ABU stole the show in Yokohama. Here in Kyoto, over a decade later, Mike Hron, Alex Hayne, and Rich Hoaen have the chance to repeat history. Standing in their way is the highest-ranked player left in the tournament: Makihito Mihara. Joining Mihara are Jun'ichiro Bandou and Kentarou Yamamoto, both with Grand Prix Top 8s to their name. The battle would be fierce, as there was quite a lot on the line. Beyond money and prestige, there is pride on the line, as Japan has a long history of defending its Grand Prix from outside victors.

The Agent dropped Hayne all the way down to one before earning the concession.

Hayne turned things around in the second game, using a hastily-assembled [Shipwreck Singer](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shipwreck+Singer)/[Returned Phalanx](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Returned+Phalanx) duo to utterly dominate the early board. Even when Yamamoto got an Anthousa, Setassan Hero, into play and enchanted it with [Aqueous Form](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aqueous+Form), Hayne was able to race it. When he flashed in a [Horizon Chimera](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horizon+Chimera) to both add to his clock and detract from Yamamoto's, Hayne's advantage was sealed.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e7/a3/e7a3d4c90369bcb40caec93e2db1c3628d0baaf73356a26aebfb2d935fbc3009.jpg)  
*Kentarou Yamamoto* 

For such a well-fought first two games of the match, the third left a lot to be desired. Yamamoto's deck failed to cough up more than two lands, and he conceded without putting up a fight.

### Mike Hron (Green/White) vs. Makihito Mihara (Blue/Black/White)

The first game of the final match was a short and sweet affair. Hron opened with a [Favored Hoplite](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Favored+Hoplite) and [Wingsteed Rider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingsteed+Rider), enhancing them with [Feral Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Invocation) and [Dauntless Onslaught](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dauntless+Onslaught). A [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) for good measure dropped Mihara to two on the fifth turn, and he was quick to reach for his sideboard.

Mihara exerted control of the second game early, stripping a [Wingsteed Rider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingsteed+Rider) from Hron with a [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize). He then let his rares do the talking, adding both [Triad of Fates](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Triad+of+Fates) and [Daxos of Meletis](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daxos+of+Meletis) to his side of the table. The Triad was used in an offensive capability against Hron, providing the safety of a [Flicker](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flicker) should Hron have anything too crazy to stop Daxos.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d8/4f/d84fa02c8237cbe942c396c6e94ae1b18641b73e27f25a93a6c40d143a62c1e7.jpg)  
*Makihito Mihara* 

Mihara tried to force his way past Hron's 2/4 [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad) with a [Battlewise Valor](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Battlewise+Valor), but a [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) kept things as they were. He tried again on the following turn with a [Boon of Erebos](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boon+of+Erebos), but a [Feral Invocation](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Invocation) again kept things static. Still, Mihara was able to ratchet up his offense. He added two [Vaporkin](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vaporkin)s to his side of the table, and Hron was unable to block them, as his Dryad was tied up dealing with Daxos.

And then, one turn, Hron began to attack. He sent his team in, leaving two mana open and a [Nemesis of Mortals](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nemesis+of+Mortals) in play. Smelling [Savage Surge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Surge), Mihara just took the damage and geared up to remove the potentially threatening Dryad with his Triad. Now safe from a trick, Mihara used [Griptide](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Griptide) to send the Nemesis to the top of Hron's deck, right where it could be removed with Daxos. A [Cavern Lampad](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cavern+Lampad) gave Daxos intimidate, and he attacked over to remove the Nemesis and gain an important six life. With that attack, the wind left Hron's sails, and he conceded the game soon thereafter, unable to deal with Daxos.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6f/c8/6fc816dac919fa005b30855effaac4f6d434ea860a80a543949b41dd618154b0.jpg)  
*Mike Hron* 

The final game was the perfect way to end an incredibly interesting event. Stalling on lands early, Hron looked like he might fall behind, yet he was still able to churn out a reasonable army, including [Leafcrown Dryad](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leafcrown+Dryad), [Chronicler of Heroes](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chronicler+of+Heroes), and [Traveling Philosopher](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Traveling+Philosopher)s. He even managed to crack through Mihara's defenses with a [Dauntless Onslaught](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dauntless+Onslaught), getting through for a reasonable amount of damage and killing one of Mihara's creatures.

From there, however, Mihara took over. He began to accrue flier after flier, adding a pair of [Prescient Chimera](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Prescient+Chimera)s to his [Coastline Chimera](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coastline+Chimera), stopping Hron cold. While Mihara was assembling his air force, Hron began to draw lands, creating larger ground creatures. With things nearing a potentially epic end, the levee broke. Mihara added a pair of [Scourgemark](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scourgemark)s to his fliers and attacked, forcing a chump block from Hron. He then added a [Cavern Lampad](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cavern+Lampad) to his largest flier, doing exactly half of Hron's remaining life in damage. With one turn left to go, Hron had no choice but to go for it. With Mihara down to eight life, he used [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) on his [Centaur Battlemaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Centaur+Battlemaster), making it an unblockable 6/6. Combined with his other attackers, he was able to force through exactly the amount of damage needed to finish Mihara off, ending an incredible match, the perfect cap to an incredible tournament.

## Top 5 Cards

*by Ben Swartz*

 

![Gray Merchant of Asphodel](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Gray+Merchant+of+Asphodel)

### 5\. [Gray Merchant of Asphodel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gray+Merchant+of+Asphodel)

One of the most powerful black commons in *Theros*, [Gray Merchant of Asphodel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gray+Merchant+of+Asphodel) was seen throughout the top tables Saturday and Sunday at Grand Prix Kyoto. Thanks to the 12 packs opened by each team during Team Sealed Deck, some teams were able to create a dedicated mono-black deck with multiple Merchants. Played into a black heavy board, one of them could swing a game back into the black deck's favor and two or three would almost always spell game over for the opponent. In the penultimate round of day one, Kenta Shouji used Gray Merchant to buy himself enough turns to fend off Martin Juza.

![Staunch-Hearted Warrior](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Staunch-Hearted+Warrior)

### 4\. [Staunch-Hearted Warrior](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Staunch-Hearted+Warrior)

When *Theros* first came out, people discounted [Staunch-Hearted Warrior](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Staunch-Hearted+Warrior), claiming it was overpriced for it's ability. It came out in force here in Kyoto, with green based decks using him to great effect all weekend. Whether the Warrior was in green-white or green-blue decks, targeting it with [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) or another combat trick usually spelled a two-for-one. When enchanted with [Nimbus Naiad](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nimbus+Naiad), [Hopeful Eidolon](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hopeful+Eidolon), or even [Aqueous Form](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aqueous+Form), the opposing player was usually done for. With many sealed decks sporting two or more copies and being featured in both Finals and Semifinals decks, [Staunch-Hearted Warrior](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Staunch-Hearted+Warrior) proved its own.

![Polukranos, World Eater](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Polukranos%2C+World+Eater)

### 3\. [Polukranos, World Eater](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Polukranos%2C+World+Eater)

One of the most powerful Mythic Rares in *Theros*, [Polukranos, World Eater](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Polukranos%2C+World+Eater), found itself in many decks over the course of the weekend. With one of the most popular sealed archetypes being green based many teams were chomping off opponents left and right with the Hydra. In both the semifinals and the finals, Polukranos made an appearance. In the Semifinals, Kentaro Yamamoto played it on turn four destroying Takayuki Kadono and sending his team to the finals.In the finals, Jun'ichirou Bandou cast it against Rich Hoaen prompting Hoaen to turn to his teammate, Alexander Hayne, and ask, "You didn't pass this, did you?" For a green player this weekend, nothing was more desired than the World Eater.

![Gods Willing](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Gods+Willing)

### 2\. [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing)

There are few cards that have been as innocuously powerful in *Theros* Limited as [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing). In a tempo-oriented format such as this, the protection it offers and the potential unblockability it affords has left many opponents in the dust all weekend long. [Wingsteed Rider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingsteed+Rider) is clearly the flashy choice for the best white common, but [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) is right there behind it, sitting alongside [Hopeful Eidolon](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hopeful+Eidolon) as reasons the 2/2 flier is so feared. Combined with the various heroic creatures in white, blue, and even green, [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) completely changes the clock for a game, often taking a huge chunk out of opponents' life totals, while leaving a monster in its wake.

In the finals of the Grand Prix, Mike Hron used [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) incredibly aggressively, more for its dual role as a heroic enabler and free pass at opponents' life. In the final game of the match, he used it to turn his [Centaur Battlemaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Centaur+Battlemaster) into a 6/6 unblockable beast, giving him exactly the damage needed to steal a game that threatened to slip away. Stories like this were common on the weekend, cementing [Gods Willing](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gods+Willing) its spot in the Top 5 cards of Grand Prix Kyoto.

![Shipwreck Singer](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Shipwreck+Singer)

### 1\. [Shipwreck Singer](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shipwreck+Singer)

One of the most popular archetypes this weekend during Sealed Deck was that of the Blue-Black. The most successful blue-black decks featured [Shipwreck Singer](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shipwreck+Singer). When paired with blockers such as [Returned Phalanx](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Returned+Phalanx), it was able to pick off small opposing creatures turn after turn. In the finals, Alexander Hayne used the combo to pick off Kentarou Yamamoto's creatures, while allowing him to profitably race against Anathousa, Setessan Hero later in the game.

It was certainly clear coming into this event how strong the Singer was, but it was in the redundancy offered by Team Sealed that really gave it a chance to shine. Black/blue was among the most common color combinations played at the top tables, and it was mostly thanks to the ability to play this powerful uncommon. The deep card pools offered a bevy of great defenders to use in conjunction with the Singer, forcing players to often trade down with weaker creatures. It also dominated one of the better aggressive creatures of the weekend: [Vaporkin](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005100444/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vaporkin).
