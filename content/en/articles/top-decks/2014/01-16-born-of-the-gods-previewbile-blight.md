Welcome to the first preview week for *Born of the Gods*. Today's preview card is sure to make a big impact in Standard, and it may have implications in Modern as well. It can be hard to weigh the value of this type of card against all the others like it, but cards as powerful as today's preview don't show up very often. 

In the old days of **Magic** we played [Terror](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terror), and it was quite the deal. We could spend a measly two mana and dispatch huge threats like [Force of Nature](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Force+of+Nature), [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel), or [Shivan Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shivan+Dragon). Many years went by and [Terror](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terror) remained one of the most archetypal, solid, and versatile spells available for black players who needed a good removal spell. 

![Terror](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Terror)

  

Through the years, we've received various two-mana black removal spells. Cards like [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion), [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade), [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat), [Smother](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smother), [Ultimate Price](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ultimate+Price), [Diabolic Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Diabolic+Edict), [Cruel Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Edict), [Devour Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Devour+Flesh), and [Echoing Decay](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Echoing+Decay) have all seen their fair share of high-level tournament play. The two-mana black removal spell has become an institution. It's something we can trust. Long after we're dead and gone, the two-mana black removal spell will still be a tournament-worthy tool. 

History tells us that today's preview card will be good, maybe even great. Let's take a look at the latest powerful two-mana removal spell. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b5/06/b5069ba6d722976e84cb0dcf17da918583f42bb2dfc1cfa5e23a80aab790fb91.jpg)  

Bile Blight may not deal with huge monsters like [Polukranos, World Eater](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Polukranos%2C+World+Eater) or [Stormbreath Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stormbreath+Dragon) in the same way that cards like [Ultimate Price](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ultimate+Price) or [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade) do, but Bile Blight is rarely stuck in your hand the way Standard's other two-mana removal spells are. [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade) is a great card, but mono-black decks are a huge factor in Standard and we have every reason to believe that they will continue to enjoy some level of success. [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade) may be the absolute best two-mana removal spell against Gruul or Selesnya opponents, but in the mono-black matchup it can only target opposing [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s, and, well, that's just not very good. [Ultimate Price](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ultimate+Price) solves this issue to some degree, but cards like [Fleecemane Lion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fleecemane+Lion) and [Boros Reckoner](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Reckoner) are sure to be adopted by a lot of aggressive strategies, and we may find ourselves getting beaten up with a spot removal spell in our hand. Bile Blight suffers no such issues. It's live in the mono-black matchup, and it's going to be great against the red-green-white color combinations and mono-blue also. 

Bile Blight's second line of text is also extremely relevant. It's hard to weigh the value of this little bit of text, but it's sure to make our opponents play the game much differently. Was our opponent planning on attacking with more than one [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)? He or she better think about that decision a bit! In fact, playing any creature with 3 or less toughness in multiples becomes a major liability when there's a chance that Bile Blight might be looming in the future.

Bile Blight's most important application in Standard will be its ability to kill [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter) for two mana. Until now, there was not a worthwhile main deck two-mana spot-removal spell outside of red that could deal with [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter). We can expect [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter) to continue doing well as long as devotion strategies are relevant. Having a two-mana instant that kills [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter), and maybe two [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter)s, will drastically decrease the card's influence on the format. Another card that's sure to be unhappy about Bile Blight is [Pack Rat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pack+Rat), whose strength in Standard will be severely neutered by the presence of this new removal spell. 

Going forward, Bile Blight will be an important tool against token strategies when and if they become popular. For just two mana, we can deal with the entirety of [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession), kill an army of Goblin tokens, or kill any and every [Pestermite](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pestermite) on the table, even if there are thirty billion of them.

I'm sure Bile Blight will see a lot of play in many different decks, but its most obvious home will be in Mono-Black Devotion. Let's put together a Mono-Black Devotion deck that utilizes this new and powerful spot-removal spell. 

Diversifying our removal spells is important. [Hero's Downfall](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hero%27s+Downfall) can be played as a four-of because it's always going to kill exactly what we want it to kill. A card like Bile Blight is inexpensive and versatile, but playing a full four copies may be a bit ambitious before we know what the *Born of the Gods* Standard will look like. Bile Blight won't be very good if the format is just a bunch of control decks, but I strongly suspect we'll continue seeing creatures do well, and that makes Bile Blight one of the stronger cards in the format. 

We should definitely be playing all the other commonly used Mono-Black tools that are available to us. [Pack Rat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pack+Rat) gives us degenerate draws that can take over games and make short work of our opponents. (As long as they don't have Bile Blights of their own to absolutely destroy us.)

[Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter) is legitimately awesome and deserves four slots in our deck for sure. [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter) provides us with a steady stream of card advantage, a great defensive body against aggressive decks, and a whopping three devotion to black. 

[Desecration Demon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desecration+Demon) is a massive flier that will continue ruining days as long as it's Standard legal. [Desecration Demon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desecration+Demon) probably becomes more powerful with the introduction of Bile Blight. Bile Blight is a good way to deal with an army of tokens that tap down the demon every turn. [Desecration Demon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desecration+Demon) is big enough to outrange opposing Bile Blights. This seems like a card that's excellent both with and against today's preview card.

[Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) provides us with some much-needed disruption to protect our [Desecration Demon](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desecration+Demon) from [Selesnya Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Selesnya+Charm), our [Pack Rat](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pack+Rat) from Bile Blight, and our [Nightveil Specter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightveil+Specter) from whatever removal spell our opponent might have to deal with it. It's also an excellent tool to nab things like [Voice of Resurgence](https://web.archive.org/web/20140116000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Voice+of+Resurgence), that can be very powerful against us, especially if they're drawn in multiples.
