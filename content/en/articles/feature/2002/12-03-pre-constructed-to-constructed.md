### Part 4: Dissecting Decay

* * *

Greetings, and welcome once again to the series of articles that show you how to juice a *Seventh EditionTM* theme deck up to a Standard Constructed-format deck. Let's take a look at the black theme deck, Decay.

Decay is actually my favorite of the theme decks because it gives a great look at most of the black card themes. I find that people who are introduced to the black cards through Decay really "get" the color. You strip away cards from your opponent's hand in a variety of ways, destroy creatures to make way for your own, and sacrifice your own key strategic resources (particularly life and creatures) to get a sudden boost in cards or damage to help you overwhelm your opponents before they can stabilize and counter-attack. Add in some return-from-graveyard effects and the occasional damage-something-and-gain-life spell and voila, that's black. So, what do we do to spice Decay up? Not the Macarena, that's for darn sure.

The first deck sticks pretty close to the Decay strategies.

**Focus:** You're going to go after your opponent's hand and life total fairly aggressively in the early game, with cards like Corrupt and Howl from Beyond giving you the chance to finish your opponent off if he or she starts to gain control. Keeping a fairly high land count will benefit your Corrupts and Howls, so drawing a lot of land won't be a bad thing. Along the same lines, if you can trade for a Nightmare or two, they're always an unpleasant surprise for your opponent. The high land count helps you avoid not drawing enough land to power some of the more expensive spells in the deck.

The only wholesale change I've made to Decay is the removal of Serpent Warrior. While the trading of life for power is a central theme of black, paying three life to get your 3/3 creature a single turn earlier than normal is only going to teach someone that such trades are a bad idea, which isn't always true.

Another change I recommend, if you're going to play an Extended or Open format game, is to replace the two Greeds with Phyrexian Arenas. True, Greed can apply a single, short-term rush better than the Arena can, but Phyrexian Arena is cheaper to cast, doesn't require mana to activate, and costs less life for each card you gain.

**Sideboarding:** The sideboard contains Chainer's Edict and Innocent Blood to help against creature heavy decks or decks with black creatures that you're having trouble targeting. On the flip side, Mind Burst and Duress can help you against creature-light or control decks. Duress is also a good catchall if your opponent plays with any particular spell that you have little defense against in your main deck.

When you sideboard for this deck, make sure you don't remove too many of your creatures to add in answers for your opponent's cards. One of your primary defenses against your opponents' strategies is simply to win before they can do whatever terrible thing it is that they want to do.

Now that the *OnslaughtTM* set is legal in Standard, I suggest swapping the Dark Banishings for Cruel Revivals. Cruel Revival can shoot down a wider variety of creatures, and what zombies there are for you to worry about probably can't be targeted by Dark Banishing anyway. Also, since Gravedigger is a zombie, Cruel Revival can retrieve it from your graveyard for reuse. This combination turns your graveyard into a footlocker for storing the creatures you want.

Holding Gravedigger in your hand gives you license to swing with whatever you have on the board. For example, if your ground creatures can't get through but you have Fallen Angel in play, go ahead and use her ability to get through more damage. The creatures you sacrifice can be brought back from your graveyard when you play your 'digger.

If you like the Decay deck's themes and don't have any compunction against trading for valuable rares (or you're just chomping at the bit, ready to go for the jugular), may I suggest this little ditty?

There isn't much difference between this and the earlier deck, at least in terms of theme. However, where the earlier deck is an upgrade from the club that is Decay to a more efficient baseball bat, the second deck is more along the lines of a sledgehammer.

**Focus:** Simply put, hit hard and fast. There are 27 spells for four mana or less, and they're either discard or creatures. Strip your opponent's hand bare with Duresses and Mind Rots while smashing with Foul Imps, Nantuko Shades, and Grinning Demons.

If you get drawn into a long game, your life total's going to show it. However, you shouldn't have a problem finding the mana to play a Corrupt or two to keep you swinging.

**Sideboarding:** Swap in Chainer's Edicts for Mind Rots if you're having trouble getting your creatures through to your opponent. You may want to swap Duresses for Dark Banishings if your opponent has a particularly creature-full deck.
