### 

This is a bonus installment of my series analyzing the cards in *Judgment* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the series [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### Judgment Limited Revisited

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/b0/74b04c551d93ace2acc47fccf6d47151fd094429877af0d378931c4b57dbcb18.jpg)Everyone makes mistakes, and when I analyzed *Judgment* before actually holding the physical cards, I knew I wouldn't be infallible. I made a lot of mistakes in white, with a few in each of the other colors, and this is my attempt to correct that. What follows is a look at those card I overestimated and those I underestimated as well as revised lists that get as close as possible to ranking the cards in each color. Keep in mind that no such list can be absolute as the contents of your deck will invariably change card values, and that everything that follows considers *Judgment* the third pack of a draft following *Odyssey* and *Torment* respectively. Enjoy.

### What Was I Thinking?

These are the cards I erroneously believed to be enormously powerful or merely more playable than they actually are:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7d/8b/7d8b2b2632ca05b1b513524c44bcdf9cd6ba29b4d2b0c646d880a78cc6439de1.jpg)**Spirit Cairn**  
 I've actually had some decent success with Spirit Cairn, but it's only been with some effort in decks that can really optimize it, suggesting it can go later because not everyone will be able to utilize this enchantment. If you get Cephalid Looter or Compulsion or both, do try to get one of these around fifth or seventh, but otherwise, if you don't have the discard methods, pass it for something more consistent.

**Guided Strike**  
 A cheap cantrip that offers the chance to kill a creature at no cost of your own while offering the versatility to do an extra point of damage while cycling, the Strike looks as good as it once did, but it really hasn't been that great for me. Obviously it shouldn't go 10th or 11th as I've seen happen, but I rated it very highly last time and it just doesn't seem to impact the game consistently enough to warrant that kind of praise.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/17/36/17366cbc6663386629d9f5f24150bf8902fbd9c232399abd2e82642c86600d1d.jpg)**Pulsemage Advocate**  
 Possessed of an interesting ability, I underestimated the brutality of its drawback, making its use only worthwhile if you have a must-kill creature you want to make unkillable. Without that, it's merely a 1/3 for three, and while that can be acceptable, you should be hoping to do better.

**Aven Fogbringer**  
 I loved this card when I first saw it, but I now know that it's just a glorified Dusk Imp. The coming into play ability can prove insane on turn four, especially if playing first and it can help you find your duplicate mana by returning your own land to your hand, but in the end you want your flyers to hit and hit hard. The Fogbringer moves down the list for being too easy to kill.

**Flash of Insight**  
 If it smells like Impulse, tastes like Impulse and feels like Impulse, it's got to at least be almost as good as Impulse, right? Nope. The Flash is a very slow and expensive card that won't get you card advantage very easily. Before I'd actually played it, it sounded like a good deal, automatically getting you two cards, but you often won't have the resources to abuse it. Playable, but not great.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/9e/749e6edd985189ab8856635a5b0b14032db37c9d6a84cd259a1f94e4234b52ef.jpg)**Firecat Blitz**  
 'It's like an X spell' people were saying in the early goings, but so was Soul Echo. The Blitz doesn't actually sink to those depths, but it doesn't offer you anything in the way of versatility, doing next to nothing until you have the bare minimum of six or seven land in play. Decent as a finisher, that's really all it is.

**Seedtime**  
 Sad to say, while I still think this is going to be a Constructed wrecking ball, Seedtime doesn't lend itself well to Limited play. Simply, it's very situational, with at most half of any one deck you play against consisting of blue cards and most of those being creatures. It's likely that your blue opponents will have instants, and Seedtime is definitely sideboardable (and potentially game winning) if you've seen a few, but there will be times where you'll die without having cast the one sitting in your hand.

### Better Than I Thought

Simply, in my talks with other gamers and my own games, these are the cards that have proven themselves to be better than I originally thought:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/a0/3aa0062a83e295d619ec2aa81420b2451eeac14739095c52d846fa67bb4d6994.jpg)**Battle Screech**  
 Wow, is this card destructive. With the wee birdies making flashback an easy thing, Screech is the way blue-white players the world round will be breaking open their tables for the next four months. Battle Screech makes Keep Watch a nightmare, attacking with fliers makes it nearly impossible to stop you from getting evasion damage in. If nothing else, Battle Screech wins the aggressive race by providing you with four chump blockers. That's a lot of good for a very affordable four mana.

**Prismatic Strands**  
 While I don't think I want more than one in a deck, I definitely want one. Stopping Overrun, Shower of Coals, Violent Eruption and the like from optimal effect (or any effect in the case of the red spells), the key to Prismatic Strands is the effective casting cost of zero once it's in the graveyard. Don't pick it too high if you have other damage prevention effects, as they're pretty much interchangeable, but if you do get one, this is the prevention to play.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4a/d6/4ad64dc56b4578067c98f58fe0f45b32a8e3fc81fd6e6e0ac7f82e9fc6028a51.jpg)**Nomad Mythmaker**  
 The Mythmaker, while appearing harmless, is a potential powerhouse that can only be judged by the other cards in your deck. The tricks you can do with this card and Floating Shield are ludicrous, with the sacrifice of Shield and activation of the Mythmaker combining to protect all of your creatures from pretty much anything, and the recurrence of Shade's Form creating havoc, leaving all of your creatures pretty much invulnerable. You get your chances at the Mythmaker in the third pack, by which time you'll usually know how good it will be in your deck, so use your best judgment: with the right enchantments, it can be a first pick.

**Benevolent Bodyguard**  
 This card is really good! Sure, it's looks innocent enough. Just a dork 1/1, right? Wrong. This is the counter to your Ghastly Demise, your protection for three attackers, your sacrificial lamb to Wormfang Drake and the beginning of your mana curve. In short, for a mid-level pick, the Bodyguard will inevitably give you a lot those cards picked before it will not, and will always find itself useful. 

**Trained Pronghorn**  
 I thought the Pronghorn looked awful when I first saw it, but it actually does serve a purpose, providing you with a threshold/flashback/madness outlet while giving you a card that can cause the green mage fits. The Pronghorn, if augmented by Seton's Desire, Arcane Teachings and the like, can become a real powerhouse, while at the same time providing some synergy with any red mass kill spells you might be using. It's not automatic for the main deck, but it can make the grade.

**Wormfang Behemoth**  
 I knew the Behemoth was strong, but I've seen the sheer size of the thing just dominate a couple of games against non-green opponents. Yes, the nightmare drawback it brings with it can be damaging if your opponent is able to get a suppressing enchantment like Kirtar's Desire or Lost in Thought on it, but you don't have to play it until it's the last card in your hand, and it seems unlikely you'll be playing it too often when there are other castable spells of importance available to you. A 1st pick as often as not.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9e/3d/9e3d72f6bd0714cf33bc40a336211d592e2c3f150c0288ebc793e1439199db7e.jpg)**Hapless Researcher**  
 This card's inclusion here is in part testimony to the strength of Wormfang Drake, which quick creatures like the Researcher will help you exploit, but I wouldn't suggest you start playing Confessors and Dedicated Martyrs. The Researcher gets you two cards closer to threshold which providing a madness outlet and a beginning to your mana curve, turning one of your hand-held lands into a random card later in the game. Not a must-play, the Researcher could often get played over more powerful cards for the synergy it will bring to your deck.

**Web of Inertia**  
 This card could prove absolutely useless in its main deck capacity, so I'd recommend it primarily as a sideboard card against aggressive decks that either use their graveyard cards for other things or have trouble getting cards into the graveyard in the first place. Throw in the potential for Advocates to soft-lock the game and you have a potential game-winner. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/86/e4/86e4ccd9baaa2ecc628dd1feea3ff55933be44c1dfd807d32f098360f8bdb977.jpg)**Cephalid Constable**  
 I saw the power in this guy, but when I wrote him up, I hadn't really worked out the importance of certain combos. Shade's Form makes the Constable an absolute monster, while Ghostly Wings can make it almost as dangerous. On its own, it likely doesn't do enough, with its 1/1 body having trouble penetrating the weakest of defenses, but look for the ways your deck can augment this particular octopus: get the Form down and attack on turn four and the game may be over before it really began. 

**Cunning Wish**  
 Of course I write this card up as unplayable and then decide to run it in my first team sealed tournament. Cunning Wish will more often than not provide you the option of being any of seven or eight sub-standard instants that may each have some effect worthy of offering to your game. Get an Envelop, Laquatus's Disdain or Pay No Heed, for example, and you have a card that can either save your damaged creature or counter most spells, and the options will often reach much further than that. Remember: if you have sac lands, don't be afraid to draft that one off color kill spell. Even if you don't run it main, you may end up wishing you could Wish for it.

**Toxic Stench**  
 I'm still not a big fan of this card, but it definitely earns its place. Being a size-altering instant, the Stench can either be used to out and out kill a one-toughness creature or to shrink an attacker during combat so its blocker might survive. After threshold, Toxic Stench becomes very powerful, and while the inability to affect black creatures is annoying, this isn't *Odyssey-Odyssey-Torment* anymore: people will have their non-black creatures, so it will always be useful.

**Worldgorger Dragon**  
 I rated the Dragon much lower than most initially because I thought too much. I thought about how I couldn't cast my other spells, I thought about Kirtar's Desire and Lost in Thought and Cagemail, I thought about losing my other permanents and I thought about putting too many eggs in one basket. Really, the only thing I should have been thinking about was attacking three times and winning the game. That seems pretty good.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bb/8e/bb8e71c64e118a7c9502a54ce5d840ba87b451f5cbdfb36b33ff97159c3ba27d.jpg)**Lava Dart**  
 This card is really good. For one mana, you're casting Fire, one of the best cards in Standard right now. The Dart will rid you of two annoying creatures and their special abilities or one two-toughness attacker that's causing you trouble, and the flashback allows you to use it in tandem with another card to kill larger creatures without worrying too much about card disadvantage. You don't want too many effects like this one in your deck, but the Dart should always be good enough for you to run.

**Ember Shot**  
 I don't love the idea of this thing in every deck, but if you have acceleration and lots of burn, you can definitely do worse. Likewise, if you have a lot of discard needs, this will fill them just fine if you aren't close to seven mana, while the spell's effect can be extreme. Not an automatic main decker, but it's far more feasible than I gave it credit for.

**Barbarian Bully**  
 While the random discard seems harsh, the mere presence of this ability makes the Bully pretty close to unblockable at times. What are you going to do, throw your Angelic Wall or Teroh's Faithful in front of it? Four life is a lot to pay against the color with Browbeat, Book Burning, Blazing Salvo and the Firebeasts. The existence of those cards means you really need to use your life total wisely. The Bully may just look like a Grey Ogre, but it packs a lot more punch than that.

**Infectious Rage**  
 First I thought this card was amazing. Then I found out the creature it lands on second is random and I became completely disenchanted with the entire thing. Now I know it's neither the nine I thought it was nor the four I thought it was, but instead it falls somewhere in between. The Rage will kill the first creature you play it on more often than not and offer the potential for more kills and possible offensive bolstering. A solid card for your more aggressive decks, it's especially good if you have a bunch of two-toughness or bigger flyers.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/magic/general/Swirling_Sandstorm.jpg)**Swirling Sandstorm**  
 I initially rated the Sandstorm very low because I was putting it into the context of red, and played with other red cards, where this thing is going to be really bad. That said, the Sandstorm can function especially well as a splash card in blue-white, with that deck often includes many flyers, damage prevention spells and ways to get to threshold. Don't pick it too high, because you'll get it low. Great with Wonder.

**Phantom Centaur**  
 The Centaur only makes the list because I didn't realize just how insane it was. Yes, I understood this was one of the best cards green had to offer, but I didn't fully grasp the way it would dominate the game the moment it hit the table. The Centaur is like a Blastoderm that can't be blocked by black creatures and doesn't necessarily go away after three turns, and is therefore, in short, amazing. Get it if you see it.
