<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, December 18: 11:27 a.m. - Round 9: Antonino De Rosa vs. Alex Lieberman</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Today's feature match action opens with a contest between two good friends sporting identical decklists. Antonino De Rosa needs little introduction. In addition to being the reigning U.S. National Champion, De Rosa had a rather successful weekend in Yokohama; he finished 2nd in the teams portion and top 32 in the individual portion. Alex Lieberman is another member of the infamous Seven Kings team. De Rosa himself had long called Lieberman &quot;the best U.S. player that no one knows about,&quot; but after the past year, the last part of the title probably needs to be revised. </p>&#10;      <p>These men are proven constructed masters, each having won a Grand Prix last season. Since the Kings are sitting at 7-1, there's a good chance that the winner of this match will repeat that feat. The Pros are playing what is likely the best deck in the format, the ill-nasty <a>Ichorid</a> dredge deck.</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 1</h3>&#10;      <p>Lieberman got to kick things off, albeit with a 6-card hand. He played a <a>Polluted Delta</a> and passed, then Antonino &quot;entered the tank&quot; for a solid minute before even playing his land. The <a>Ichorid</a> deck is quite explosive, but one must really work to maximize its potential; any misstep can cost a player the game. (Besides, if the game's only going to last a few turns, one might as well take the time to consider one's plays carefully). Lieberman played a turn 2 <a>Zombie Infestation</a> to answer Antonino's turn 1 <a>Putrid Imp</a>. Antonino then cast <a>Tolarian Winds</a>, chaining several dredge creatures and filling his graveyard. He attacked for 2, then flashed back <a>Cabal Therapy</a> naming <a>Psychatog</a>. Lieberman made a Zombie token in response by pitching an <a>Ichorid</a> and a <a>Psychatog</a>. Antonino, unfazed by Lieberman's ruse, named <a>Psychatog</a> anyway; Alex in fact had another one that he was forced to discard. Since Antonino's prior dredging action had netted him 3 <a>Ichorid</a>s, Alex wasted little time scoopin' em up.</p>&#10;      <p>De Rosa 1 Lieberman 0</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 2</h3>&#10;      <p>Alex played a <a>Watery Grave</a> tapped, and once again Antonino spent a long time thinking before even making a land drop. Antonino ultimately fetched an <a>Overgrown Tomb</a>, imprinted an <a>Ichorid</a> on a <a>Chrome Mox</a>, and dropped into play the Kings' mirror match tech--<a>Compost</a>. Lieberman had to content himself with a turn 3 <a>Psychatog</a>. Antonino cast <a>Careful Study</a> and answered back with a Tog of his own. Alex debated the pros and cons of going all-in with his <a>Psychatog</a> before sucking it up and going for it. After a <a>Tolarian Winds</a> and a lot of dredging, he managed to fill his graveyard enough to make Tog lethal and find a <a>Filth</a> to make said Tog unblockable.</p>&#10;      <p>De Rosa 1 Lieberman 1</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 3</h3>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/dc/61/dc61017f0421bae677e45ab1b14b21cc418484968317ad22569e0b9eeb8a47fe.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>De Rosa and The Wolf chat about Ant's win.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Each player mulliganed, then it was Alex's turn to start the game with a <a>Compost</a> in play. De Rosa played one of his own, then Lieberman dropped a <a>Psychatog</a>. Antonino then played a <a>Zombie Infestation</a>. Lieberman sent Big Toofs into the red zone before playing another, and Antonino played one of his own. A <a>Deep Analysis</a> and <a>Flash</a>back over the next few turns netted Alex several extra cards, making his Togs much more potent than Antonino's, while De Rosa played another <a>Compost</a>. Somehow, even after several turns of hot Tog-on-Tog action, neither player allowed the other to draw any cards off <a>Compost</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Each player played a <a>Stinkweed Imp</a>, then the two spent several turns passing without attacking or playing any spells. Time ran out before Lieberman could break through with his <a>Psychatog</a>s. On his first turn of extra time, Lieberman commented that he had a plan, but he decided to sit tight for the turn. Antonino drew a card and passed. </p>&#10;      <p>At the start of Lieberman's second turn, Lieberman had two Togs, two <a>Stinkweed Imp</a>s, and 13 life to Antonino's <a>Infest</a>ation, <a>Stinkweed Imp</a>, and 9 life. Nothing of relevance was in either player's graveyard. Lieberman tapped all but one of his mana sources to hardcast an <a>Ichorid</a> and attacked with all his men. Antonino made a Zombie token, blocked the Togs, and went down to 4 from the unblocked creatures. </p>&#10;      <p>At the end of the turn, up to seven cards in hand compliments of his <a>Compost</a>s, Antonino decided to make a move. He cast <a>Tolarian Winds</a>, milling almost his entire deck--including all four <a>Ichorid</a>s--in the process. With the three Zombie tokens he would be able to make, Antonino actually found more than enough damage to kill Lieberman on his final turn. If Alex had played more conservatively, he would have been able to <a>Coffin Purge</a> all of Antonino's <a>Ichorid</a>s and likely force the draw. He didn't seem to mind too much; since one of the friends would enter round 10 with an 8-1 record, at least <i>one</i> of them would have a very good chance of making Top 8.</p>&#10;      <p>Antonino De Rosa wins 2-1.</p>&#10;
