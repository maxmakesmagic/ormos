"Mou kari makka?"

It is a traditional Osakan greeting that literally translated means, "Have you earned money today?"

"Bochi - bochi - denna," would be Takuma Morofuji's reply. Translation: "Not bad at all."

Not bad, indeed! The Fukuoka pharmacist earned himself $20,000 by defeating Jun'ya Iyamaga in a five game finals. Five games and then some actually as it was an unprecedented finals in which the instant replay rule was instituted and the game was backed up with the help of video to rectify an incorrect ruling. 

Originally both players in the finals had planned to rescind their invites but now that Takuma was the Japanese National Champion - the tenth time that award has been handed out - he was having second thoughts. Should Takuma be able to play he was have two wingmen that are more than capable. Masashi Oiso won the third/fourth playoff with Ichirou Shimura. With Jun'ya officially rescinding, Ichirou joined the team as well. The new alternate was fifth place finisher Makahito Mihara.

He has many Grand Prix Top 8s and shocked the Magic community when he led a squad of Japanese players into Boston to win an Extended Grand Prix. He has recently started writing strategy articles for an English language strategy site as well.</p>&#10;      <p>What he has not accomplished in his career is any kind of Domestic title. Many people expect that to change this weekend. </p>&#10;      <h3>Game 1</h3>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Condescend" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Condescend&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Both players led off with <a>Sensei's Divining Top</a> although Mihara's was paid for with Yavimaya Coast. For Game 1 he mixes <a>Condescend</a> into the Tooth package and has <a>Shifting Borders</a> after board. (His white is for COP Red against the burn decks.)</p>&#10;      <p>Oiso kept the multicolored creation stalled at two mana by <a>Condescend</a>ing <a>Sylvan Scrying</a> and then <a>Annul</a>ling a green-white Talisman. He then kicked Mihara while he was down and untapped into <a>Meloku the Clouded Mirror</a>. When Mihara <a>Condescend</a>ed his own Top to look for help, Oiso <a>Mana Leak</a>ed the Top - and also countered the scry effect of the <a>Condescend</a> in the process. Oiso kept up his pressure and added a <a>Triskelion</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>By stunting Mihara's mana development Oiso had more than enough time to finish him off with those two BlueTooth staples. </p>&#10;      <p>Oiso - 1 Mihara - 0</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 2</h3>&#10;      <p>Katsuhiro Mori came over to watch his deck in action. Mori designed the deck that Oiso was playing this weekend and lost to his friend during the opening rounds of the tournament. Mihara was not without his own supporters and they cringed as he had to mulligan his first set of cards. He kept the next six and led off with <a>Sensei's Divining Top</a>. Oiso's draw seemed less menacing than last game without a Top of his own; although he did discard one to <a>Thirst for Knowledge</a> as Mihara began to assemble his pieces - including the counterstopping Boseiju.</p>&#10;      <p>Oiso drew into another Top and flipped it to complete his tron. Mihara, who had played a second Top on the previous turn, cycled it for a card and sacrificed his <a>Sakura-Tribe Elder</a>. He was one piece away from the tron himself and he got to see plenty of cards. He spun the Top into the missing Mine and cast an uncounterable, entwined <a>Tooth and Nail</a>. Morofuji made the classic mistake of tapping out against red to activate his Top at the end of the turn and Iyamaga finished him off with <a>Shrapnel Blast</a>. </p>&#10;      <h3>Game 3</h3>&#10;      <p>Morofuji mulliganed on the play but kept his next six.
