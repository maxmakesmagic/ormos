Salutations and welcome to unofficial Sliver Week! Or, at least, my *Premium Deck Series: Slivers* article. I've been asked to share with you a behind-the-scenes account of the **Magic** R&D process that went into creating the *Premium Deck Series: Slivers* product.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d0/72/d0725e8026e8ad6559e0be207accc93f2a80d44fee36c45543d845b9735eef3d.jpg)  

Usually, ideas for new products come from R&D and then work their way through all the other departments. This time was different. As the lead game designer for this product, I wasn't involved until a strange-looking white-and-clear box was dropped off on my desk with an ultimatum to "find something compelling to put inside that."

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/97/fa976c8368e9e424970f4ff85e3150d02982f2aa2d532b028ec35fe33f411e60.jpg)*Initial stages, first rudimentary concepts from the creative team*  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/c1/6bc11b111f77bd35318b3f8359578b0e07d8f6a3437ded67cc73b806b29c8197.jpg)*Unprinted mock up from vendor of the creative teams final submission*  

### The Search for Themes

After some brainstorm meetings, an all-foil 60-card deck won the right to be inside this box. As a bling-matters deck designer, it was my task to determine exactly what 60 cards were going into the deck.

Our most compelling boxed sets always exude a strong theme. However, we didn't want to repeat any of these recently used themes:

*   Elves (*Elves vs. Goblins*)
*   Goblins (*Elves vs. Goblins*)
*   Dragons (*From the Vault: Dragons*)
*   Angels (*Divine vs. Demonic*)
*   Demons (*Divine vs. Demonic*)
*   Beasts (*Garruk vs. Liliana*)
*   Elementals (*Planechase*: Elemental Thunder)
*   Myr (*Planechase*: Metallic Dreams)
*   Zombies (*Planechase*: Zombie Uprising)
*   Vampires (*Zendikar* Intro Pack)

After some asking around, I had a short list of themes to choose from, and one of the lowest-hanging fruits was indeed Slivers. While it's a great idea, the problem is that it is quite possibly the *best* idea. Why is that a problem? Let me explain.

If you've ever been in an entertainment business that releases content on a regular basis, you come to realize that it's best to "spread out the awesome" across multiple releases. That way your audience finds compelling content in all your products rather than being spoiled by all the good stuff at the beginning, and the really good stuff has breathing room. You've probably noticed that TV series have mastered this art with cliffhangers and payoffs. However, movie sequels are notoriously poorer than their predecessors because they never "spread out the awesome"; there's never a sequel to a movie that bombs.

So, I was a bit nervous about Slivers because it fires what I felt was our very best deck-building bullet on the initial launch of Premium Deck Series. It's an entire foil deck in a foil deck box, ready to play! Does it really *need* any help to fly off the shelf beyond that?

But in the end, I bit the bullet.

### The Selection Process

I know that some Slivers are more beloved than others. However, I have a very large audience to serve. There are players who want four of each of their favorite Slivers sitting next to EDH players who want never-before-foiled Slivers as one-ofs. With only 60 slots to work with, I knew I couldn't please everyone.

**Bombos**

**Magic** deck building is about finding synergy so that your deck runs like a well-oiled machine. Slivers are a tribe all about synergy. However, even in a tribe as synergistic and sharing as the Sliver hive, there are times when two Slivers just don't enjoy each other's company.

First up we have [Crystalline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crystalline+Sliver) versus [Opaline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opaline+Sliver).

<table><tbody><tr><td><div><div><img alt="Crystalline Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Crystalline+Sliver"></div></div></td><td><div><div><img alt="Opaline Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Opaline+Sliver"></div></div></td></tr></tbody></table>

[Crystalline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crystalline+Sliver) prevents your Slivers from being targeted. [Opaline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opaline+Sliver)'s ability can only trigger when an opponent targets one of your slivers, which [Crystalline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crystalline+Sliver) flat out disables. These two also happen to be the only white-blue gold Slivers ever printed.

Next up we have [Magma Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Sliver) versus [Crystalline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crystalline+Sliver).

[Magma Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Sliver) lets you skyrocket the power of your Slivers with its sick tap ability. However, it requires targeting your Slivers, which [Crystalline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crystalline+Sliver) prevents. Even though we definitely enjoy the power boost of [Magma Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Sliver), we've already chosen [Crystalline Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crystalline+Sliver) from above.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ef/41/ef41481d9e1f4ae52f23492b6668226d22ecab0de7a9bff1c8bb8231019bc9d4.jpg)  

Next up we have [Metallic Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Metallic+Sliver) versus [Harmonic Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonic+Sliver).

<table><tbody><tr><td><div><div><img alt="Metallic Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Metallic+Sliver"></div></div></td><td><div><div><img alt="Harmonic Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Harmonic+Sliver"></div></div></td></tr></tbody></table>

[Metallic Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Metallic+Sliver) is one of the original slivers (and Mark Rosewater wishes he'd had named it Silver Sliver). It doesn't do much except be a 1/1 Sliver (using dated "This counts as a Sliver" technology), but you can cast it with any color of mana. That's pretty snazzy for a five-color deck. [Harmonic Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonic+Sliver) is a silver bullet sliver that can single-handedly dismantle your opponent's Myr deck or [Sigil of the Empty Throne](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sigil+of+the+Empty+Throne) deck. However, since [Harmonic Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harmonic+Sliver)'s effect is mandatory, sometimes you will be forced to destroy your own [Metallic Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Metallic+Sliver). In general, players absolutely despise this kind of "bad tension."

![Dormant Sliver](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Dormant+Sliver)

  

To the designer in me, [Dormant Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dormant+Sliver) seems like a cruel joke. On one hand, it enables my Slivers to draw into more Slivers but on the other hand, it grants all my Slivers defender, preventing me from attacking my opponent. There's certainly tricks to be had with [Victual Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Victual+Sliver) and whatnot, but there's always tricks with **Magic** cards, and on the whole I don't feel that [Dormant Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dormant+Sliver) is high on players' lists of most-loved Slivers.

Whoever designed [Dormant Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dormant+Sliver) must love [Jungle Barrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jungle+Barrier) way more than everyone else does.

**A Mythic in the Making**

Making new products with old cards has always had it fidgety compromises, like making an old radio using new transistors or playing old sheet music compositions with modern instruments.

*   We have modern card frames.
*   We have modern rules, keywords, and templates.
*   We have a new Mythic rarity.
*   We even have [modernized Creature subtypes](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/grand-creature-type-update-2007-09-26).

For the most part, we update all aspects of old cards accordingly. While there's nostalgia in old frames/rules/templates, that nostalgia is forever locked in the old cards already in circulation. We can add zing to new cards with new frames/rules/templates during the modernization process. As a player, I appreciate that the cards in circulation offer both the old and the new.

The trickiest upgrade is the recently added Mythic rarity. While reprinted cards have moved up and down in rarity throughout **Magic** history, it's only been across a 3-rarity system of common/uncommon/rare. Mythic adds a fourth possible choice for a reprinted card's rarity.

If I were make a list of "the most Mythic cards in all of **Magic: The Gathering** history" it might look something like:

1.  [Black Lotus](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Black+Lotus)
2.  [Nicol Bolas, Planeswalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nicol+Bolas%2C+Planeswalker)
3.  [Progenitus](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Progenitus)
4.  [Darksteel Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Darksteel+Colossus)
5.  [Platinum Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Platinum+Angel)

In my opinion, [Black Lotus](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Black+Lotus) is the "most Mythic" of all Magic cards; I had heard whispers and tales of the [Black Lotus](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Black+Lotus) years before I actually saw one with my own eyes. And indeed, as of ***Magic** 2010* we have upgraded both [Darksteel Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Darksteel+Colossus) and [Platinum Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Platinum+Angel) to the well-deserved Mythic rarity.

Slivers aren't without their Mythic candidates for upgrading. From the complete Sliver list above, the three candidates are:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/34/5d/345d14a28c5099772dbb33b5c4d92b7a30333df74efc26343c647c4185c6cea6.jpg)  

We can safely assume these three cards would be mythic rares if such a thing existed so many years ago. The Premium Deck Series is slated to have just one, though, so which one should it be?

Short answer: [Sliver Overlord](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sliver+Overlord). [Sliver Queen](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sliver+Queen) is on the Reserve List, and thusly can't be reprinted as long as it's on there. [Sliver Legion](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sliver+Legion) is a glorified [Coat of Arms](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coat+of+Arms) and much less of a deckbuilding centerpiece. Incidentally, [Sliver Overlord](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sliver+Overlord) makes for the best EDH General for a Sliver deck, though [Sliver Queen](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sliver+Queen) works better in a non-Sliver EDH deck built around her infinite combos.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1d/26/1d26d868771a08f530950f97caad9ca1d42dacdee17f77ea78bd77635ac971ef.jpg)  

Next up is [Muscle Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muscle+Sliver) and [Sinew Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sinew+Sliver).

<table><tbody><tr><td><div><div><img alt="Muscle Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Muscle+Sliver"></div></div></td><td><div><div><img alt="Sinew Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Sinew+Sliver"></div></div></td></tr></tbody></table>

Both these Slivers grant +1/+1, a "Lord" in the most traditional sense. [Muscle Sliver](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Muscle+Sliver) is particularly famous—IIRC the first ever "better than [Grizzly Bears](https://web.archive.org/web/20091109000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grizzly+Bears)" bear though it still avoids the "strictly better" comparison.
