Monoblack control has been one of my favorite archetypes since playtesting for Pro Tour-Osaka and when testing showed that it would be viable in the Masters, I jumped at the opportunity to play it. A number of my Your Move Games teammates and friends at my store contributed to designing this particular version. Most notable was Darwin Kastle, whose last-minute changes definitely improved the deck.

### Win Conditions

Traditionally, monoblack control won either by burning its opponent out with Corrupt and Soul Burn or with a couple of swings from Nantuko Shade. Neither option seemed acceptable in the current metagame. First of all, playing four Corrupts slows the deck down a bit too much in many matchups. Of course, there are also matchups where you really want them, so I settled on adding a third Corrupt to the sideboard. There are only two Corrupts in the main deck, but it still remains a viable victory condition as you will often utilize Mirari and Diabolic Tutors to set up the win.

Nantuko Shade may be a very powerful and quick victory condition, but you can easily lose it to a top-decked Chainer’s Edict. Somewhat slower, but a lot less fragile, is the only *Onslaught* card to make it into the deck – Riptide Replicator. Able to generate a stream of large monsters, the Replicator is difficult to remove for most decks. It also has the minor additional benefit of getting around Circle of Protection: Black.

Even if both Corrupts and a Replicator have been used up or countered, you are not entirely out of options. The deck can win a fair number of games by casting a well-timed Haunting Echoes. Even a single Echoes can be crippling and you can follow it up with a second one a few turns later to pick out whatever few spells your opponent was left to work with.

Finally, there is Undead Gladiator. It’s a 3/1 creature that will just keep coming, if you choose to use it in that way. Of course, in most situations it will be more useful as a card cycling mechanism, but a number of games will be won simply by attacking for three every turn.

One of this deck’s greatest strengths is that, aside for a single Replicator, all of its victory conditions are incidental. All of them serve other functions. Corrupt is a removal spell, Haunting Echoes is a powerful weapon against most decks and Gladiator is a cycling engine. The ability not to “waste” slots on win conditions greatly improves your ability to control the flow of the game.

### Creature Kill

There is nothing you want to see more at the other side of the table than a creature deck. A full compliment of 4 Mutilate, 4 Chainer’s Edict and 4 Innocent Blood are enough to give most creature decks nightmares.

There are two philosophies as to how to utilize your removal early in the game. Against decks that can burn you out (such as Sligh) or can counter Mutilate (such as blue-green), your goal is to take their creatures out as fast as they can play them. You won’t mind using Mutilate to take out a single creature. In matchups where you feel an opponent has no way to stop the Mutilate, you should be willing to take some early punishment in order to either clear the board on turn 4, or Tutor on turn 4 and cast Mutilate on turn 5 for major card advantage.

Another important tip is to conserve your mana. If you played first, do not use Innocent Blood on turn 2 if you could Chainer’s Edict instead. There is always a chance that you will want to both Blood and Pact on turn 3. Do not use a removal spell from your hand if you have Chainer’s Edict in the graveyard and the mana to flash it back. 

Notably absent from the deck is Smother. It is an excellent removal spell, but in the end just not as good as Chainer’s Edict or Innocent Blood. It is certainly a very viable sideboard option if most of the decks you expect to face are beatdown-oriented.

The huge amount of creature kill in the deck can become a major liability if you are facing a creatureless deck. Fortunately those are few and far between in today’s metagame and the matchup against most of them is much improved after sideboarding.

### Disruption

The deck is fairly low on disruption, but the anti-control spells it does have are extremely powerful. We’ve got a pair of Haunting Echoes, a pair of Mind Sludges and four Duresses.

Duress is a multipurpose card good against virtually everything. They never get sideboarded out and you would play eight if you could. There are only two Mind Sludges because you only ever want to cast one during the game and you only want to cast it sometime around turns 4-7 at the latest. Against many decks, turn four Diabolic Tutor for Mind Sludge, turn five Mind Sludge is a path to victory.

Haunting Echoes might be the most powerful spell in the deck. It is good against virtually any archetype, and crippling against many. If you are facing a combo deck, catching a piece of their combo such as Lightning Rift, or Mirari’s Wake can simply end the game. Against control decks, you can do the same by removing their victory conditions like Psychatog. Although Haunting Echoes does get sideboarded out in some matchups, you will do so reluctantly. If your metagame features a lot of Slide, Psychatog and Wake, you will want a third Echoes in your board.

### Card Economy and Library Manipulation

Diabolic Tutor is rather self-explanatory. Go get whichever card is devastating for your opponent at the moment, when you need it. Tutor gets truly broken with Mirari in play. In that case, you always use one of two Tutors you are casting to go get another Tutor. You can “double up” all of your library manipulation spells, which is the most efficient use of Mirari in the deck.

Skeletal Scrying may not be great against ultra-aggressive decks, but it sure helps you gain an edge on control archetypes. One of few instants in the deck, you will almost always find a way to sneak it past counterspells to draw an array of new cards suddenly. Last but not least is Tainted Pact. Early in the game, it almost always gets an extra swamp for an ever-mana-hungry deck. Later on, if you no longer need lands, never stop at a swamp. If you flip over two in a row, the loss of card economy is more than made up for by the fact that you just saved yourself two draw steps.

The deck needs so much mana, that mana flood rarely becomes a problem. If it ever does, you do have an answer in the form of Undead Gladiator. In every matchup, there will be cards you want to throw away. For example, when playing against Psychatog, those Mutilates aren’t doing much for you. Undead Gladiator is there to help you replace any undesirable card in your hand for a cost of four mana. One of this deck’s weaknesses is that, if forced into topdeck mode later in the game, it will often mana flood. Gladiator guarantees that this will not happen. If you cast Diabolic Tutor against a control deck and are fairly sure they have the ability to counter whichever card you go for, Tutor for a Gladiator and set yourself up with a monster hand before making your move.

This is the one deck that could never, ever splash another color. Its swamps are just too important, fueling Mutilate and Mind Sludge and powering up Cabal Coffers. One of the greatest differences between this version and other monoblack control decks is that it utilizes only two Cabal Coffers, instead of the usual three or even an occasional four. Without Soul Burn in the deck, you do not need quite the same ridiculous amounts of mana and not stumbling over Coffers early on when you badly need a swamp is more important than having access to multiples. This means you will use up a Diabolic Tutor to find Coffers a little more often, but that’s just fine.

Psychatog: +4 Persecute, +4 Rancid Earth, +1 Haunting Echoes, -4 Mutilate, -2 Corrupt, -3 Innocent Blood

Persecute is not the obvious choice here, but it is very potent and one of few ways to deal with a multiple Blistering Firecat draw. The matchup is generally not favorable, but it’s really not too bad either.

Astral Slide: +1 Corrupt, +1 Haunting Echoes, +4 Rancid Earth, -4 Mutilate, -2 Innocent Blood

This is your matchup to lose. Slide has VERY little chance to beat this deck, as they have no significant disruption and no countermagic. Just make sure to Corrupt enough to stay ahead of whatever damage they can deal via Lightning Rift. Mind Sludges and Haunting Echoes will do the rest.

Mirror Match: +4 Persecute, +4 Rancid Earth, +1 Corrupt, -4 Mutilate, -4 Innocent Blood, -1 Chainer’s Edict

Turn 3 Rancid Earth, turn 4 Persecute is the name of the game.

Monoblack control may not be the deck to beat all decks – but nothing is. It is, however, a very solid tier 1 archetype that you should consider as an option for any Standard tournaments you plan on attending.
