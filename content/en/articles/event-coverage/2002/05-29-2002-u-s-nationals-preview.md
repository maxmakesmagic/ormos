U.S. Nationals is certainly a tournament rich in history and tradition, remaining one of the most difficult to win each year. We Americans pride ourselves in maintaining a strong tradition of success. Looking around the world, things won't be as easy for Americans as they might have been in the past. With Finland putting Tomi Walamies in their A seat for the team drafts in Sydney this year, Belgium placing Geoffrey Siron and Jan Doise on their National Team, Sweden sending Anton Jonsson to the top, Germany boasting the best player in the world in Kai Budde on their team, and the Netherlands forming the uber-team of Tom Van de Logt, Kamiel Cornelissen, and Noah Boeken, the United States will be up against the best come August.

What winds up happening this summer remains up in the air, but one thing that all can be assured of is that there will be many stories to be shared following the 2002 U.S. Nationals. Each Nationals tournament adds new tales to be told for years to come. To recap the past half-decade:

### 1997 U.S. Nationals

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d0/5c/d05c56fcc6f953b087b2105f8fb3edbcd12b0db6045a3425dd965948ad98498b.jpg)Justin Gary was able to pilot his Frenetic Burn deck to the top of the Swiss, with Kyle Bigos in second. The rest of the team included Bob "TGO" Maher and Jeff Butz, both piloting Buried Alive variants. Since then, nobody has heard much of Butz and Bigos, while Maher went on to win Pro Tour-Chicago. Justin Gary is better now than he was then, recently finishing 2nd to Alexander Witt in the Masters tournament in Nice. Gary looks to become the first man to win U.S. Nationals twice. Interestingly enough, his was the only U.S. team NOT to win the Worlds Team Competition - many felt that had the bottom half of the Top 8 been the team (Jon Finkel, Nate Clarke, Casey McCarrel, and Mario Robaina), the United States would have won easily.

### 1998 U.S. Nationals

![](https://i.ormos.uk/20/0d/200d1105bdbbbe53118eb72d22aa59cd5f237fc4d710b630175fcf4bbb65954c.jpg)Matt Linde and his white weenie deck squared up against Mike Long's Prosperous Bloom combo deck in the finals. Despite an early Gloom from Mike, his own Natural Balance allowed Linde to search out a fifth land, giving his the needed mana to cast a game ending Abeyance in the middle of Long's turn, clinching the title. Jon Finkel, beginning to show his dominance in the game, also made the team, along with Bryce Currence. Mike Long, the man controversy seems to follow, was deeply involved again in this tournament. With an 11-0 record and assured a spot in the Top 8 going into the last round, Mike Long was found with a copy of Cadaverous Bloom in his lap. Considering the situation and the high possibility that it had slipped out of his deck during shuffling, Long received a match loss and would go on to make the team the next day.

### 1999 U.S. Nationals

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4b/cc/4bccad490b92d12d0497c59a8a06396b41369660d4f6ff6e08ec0e13adadaf5b.jpg)Once again a white weenie deck, this time featuring the "Ball Lightning" mechanic of Waylay and in the hands of Kyle Rose, made the finals only to face down a very difficult match-up in John Hunka's Enchantress combo deck. Having already won the first two games, Hunka began going off again in game 3 for what appeared to be a clean sweep. The combo fizzled, however, and with 10 mana in his pool, Hunka was forced to concede the game. Good draws from Rose in the final two allowed him to complete the full comeback and be crowned U.S. Champion. Zvi Mowshowitz and Chris Tobler filled out the team, but being unable to attend Worlds, Charles Kornblith would take Tobler's place as the U.S. won the Team Competition for the second year in a row. Tobler was never to be heard from again.

### 2000 U.S. Nationals

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d4/29/d42901b391d1b76fcb695091b546de96207915eadc3dcee0534e61b5063df658.jpg)Showing his unbelievable mastery of the game, Jon Finkel took an undefined draft format, with the recent addition of *Prophecy* to the mix, and swept through the Limited portion of the event. Finkel would eventually take the title, winning with a monoblack deck filled with silver bullets and Tutors to get them. Earning incredible card advantage off of his single copy of Persecute, Finkel swept Chris Benafel's Ponza deck in the finals. Aaron Forsythe joined them on the Nationals Team - his last major individual finish before leaving the professional scene to work in Washington. Frank Hernandez rounded out the team which would take the Worlds team title for the third year in a row. This tournament also featured the introduction to what would be the Masters tournament as the "U.S. Draft Challenge." After defeating Kyle Rose in the first round of this 8-man single elimination tournament, Mike Long, often the center of controversy, earned a disqualification without prize for stacking his deck against Trevor Blackwell in the semifinals.

### 2001 U.S. Nationals

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d4/17/d41798f507424f6d2fb1e57bc520f2cdcb28119a14ca2128244a009cfebe6014.jpg)A year after winning both Pro Tour-Los Angeles and the U.S. Draft Challenge, Trevor Blackwell piloted his Fires deck to a national title. In the finals, he would meet a man who looked to be well out of the Top 8 after the culmination of the final round of the Swiss. In his round 12 match against Pete Leiher, the final 6 turns of the match ended up elapsing almost 25 minutes, thanks in large part to an end of turn Hull Breach (a sorcery) cast by Leiher targeting an irrelevant Tsabo's Web. The "new sheriff in town", Collin Jackson, after 45 minutes of deliberation with judges watching the Featured Match, upgraded Leiher's slow play to a match loss, putting Hegstad into the Top 8. But Brian Hegstad's opponents weren't through trying to take advantage of him as his quarterfinals opponent, Casey McCarrel, was caught manipulating Hegstad's deck during shuffling, resulting in 11 non-lands on the top of Brian's deck. Questioning McCarrel's shuffling method, Hegstad called for a deck-check which resulted in McCarrel being disqualified without prize. The final member of the U.S. Team (the 2001 Nationals tournaments were the first to feature 3-man teams instead of 4) was a man by the name of Eugene Harvey who came to Orlando unqualified. After grinding his way through the U.S. Open tournament, Harvey wound up finishing third in the main event. The unlucky recipient of the first fourth place finish after the Worlds team competition was reformatted to include only 3 man teams was Dave Bachmann. Bachmann would serve as the alternate for the U.S. team, which would again take the title despite a horrendous 6-12 Day 1 performance, leaving them in 47th place after Day 1.

For this year's Nationals preview, I have polled 50 of the top professionals from around the globe, asking each who they felt were their top choices to walk away with the top prizes - each ranked 5-10 individual Americans.

As we all know, however, **Magic** doesn't go strictly by the numbers; complaints of mana screw and top-decking prove this fact so. If the tournament goes exactly according to plan, however, these are the predicted final standings. And so, for those of you looking to score on the Fantasy Pro Tour, these are the players to ride:

### 9th/10th

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/759.jpg)**Ken Ho** - "The Champ" took Japan by storm, piloting his Squirrel Nest/Upheaval deck to the top spot and solidifying himself as one of the best. Ho has struggled when it comes to Limited tournaments, failing to make Day 2 at both San Diego and Nice. He now has another chance to show his doubters that he knows what to do when he has to open the packs.

**Neil Reeves** - Known affectionately to many as JT Money, Neil Reeves breakthrough performance came at Pro Tour-San Diego where he rose to the top of the Swiss and clinched his first Top 8. Considered one of the top drafters in the world by players who know him best, Reeves proved he can play with the best of them in both fields. Taking a fairly rogue u-b-r Psychatog deck, splashing for Flametongue Kavu and Fire/Ice, Reeves finished in the Top 4 of the largest North American Grand Prix in Milwaukee. He will look to further prove himself to the rest of the world this weekend.

### Top 8

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/714.jpg)**Chris Benafel** - This man has simply owned U.S. Nationals. Back-to-back Top 8s here in Orlando are just the beginning of what Benafel has done since his second-place finish in Los Angeles back in 1999. A few Grand Prix wins and a victory with AlphaBetaUnlimited in the Team Masters competition have left Benafel with little to prove, with many already considering him among the top 5 players in the game. He has another chance this year to capture the U.S. National crown he has been so close to in the past.

**Rob Dougherty** - One of the best deck builders in the world, even if he does have a thing for 61 card decks. As Dougherty stated in his recent interview on the Sideboard, he is especially skilled in breaking new Constructed formats. With all of Europe having played their Nationals tournaments, as well as a 600+ man Grand Prix in the books, this format is far from new. Still, Dougherty has been red hot and looks to bounce back strong from a mediocre finish in Nice.

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/MI00/838.jpg)**Jon Finkel** - What can be said about the Shadowmage Infiltrator that hasn't been said 100 times? He has earned more money playing this game than any other man, and he earned the vast majority of it before the advent of the Masters series. While some are of the opinion that he is no longer the best player in the world, he still has the best résumé of all time. Finkel has amassed more Pro Tour Top 8s than anyone, as well as earning the U.S. National crown, Player of the Year title, and even the Invitational two years ago. Needing a Top 4 finish just to qualify for Worlds, don't expect Jon to fade into the darkness without a fight. The last time Nationals featured an undeveloped draft format, Finkel stood atop of the Day 1 standings going 6-0 in the draft portion and eventually taking the title. One can only imagine that another undefined draft format favors Jon immensely - look for him to rise to the top, yet again.

**Eric Froehlich** - (too humble to write about myself, this profile is courtesy of my good friend, Brian Kibler) When I got a message on IRC a year ago from someone named EFro, asking me to team with him and Kyle Rose for the Team PT, I had no idea who he was. How a year can change things. When I couldn't commit to their team, the two Virginians picked up Kamiel Cornelissen as their third and made a run at the title, just barely missing the Top 4 in New York. Since then, poker has caused Kyle Rose to fall off the Pro Tour radar, but Froehlich has stepped up to fill his shoes, posting a Top 8 finish at PT-San Diego, followed by back-to-back Top 32's in Osaka and Nice. With a work ethic that has him playtesting on **Magic** Online an average of several hours a day, EFro is definitely a player to watch in the battle for the National title in Orlando. 

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTCHI00/942.jpg)**William "Baby Huey" Jensen** - This mammoth man carries a massive résumé. With his Top 8 at Pro Tour-London, more than half a dozen Grand Prix Top 8s, a Masters win, and a Top 8 finish here last year, Huey is one of the game's elite. Quite possibly the best drafter in the country, his moving in with Dan Clegg and Brock Parker, next door to long time friend Matt Linde, has allowed him to emerge as one of the very best when it comes to Poker, Spades, and **Magic**.

**Brian Kibler** - Another player known for his deck building talents, Brian Kibler has been redefining metagames for quite a while. With his column on the Sideboard, Brian has been sharing his "Top Notch Tech" with the world, proving he clearly has the ability as a deck designer. With Top 8 in each of the last three American Grand Prixs, Kibler has proved he can play with the best of them as well. He should be armed and ready with something excellent for the Constructed portion of Nationals and is already working towards breaking the draft portion.

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNY01/871.jpg)**Ben Rubin** - The man who has made the finals of virtually everything, including Pro Tours, Worlds, Masters, Grand Prixs, and even the Invitational, will be fighting for one of few finals appearances he has yet to earn. Ben, who had announced he would be unable to make the trip due to a professor forcing him to be in class during Day 1 of Nationals, had a quick change of plans when his professor realized that there was no class - just one week prior to the event. While Rubin has been unable to get in as much testing and practice as he may be used to, he still remains among the very best in the world and is one of the favorites to take it all.

**Mike Turian** - The Potato himself has been around forever, and has accumulated numerous Top 8s and a Pro Tour win as part of Potato Nation two years ago. What looked like a sure Top 8 from Mike last year turned into a huge disappointment as a 7-0 start culminated in an 8-4 Top 16 finish. Turian is one of the best in the game and is ready to lock down another Day 3 appearance this time around.

### Those to Look For Who Didn't Quite Make the Top 10

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpkob01/922.jpg)**Alex Shvartsman** - The man with the most pro points among U.S. players and more Grand Prix Top 8s the any other, despite his Day 3 appearance with Team Illuminati earlier this season, still failed to record a single vote. Without any impressive results in individual Pro Tours, Alex will look to show that he can get it done with the best in the country, proving he deserves to be on the top of the Pro Tour points standings.

**Zvi Mowshowitz** - The Pro Tour-Tokyo champion and superb deck builder seems to have lost a bit of passion for the game. Using Nice as an experimental Pro Tour featured Zvi sporting his Teroh's Faithful shirt and the quote "And all the drafters say I'm pretty fly for a white guy" on the back - the white gambit didn't quite pay off. While forcing white may not be as risky this time around, with the inclusion of the white-green set in *Judgment*, it will be interesting to see if Zvi "wears his draft on his shirt" again. Either way, he tells me he really likes his Constructed deck, so look for him near the top.

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/USNAT01/890.jpg)**Brian Davis** - 1.5X +.5Y + 1, gaining life off his Drains, and blocking attacks that deal lethal damage may not be Brian Davis's strong points, but this man remains red hot. With consistent high finishes on the Tour, including Top 8s in both draft and Constructed, and more high finishes on the Grand Prix circuit, he won't necessarily play a perfect game, but he will play well enough to hover near the top of the standings. 

**Dave Humpherys** - Rob Dougherty claimed in his recent *Sideboard* interview that Dave Humpherys is likely the best technical player on Your Move Games, and The Hump has recently been racking up an impressive string of results to back up that high praise. With his recent semifinals appearance in New Orleans and Masters Finals showing in San Diego, it seems that despite Dave's rigorous school schedule (he's currently working on his Masters thesis), he can still compete with the best of the best. If Dave could only speed up his game - his record at PT-Nice in the first draft was 1-1-2 - he'd easily be one of the top competitors for the U.S. title. 

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTCHI00/967.jpg)**Steve OMS** - Formerly considered the number one drafter in the world, SteveO, as well as his Antarctica teammates (Jon Finkel and Dan OMS) have been suffering from a lack of finishes of late. With his brother having already fallen off the gravy train and considering giving up the game, Steve remains in the same situation as Jon, needing a Top 4 finish at Nationals to gain his invite to Worlds. 

### Controversy?

Before U.S. Nationals even begins, many are wondering what did and will become of the Ed Fear challenge posted on Misetings.com prior to Pro Tour-Nice. Fear offered to put up $400 of his own money if both Sol Malka and Peter Szigeti, two men who have had a bit of a feud of late, would each put up $100 to play a best of three formats competition. While Szigeti eagerly accepted the chance to make $500 while risking only $100 versus any opponent, Malka turned down the offer. When level 5 judge Jeff Donais questioned Malka in Nice about whether or not he would accept the challenge, Malka is quoted to have replied "Peter is absolute (expletive deleted). For me to accept that challenge would be like the Rock fighting Barry Horowitz. You have to prove yourself first son." With PTR finishing in the Top 32 of Pro Tour-Nice, he has done just that. Whether Malka will accept the challenge now that Szigeti has officially "proven himself" is yet to be seen, but should lead to some interesting stories outside of the tournament.

So as we approach the third National tournament in sunny Orlando, it truly is anybody's tournament for the taking. Questions whether the established stars from long ago or the new kids on the block have the advantage in this tournament is up in the air. Will we have another Eugene Harvey grinding his way from the unqualified to the National Team? The only thing for sure is that U.S. Nationals will be an exciting tournament from beginning to end, while whoever emerges as the U.S. team will have some steep competition in front of them come August.
