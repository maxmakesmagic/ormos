### 

While Eric Taylor was busy winning Grand Prix-Milwaukee, on the other side of the pond, the Germans had their National Championship. Chock full of star power, German Nationals produced some good insight into what would be played at the upcoming U.S. Nationals. 

Germany has the distinction of being of the most competitive nations in the world, while simultaneously being the most predictable. Even with the formidable power of pros like Janosch Kuhn, Christian Lhurs, Peer Kroger and Patrick Mello, all players who could easily win the next Pro Tour, you know Kai won German Nationals. Piloting a classic Psychatog deck, Budde defeated Mark Ziegner in a tight finals, 2-1. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/d5/b7d5f3fc60249580ff4cb2814511e3d9918c4be0c53cefaeeae11621c3c6de0f.jpg)

Ziegner played a very interesting, almost creatureless monored deck built around Ensnaring Bridge, Grafted Skullcap and burn spells. The only creatures were Grim Lavamancers in his sideboard. This seems like an interesting choice in a field with so many Psychatogs. With a metagame based around attacking with large creatures, Ensnaring Bridge gets a lot of mileage for the price tag, especially with the low amount of artifact removal currently being played. Most players who can opt for cheaper enchantment removal, like Aura Blast, because of the dearth of good artifacts. Ziegler's deck could have trouble with Upheaval, but his deck won in a Top 8 heavy with Psychatog. Half the Top 8 brought Psychatog and Upheaval, with three of the decks being almost exact copies, including Kai's winning deck. Rounding out the Top 8 were two u-g madness decks and a Goblin Trenches deck.

Planetmtg.de did some great coverage of the event. Check it out [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20050113013204/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.planetmtg.de/events/coverage.html?id=6&origin=sideboard_news_20020516b).
