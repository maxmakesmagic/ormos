 A prohibition imposed by law or official decree.  
 Censure, condemnation, or disapproval expressed especially by public opinion.  
 A curse; an imprecation.  
 A summons to arms in feudal times.*** 

Amen.

Good riddance.

No more losing the Sligh match up to the stinking die roll. No more playtesting match ups where you take three turns then wait half an hour for your opponent to finish going off. No more turn two Verdant Forces. Or turn one Verdant Forces for that matter. Think I’m joking?

Poor Christopher McGuire. Who? Huh? Never heard of him…

Christopher McGuire is among the thousands who have worked their collective tails off at improving their M:tG-ing to the point where qualifying for the Pro Tour is a realistic possibility. He finally managed to do so for PT-Houston last year, the first major tournament to use the new Extended. I don’t know him personally, but I’m sure he sweated over the environment, slaved over numerous decks and finally found one he liked enough to place his trust in for his first PT.

Think about it: You’re there, finally, ready to lock horns with Kai, and Jon, and Dirk, Huey, Marco (congrats big guy!) and the rest. You sit down for the players’ meeting, seeing all around you the players whose faces until now were mere internet characters at the pinnacle of your passion. Finally, the rules gibberish dispensed, you head over to the pairings and find your name opposite that of one of the true greats, Bob Maher. 

\*Gulp\* 

You sit down, maintaining your calm, ready to face the great man. PT Champ. Player of the Year. GP Champ. The Golden Boy. Courtney’s boy. He arrives at the table; you exchange the usual pleasantries while you try to keep from falling apart in the face of his obvious confidence. The die is rolled and he chooses to play first: you draw seven, and decide to keep, understanding that these are the seven cards with which you’ll embark on your PT career.

Then it’s time for game 2. 

Huh? 

“Gary, you missed a detail or two”

![Entomb](https://i.ormos.uk/ad/27/ad27eb78e7ee9d9bc2e81cd2981dce5e1dacc1ceddcbd2fff73cab0301b59518.jpg)

Here’s that chunk: Bob, playing Angry Hermit (the one with Sutured Ghoul, not the old Blastoderm deck), kept his hand, played a land and pitched another, casting Mox Diamond. He then cast Entomb, retrieved Verdant Force, and Reanimated it. Welcome to the PT kid.

How upset would you be? You’ve been reading for years how this is a game of skill, and while yes, there’s a luck element inherent in the random draw, more often than not, the former should play out. Now, you’ve left the fish behind to swim with the sharks; you’re ready to beat up on the big boys, and before you’ve even taken a turn, you’re facing down a 7/7 player and a bunch of his 1/1 barns. How are you supposed to feel about the game after that?

Pretty bad. That’s why this had to happen. Extended had gotten too fast. The turns after the third didn’t matter because half the time they didn’t exist, and frankly, that’s not what this game, at least my game, is supposed to be about.

“It’s sort of like chess, but you get to choose your pieces.” That’s the crux of how I describe **Magic** to interested parties who have never had the pleasure of the game. You’re supposed to be afforded the opportunity to outmaneuver your opponent, not only through superior deck building, but superior play as well. In the locking of wills that is **Magic**, one should be able to outthink the man, woman or child seated across from them. That notion was a lost one in the Extended of August 31st. 

Now though, Entomb is gone. No more Verdant Forces, no more Akromas, no more Nishobas, Visaras or Multanis. The legends count for New Orleans just got chopped down by 80% because this one little, seemingly insignificant card is gone. Is that a good thing? Well, the kiddies may be a little disappointed to see fewer of their favorites featured in the *Sideboard* coverage of the event, but the good news is their favorite players may actually have a say in whether or not they win. Seems like a positive to me.

![Frantic Search](https://i.ormos.uk/04/ce/04cec7cb0c5ee07e4149ee0a46bd563bcbc9247d432ef984c4957182e140db3a.jpg)

Frantic Search, of course, is a card that has ONLY functioned as part of degenerate combos. At its base, this is card disadvantage and therefore incapable of functioning within the parameters of control (rock) or beatdown (scissors), but when it comes to combo (paper), this is the card that’s always been the crux of the problem. The idea when the ‘free’ spells of Urza were created was to have spells that didn’t drain one’s mana pool, not to have spells that enhance it. R&D is human and missed out on the fact that these cards, including the long-gone Time Spiral, would act as mana acceleration in decks that contained lands that produced extra mana or Sapphire Medallions. This was true in Academy, it was true in High Tide, and now it’s the case with Enchantress and Mind’s Desire. If you want excess mana, play a Mana Flare; Frantic Search was a bad card with a broken mechanic, and that it’s gone is going to be good for the game, because some other stupid combo was going to come along and ruin a lot of Christopher McGuire-type dreams.

The honor of being the best of the three would have to go to Goblin Lackey. A 1/1 for one. According to Itaru Ishida, not even the best goblin ever printed. The thing about the Lackey is that on turns two through infinity, it’s an acceptably powered card. Before the release of *Scourge*, it was acceptable on turn one, even if its caster was playing first, but now? Now, if you cast a Lackey on turn one, when playing first, there’s just about nothing your opponent can do to avoid dying a couple of turns later, at least nothing that wouldn’t otherwise destroy all integrity of the other decks in the environment. Don’t even think of asking me to play Innocent Blood in my Rock decks (opinions Jeroen?).

![Goblin Lackey](https://i.ormos.uk/02/b7/02b7213450a9cfc370794e84f0a22c9d8ebff6e47edfb1c21ac34177142fb83a.jpg)

When asked to compare the Lackey to another card from Extended lore, the card that comes to mind is Dark Ritual. See the similarities? Obviously, these are two completely different cards, but they share the common link of being absolutely ridiculous on turn one. Like or not, you have to admit that like Ritual, the lil’ fella is a card around which decks are built, decks that are solid without drawing that key card, but at the same decks whose explosive potential based on that central figure become pretty much impossible to deal with. It was for these reasons, namely luck and a far too profound effect on the game in question, that Lackey had to go.

So no more second turn Siege Gangs, no more casting of 500 spells on turn three, no more first turn fatties. These are good things, but of course, with every round of bannings come questions of what *will* continue happening, and there’s been no shortage of continued questioning and second guessing of R&D’s decisions, just for a change of pace.

The biggest eyebrow raiser for most of CMU was the decision to leave Vampiric Tutor legal. To quote fellow road tripper Josh Rider “Entomb is a tutor for Reanimator, Vampiric is a tutor for everyone.” The suggestion being that the Tutor is obviously the more powerful card and that the exclusion of the former and not the latter doesn’t make a lot of sense. By his logic though, it makes more sense than I previously thought. The thing is, Entomb is a powerful effect for one type of deck that might be seen as too fast and/or unfair. At the same time, while the Tutor duplicates that effect to an extent, it does so for every deck, equally, and when you throw in the loss of turn you don’t get with Entomb (and the 2 life. Oh no!), you have a card that, while very possibly the most powerful card in Extended (now), isn’t the worst thing to be keeping around.

![Oath of Druids](https://i.ormos.uk/8d/96/8d96b3bfdf6b0eb6f3ecdf3d7da73b059182c0663c82595a8bb82668ff3af9ef.jpg)

Next up was Oath of Druids, the old green creature hoser that, with or without Gaea’s Blessing, has been known to do ridiculous things for all of two mana. Oath by itself forces creature decks to either a) be Sligh or b) play cards capable of dealing with Oath, but at the same time it maintains a balance in that without it (especially now that Entomb is gone), little dorks would overrun the format, and as fun as that might be, it doesn’t exactly lend to the creativity, which feels like one of the cornerstones to competitive play, especially in its broadest format.

While I had no problems with either of those cards sticking around, the one whose absence from the Sept. 1st announcement made me groan was Grim Monolith. The reason first Ritual then Lackey were removed from their respective Extended environments wasn’t because they were the best cards so much as the most explosive ones: after turn one, both cards were just fine power-wise, but the potential for turn one Necropotence or turn two Warchief or Siege Gang made these two cards just dumb on turn one. Same goes for Monolith, albeit to a lesser extent.

![Grim Monolith](https://i.ormos.uk/ac/24/ac24bfaa1dd8165a7917f7b12186f566c484fbad54bf1142456be3f60e728d28.jpg)

Granted, for the two-casting cost Monolith to get out that early, your deck has to be built around it, using cards like City of Traitors and Ancient Tomb, but \*\*newsflash\*\*, it’s already being done, has been for the last few years now, and turn one Monolith, tap, cast Voltaic Key, untap, cast whatever broken artifact you please is ridiculous, unfair and beyond the spirit of the game. But that’s not why I wanted the card gone.

With the other ‘Ritual’ cards gone, Tinker is left to reign supreme, and that’s before we know the contents of *Mirrodin*, which if internet rumor-mongering and MagicTheGathering.com columns are any indication, is the artifact set, and that means Tinker can only get better. Reanimator’s gone, Hermit is weakened, as are Sligh and Enchantress, while Mind’s Desire might as well be Mind over Matter now… I suppose this will bring Extended back to the state it was in two years ago where there’s one metagame deck and decks designed to beat it, but I was really hoping the Monolith would disappear with the others. Call me a beggar; I don’t get to choose.

There are other cards that were worth considering: Reanimate would have crippled Hermit if it had been chose over Entomb, Intuition is always going to be a problem as long as Accumulated Knowledge and Sapphire Medallion are around, and Medallion will eventually lead to another ridiculous blue combo deck, but I think R&D got the big three right: I just might have added number four is all. I’m looking forward to New Orleans a lot more now. Heck, maybe I’ll see a turn five. 

Have a good week.
