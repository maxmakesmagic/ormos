## GRAND PRIX OAKLAND 2013

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ca/c4/cac46c797a3f009b1de94e2455979dc3422c3e3a68cfbd859f44260b89173a26.jpg)

It didn't take long for William Jensen to show the world they were right to elect him to the Hall of Fame.

Capping off his comeback tour that started in Grand Prix Boston-Worchester exactly a year ago, Jensen added a punctuation mark to his resume by defeating Neal Oliver in three tense games to finish off an impressive performance at Grand Prix Oakland. Drafting and playing at a level he's been building to all year, Jensen not only celebrated his entrance into the Hall, it put the rest of the world on notice that "Huey" is a force to be reckoned with once more.

But let's not overlook his opponent in the finals, Neal Oliver, who last played in a major tournament a few months back at Grand Prix Las Vegas—a tournament he won, by the way. That makes two straight Grand Prix finals appearances for the player who wowed pros with his patience and tight play all weekend. If Oliver starts to travel more—and he says he will—don't be surprised to see him spike another Top 8 sometime soon.

And if that wasn't enough storylines, the Woo brothers, Travis and Elliott, made yet another brother-brother duo to make the Top 8 of a major tournament. Though they both fell in the quarterfinals, they spawned countless puns and rhymes during their run to the top. Woo know who woo are.

But the weekend truly belongs to Jensen, a Hall of Fame performance for a Hall of Fame player.

Congratulations, Huey Jensen, Champion of Grand Prix Oakland 2013!

## Quarterfinals - Benjamin Lundquist vs. Carlos Ale

*by Jacob Van Lunen*

 

Both players shuffled their decks quietly as the judge explained the rules for Top 8. Lundquist, thanks to his tremendous performance in the swiss, had the option to play or draw. It wasn't an easy decision; he looked through his deck a few times and decided to draw first.

Ale joked with Lundquist about looking through his deck when deciding to play or draw as the players drew their opening hands for the first game. Lundquist smiled and said some disparaging things about the tablecloth they were playing on.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6d/30/6d30fc48c86c7f9c51c2996c75c01238866f30ff4cd74ab63d4ec99e08d06c62.jpg)  
*Carlos Ale* 

Ale went down to five cards and Lundquist went to six. [Angelic Wall](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Wall) held down the fort for Lundquist until he was able to stick [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel). The Angel was soon joined by [Charging Griffin](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Charging+Griffin) while Ale was unable to find a flying or reach creature of his own. The flyers proved to be too much for Ale and they were off to a second game.

Lundquist bemoaned the cost of parking between games. Apparently his car rental cost him less money per day than his parking.

The mulligans continued in the second game for Ale, but Lundquist was content with his opening seven. Ale had an early [Rumbling Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Baloth) to one-up the [Imposing Sovereign](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Imposing+Sovereign) on Lundquist's side of the table, but [Seraph of the Sword](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seraph+of+the+Sword) came down and seemed to change the tides in the New Yorker's favor.

[Pacifism](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pacifism) from Ale let him crash into the red zone with his [Rumbling Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Baloth) and use his [Giant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Growth) as a [Lava Spike](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lava+Spike). Lundquist seemed unphased.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8b/73/8b731ef8cbf9094b3b4f073683b75aaa5158fc04f22872f6d93f050e3e65d993.jpg)  
*Ben Lundquist* 

[Howl of the Night Pack](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Howl+of+the+Night+Pack) from Ale was negated by Lundquist and [Seraph of the Sword](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seraph+of+the+Sword) jumped back into action when [Brave the Elements](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brave+the+Elements) helped it shed its pacified demeanor. [Rumbling Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Baloth) had been smote by the rare Angel and Ale found himself with no creatures on the battlefield.

Ale tried to get back into the game with [Sporemound](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sporemound), but it wasn't enough, and Ben Lundquist reserved himself a seat in the Top 4.

## Quarterfinals - Travis Woo vs. Joshua Goldman

*by Blake Rasmussen*

 

It's difficult to overstate just how impressive it is that the Woo brothers, Travis and Elliott, both made the Top 8. We've seen the Ruels do it a number of times, and the O'Mahoney-Schwartz brothers do it back in the day, but for both brothers to barrel through a 1,631-man Grand Prix on the same weekend is astounding, to say the least.

Travis Woo, however, wasn't thrilled with his deck this time around.

"Oh, it's totally unplayable," he said of his Black White deck. He'd once again drafted [Angelic Accord](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Accord), but the deck hadn't exactly come together as he would have liked.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/53/56/5356b004287490d068b14ae4d5aceeec1592d399cce68b25ca3374f382f3ee2b.jpg)  
*Travis Woo* 

He also drafted [Angelic Accord](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Accord) in earlier rounds and had success, but he said it wasn't on purpose.

"I just get sucked into it. I don't look for it, it looks for me," he said.

Joshua Goldman is also pretty excited the brothers made the Top 8.

"I'm just very happy to be here and extremely excited to be playing you!" Goldman said when he sat down. "I watch you on stream all the time!

"I wasn't planning to make it past the quarterfinals, but I wanted to play against you."

But he still was looking to end Woo's run. His Black-Green deck had some pretty powerful cards, notably [Kalonian Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalonian+Hydra) and [Garruk, Caller of Beasts](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garruk%2C+Caller+of+Beasts). He might be a Woo fan, but he wouldn't let that get in the way of his own story.

### The Games

![Kalonian Hydra](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Kalonian+Hydra)

And he showed that he wouldn't back down right off the bat. As was the custom in the ritual that is M14 limited, the players traded early plays back and forth. [Banisher Priest](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Banisher+Priest) removed an [Accursed Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Accursed+Spirit), but [Quag Sickness](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Quag+Sickness) brought it back.

The ground ground to a halt when [Minotaur Abomination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Minotaur+Abomination) on Woo's side made attacks virtually impossible for either player. In fact, things were so stymied that even [Kalonian Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalonian+Hydra), when it made its first appearance, didn't shift things much.

"I am impressed that you've managed to stall a board that has a [Kalonian Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalonian+Hydra) on it," Goldman said.

"It's tenuous," Woo acknowledged.

And it was. It took an unfortunate attack from Woo to loosen things up. He attached a [Fireshrieker](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fireshrieker) to Stone Chanter and came in with lifelink. But when a maindeck [Naturalize](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Naturalize) took out the equipment, letting [Rumbling Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Baloth) trade for the pachyderm, Woo's advantage dissipated.

Then Goldman drew Troll Hide.

"This is exactly what I needed."

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2d/44/2d440ab6a24b4ac0f62b9f1fb43457d81e06222e136301992a0eb4c2aa6d6aa6.jpg)  
*Joshua Golman* 

He played Troll Hide on [Kalonian Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalonian+Hydra), and was suddenly very much in command. Two attacks later and Woo was ready for a new game.

"I am going to concede."

As the players started game two, Woo was looking pretty blue. He wasn't happy with his deck or his chances, and he wasn't quite sure what to do. The [Kalonian Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalonian+Hydra) just grew and grew, and Woo knew his deck lacked the right kind of kung fu.

And Goldman did exactly the same thing in two.

Some creatures traded off the bat, there was a [Rumbling Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Baloth) and an [Accursed Spirit](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Accursed+Spirit) that Woo tried to fight through, but he used too much of his ju-ju to deal with what was next in Goldman's brew.

So when [Kalonian Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kalonian+Hydra) showed its many heads again, Woo knew just what he had to do.

He extended his hand and wished his fan the best of luck.

"My deck is horrible, it's really bad," Woo said. "Hey man, good luck. Unless you play my brother next round."

And with that, we were down to one Woo. Goldman's aim was true, and he knew exactly how his Hydra grew. And though it was his first big to-do, the rookie was the one who ultimately flew.

### Joshua Goldman defeats Travis Woo 2-0 to move on to the semifinals

## Quarterfinals - Neal Oliver vs. Patrick Griffin

*by Blake Rasmussen*

 

"I might just have to start traveling to Grand Prixs now."

Neal Oliver might not be a regular on the Grand Prix circuit, but he's quickly become a fixture in the Top 8s of the ones he does attend. Fresh off his win at Grand Prix Las Vegas—the largest **Magic** tournament ever held—Oliver's penchant for punching through in large limited GPs could become a thing if he decides to actually, you know, start going to them.

And, to his credit this weekend, he has a few things working in his favor. For one, I was on hand for his Vegas victory as well, making me something of a good luck charm. For another (much more important) reason, he likes his Black-Blue deck very much.

"I think it's the stone cold nuts," he said, saying he was slightly more confident in this deck than the one he had in Las Vegas. And he was very confident in that deck.

On the other hand, it's almost too bad he really likes his deck, because his opponent this round, Patrick Griffin, is playing very much the same Black-Blue control deck. Sure, some cards are the same, but both players lack [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination) and [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) to pull ahead (yup, neither player has a single copy of either). Griffin's deck is a bit more aggressive in the air and has a strong ground game to hold the fort, but Oliver has a few bombs, most notably the Islandwalking [Colossal Whale](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Colossal+Whale).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c9/d6/c9d6ae226d3da38934f9b2d0bf46df694bc3274ec191c4726932e6cceaa55da0.jpg)  
*Patrick Griffin* 

Oliver has the experience, they both have the tools, but only one Black-Blue deck could move on.

### The Games

The colors each player was sporting were certainly appropriate, as the match left both Oliver and Griffin metaphorically black and blue. There was so much back and forth, so many punches and counter punches, that they didn't even start their third game till literally every other match in the Top 8 had long since finished.

The first game started with a lot of back and forth. Oliver landed a few fliers, while Griffin did the same. An [Essence Scatter](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Essence+Scatter) caught a [Nightwing Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nightwing+Shade) while [Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) picked up a [Mark of the Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mark+of+the+Vampire). I'm not sure if that's a flavor fail or not.

But little of that mattered when the dust settled. What did matter was the [Air Servant](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Air+Servant) on Griffin's side of the board. It shut down Oliver's Marked Sengir and, over the next few turns, kept the air clear while it hit for four damage repeatedly. It only took a matter of moments for Griffin to end it from that point.

Still, the game was deceptively close.

"It was [Air Servant](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Air+Servant) versus [Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) versus you not drawing removal," Griffin said. "If you had drawn removal, that game could have gone the other way around.

In the second game, Griffin again had his [Air Servant](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Air+Servant), but a [Mark of the Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mark+of+the+Vampire) wielding [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) eventually forced a trade with the flying elemental, less Griffin fall too far behind.

That proved to be his undoing, as the ground stalled out long enough for too long to help Griffin get going.

The players kept trading resources back and forth. With no [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination)s or Opportunities in either player's decks, no one could get ahead from a stalled position.

Eventually, Neal amassed enough of an air force to push through the last of the damage, exclusively with flying creatures that would have otherwise bowed before the [Air Servant](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Air+Servant).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bf/28/bf280810257828b3c1cb4931d3401f9e983fa5159256a72582a99154f687cba2.jpg)  
*Neil Oliver* 

Oliver had one somewhat interesting play along the way to keep that air force intact. With a Sengir locked under [Claustrophobia](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Claustrophobia), he [Time Ebb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Ebb)ed his own vampire to ensure he'd draw it the following turn.

After that epic second game and close first, the match unfortunately ended a bit anticlimactically. Griffin drew an extra card and, realizing his mistake too late, was awarded a game loss. He had called a judge on himself, but because he had already made a game action, the rules were clear: he was awarded a game loss.

### Neal Oliver defeats Patrick Griffin 2-1 and moves on to the semifinals

## Semifinal - Joshua Goldman vs. William Jensen

*by Jacob Van Lunen*

 

William Jensen is staging a comeback. The Hall of Fame Elect found himself on top of the standings after fifteen rounds of swiss. Only two more opponents stand in the way on his path to victory.

Joshua Goldman is in his first Grand Prix Top 8. He regularly watches Travis Woo's stream and was very excited to tell the onlooking crowd about his victory over the **Magic Online** Community Cup competitor. He's here to have a good time, and being in the Top 4 of this 1632 player tournament is the best he could hope for.

Both players chatted as they shuffled their decks before the first game. Joshua pointed out that both of them had beaten Woos in the quarterfinals and made it very clear that he was here to maximize the amount of fun he was going to have.

Goldman got on the board first with a [Corpse Hauler](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corpse+Hauler) and started cracking Jensen for two a turn.

![Deathgaze Cockatrice](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Deathgaze+Cockatrice)

Jensen tried sculpting his hand with [Academy Raider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Academy+Raider), but Goldman's board was getting out of hand quickly as [Deadly Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deadly+Recluse) and [Rumbling Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rumbling+Baloth) joined the [Corpse Hauler](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corpse+Hauler).

Jensen tried to stabilize with [Deathgaze Cockatrice](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathgaze+Cockatrice), but [Quag Sickness](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Quag+Sickness) from Goldman meant he would be crashing in for another seven points of damage. Goldman was extremely talkative throughout the game, announcing each card and interaction with clear and loud statements.

Jensen was finally able to put the brakes on Goldman's aggression with [Liturgy of Blood](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Liturgy+of+Blood) into a [Corpse Hauler](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corpse+Hauler) of his own.

The board had been stabilized and Jensen continued to sculpt his hand with [Academy Raider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Academy+Raider). Liliana's Reaver came down for Jensen and Goldman accurately exclaimed, "Well, that, is, terrifying."

Goldman couldn't find any more gas and quickly found himself dead to rights when the Liliana's Reaver on the other side of the table found [Shiv](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shiv)'s Embrace and took over the game.

They shuffled for a few minutes and Jensen stared at Goldman for a few seconds, "It's your choice."

![](https://i.ormos.uk/21/56/2156eb81d7c167c85eeb6ce0f77134efe3d9faafd045b06bccbe0bbc7811f5fe.jpg)  
*William Jensen* 

"It's always difficult when I have a choice, but I believe I should play first. I know the pros like to draw first, but I am not a pro. I don't think I am anyway, not even after today."

Goldman struggled with his mana while Jensen molded his hand with [Academy Raider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Academy+Raider).

[Ratchet Bomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ratchet+Bomb) was ticking up for Goldman, who affectionately referred to the rare artifact as his "[pineapple bomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ratchet+Bomb)."

[Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) came down while Goldman still had two lands in play, his [pineapple bomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ratchet+Bomb) was getting in range to deal with the vampire as he found a third land, but he continued to take four points at a time.

[Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) was blown up by Goldman's [pineapple](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ratchet+Bomb), but Jensen had Liliana's Reaver and it seemed that Goldman was not long for this world. [Corrupt](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupt) dealt with Goldman's only blocker and Liliana's Reaver got in to put the game completely out of reach for Goldman. One more turn of beats was all it took for Huey to find himself a spot in the finals of Grand Prix Oakland.

## Semifinals - Neal Oliver vs. Ben Lundquist

*by Blake Rasmussen*

 

Both players have been here before. Ben Lundquist may have the experience—having been a threat to Top 8 for nearly a decade—but Neal Oliver had the more recent success, having outright won Grand Prix Las Vegas. Anyone who can win a 4,500 man tournament has clearly got some skills.

And this weekend he's proved Vegas wasn't a fluke. He narrowly edged past a Black-Blue mirror in the quarterfinals, but he was quite happy with his deck. He lacked the [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination)s and [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) that normally marked a Black-Blue deck like his, but he had plenty of removal and plenty of power, especially in the form of a hungry, hungry [Colossal Whale](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Colossal+Whale).

But Lundquist was certainly no slouch. He has four Grand Prix Top 8s and a U.S. Nationals team appearance in '06. He was at or near the top of the standings all day, and a win here could potentially pair him in an all-old school finals, with Hall of Famer William Jensen on the opposite side of the bracket.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c9/f5/c9f55b6208d4a47ffe8d2ef5736f3d7172d9979c645b94383ce8c07dee9e2220.jpg)  
*Ben Lundquist* 

His weapon of choice for the Top 8 was a tempo-oriented Blue-White deck with a pair of [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel)s headlining it, not to mention a [Fiendslayer Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiendslayer+Paladin) and an [Imposing Sovereign](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Imposing+Sovereign) to make life difficult on opponents early.

Both demonstrated remarkable talent all weekend, and a win here would cement their status as either an up and comer, or a pro who never lost his touch.

### The Games

What was quickly apparent was that neither of them showed any signs of rust. Right off the bat each player was playing around cards the other actually had, baiting with the right spells, and generally putting on a clinic for the gathered crowd and the video audience.

Much of the first game centered on the pair of [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel)s Lundquist had in his hand. He started out by baiting a [Cancel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cancel) with a [Master of Diversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Master+of+Diversion). Oliver, however, fired back by having a second [Cancel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cancel) to stop the first Angel Lundquist attempted.

The second one stuck for exactly one turn until a [Time Ebb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Ebb) gave it a temporary time out.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/17/55/1755b30ec2dc195d1010fa4d121b60c35bb74283a08ed2f0f327e55ed764e0ef.jpg)  
*Neal Oliver* 

That time, however, was exactly what Oliver needed.

Tapping seven on his very next turn, Oliver summoned a [Colossal Whale](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Colossal+Whale) to put a, well, colossal hurting on the Island-wielding Lundquist.

In succession the whale swallowed a [Stonehorn Chanter](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonehorn+Chanter) and the second [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel) in succession. But Oliver wasn't content to just bang his bomb against his opponent's board. Instead he showed some smarts that impressed a bevy of pros paying attention.

Well ahead on board, but with several creatures tucked precariously under the [Colossal Whale](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Colossal+Whale), Oliver had the patience to wait a turn to play around [Celestial Flare](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Celestial+Flare). He played a [Minotaur Abomination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Minotaur+Abomination) to ensure [Celestial Flare](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Celestial+Flare) didn't hit his most important target, and, as a result, took the first game by riding his Whale cautiously to victory.

In the second, Oliver didn't have much of an opportunity to display the talents people were commenting on. After playing three successive Swamps, Oliver missed a land drop and had to discard. It was really only a matter of Lundquist casting a [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel) and [Stonehorn Chanter](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonehorn+Chanter) and turning them sideways, briefly protected by a [Celestial Flare](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Celestial+Flare), until he sent it to a deciding third game.

And what a third game it was. [Imposing Sovereign](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Imposing+Sovereign) and [Fiendslayer Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiendslayer+Paladin) set the tone early, but both players began to dig in when removal and various 4/4 fliers became involved. [Serra Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Angel) caught a case of [Claustrophobia](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Claustrophobia), and [Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) was forced to sit back when a [Lifebane Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifebane+Zombie) revealed a pair of [Celestial Flare](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Celestial+Flare) in Lundquist's hand.

Unfortunately for Lundquist, his draws from that point on were mediocre. Oliver tried several ways to make [Celestial Flare](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Celestial+Flare) untenable, adding more and more creatures to the board without doing much more than blocking [Fiendslayer Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiendslayer+Paladin) with a 1/3 now and then. Lundquist refused to bite.

![Time Ebb](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Time+Ebb)

But when [Time Ebb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Ebb) on the Paladin freed Oliver up to attack with five creatures, suddenly Lundquist couldn't afford to sit back anymore.

And Oliver knew it. He started snapping his cards briskly, dong the math and attacking with his entire crew. Lundquist drew a few creatures to help block, but a [Corrupt](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupt) and some blocks cleared them out.

Eventually, and with a crisp finality, a [Time Ebb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Ebb) cleared away enough creatures to let Neal Oliver crash through for yet another trip to a Grand Prix finals. In his first Grand Prix since winning Las Vegas, Oliver had found himself in a familiar position.

Playing for the title.

### Neal Oliver defeats Ben Lundquist 2-1

## Finals - Neal Oliver vs. William Jensen

*by Jacob Van Lunen*

 

Hall of Fame Elect, William Jensen, is showing the world of **Magic** why he deserves his spot in the Hall with a finals appearance here at Grand Prix Oakland. It's been a long road to this point, and his Finals opponent is sure to put up a formidable battle.

His opponent, Neal Oliver, is quickly building a resume for himself after winning the largest **Magic** tournament in history at Grand Prix Las Vegas. This is his first **Magic** tournament since his victory in Vegas and he's proven that he has what it takes to compete at the very highest level of Limited competition.

"Is this your first big finish?" asked Jensen.

Oliver smiled, "well, I won Vegas."

"I'd say that's pretty big."

Both players kept their opening hands. Oliver made the first play of the game with [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) while Jensen had an [Academy Raider](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Academy+Raider) to sort of keep up.

[Seacoast Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seacoast+Drake) and [Wring Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wring+Flesh) from Oliver cleared Jensen's board. Oliver attacked with his [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) and drew an extra card.

Huey's [Canyon Minotaur](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Canyon+Minotaur) was lost in a [Time Ebb](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Ebb) as Oliver's [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) continued to generate card advantage and ping away at Jensen's lifetotal. Jensen attempted to stabilize with [Pitchburn Devils](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pitchburn+Devils), but Oliver's [Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) would present a serious problem.

[Wild Guess](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Guess) found Chandra's Outrage to deal with Oliver's Vampire, but the recast [Canyon Minotaur](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Canyon+Minotaur) was Domesticated and Jensen had his back against the wall.

Jensen netted a lot of cards by using Altar's [Reap](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reap) in conjunction with [Pitchburn Devils](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pitchburn+Devils) that had been enchanted with [Sensory Deprivation](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sensory+Deprivation), but he was still struggling to keep up with the Scroll Thieves that Oliver continued to force through.

Oliver's thievery proved too much for Jensen, despite a reasonably strong draw, and they were on to a second game.

Jensen liked his chances better on the play after seeing the combination of [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) and removal in the first game. Oliver's [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) was, once again, the first play of the game. Jensen decided to use a removal spell right away to prevent Oliver from winning in the same fashion as the first game.

*William Jensen*

[Liturgy of Blood](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Liturgy+of+Blood) cleared the way for the reaver, and Oliver used [Wring Flesh](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wring+Flesh) to avoid a trigger.

Oliver had been forced to use three cards and two turns to deal with Jensen's bomb and Jensen continued to develop with [Mindsparker](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindsparker), [Gnawing Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gnawing+Zombie), and [Pitchburn Devils](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pitchburn+Devils).

[Nephalia Seakite](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Seakite) and [Warden of Evos Isle](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warden+of+Evos+Isle) began to stage a defense. The [Pitchburn Devils](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pitchburn+Devils) and [Corrupt](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupt) in Jensen's Hand would likely be enough to finish off Oliver, but he refused to give up hope.

Jensen put Oliver in range for [Volcanic Geyser](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Geyser) or [Corrupt](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupt) and passed the turn. [Volcanic Geyser](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Geyser) caught a [Cancel](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cancel), but the follow-up [Corrupt](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupt) was good enough to move things on to a third game.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a1/08/a108f0f1dfcbe0b844f1377c1bac6cfb0af906bb32d239178774c7153d874ef4.jpg)  
*Neal Oliver* 

"It's been a good match so far."

"Yeah, it has," nodded Jensen, "Good luck, you're playing, right?"

Jensen's opening hand was good enough, but Oliver was forced to go down to five cards.

"This game might be less interesting."

But it wasn't!

[Seacoast Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seacoast+Drake) and [Corpse Hauler](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corpse+Hauler) stared at one another as both players developed their mana. [Trained Condor](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trained+Condor) from Oliver threatened to break the stalemate, but Jensen had the [Shock](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shock) he needed to keep Oliver at bay.

[Mindsparker](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindsparker) from Jensen was met with [Scroll Thief](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scroll+Thief) from Oliver. Jensen's [Sengir Vampire](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sengir+Vampire) was [Corrupt](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupt)ed, but he continued to apply pressure with [Pitchburn Devils](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pitchburn+Devils).

[Warden of Evos Isle](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warden+of+Evos+Isle) wanted to block something, but [Act of Treason](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Act+of+Treason) and [Altar's Reap](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Altar%27s+Reap) made short work of that plan.

## Top 5 Cards

*by Jacob Van Lunen*

 

 

![Elixir of Immortality](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Elixir+of+Immortality)

 

### 5\. [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality)

[Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality) isn't a card you would expect to make the maindeck of a professional's draft, but some of the most powerful cards in ***Magic** 2014* are card draw spells. Decks with many copies of [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) and [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination) often put themselves in danger of decking. A single copy of [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality) helps the deck survive quick aggression in some cases, increases the card quality of a remaining library when a player already has a lot of lands in play, and prevents the player with multiple copies of [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) or [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination) from decking themselves.

### 4\. [Brave the Elements](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brave+the+Elements)

Ben Lundquist used [Brave the Elements](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brave+the+Elements) to make [Pacifism](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pacifism) fall off his [Seraph of the Sword](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Seraph+of+the+Sword) so he could successfully survive an attack and swing back for the win in the quarterfinals. [Brave the Elements](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brave+the+Elements) was seeing extensive sideboard play throughout the second day of competition. Often allowing players to alpha strike for the win, and often serving as a one-mana [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate) against decks with a lot of removal.

### 3\. [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination+)

Drawing cards is very good in ***Magic** 2014* where very few non-rares are capable of netting card advantage on their own. Brian Kibler, Ben Stark, Tom Martell, and William Jensen all identified [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination) as the most desirable common in ***Magic** 2014* Sealed Deck.

### 2\. [Enlarge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlarge)

Described as a [Lava Axe](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lava+Axe) and [Cruel Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cruel+Edict) rolled into one by Ben Stark, [Enlarge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlarge) stole games and killed bombs all over the room throughout this weekend. [Enlarge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlarge) was also a big skill tester. Better players would leave a less relevant creature untapped when they cast an important creature into potential [Enlarge](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlarge) mana from the opponent, while less experienced players would freely attacked and lose their most important piece of board presence to the Green sorcery.

### 1\. [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity+)

[Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) was a clear favorite amongst the set's uncommons. ***Magic** 2014* sealed play involves a lot of one-for-one trades wherein each card is exchanged for a single card of the opponent's without help. In ***Magic** 2014* , card advantage (The overarching strategy that involves trading cards of your own for more cards of your opponent's) is usually achieved through combat tricks or instant speed removal. [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade) in response to [Giant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Growth), [Shock](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shock) during a double block, or [Giant Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Giant+Growth) to counteract an opposing pump spell are all common examples of how a player might get ahead in the card economy trade. This makes the format extremely skill intensive and rewards players for reading their opponents and casting spells at the most opportunistic moments. A card like [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) breaks all the rules and provides the caster with four fresh new cards at the expense of a single card. If both players are casting and trading cards at a similar rate, the player with [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20221005003305/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity) will have three more cards in hand when their opponent is all out of gas.
