### Grand Prix-New Orleans

A number of weeks after Pro Tour-Houston, the Extended season returned to the region with GP-New Orleans. The city is not a **Magic** haven, as you will often hear the natives lament. There are comparatively few local players and places to play **Magic** – but it makes for a great tournament location, with Bourbon Street luring many a young pro. Despite this, the turnout was extremely light as compared to other North American GPs. Fewer than 400 players attended. There are a number of theories to explain this, not least of which is the nearness of Pro Tour-Chicago and the availability of **Magic Online** as a testing tool to substitute for live testing at the GP.

Historically, the first North American GP after a Constructed Pro Tour sees a metagame very similar to that exhibited at the Pro Tour itself – regardless of innovations introduced at foreign GPs and various PTQs in the weeks between. This is due to the fact that many of the pro players that populate the top tables have not played the format since the Pro Tour and would rather go in with the deck they know well than experiment with something new for the sake of a single GP. New Orleans was no exception, with Rock being the most popular deck. It was followed by blue-green – a deck that only became truly popular after the Pro Tour was over, but was seen in Houston. Psychatog and Oath were the next most popular archetypes, followed by the ubiquitous Sligh.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpno03/919.jpg)To everyone’s surprise, it was one of the least-played archetypes in the tournament that won the event – Zvi’s Turboland. Zvi Mowshowitz created Turboland fairly early in his career as Magic’s ‘mad scientist’. The deck was very cute, but it was definitely not tier 1. Zvi played it in a number of tournaments with varying levels of success. His best record previous to New Orleans was a Top 16 at Grand Prix: Houston – an excellent result in itself, but hardly enough to convince anyone that Turboland was a viable tier 1 archetype. Zvi’s quest continued in New Orleans where he made Day 2 with ease. Sunday morning, Zvi experienced a minor setback as he started the day with a 1-2 record, but he rallied, winning the next three rounds and defeating Tony Tsai to earn a Top 8 spot. While Zvi was the only Your Move Games player to make Day 2, he defended the team’s honor by winning the tournament. In all fairness though, it was his own achievement, as no one else on the team was helping with Turboland.

Mowshowitz defeated Harvey in the finals of this tournament. The U.S. National Champion and GP-Atlanta winner Harvey’s archetype of choice for this tournament was Psychatog. Harvey earned his second Grand Prix Top 8, edging up into the Top 25 of Player of the Year race, currently led by Bob Maher.

Michael Pustilnik came in third. Pustilnik has any number of accomplishments to his name, but he certainly seems to excel at Extended format Grand Prix. Psychatog was his weapon of choice also, but he splashed red whereas Harvey used a black-blue version of the deck. In fourth place was Worlds semifinalist Diego Ostrovich. His was an historic finish, as Ostrovich became the first player from outside North America to earn a Top 8 finish in an American GP. Godinez became the first player to do so from outside of U.S. and Canada just one tournament back. As there are fewer GPs now, it seems that more pros will be willing to travel to attend one – and that means European and South American players showing up in United States, not just American pros traveling the world. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpno03/932.jpg)Jeff Cunningham won Grand Prix-Philadelphia just months ago. He posted another excellent finish by coming in fifth in New Orleans. Cunningham was among a handful of competitors to play u-g in Houston. He kept at it and was the only player to represent the archetype in the Top 8. Although he lost to Mowshowitz in quick two games of the quarterfinals, even Mowshowitz admits it was his toughest matchup in the Top 8 and that he was extremely fortunate to win it. Morgan Douglass surprised everyone by resurrecting the Tinker deck and playing it well enough to make Top 8. Likewise, Carl Lobato was the only player at the top tables with Angry Hermit. This was Lobato’s first career Top 8, but he impressed pros and reporters with his level of confidence and ability to pilot his deck, so it probably isn’t his last. 

Finally, squeaking into the Top 8 on tiebreakers despite having no byes was Trey Van Cleave. Having ‘done time’ for his attempted bribery at Pro Tour-New Orleans a little over a year ago, Van Cleave just recently returned to competitive **Magic**. He played in GP-LA, but did not do well there. In New Orleans, he brought out a Sligh deck and managed to take it all the way into the Top 8. In ninth place was last U.S. GP champion Philip Freneau, also with monored.

Of course, New Orleans was not all about playing Magic. A number of stories of players’ adventures on and around Bourbon Street circulated, few of which are printable here. Among the G-rated ones is that of Gerard Fabiano, singing “It’s Raining Men” in front of a hundred screaming girls in a Karaoke joint. Those of you who remember the last GP-New Orleans know that something bizarre happens to me when I attend a tournament in that fine city. This year was no different, and I present this anecdote for your reading pleasure:

It’s late Sunday evening and a large group of us is out on the town. Josh Bennett, The Ferrett, and several others want nothing more than to play Dance Dance Revolution, which is available at some casino on the outskirts of town known as “The West Bank” (the place sure looked desolate enough to fit the name). A dozen of us head there. They get very little DDR time in, as the arcade closes at 11 o’clock – and so does the restaurant. Most of us are ravenous enough to choose the restaurant and we are done eating at just about eleven. Most of the crowd heads back to town, but Ben Bleiweiss, Tony Tsai, Jeff Taylor, The Ferrett and I remain to check out the casino itself. We walk into its poker room (roughly the size of my living room or only a little bigger) to find ourselves as the only customers there under the age of sixty. 

Ben and I try our luck at one of the tables. We play for about half an hour. Eventually one of the old-timers decides to start up a conversation. He looks up at me, points at Ben and loudly asks:

“Is that your daddy?”

### Coming Up: Pro Tour-Chicago

The season’s third Pro Tour is only a week away and there is great reason for everyone – qualified or not – to make the trip. Over the course of last season, Wizards of the Coast has been making a push to increase side event attendance. As a result, there are some excellent tournaments available to those not competing in the Tour itself. Here are just some of the things you can be doing if you travel to Chicago:

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>On Saturday and Sunday, there are TWO Pro Tour Qualifiers for Venice and TWO Junior Super Series Challenges, as well as a Grand Prix-Boston Trial. </i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

On Thursday, there is a “Last Chance” qualifier. Instituted at the beginning of this season, it is a four-slot qualifier for the next day’s Pro Tour. Considering that it is not a Grand Prix and pros are not allowed to play, this is anyone’s best chance to play on the PT. And if you are already qualified for the Pro Tour, you can also spend a day trying to grind into the Masters, which is set to begin on Thursday evening.

On Saturday and Sunday, there are TWO Pro Tour Qualifiers for Venice and TWO Junior Super Series Challenges, as well as a Grand Prix-Boston Trial. There are also a couple of dealer-sponsored tournaments, including Chicago Open (first place is a Mox) and Female Only tournament (first place a box of *Onslaught*). And, of course, do not forget the Question Mark trivia show on Saturday evening.

There are also two promotions open to anyone signing up for side events in Chicago. Every side event entry is an entry to a drawing for each of these promotions. One of them will win a player a free entry into a premier event run by Pastimes in Chicago area. Another one, sponsored by Kings Games, will earn one lucky player free airfare to Grand Prix-Boston (within United States only)

Here is a [complete list](/en/events/2002/chicago02/2003-pro-tourchicago#sides) of side events in Chicago. Hope to see you there!

### Play of the Week

![](https://i.ormos.uk/12/4c/124ce4ac0ccd551af7ec7a4d4f836efff824e46d1d3ac40c70e3276586c9bed6.jpg)Gary Lee drafted a solid blue-white deck in an *Onslaught* booster draft. He stalled his opponent’s early offense with Battlefield Medic and Mistform Wall and then brought out a Riptide Shapeshifter. His opponent was shocked to be facing the wrong end of Visara the Dreadful, as Gary activated the Shapeshifter naming gorgon at the end of the next turn. 

Lee drafted a pair of Shapeshifters and, when he opened Visara in the third pack, felt that the risk of drawing the card he could not possibly cast was well worth the ability to get it directly into play with one of his two Shapeshifters. He proceeded to do so at least twice during the draft, and won the whole thing.

### Bad Play of the Week

A group of upstate New York players met Friday night to practice for the upcoming Pro Tour Qualifier in Edison, New Jersey. They were more than willing to suffer the four hour drive each way in order to play in the PTQ. Furthermore, their plan was to stay up all night playtesting for the tournament in order to come up with an ultimate strategy. Testing was going well. So well, in fact, that the hapless group did not realize it was morning. By the time they looked at the clock, it was 9 AM, far too late to make it to the tournament. One might only hope that the decks they came up with were so good, they’ll win the next qualifier.
