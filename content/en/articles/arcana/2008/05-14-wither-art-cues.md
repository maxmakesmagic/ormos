In the creepy, cruel world of Shadowmoor, some damage is worse—and decidedly more *permanent*—than usual. Mechanically, something with the wither ability deals damage to creatures in the form of -1/-1 counters. But what's the flavor of wither? We're glad you asked.

According to the *Shadowmoor* Style Guide, the boggart warrens have become gangs—loose, semi-nomadic, wolfpack-like social groups. The boggarts seek out new sensations relentlessly, but they also have a strong destructive urge. They hunt with rusty weapons, which have lasting effects on their victims:

 ![Rustrazor_Butcher_640](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/cardart/SHM/Rustrazor_Butcher_640.jpg)  
 *Rustrazor Butcher art by Ron Spencer* 

Contrariwise, some cards effectively start out already being affected by wither. The reason that Witherscale Wurm treats other sources as having wither is because its skin is constantly sloughing off:

 ![Witherscale_Wurm_640](https://i.ormos.uk/4f/1d/4f1d8e2df49ce34e0986df13b8d9ec1a41bf0298b7c75360cf48834dc81dd511.jpg)  
 *Witherscale Wurm art by Thomas M. Baxa* 

In the sunlit world of Lorwyn, some flamekin warriors used the fire of their own bodies to heat their weapons. The flames of their counterparts in Shadowmoor, the cinders, have guttered, but their bodies still glow warm enough to heat a blade—and what they lack in fire, they make up for in sheer cruelty.

 ![Kulrath_Knight_640](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/cardart/SHM/Kulrath_Knight_640.jpg)  
 *Kulrath Knight art by Daarken*
