*Scourge* has been in stores for well over a month now and it even hit ***Magic** Online*. There were a ton of people that wrote reviews of the new cards based on just reading them, but by now, I feel it should be possible to rate the cards with a deeper understanding of the format. You can't really get everything right without ever playing with them in my opinion.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0d/5e/0d5e392537c216511eff6fe51c431ed8645f7f4235863c5aa04913fcbf8cc8f1.jpg)

I've played about a hundred drafts, went 5-1 at German Nationals in *Onslaught/Legions/Scourge* Rochester Draft with the one loss being the match loss you've most likely heard about by now and went undefeated in Team Rochester Drafts together with Dirk Baberowski and Marco Blume at Grand Prix-Amsterdam. I am not claiming that I fully understand the format, but I think I have a good enough idea of what is going on.

The first thing that has to be said is that the set doesn't have a ridiculous common card like *Onslaught* (Sparksmith) and *Legions* (Timberwatch Elf) do. That doesn't mean that there isn't a format-defining common though. Zombie Cutthroat doesn't have as much raw power as the goblin and the elf do, but it still changes the way you play the format. The days that you could just attack into a morph when your opponent was tapped out are gone. Doing so with a creature like Battering Craghorn, Gravel Slinger or Snarling Undorak can cost you the game if you spend your fourth turn to unmorph and then lose your creature to the zombie anyway.

So far, the card sounds pretty ridiculous, but fortunately it isn't all that good. Five life is a hefty price to pay after all and if you aren't playing black and draw into it with a single-digit life total, you most likely can't afford to unmorph it. In a black deck, that argument doesn't count obviously. ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) is a fine price to pay for a 3/4 creature. Still, if you know or suspect that your opponent has a Cutthroat on the table, you can easily play around it. Not blocking a morph for a few turns doesn't decide games like an active Sparksmith and Timberwatch Elf do. It is, after all, just a 2/2 creature. Zombie Cutthroat is certainly very good, but it isn't even the best common in my opinion, but I'll address that later on.

I am not going to talk about rares here, as it they don't show up frequently enough in draft or sealed deck. But if you take a look at the commons in the set, you have to notice that green came up short. There are only seven playable ones and none of them is exciting. In fact Accelerated Mutation isn't that only one that I'd grade “slightly worse to strictly worse than mediocre”. Looking for powerful green cards in pack 3 might not be a good plan after all. Between the commons and uncommons, the only highlight is Krosan Warchief. And that guy is only the third-best Warchief as well. The other uncommons aren't as bad the commons fortunately, but the problem is that they are all very situational. Hunting Pack is very expensive, while Kurgadon and Alpha Status need the right circumstances to do something, but if things happen to be right, they are bombs. Red is in a quite similar position. The commons aren't great, but a few of the uncommons are really good. Extra Arms, Skirk Volcanist and Carbonize are all excellent cards. Goblin Warchief should almost always make your deck, as a 2/2 with haste for three mana is good enough on its own in this format and it can possibly be really, really powerful if you happen to have goblins to go with him. The third best color is blue. The commons are clearly better than the ones in green and red. The uncommons can't really keep up, with the exception of Pemmin's Aura. I don't think that the card is as powerful as some people claim it is, but it's still blue creature removal and if you happen to put it on a card that combos with it, the game is over sometimes. Timberwatch Elf, Grassland Crusader and Daru Stinger all work quite well.

Now we're getting to the more spicy stuff. White and black both have an amazing number of great cards. White clearly has the upper hand when it comes to uncommons. Daru Warchief trumps its black counterpart while Silver Knight, Dragon Stalker, Wing Shards and Karona's Zealot outclass Putrid Raptor, Bladewing's Thrall and Chill Haunting, although the Raptor is clearly the best card of that non-Warchief group. The commons, however, push black over the top.

The following are my personal ratings of commons in each color. But you always have to keep in mind if you open your *Scourge* booster in a draft, you already have 30 cards in your pile. That changes things a lot. Soldiers move up if you have more then one Daru Stinger or Aven Warhawk, removal becomes more important if you have none and so on.

10 of 11 commons playable is quite an impressive ratio. The high quality easily makes black the most powerful color in *Scourge*. The only real problem is that a black mage has to share his second-best common with all the other players, as Zombie Cutthroat is better then almost all commons the other colors have. It still isn't the best black card, as it is vastly inferior to Twisted Abomination. If you have played with that card before, it should remind you of an *Onslaught* rare – Jareth, Leonine Titan. Both are almost impossible to remove, can't really die in combat and force your opponent to start chump-blocking really fast. Sure, the protection ability Jarteh has is great, but in the format of morphs, even the mighty cat warrior can be blocked most of the time if necessairy. The Abomination, on the other hand, has a great ability for a six casting cost card: landcycling. Being able to trade it for a swamp early on is great as it turns a dead card, if you are stuck on two or three lands, into a valuable mana source. And as Twisted Abomination is a zombie as well, it is usually not all that difficult to get him back into your hand.

Well, that was a lot of text for just one common – but believe me, it is that good. Undead Warchief, Soul Collector, Decree of Pain and Call to the Grave are the only cards I'd pick over Twisted Abomination. The second slot is quite easy as well, as the Cutthroat is even better if you run swamps and can cast it. Next up are Lingering Death and Clutch of Undeath. Those two are very close. I'd say that Clutch is more powerful and also the better removal, but I still think that it is correct to pick Lingering Death higher. The mana curve issue is quite important and there are few things I hate as much as losing to turn 2 Sparksmith or turn 3 Timberwatch Elf. Clutch deals with them. It is usually too late for you to kill the goblin on turn 5 and that is why I go with Lingering Death here. Just make sure that you don't end up with too many of them; I would not want more than two most of the time.

Carrion Feeder is another card that was underrated early on. As soon as you trade one creature in combat, it can attack and trade with a morph, but I've seen plenty of games where the little 1/1 was suddenly 5/5 and the other guy just couldn't keep up any more. It is best used in green-black and black-red. I don't like it all that much in black-white, due to the defensive nature of that deck. If your deck is going to attack every turn, he's excellent.

Reaping the Graves is one of those cards you almost always want to have one of in your deck, but never really want to play more than that. I've sideboarded the second one against removal-heavy decks but more than that is surely a bad idea. That's why I don't pick it too highly. Vengeful Death, on the other hand, works best in high numbers. I saw one guy with three of those in play once and that was quite a beating. The problem is that you get a creature with toughness two for four mana. That isn't all that great in a world of 2/2s for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif). Still, if you have lots of zombies, he can easily be a game winner by dealing four or five extra damage.

Death's-Head Buzzard is okay, but nothing to be excited about. The double black casting cost makes it tough to cast it on turn 3 and the effect is a drawback quite often. Sure, sometimes you can kill several creatures on your opponent's side, but I've left that guy in my sideboard a lot, especially with eight or less swamps in my deck. Dragon Shadow is usually not maindeck material, although it can be very good in black-green. It is a great sideboard card against non-black green and/or white decks.

In the very last slot we got Unburden. I've played with the card quite a bit and I found that I never really wanted to cast it. It's nice if your opponent mulliganed, but otherwise, you usually end up cycling it and that is not very exciting. It's a good filler if something went wrong in the draft, definitely better than an off-color morph.

Dragon Scales was probably the most underrated card in Limited when *Scourge* was released. I heard from multiple people that they didn't run that in the Prerelease and that had to be wrong. If you enchant a morph with this, you end up with a 3/4 creature that doesn't tap to attack. That's quite good for turn four. If you happen to have a better target for the Scales, your opponent is in a world of pain. Deftblade Elite + Scales is game over quite often, Aven Redemer gives you a 3/4 flier that can prevent two damage each turn. With Lowland Tracker, you have a power 3 provoker with first strike and Daru Stinger or Grassland Crusader enchanted with Dragon Scales mean that your opponent better have a Cruel Revival or Skinthinner in the next couple of turns, or else.

It gets a lot more interesting after Scales and Cutthroat. Frontline Strategist, Noble Templar and Aven Farseer are all quite close. The only exception is if you have drafted a soldier-heavy deck. That puts the Strategist over the top by far. If you have less then five soldiers, the Templar is your pick. If you have a lot of good morphs, the Farseer gets the nod. Behind that, things get rather straightforward. I am not a big fan of the Inquisitor at all and I've left it in my sideboard several times so far. Aven Liberator is a decent creature but the morph cost is very expensive. A 2/3 flier isn't bad though and I'd always play this guy. Astral Steel and Daru Spiritualist are both cards that usually shouldn't make your maindeck. Steel can sometimes be okay if you have either cheap spells or are very desperate for some kind of combat trick. The Spiritualist, on the other hand, is usually unplayable, but he can be very good in the right deck – namely if you have a lot of Whipgrass Entanglers. With both in play, any cleric gets +0/+2 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif), as often as you wish. That combo has won me a few games on its own.

Blue is a bit strange, as all commons are playable to some extent. I mean, you don't want Temporal Fissure or Hindering Touch in your deck, but there are surely situations where they are good and I can easily see myself playing those in Team Rochester, when I know that I have to handle certain cards my opponent has. But compared to black and white, the cards just aren't as powerful. Rush of Knowledge is better then Zombie Cutthroat, but after the third best card, Shoreline Ranger, there is quite a drop to the next one, Raven Guild Initiate. You might be surprised to see Frozen Solid that low on the list, but I don't like the card all that much. I don't like taking damage from the attacking creature once and it can't deal with Spitting or Needleshot Gourna other then trading 2-1 if you run some creature into it. It does deal with Sparksmith and Timberwatch Elf, but you usually lose another creature before they actually tap the enchanted creature and it costs double blue so you might not be able to cast it on turn 3.

Coast Watcher is the first card on this list that shouldn't make your maindeck most of the time. A table has usually three green mages and that means it is just not worth it. Mercurial Kite and Scornfoul Egoist are better maindeck cards, but I'd call those emergency solutions and nothing else. A power 2 flier with an almost non-existent ability isn't good enough and the Egoist isn't much better than an off-color morph if you don't need Skill targets or have three or more spells that check for the permanent with the highest casting cost in play. Dragon Wings, on the other hand, becomes very good if you have several non-flying high casting cost creatures. In blue-green, it moves several spots up in the list, just behind Shoreline Ranger.

Alright, if you thought red was bad, then take a look at this. If you don't have some broken high casting cost creature, I'd prefer to have cards rated 5th and lower in my sideboard at all times. Green usually has to race and the Mutation helps quite a bit here. Green decks should be able to get a five or six casting cost creature into play and that is usually good enough.
