Posting a good result at a premier event with an original deck is every deck builder's dream. One player who realized that dream at [GP Madrid](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/grand-prix-madrid-2014-coverage-events) is Steffen Van De Veen, with his quirky mono-white lifegain deck.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Martyr+of+Sands)](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Sands)

Normally, decks like this have [Soul Warden](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soul+Warden) in them, bolstered by creatures like [Serra Ascendant](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Ascendant) that give more meaning to gaining life, and these types of decks have become collectively known as "Soul Sisters." However, that is probably a misnomer for this deck, as it plays a completely different game with a totally different way of winning compared to the traditional Soul Sisters.

The usual pattern for Soul Sisters is lifegain paired with creatures like [Serra Ascendant](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Ascendant) and [Ajani's Pridemate](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani%27s+Pridemate), that have a bonus triggered by lifegain. The deck uses [Ranger of Eos](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ranger+of+Eos) to fetch them up and is essentially a beatdown deck that looks to win with a creature rush. In comparison, Steffen's deck is much higher on the control axis. Pretty much the only thing it can attack with in the early game is [Serra Ascendant](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serra+Ascendant), but only if it can get lots of life off of [Martyr of Sands](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martyr+of+Sands). The primary game plan here is to use [Wrath of God](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wrath+of+God) to reset the board state and then slowly establish a superior position.

You can also see how this deck is playing the long game by the fact that one of the creatures included as a Ranger target is [Weathered Wayfarer](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Weathered+Wayfarer). The end goal is to set up a lifegain loop by having the Wayfarer grab [Emeria, the Sky Ruin](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Emeria%2C+the+Sky+Ruin), or to use [Ghost Quarter](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ghost+Quarter) to take out lands. [Ghostly Prison](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ghostly+Prison) is an excellent choice here as a support card. Not only does it help the deck buy time to set up its win condition, it's also a great deterrent for [Splinter Twin](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter+Twin) decks.
