Templating, in the context of **Magic** R&D, is the process of bringing the wording of a **Magic** card's rules text into alignment with the text of similar cards, or coming up with sensible wording for new rules text.

The indestructibility mechanic took some serious templating.

At first, indestructible was a keyword mechanic, meaning that it would be like [Severed Legion](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Severed%2BLegion) or [Darksteel Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Darksteel%2BColossus) or [Birds of Paradise](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Birds%2Bof%2BParadise). So a card like [Darksteel Pendant](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Darksteel+Pendant) would have been templated initially this way (shown here with a first pass at some reminder text):

> Darksteel Pendant  
> ![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/colorless3.gif)  
>  Indestructible *(This card can't be destroyed but can be sacrificed or removed from the game.)*  
> ![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/colorless1.gif), ![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/tap.gif): Look at the top card of your library. You may put that card on the bottom of your library.

The templating team worked on the reminder text, and came up with this (as seen on the playtest card at left):

> Indestructible *(If this card would be put into the graveyard from play, instead leave this card in play. This ability does not work if this card is sacrificed.)*

(Note #1: This playtest card, a 2/2 indestructible artifact creature called "Indestructo," never became a card in a real **Magic** set. Note #2: indestructibility was once going to be included in "Bacon," a.k.a. *Mirrodin*, but was pushed off into *Darksteel*.)

As Randy Buehler's [January 9 article](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=mtgcom/daily/rb105) mentions, using "indestructible" as a keyword caused some weird phrasing. Cards would "gain indestructible until end of turn" (via [Slobad, Goblin Tinkerer](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Slobad%2C+Goblin+Tinkerer)'s ability, for example), which just didn't sound natural.

So "indestructible" is now templated the way "[Neurok Spy](https://web.archive.org/web/20040302000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Neurok%2BSpy)" is, as a regular English adjective that has specific rules implications.
