### 

This is the second installment of my series evaluating the cards of *Legions* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the first installment [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/2003/03-27-legions-limited-review-black).

### Blue

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5a/c0/5ac0c13466add21bce7d53cc1075f51488390006fe90d83f85a6890fabb07556.jpg)It’s funny, everyone seems to think blue got a lot better in *Legions*, but looking at this list, it seems to me that it got the shaft again. The color only has three commons I’d call good (Echo Tracer, Keeneye Aven and Mistform Seaswift), got the worst of the Muses, lacks a true bomb and has a lot of overcosted cards.

I suppose the blue cards are more solid overall, but that feels balanced by the loss of Lavamancer’s Skill and Sparksmith in pack three. Those are the two cards that made blue so good in the first place. Ironic that they aren’t in the color, huh? Even worse is the fact that other colors have wizards now, meaning nonblue drafters are more likely to pick up Skills then they were in triple *Onslaught*. You just can’t win.

Blue did improve in one way, diversifying itself with color-partner options increasing by and large to red, black or white in most people’s opinions, where before those same folks felt it could only be played in conjunction with mountains. The basic idea behind the two new decks is to use blue’s superior evasion to steal a victory from larger, ground-based creatures. That it does quite well.

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/32/7e/327e8dd894d1422ea66b038c0c2b3d64809338f4f1ceb40a3f1303267c4c80b5.jpg)**Weaver of Lies**  
 The morph you must morph. Without the morph ability, the Weaver would be a truly terrible card. With it though, you have a potentially devastating effect, allowing you to flip over Echo Tracer, Skirk Marauder, Skinthinner and other when- turned-face-up creatures, while making large opposing morphs small again, usually during combat. The Weaver can completely turn a game around in one combat step.

**Chromeshell Crab**  
 The Crab’s ability is more directly powerful but less diverse, which is why it finishes a close second. That said, the Crab is great, allowing you to trade a creature with lethal damage on its stack for your opponent’s best man, woman or child, providing card advantage with a potentially game-breaking twist. Not a lot to complain about here.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/b5/3ab52f6a86dc802d20bae6b92dad82a1e5163ff5613e0d874c580cdbfa43ec92.jpg)**Wall of Deceit**  
 Walls just keep getting better and better. This one is amazing, with the possibility of killing an opposing morph on turn four for a one mana payment, meaning you can cast another creature that same turn. Once your opponent knows what it is, there still isn’t all that much they can do about it, with the Wall’s backside proving so grandiose that it’s just about impossible to kill. Did I mention it costs two mana? Two mana!

**Echo Tracer**  
 Early in the game, the Tracer is tempo advantage combined with the potential for card advantage, potentially returning a Skinthinner or Marauder after damage is on the stack, only to see them eventually go face up again. Later on, it becomes this super-obnoxious un-killable creature, alternatively disrupting your opponent’s fatal attack or attempt on your most important creature’s life. An incredibly diverse card.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cf/b3/cfb349a420cb0222ec400099c58d06f07f3d166acb8bd766cd8b1beba91c8498.jpg)**Willbender**  
Willbender’s ability is more immediately devastating, but the Tracer’s extra point of power is huge when the measuring stick for the environment reads ‘2/2’, so the ‘Bender loses out in this comparison, though by very, very little. The ‘Bender is powerful, killing Sparksmith or Visara, redirecting Skill and combat tricks and really, there’s little your opponent can do, because the morph cost is so low that you’ll almost always have it available. A great tide-turner.

**Riptide Mangler**  
 Oh man, I love this guy. Best in green-blue, the color combination you don’t really want to be drafting, the Mangler gets pluses from Timberwatch then activates, holding that shape, then does it again next turn, and next turn, and next turn… it becomes pretty ridiculous. Conversely, you can just play a Treespring Lorian and have a 5/3 for two mana… sound good?

**Mistform Ultimus**  
 Yes, there are drawbacks: Your opponent draws from Savage and can block with Biologist and your Shapeshifter can disappoint, but the Ultimus is a 3/3 for four that can’t be Revivaled in a color that needs all the fat it can get. Throw in the sheer number of tribal effects that become more important with *Legions* hordes and you have a card that I for one almost always want in my deck and am willing to pick early in order to get it there. Remember, it always helps with amplify.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/43/744341ec0ab2e493512a55d84d2ce81e97338e9515d5b1ceed5ae0b1d39b55aa.jpg)**Keeneye Aven**  
 I’ve always hated the 2/2 flyer for four, but an extra point of toughness in the 2/2 environment can change a lot and when you add the ability to sacrifice itself for the good of a better mana draw, you have in your possession a strong Limited card that makes your deck more functional. The Aven rules the skies, showing those 2/2 flyers who’s boss, so you really want as many as you can get your hands on.

**Mistform Seaswift**  
 Despite its superior firepower, the Seaswift loses out to the Aven because it’s so easy to kill. All your opponent needs is any piece of creature kill or any flying creature and its impulses of freedom are sated. That said, the morph ability is undercosted and the Illusion aspect of its multi-persona is a nice little bonus, so there’s little complaining to do here.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/17/8b/178bcde214e2b544c3499620b205ebe18c638bb39193374a4180ef820c33ab70.jpg)**Synapse Sliver**  
 More often than not, the value of this card is determined wholly by the number of illusions you have in your deck. On its own, while the Sliver is indeed large, it seems unlikely to break through opposing ground defenses, so it needs a little help from its friends to become functional, with the results often proving devastating. 

**Primoc Escapee**  
 Yes, it’s huge and it cycles, meaning that it’s diverse, but even once it’s in play the Escapee has drawbacks by comparison that make it pale to its Fateshaper cousin. First, that point of toughness is the difference between life and death where Pinpoint Avalanche is concerned. Second, the constant rearranging of the library is a pretty big deal and while the cycling negates some of that, it doesn’t negate it all.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/55/5d/555d63bf4f80444feb34d75bc24bc9bcfd5bf07f9e4b440607f888dea23ed5d6.jpg)**Riptide Director**  
 This guy looks really powerful on the surface doesn’t he? Slow in a fast environment, the Director survives combat with an unactivated morph and acts as a Jayemdae Tome without any assistance from its wizardly buddies, but if they do come out to party, this becomes Slate of Ancestry with a killer body. Not much to complain about.

**Master of the Vale**  
 I like the Master a lot. It survives morph-on-morph combat by de-morphing and morph on de-morphed morph combat by un-morphing their morph when it de-morphs itself and its de-morphing un-morphs the de-morphed morph that you want un-morphed again so you can re-de-morph it. I feel like one of the PR guys for the Smurfs. They’re blue too and that reference really dates me. I’m old. This card just gave me a midlife crisis.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/41/d9/41d96827af7e3a28d433b7cb2236962189d865d125f1f82c3df0b13aa759278a.jpg)**Warped Researcher**  
 This card is a lot better than a lot of people seem to give it credit for. Wizards this large only come along once in a very long time and the cycling ability is very powerful, potentially stopping opposing flyers from attacking based alone on the potential for a cycling card in your hand. Toss in how difficult it is to the kill and the fact it can serve as a win method and you have a card that can move way up this list in the right deck.

**Shifting Sliver**  
Blockade Runner was a card that players were always happy to have in their decks, but this is a different day. The Sliver is slow for the format, especially considering the fact it can be blocked if your opponent has the appropriate creatures, be they other slivers or illusions and the ability of opposing illusions to go unblocked can prove to be really annoying. It’s playable, but I wouldn’t be picking it too high.

**Covert Operative**  
 More reliably unblockable than the Sliver and a far more finite clock to boot, the Operative is still given the boot by the 2/2 because of its higher casting cost. Covert Operative is not an automatically playable card, because it’s so one dimensional with a toughness that hardly suits a five-casting-cost creature. Best in a deck that’s good at stabilizing the table and needs a way to finish off the opposition, like clerics.

  

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/magic/general/Dermoplasm.jpg)**Dermoplasm**  
 Totally deck dependant, Dermoplasm can be a very slow way of getting very minor card advantage while your opponent’s fast deck runs you over, or can be a great way to get one of those more powerful morphs out on turn four. Solid in the late game where a four mana activation won’t prove as unrealistic, the ‘Plasm will make most decks on the basis of being a morph: it’s up to you to decide if its going to be better than just a 2/2 for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif) though.

**Voidmage Apprentice**  
 One half of my new favorite combo (with Skirk Alarmist), the Apprentice is otherwise extremely slow, proving more often than not to either be a seven mana counter or a morph you shake your head at while thinking “if only I had left my mana open”. Here’s a hint: if you had, your opponent would have either run you over or forced you to spend seven mana countering a morphed Foothill Guide. Neither option sounds great.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8a/ca/8aca4a91d1daf5ce3ac4ab059125d166f9b0fa16cb44b298f338f43252bbb1b1.jpg)**Glintwing Invoker**  
 Still lurking in the realm of playability here, the Invoker is obviously not a great deal before you reach eight mana, but once there, it can randomly win the game for you. I like randomly winning games. It seems a lot better than randomly losing games, which I tend to do from time to time. 

**Mistform Wakecaster**  
 While 2/3 for five is obviously a costly deal, there are times where the Wakecaster will prove very powerful. Opponent has a Gempalm? No problem. Harsh Mercy? No problem. You have Toxin Sliver? Well, good. The possibilities are endless. Look for sweeping tribal effects when trying to determine how good the ‘Caster will be. 

**Gempalm Sorcerer**  
 The weakest of the Gempalms, if nothing else, it’s still a 2/2 for three or a cycling card. Is it going to prove spectacular? Seems unlikely and card advantage will even be tough to attain, but you could definitely do worse.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/23/fd/23fdf7cebf6e4d87debf4b34369fc78b73f56928b0e29183e73f2ecaf38745eb.jpg)**Keeper of the Nine Gales**  
 The point of having evasion creatures in your deck is to create an unstoppable offense that your opponent needs to outrace. That’s not to say that the Keeper is unplayable. Obviously, its ability is a powerful one at the right moment, but tying up three flyers in bouncing one permanent a turn doesn’t seem all that great. Play it if you have the bird deck, but don’t force its activation when unnecessary.

**Crookclaw Elder**  
 3/2 for six huh? Sure, you can get some fairly hefty card advantage out of this thing in the long run, but they couldn’t tack an extra point on that posterior? It can be run in times of desperation and is improved by the presence of many Mistform Walls in your deck, but I’d try very hard to keep it in your sideboard.

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/db/f9db78fbf4b189772d439ddf18b46d72ac5435b77132d9f67b3652fd2382be0d.jpg)**Merchant of Secrets**  
 Yes, the Merchant provides “card advantage”, but the 1/1 body seldom makes a difference in the grand scheme of things. If you have two Skills and not enough wizards, this is definitely a viable option, but other than that, it’s pure board material.

**Dreamborn Muse**  
 If this were 3/3 like the rest, it would be great, but it isn’t, so it’s not. Blue got the shaft on this one; a card which I’m really hoping is completely unplayable.

**Mistform Sliver**  
 The mistform ability is very relevant here as you can make this the weakest member of any tribe you like. 

  

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ee/20/ee2001dccb6253c33de8392a3ced1ce926ac85b34eccaff13c05b08ecf9076a8.jpg)**Cephalid Pathmage**  
 Sorry kids, unblockable or not, one-power offensive ‘weapons’ just don’t cut it. The sacrifice ability could prove okay I suppose, but on a whole, it isn’t going to do enough to be a viable main deck choice.

**Aven Envoy**  
 Counts as both a bird and a soldier!

**Fugitive Wizard**  
Imagecrafter this guy ain’t.
