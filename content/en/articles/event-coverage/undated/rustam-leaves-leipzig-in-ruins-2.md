Rustam Bakirov is the winner of Grand Prix Leipzig and becomes the first Russian player to win a Grand Prix!

As it's the holiday weekend, Leipzig wasn't quite the monster anticipated although the turnout was still a hefty 900 players. On Day One, the field was split into a more manageable two tournaments and we got an early finish for a change. The few big-name players that made the trip had a fairly rocky time of it although most of them still managed reasonable finishes.

After six rounds of Booster Draft we were left with a fairly inexperienced Top 8, with only three previous Grand PrixTop 8 appearances between them (and included two amateurs). The biggest name, Bernado Da Costa Cabral, fell in the quarters as his green-black deck featuring Kokusho did not hold up against Niki Jedlicka's snake family special. But the day belonged to Rustam Bakirov and a red-blue deck that contained [Kumano, Master Yamabushi](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kumano%2C+Master+Yamabushi) and an amazing four copies of [Yamabushi's Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Flame).

Rosario Maij faced off against Bakirov in the finals with a solid black-white deck. The first game was a virtual non-event as the German player mulliganed to four. It looked like Bakirov would roll him in the second game before Maij pulled off a stunning ambush with [Call to Glory](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Call+to+Glory) to turn the game right around. He simply had no chance in the third game though, as Bakirov pulled off a perfect curve from turn one before dropping Kumano on turn five.

Congratulations to Rustam Bakirov!

## BLOG

## Sunday, March 27: 7:13 pm - A (Very) Brief Who's Who of the Top 8

The top 8 of Grand Prix Leipzig has been decided. It features players of five nationalities and two amateurs.

Perhaps the most well known is the Belgian player Bernardo Da Costa Cabral. Da Costa Cabral has the best previous finish as he made the final of Grand Prix Madrid 2004, losing to Kai Budde in the final. Da Costa Cabral was recently seen in action on a team with Kai at PT Atlanta although it was not a successful tournament for them.

Niki Jedlicka, from Austria, has a quarter-final appearance from Grand Prix Birmingham 2004, where he lost to his older brother and eventual tournament winner Stefan Jedlicka.

Level two judge Rustam Bakirov proves wrong the old adage that judges can't play. This isn't new territory for him as he made the quarter-finals of GP Moscow back in 2001.

The remainder of the top 8 are unfamiliar names to me. Mateusz Dabkowski is from Poland. Sune Ellegaard is one of two amateurs in the top 8 and hails from Denmark.

The other amateur is Philip Fetzer, one of three German players in the top 8. He had a blazing run with a perfect record that was only broken in round 13 by Bakirov. The other two German players are Maximilian Bracht and Rosario Maij.

  

## Sunday, March 27: 8:46 pm - Top 8 Draft: Bernardo Da Costa Cabral

Bernardo's draft started pretty well: The first card he saw was a [Kabuto Moth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kabuto+Moth), so he immediately knew he would get a nice first pick. It got even better when he got to see the rare: [Kokusho, the Evening Star](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kokusho%2C+the+Evening+Star). It wasn't really a hard decision, and Bernardo passed the Moth along with a [Pain Kami](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pain+Kami) and a Painwrecker Oni. The next booster offered a choice between [Nezumi Cutthroat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nezumi+Cutthroat), [Order of the Sacred Bell](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Order+of+the+Sacred+Bell) and [Consuming Vortex](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Consuming+Vortex), and Bernardo chose to stay in color and took the Cutthroat. His third pack was very weak, and he took [Kami of the Waning Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kami+of+the+Waning+Moon) over the only other interesting card, [Honden of Life's Web](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Honden+of+Life%27s+Web). The next card was [Nezumi Ronin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nezumi+Ronin), and in the sixth booster he needed a while to choose between [Hankyu](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hankyu), [Orochi Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orochi+Ranger) and another [Kami of the Waning Moon](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kami+of+the+Waning+Moon). He settled on the [Hankyu](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hankyu). He avoided taking cards in another color until that point, but he was forced to take one when his only options were [Feral Deceiver](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Deceiver) and [Yamabushi's Storm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Storm). He took the Deceiver and followed it up with another green card, [Orochi Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orochi+Ranger), over Night of Soul's Betrayal. He picked up another [Hankyu](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hankyu), and then had a really tough time deciding between Painwrecker Kami, [Matsu-Tribe Decoy](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Matsu-Tribe+Decoy) and [Soulless Revival](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Soulless+Revival). The other cards in the first round of boosters would all end up in his sideboard.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a7/a2/a7a26219fae1a4fcb4bf7013c16055a0b48df5915fe47bad0e4c07436fee1282.jpg)*The Top 8 Draft.*

Russian Rustam Bakirov on Bernardo's right side had also opened a rare bomb in [Kumano, Master Yamabushi](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kumano%2C+Master+Yamabushi). He stayed in red and took some blue, so Bernardo would not have to fight for his colors with him. Philip Fetzer to his right stayed out of his colors, too, but his deck looked much worse then Bernardo's and Rustam's. He was drafting equal amounts of green, red and blue, and had only a few high-quality cards.

The second round started almost as good as the first one for Bernardo: He opened a [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) he gladly took, although he didn't like passing [Yamabushi's Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Flame), [Cage of Hands](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cage+of+Hands) and [Blood Rites](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Rites) to Rustam. [He Who Hungers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=He+Who+Hungers) also fit nicely, but Bernardo had to pass another [Yamabushi's Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Flame). The third booster looked a lot like the first: [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter), [Yamabushi's Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Flame) and [Cage of Hands](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cage+of+Hands). The Laughter obviously would improve the deck further, but Bernardo was shaking his head when he passed the third Flame. He didn't even know that Rustam had opened a Flame for himself as well... The rest of the round didn't feature any real hard choices, and Bernardo was able to pick up [Devouring Greed](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Devouring+Greed), [Ashen-Skin Zubera](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashen-Skin+Zubera), [Waking Nightmare](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Waking+Nightmare) and [Kami of the Hunt](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kami+of+the+Hunt) along with a few sideboard cards.

Rustams deck was getting out of hand with the four Flames, while Philip's deck seemed good enough for one round of top-eight-play only.

Bernardo took a [Takenuma Bleeder](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Takenuma+Bleeder) out of the first pack and put it on top of his pile, but quickly took the card back when he realized the otherwise weaker [Lifespinner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifespinner) would be able to fetch Kokusho for him. The next cards he took were [Unchecked Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unchecked+Growth) and [Budoka Pupil](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Budoka+Pupil), but after that the Betrayers packs weren't very nice to him, although he got some playables like [Child of Thorns](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Child+of+Thorns), [Matsu-Tribe Sniper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Matsu-Tribe+Sniper) and [Skullsnatcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skullsnatcher).

Overall Bernardo's deck looked very solid, although he didn't really have a way to get rid of larger creatures, as his removal consisted of two [Hankyu](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hankyu)s and two [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter)s. He should have no problems with the weaker decks at the table, but it seemed unlikely that he would be able to beat the stronger decks, like Rustam's.

## Sunday, March 27: 9:33 pm - My Top 8 Predictions

Or more correctly described as putting the mockers on, the cursing or other such words indicating doom, gloom and shattered hopes. Predicting the outcome of top 8's is a largely fruitless activity as we never ever get it right and this time it's even worse as it's just me and my … ahem … fantastic limited prowess.

So here we go for what it's worth.

Quarter-finals

![](https://i.ormos.uk/eb/e0/ebe0e0207a2fdef5f7158494afb9250fe5a97f57bf5d54a3842b2215c7aabfe7.jpg)*The judges go over the decklists.*

Mateus Dabkowski and Rosario Maij both drafted black-white. Dabkowski's curve looks a little high though with three [Silverstorm Samurai](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silverstorm+Samurai). Maij's deck has double Moth, [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) and some fat Demons. I think he'll shade this.

Niki Jedlicka managed to draft the snake clan with Seshiro, two Sachi and Sosuke, as well as Summons and a lot of other snakes. His spells aren't quite as exciting with [Glacial Ray](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glacial+Ray) being pretty much his only removal. He's also splashing for a blue honden.  
 Bernardo Da Costa Cabral is playing black-green and is also short of point removal. However he does have double [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) and Kokusho. I think this will be close but the snakes will pull it through.

Rustam Bakirov managed draft a blue-red deck featuring 4 [Yamabushi's Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Flame) and Kumano the master to go with it. His curve is also really nice. Sune Elleegaard doesn't have a deck. Bakirov wins.

Philip Fetzer has a lighting fast green-red that isn't going to be happy with the three [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) on the table. Maximilian Bracht has a three color abomination and is unlikely to hold together in the face of Fetzer's rapid beats.

Semi-Finals

Jedlicka and his snakes might be enough to overwhelm Maij's black-white deck. Could be tough though.

Bakirov will block enough of Fetzer's early beats with annoying critters like [Floating-Dream Zubera](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Floating-Dream+Zubera) to hold on for Kumano to machine gun the board.

Final

Snakes get crispy fried and Bakirov emerges victorious.

There we go. Now you can laugh at me when my predictions prove hopelessly inept.

  

## Sunday, March 27: 9:57 pm - Quarterfinals: Niki Jedlicka vs. Bernardo Da Costa Cabral

This quarter-final features two of the more experienced players in this top 8. Bernardo Da Costa Cabral made the final in GP Madrid last year while Jedlicka was dumped out of the quarter-finals of GP Birmingham by his elder brother. Da Costa Cabral has a good black-green deck with Kokusho while Jedlicka is running a snake special with most of the family.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bd/d9/bdd9e86bfa39a883691031e44166844d1a8799e69ddd1d69604371a4ee039d20.jpg)*Niki Jedlicka.*

Da Costa Cabral started and didn't have a play until a [Nezumi Ronin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nezumi+Ronin) came out on the third turn. Jedlicka had plays for the first three turns: Leafcaller, [Child of Thorns](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Child+of+Thorns) and an [Orochi Eggwatcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orochi+Eggwatcher). In contrast Da Costa Cabral's deck appeared to be stuttering as the fourth turn went with no play.

Jedlicka's deck went explosive as he dropped Sachi and then tapped his other two snake shamen to summon a Springcaller. Either Da Costa Cabral had nothing or was shaping up for a Hideous laughter. Sachi made that plan awkward as it was only likely to net a [Child of Thorns](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Child+of+Thorns) and Leafcaller. Still it turn 5 and all he had on the table was the solitary Ronin.

Jedlicka thought for a while before sending in the lone Springcaller. Da Costa Cabral blocked and Jedlicka might have thought he'd forgotten about the extra toughness. He hadn't, and all that happened was Jedlicka threw an extra creature away to the [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) that followed.

Da Costa Cabral was hardly out of the woods. He brought out a [Lifespinner](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lifespinner) to slow down. A Sosuke / Decoy tag team killed that with a basilisk effect when it was forced to block.

It didn't look like Da Costa Cabral was coming back from this one. He made a rather insignificant [Hankyu](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hankyu). He did have a second [Hideous Laughter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+Laughter) to buy him a turn though. That turn bought him the devastating … Kami of Waning Moon. The decoy forced the Kami to block only to find it suddenly grown gigantic with [Unchecked Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unchecked+Growth). Jedlicka was down to just Sachi and Sosuke now as Da Costa Cabral summoned a [Feral Deceiver](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Deceiver). Da Costa Cabral was holding on with 9 life. The [Feral Deceiver](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Deceiver) found a land and took down Sosuke.

Jedlicka still gas. Bakirov sent in all his men…only to find himself on the receiving end of an ambush. [Call to Glory](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Call+to+Glory) untapped the Moth and Ronin and left them available to block. [Otherworldly Journey](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Otherworldly+Journey) took care of the overlapping Kami while a spliced [Hundred-Talon Strike](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hundred-Talon+Strike) gave the Ronin first strike.

It was a bloody massacre. Bakirov was able to sac the Kami to save the Initiate, but everything else died without a single loss to Maij's forces. The Initiate returned and picked up the Maul. The initiative had gone to Maij now though. The Ronin swung in for another four damage and he added a [Painwracker Oni](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Painwracker+Oni) onto the board. Maij needed a quick kill. Bakirov hadn't yet played a [Yamabushi's Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yamabushi%27s+Flame). Maij had to assume he'd picked one up by now and had to end the game before he found a second.

Maij was tapped out. A [First Volley](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=First+Volley) was aimed at the exposed Ronin, inviting Maij to save it with the Moth. This would leave it open to the Bo and Initiate, although this didn't cause the Initiate to flip because of the Bo removing the spirit from the game. Sure enough, this was what happened, the [Cursed Ronin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cursed+Ronin) was too important to lose.

The Oni had to sacrifice to itself on the following upkeep but with seven swamps Maij's [Cursed Ronin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cursed+Ronin) put Bakirov into chumpblock mode. A [Frost Ogre](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frost+Ogre) went, then the Initiate, then a topdecked [Battle-Mad Ronin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Battle-Mad+Ronin) before Bakirov ran out of creatures and it was time to go to a decider.

Bakirov 1-1 Maij

![](https://i.ormos.uk/17/29/172900c1502e4bccbd61c3177851770cb3a5c9edbdb514443779d627dcd32c0e.jpg)*Rustam Bakirov prevails!*

One more game to go and unfortunately it looked like mulliganing would play a part again as Maij had to send back his first hand. Maij stayed with six cards as Bakirov came out of the gates fast with Cohort and then [Hearth Kami](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hearth+Kami). [Callous Deceiver](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Callous+Deceiver) followed and Maij still had no play. He was missing plains. Thankfully for the German Bakirov played no threats on turn four and could only attack with the [Hearth Kami](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hearth+Kami) and Deceiver.  
 Maij finally made his first play on turn four as a [Kabuto Moth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120075435/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kabuto+Moth) cam into play. This game Bakirov had drawn one of his many Flames and he shot the Moth out of the sky at end of turn.

The Russian untapped, dropped his fifth land and then summoned Kumano, Master Yambushi. He didn't need to attack as Maij was already extending his hand in concession. Bakirov's draw in the deciding game had been just perfect. Maij had not been given the slightest glimmer of a chance.

Rustam Bakirov beats Rosario Maij 2-1 and is champion of Grand Prix Leipzig!
