It's been an incredible weekend for **Magic** here in Charleston. 661 players showed up to compete with their favorite Standard decks. The field was whittled down to 81 players for the second day of competition. When the dust settled only eight players remained. Reid Duke, Peter Kelly, Patrick Cox, Brian Eason, Matt Keene, Gerry Thompson, Morgan Chang, and Jon Bolding were the only Planeswalkers left in the heat of battle. 

In the quarterfinals, Jon Bolding dispatched Morgan Chang's four color Unburial Rites deck in a tight three games. 

The semifinals saw Jon Bolding up against his worst matchup in the top 8, a significantly more aggressive Rakdos deck being piloted by Matt Keene. Bolding managed to curve out perfectly while Keene stumbled a bit with lands that come into play tapped and Jon Bolding was able to escape the semifinals despite being a massive underdog. 

Only two were left standing. Brian Eason's powerful Black/Green/White Tokens deck stood no match against Jon Bolding's Rakdos deck that featured Thundermaw Hellkite and Bonfire of the Damned. Jon Bolding goes home with $3,500, a trophy, and the title of Grand Prix Charleston Champion. 

This is Jon Bolding's first major finish and it qualifies him for his first Pro Tour. Be sure to check out Pro Tour Gatecrash to follow Bolding and see if he has what it takes to compete with the best in the world!

### Quarterfinals Round-Up

*by Jacob Van Lunen* 

  

### Pat Cox (Blue/White Flash) vs. Brian Eason (Black/Green/White Tokens)

### Game 1

Brian started to accelerate with Arbor Elf while Pat set up his hand with Augur of Bolas. 

Brian began making a board presence with Lingering Souls, establishing a dominant board presence. An attack for two and Flashback put Pat Cox in quite the predicament. Avacyn's Pilgrim came down and things were looking good for the GP top 8 rookie. 

Pat Cox had Restoration Angel when Brian attacked, but Pat tapping out meant that Brian had a chance to resolve Collective Blessing. The next turn he was swinging for twenty damage and Pat could barely hang on, another Restoration Angel came down and Pat was in chump blocking mode. One more attack was all it took for Brian to get the first game. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/d5/74d585b9466315a57f85c3dfb13ec1e6f65262ae639196308f6638ef573d1b82.jpg)  
 *Pat Cox* 

### Game 2

Game two won't be quite as easy for Brian Eason once Pat Cox has access to Supreme Verdict and Detention Sphere. Pat is hoping his sideboard will be strong enough to overcome one of his more difficult matchups. 

Again, Brian ramped into Lingering Souls with Avacyn's Pilgrim and once again the game looked like it was swinging heavily into Brian's favor very early. 

An all out attack from Brian on turn three met a Snapcaster Mage from Pat Cox that traded with Avacyn's Pilgrim. 

Brian continued charging into the red zone, but didn't want to overcommit to the board, knowing that Pat had sideboarded in his Supreme Verdicts. Pat declined to cast his Restoration Angel, intending on wiping the board before he spent any resources. 

A Sphinx's Revelation for two from Pat made it seem like he was a huge favorite to win the game. 

An all out attack from Brian dropped Pat to nine, but he was casually leaning back in his seat, hoping that Brian would commit more resources before he cast Supreme Verdict. 

Pat pulled further ahead with another Sphinx's Revelation, this time for three. 

Detention Sphere came down for Pat and he targeted a Spirit, Brian decided to cast Ultimate Price targeting his own spirit, but Pat had Dissipate to ensure that all the spirits would be going to detention. 

Brian reestablished his board presence, with Borderland Ranger and another Lingering Souls. Intangible Virtue came down and once again Brian was looking to be in the driver seat. 

Pat had a lot of digging power and he ran through his deck looking for another Detention Sphere or a Supreme Verdict. Despite seeing a ton of cards, Pat was unable to find one of these important spells. 

Brian looked like he was running out of gas, though, and Pat still had plenty of cards in hand. Pat continued digging and eventually found a Supreme Verdict. 

Brian had lethal in the air with his Spirits and Pat was forced to cast Surpeme Verdict despite Brian still having two copies of Lingering Souls in his graveyard and Intangible Virtue in play. One copy of lingering souls met Negate, but the second copy resolved. 

Pat found another copy of Supreme Verdict on the top of his library and began to completely dominate the game. 

Brian was out of cards and things didn't look good against Pat's full grip, but Brian wasn't out of this just yet, he cast Garruk, Primal Hunter and made a token. 

Pat wasn't going to let Garruk get in his way, though and he quickly killed Garruk with Restoration angel and a Spirit. 

With Dissipate and Rewind in hand, and a dominant board position, Pat quickly closed the game out with a Restoration Angel, some spirits and Runechanter's Pike while Brian floundered, looking for some glimmer of hope. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/20/dd/20dd33a548bce2fe56837137a6b9a663190f883ec8615a2d678f1caf542fd637.jpg)  
 *Brian Eason* 

### Game 3

Brian had Avacyn's Pilgrim on the first turn and Arbor Elf on the second. He went for Garruk Relentless on the third turn and Pat was forced to let it resolve. 

Brian tried to give Garruk a friend in Sorin, Lord of Innistrad, but Pat had Dissipate. Garruk continued powering out tokens and Pat struggled to find an answer to the powerful Planeswalker. 

Restoration Angel proved good enough to deal with Garruk Relentless, but Pat was facing down a massive army of wolves. 

Sorin, Lord of Innistrad came down for Brian on his fifth turn. He made an Emblem and was able to attack for more than enough damage to kill his opponent. 

Brian Eason 2 - 1 Pat Cox

### Game 1

Another Bump in the night dropped Gerry down to five before he had his fourth land in play. Gerry tried to stabilize, but Matt's draw was far too aggressive and Gerry found himself dead before he had a chance to get fancy.

### The Top 5 Card of Grand Prix Charleston 2012

*by Steve Sadin* 

  
  

 ![Garruk Relentless](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Garruk%20Relentless&options=) 

5) Garruk Relentless

The relatively underplayed Planeswalker Garruk Relentless managed to overperform for eventual finalist Brian Eason's token deck all weekend long. Garruk Relentless was largely responsible for Brian's wins in both his Quarterfinals, and his Semifinals matches -- completely dominating the board, killing pesky creatures left and right, and flooding the table with wolves. 

  
  

 ![Cavern of Souls](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Cavern%20of%20Souls&options=) 

4) Cavern of Souls

Counterspells have been increasingly popular in *Return to Ravnica* Standard, and Cavern of Souls is one of the best ways to fight them. It's gotten to the point where even the majority of dedicated control copies are running 2-3 Cavern of Souls, and just about every aggressive deck is packing a full four copies to help them power their way past countermagic. 

  
  

 ![Supreme Verdict](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Supreme%20Verdict&options=) 

3) Supreme Verdict

Supreme Verdict catapulted from being a reasonably good sideboard card in Blue White decks to an absolute all star as Black Red Zombies, White Green Humans, and a host of other creature decks packing 4 copies of Cavern of Souls have risen to prominence. 

The format is no longer defined by control mirrors, and consequently the latest iteration of Wrath of God will be an important tool going forward. 

  
  

 ![Thalia, Guardian of Thraben](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Thalia,%20Guardian%20of%20Thraben&options=) 

2) Thalia, Guardian of Thraben

Blue White Flash was considered by many to be "the deck to beat" coming into this event. Many players turned to Cavern of Souls as their anti-control card -- but Cavern does nothing to slow down Supreme Verdict. 

And while Black Red Zombie decks have burn, haste, and undying creatures to allow themselves to take games after getting their boards swept -- aggressive white decks just don't have that flexibility...

That's where Thalia, Guardian of Thraben comes in. 

Thalia, Guardian of Thraben caused some serious headaches for control players this weekend, allowing White Green Aggro players to put their Blue White Flash, and Bant Control opponents off of key spells just long enough to win their matches with minimal (Sphere of) resistance. 

  
  

 ![Thundermaw Hellkite](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Thundermaw%20Hellkite&options=) 

1) Thundermaw Hellkite

Jon Bolding's Thundermaw Hellkites earned their keep in the finals against Brian Eason's token deck. The ***Magic** 2013* Mythic Rare is putting itself in a position to supplant even Thragtusk as the premier five-mana spell of the Standard format. 

It kills tokens, Planeswalkers, and people!
