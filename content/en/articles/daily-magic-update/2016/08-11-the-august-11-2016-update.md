The Daily *Magic* Update is a roundup of everything *Magic* you should know on August 11, 2016. Today's Update is brought to you by the 2015–2016 Pro Season. We hardly knew ye.

# Today's Must

1.  [Pantheon Deck Tech: RUG Eldrazi](http://www.channelfireball.com/articles/pantheon-deck-tech-rug-eldrazi/) | ChannelFireball | Jon Finkel

Jon Finkel emerges from whatever hyperbaric chamber they store him in between Pro Tours and pens an article about the deck that took Owen Turtenwald to second place and a Player of the Year trophy at Pro Tour *Eldritch Moon*. Typically, “Jon Finkel writes something” is all I would need to put something on the must list, so what are you waiting for?

# After That, Read, Watch, or Listen to These

1.  [Vancouver to Sydney: Top 5 Major Events in MTG this Season and the Impact of Pro Tour Eldritch Moon](http://www.manaleak.com/mtguk/2016/08/vancouver-to-sydney-5-major-events-in-mtg-this-season-and-the-impact-of-pro-tour-eldritch-moon/) | ManaLeak.com |Kerry Meyerhoff

This is a nice little summary of the wild ride that was the 2015–16 Pro Season—from significant changes, both popular and unpopular, to a rolling metagame that shifted in enormous ways with the advent of the two-set-block paradigm, this was a season to remember. So Meyerhoff remembers it for you.

1.  [The Hanweir Horror: An Amazing Amalgam of Story Awesomeness](http://tumblhurgoyf.tumblr.com/post/148765156920/the-hanweir-horror-an-amazing-amalgam-of-story) | Tumblhurgoyf

With *Eldritch Moon* came the end of a project we had been working on and sowing with the public called the Hanweir Chronicle, which told the story of Hanweir through letters, newspaper clippings, and various other in-world relics and artifacts. Here, Tumblr user Tumblhurgoyf (five points for the name) looks at the story-telling medium and analyzes its effectiveness. Spoiler alert: they're a fan.

1.  [Representation Done Right](http://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2016/08/representation-done-right/) | Hipsters of the Coast | Jess Stirba

Hipsters of the Coast, if you're not familiar, does more commentary on social issues related to *Magic* and gaming than any other site. It's a welcome and needed change of pace from the cacophony of strategy sites (though they dabble in that as well). So it's no surprise that Stirba took a look at representation when it comes to Kaya, our newest Planeswalker. And the verdict? Well, the title kind of gives it away, but read on anyway.

1.  [*Conspiracy: Take the Crown* Spoilers - Kaya, Ghost Assassin – New Planeswalker Revealed – MTG!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vjnMHI-7900) | The Mana Source | Wedge

You could tell Wedge was just a touch excited about his sneaky preview card yesterday, and more so when that card was the awesome Kaya, Ghost Assassin. Watch him break down the latest to join the Planeswalker pantheon in this seven-minute video.

1.  [Against the Odds: Harmless Pact (Standard)](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/articles/against-the-odds-harmless-pact-standard) | MTGGoldfish | SaffronOlive

I mean, you knew this was coming, right? The most Johnny of cards in the set was 100% guaranteed to get a spot in the Against the Odds rotation, as SaffronOlive tries to make the card good. I mean, it at least has to be in the running for best kitten-related card in Standard, right?

1.  [Everything You Need to Know About Tempo / Modern Vampires](https://rocketjump.com/listen/everything-need-know-tempo-modern-vampires) | Masters of Modern | Alex Kessler and Ben Bateman

Modern Vampires shares the headline spotlight, but this podcast is all about [tempo, tempo, tempo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xDAsABdkWSc). Learn about the applications of this important concept as it applies to Modern.

Also there are Vampires. Did I mention the Vampires?

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Arrogant Bant](/en/events/2016/competitive-standard-constructed-league-2016-08-10/competitive-standard-constructed-league)

I just made that deck name up, and it's already my favorite deck name ever.

But besides a semi-clever deck name that I'm overly pleased with, this is an intriguing new entrant onto the Standard scene. I played against one of these the other day and didn't realize it wasn't Bant Company until they played their first [Void Shatter](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Void+Shatter). To say I was surprised was an understatement.

The deck can look deceptively like a Bant Company deck right off the bat, as the lands and an early [Sylvan Advocate](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sylvan+Advocate) or [Spell Queller](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Queller) might make things appear one way. But the deck is pure control. And it's good, too.

Advocates hold the fort while also providing a late-game finisher. [Spell Queller](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Queller) lets the deck play a metric ton of counterspells—thirteen in total, which is more than we've seen in a long, long time. And [Dragonlord Ojutai](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragonlord+Ojutai) is still a premier finisher.

The deck's main strength is its ability to play at instant speed, much the same way Bant Company can. [Archangel Avacyn](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Archangel+Avacyn) and [Stasis Snare](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stasis+Snare) are additional cards with flash, and [Blessed Alliance](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blessed+Alliance) is an underrated removal spell.

The deck's namesake (the name I gave it just now) is [Tragic Arrogance](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tragic+Arrogance), a powerful spell that's excellent in a Bant Company metagame but might be a little soft if all people are trying to do is Emrakul you (that's what the [Summary Dismissal](https://web.archive.org/web/20160811000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summary+Dismissal)s are for in the sideboard).

All in all, I'm excited to see where this deck goes.
