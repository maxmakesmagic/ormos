Grand Prix Malmo is over.

Outside it's been a hot sunny day in Sweden, but inside players got to feel the chill as *Coldsnap* debuted on the Grand Prix circuit for the first time. But first the 534 players had to navigate a day of *Ravnica* Sealed deck. Day one was not without incident, with the biggest news provided by the shock disqualification of top European pro Olivier Ruel.

64 players returned today for the ripple fuelled craziness that is *Coldsnap* draft. Stories abounded of Thrumming Stones and 9 Disciples of Tevesh Szat, or a first-striking army of 9 Surging Sentinels appearing on turn 3.

In the end the Dutchies were dominant as the Netherlands put 4 players - Wessel Oomens, Kamiel Cornelissen, Jelger Wiegersma and Wilco Pinkster - into a very experienced top 8 that also included Worlds top 8'er André Coimbra and PT top 8'er Vasilis Fatouros. Scandinavia was also represented by amateurs Asbjørn Fallesen of Denmark and Axel Berglund of the host nation.

In the end it came down to a clash of swamps between Vasilis Fatouros and Wessel Oomens. Fatouros was running Mountains for a removal suite that included 4 Surging Flames while Oomens had blue for a collection of Krovikan Mist. In Game 1 Fatouros's draw was too powerful, but Oomens was able to level the match through the aid of a Tresserhorn Skyknight that just wouldn't stay dead (or undead). Game 3 the Dutchman managed to overcome a double mulligan to claim his first Grand Prix title through the aid of his evasion monsters and removal.

Congratulations to Wessel Oomens, champion of Grand Prix Malmo!

     <p>Fatouros has a very useful looking red-black deck while Oomens is running black-blue.</p>&#10;      <p>Fatouros opened with a <a>Goblin Furrier</a>, but was stalled by Oomen's <a>Rimebound Dead</a>. He added a <a>Gutless Ghoul</a> and swung it into Oomens's freshly summoned <a>Zombie Musher</a>. Oomens blocked and then picked up the Musher, expecting a trick to finish it off. This time it was <a>Martyr of Ashes</a>. The earthquake for one took out the <a>Rimebound Dead</a> as well.</p>&#10;      <p>Oomens restocked with <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a> and a second Musher. Fatouros had the always useful Sek'Kuar, which Oomens had to pick up and read. Oomens's knowledge of <i>Coldsnap</i> didn't appear to extend to the orcs as he'd had to look at Bloodpainter when Fatouros had revealed it with the Martyr. Probably trying to lull Fatouros into thinking he was a noob. You know these sneaky Dutchies.</p>&#10;      <p>The Bloodpainter entered play and was followed by an <a>Ohran Yeti</a>. Oomens matched with a <a>Chilling Shade</a> and it didn't seem like the stalemate was going to continue for much longer.</p>&#10;      <p>The Shade nipped in and then Fatouros started to abuse his Sek'Kuar, Bloodpainter combo. The Bloodpainter threw a <a>Goblin Furrier</a> at a <a>Rimebound Dead</a> and Sek'Kuar replaced it with a much more useful 3/1 graveborn token.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Tresserhorn Skyknight" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Tresserhorn%20Skyknight&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Oomens made <a>Garza's Assassin</a>, but it couldn't touch Sek'Kuar or the freshly summoned <a>Deepfire Elemental</a>. One of the interesting thing about Fatouros's deck is that it starts out quickly, but also has a very good long game. In the Semi's the <a>Deepfire Elemental</a> had turned mana flood into a resource.</p>&#10;      <p>Oomens had plenty to think about. He had the evasion creatures, but had to keep them back on defence. <a>Garza's Assassin</a> showed the Bloodpainter a knife or three about killing, but Sek'Kuar meant it would still serve faithfully after its death.</p>&#10;      <p>With the pinger out the way Oomens was able to use <a>Grim Harvest</a> to fetch back a <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a>. A <a>Skred</a> left him with an awkward decision about whether to tap out to get back the Harvest or pay half his life for <a>Garza's Assassin</a>. The Assassin was the casualty and was removed from game.</p>&#10;      <p>This left Oomens with just two <a>Zombie Musher</a>s and no snow land untapped. Fatouros found that crucial ninth land and one of the Mushers felt the touch of Deepfire. Now Oomens was left with a solitary Musher as Fatouros steamed in with everything. At this point there was no way back.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;And I thought that was my good draw,&quot; Oomens said.</p>&#10;      <p>Fatouros 1-0 Oomens.</p>&#10;      <p> Oomens led off with <a>Rimebound Dead</a> and cycled a <a>Thermal Flux</a>. He didn't have any snow land and I think he was hoping Fatouros might waste a <a>Surging Flame</a>s. <a>Rimebound Dead</a> isn't much of a target though, a minor nuisance at best.</p>&#10;      <p>Fatouros's two Bloodpainters put in an appearance as Oomens developed his board with a <a>Garza's Assassin</a> and then a <a>Gutless Ghoul</a>. Both marched into the red zone as Bloodpainter and Assassin once again demonstrated the art of killing.</p>&#10;      <p>Once the dust settled only a regenerating <a>Rimebound Dead</a> was left.</p>&#10;      <p>Fatouros was still stuck on three land to Oomen's six. A <a>Skred</a> took care of the <a>Rimebound Dead</a> and left both players with nothing.</p>&#10;      <p>At this point my laptop battery ran out. So I had to pick up a summary afterwards and I apologize if it's not in as much detail as I'd like.</p>&#10;      <p>Basically Oomens made <a>Tresserhorn Skyknight</a>. Fatouros was able to kill it with <a>Surging Flame</a>s and rippling into a second. But at this point Oomens had enough mana to continually recur it and recover <a>Grim Harvest</a>. Eventually the 5/3 stuck around long enough to take the match to a decider.</p>&#10;      <p>Fatouros 1-1 Oomens</p>&#10;      <p>The game started badly for Oomens as he was forced to take a double mulligan. Fatouros was unable to fully capitalize though as Oomens had the answers. Furrier was answered with <a>Rimebound Dead</a> and a <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a> immediately fell prey to <a>Feast of Flesh</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Now Oomens was able to take the offensive with <a>Krovikan Mist</a>s and then a <a>Phobian Phantasm</a>. The upkeep was missed on the Phantasm, but this just allowed Oomens to drop a <a>Ronom Serpent</a> as a massive wall.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/2f/58/2f58a78fbad9ee128f49e8a9df389953990743e77a121c6ec5e18f2a61f83bef.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Vasilis Fatouros</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>With the ground blocked Oomens now started to take advantage of his air superiority. A second Mists increased the damage. Meanwhile Fatouros's deck was showing him little love as he was drawing land or irrelevant early drops like <a>Krovikan Scoundrel</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>A <a>Surging Flame</a>s on the Mist slowed down the damage and now Fatouros was able to slowly hit back with a <a>Karplusan Wolverine</a>. Unfortunately for the Greek player Oomens had now got <a>Scrying Sheets</a> online and had not only overcome the disadvantage of mulliganing, but was now pulling far ahead.</p>&#10;      <p>Fatouros kept trying to fight with removal but was still drawing land. A Disciple started to eat through the Greek player's team. Oomens had 8 monsters, Fatouros had just 3. Even crazy <a>Surging Flame</a>s ripple technology couldn't save him as three <a>Surging Flame</a>s were already spent and in the graveyard.</p>&#10;      <p>An all-out attack dropped Fatouros to 2 life. With no answers left in his library he scooped up his permanents.</p>&#10;      <p>Wessel Oomens beat Vasilis Fatouros 2-1 and is the Champion of Grand Prix Malmo. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
