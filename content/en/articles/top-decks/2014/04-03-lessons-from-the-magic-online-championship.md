Last weekend, sixteen of the world's greatest **Magic Online** players descended upon San Francisco with hopes of being crowned the 2013 **Magic Online** Champion. When the dust settled, only Lars Dam, 2013 **Magic Online** Limited Champion, remained. Today, we'll be taking a look at the most successful decks from the Standard and Modern portions of the [**Magic Online** Championship](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/dam-crowned-magic-online-champion).

### Standard

Let's start by taking a look at Standard. [Last week](/en/articles/top-decks/2014/03-27-out-of-control), we asserted that Esper Control strategies had risen to power. As a result, we saw some interesting card choices in many players' decks at the **Magic Online** Championship. Is Esper still the deck to beat in Standard going forward? Let's take a look at the most successful decks from the Standard portion of the event.

The most successful deck in the Standard portion of the **Magic Online** Championship was Tamas Glied's interesting take on Black Devotion. Glied didn't expect a lot of early aggression last weekend. Even the red- and green-based strategies are, in most cases, trying to go a lot bigger than they might have been in previous Standard formats. [Ashiok, Nightmare Weaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashiok%2C+Nightmare+Weaver) dominates when played on the third turn in a game where the opponent isn't planning on attacking until the third or fourth turn. We can look forward to [Ashiok, Nightmare Weaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashiok%2C+Nightmare+Weaver) versions of Black Devotion enjoying success in the coming weeks.

Resolving [Sire of Insanity](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sire+of+Insanity) with an [Underworld Connections](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Underworld+Connections) in play seems very strong. After sideboarding, [Slaughter Games](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Slaughter+Games) gives Dam the ability to strip his control opponents of all their [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation)s.

### Modern

Modern's player base is growing fast and the massive card pool of the format means there will always be room for innovation. After [Grand Prix Richmond](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/liu-shatters-richmond-record), many had assumed that [Melira, Sylvok Outcast](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Melira%2C+Sylvok+Outcast)/[Birthing Pod](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Birthing+Pod) combo strategies and aggressive Affinity decks were going to be somewhat dominant for a foreseeable future. Modern will never be that predictable. The format's capacity for evolution is so far-reaching that the format in its current state, if allowed, could exist in constant flux well beyond our lifetimes.

Jund is back and it performed better than any other Modern archetype at the **Magic Online** Championship! The deck uses a mix of powerful disruption in the form of [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) and [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek); removal; some of the best creatures in the format; and [Chandra, Pyromaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Pyromaster). The versatility of the strategy means that it has no truly bad matchups. We'll surely be seeing more of this going forward and it behooves all aspiring Modern masters to familiarize themselves with the deck, even if they don't plan on playing it.

We don't need to put together an infinite combo with our [Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kiki-Jiki%2C+Mirror+Breaker) every time. The card does a pretty good job of winning the game by itself when combined with things like [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage) and [Wall of Omens](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wall+of+Omens). [Restoration Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Restoration+Angel) is a pretty awesome card on its own, but it enables this deck to attack for infinite damage by repeatedly blinking the [Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20140403000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kiki-Jiki%2C+Mirror+Breaker) that copied it. This deck gets to be a startlingly efficient control strategy that just happens to have an infinite combo in it. This has been experimented with before with some success, but I expect the archetype's following to grow in the coming months.
