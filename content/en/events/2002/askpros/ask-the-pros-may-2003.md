<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;                 <b>May 13, 2003</b></td>&#10;          <td> </td>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f0/9f/f09f4d2a172231ed02bcfadb60e0264389ebd0a5d2bbaaf40ad6a927154a8f0e.jpg">&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;        <tr>&#10;          <td colspan="3">&#10;            <table>&#10;              <tbody><tr>&#10;                <td>&#10;                  <p>&#10;                    <b>Question</b>: Where did the word &quot;mise&quot; come from?</p>&#10;                  <p>&#10;                    <b>Answer:</b>&#10;                  </p>&#10;                  <p>&#10;                    <b>Mike Flores</b>: The alleged word &quot;mise&quot; (also &quot;mize&quot;) is short for previous truncations/verbal aberrations &quot;misewell&quot;, and previous to that, &quot;misewellamiright&quot;.</p>&#10;                  <p>As you might have guessed reading the above out loud, mise actually represents &quot;might as&quot; in the ordinary English phrase &quot;might as well, am I right?&quot;, though in the late '90s (circa 1997-98 when I believe mise entered the <b>Magic</b> vernacular), it was for some reason en vogue to ape various regional dialects when speaking, which could explain might as being pronounced as mise.</p>&#10;                  <p>As for what it means, mise's original connotation was to express displeasure at the luck exhibited by an opponent during a crucial point in the game. Examples include &quot;mise well draw your last Wrath of God, am I right?&quot; addressed to a control player on one life or, later shortened to its current incarnation, &quot;He mised the Disenchant&quot;, when recounting the story of how your Cadaverous Bloom deck lost in round one to a Small Child<sup>TM</sup> playing a 74 card White Weenie deck. Mising has since grown away from its strictly negative connotations and can be used in a variety of settings, from luck (&quot;I attempted to mise...&quot;), to trying to get, pick up, or topdeck something in general (&quot;hey, can I mise one of those crispy McDonalds freedom fries?&quot;).</p>&#10;                  <p>Though I always assumed Team Sped invented it, the coining of the word mise has been widely attributed to me, including when Brian David-Marshall gave us the character Mize for his Marvel Comics limited series The Craptacular B-Sides. If this is the case, mise's creation was not intentional (unlike onetime successful experiment &quot;Holy Pikula!&quot;), though I cannot deny having had some hand in popularizing the term over time.</p>&#10;                </td>&#10;              </tr>&#10;            </tbody></table>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
