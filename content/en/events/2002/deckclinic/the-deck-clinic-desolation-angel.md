For this installment of the deck doctor series, I have chosen a deck based on Desolation Angel from *Apocalypse*. This deck is played mostly at Friday Night **Magic**. Since it's owner has limited access to cards, I will not be adding any rares. Here is his complete submission. 

### Comments

![](https://i.ormos.uk/44/94/4494b94160afaec261fe9e11aa9e093d10c3c5a9bf0d811b0270a366cde43262.jpg)

This deck is a control deck, taking control of the game and keeping it. It should be able to establish a lock quickly. To do this, it uses cheap spells to remove or counter threats. Spectral Lynx can hold off attackers, swinging for 2 when the board is clear. Desolation Angel is the win condition. Resolving it means that the opponent should scoop right there. Please don't use Psychatog in this deck since with the control theme and Yawgmoth's Agenda, it's good to keep your hand and graveyard so you don't run out of gas. I have a hard time getting pain lands. Also, I can't afford Shadowmage Infiltrator, though if a card drawing creature is needed, I can get Thieving Magpie. Also, Meddling Mage is almost impossible to get in my area.

As I look at this deck, I see a few areas that concern me straight away. 

*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/a5/32/a53291520cb17a08eb5984d8133ff6688161b4f12219660238e5b3438db863b7.jpg) Desolation Angel is very vulnerable to Innocent Blood. There are a lot of decks running Innocent Blood these days, and it is going to kill the Angel every time.
*   Spectral Lynx clashes with the deck's own Innocent Blood. I expect to find a significant amount of time that I cast the Lynx, then draw the Blood, and can't use it without killing the Lynx.
*   Some of the counterspells in the deck are often suboptimal. Syncopate is very good against Call of the Herd and other flashback cards, but is very poor against black with Cabal Coffers in play. Memory Lapse is generally a poor counter, as you just have to deal with the card again next turn. Since you might draw a land or some other card that isn't useful next turn, and they are guaranteed to draw a good card, Memory Lapse gets relegated to the substandard or special purpose counterspell pile. It does shine when you are countering something targeting your Angel, and you guarantee that your opponent is not going to draw a land next turn.
*   There are 7 pain lands in this deck. That is an awful lot of damage to be inflicting upon yourself.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f2/fe/f2feaac81270a5122424eba69de5d2a61336dc30077c5a81b0682a80c0180194.jpg)

Ok, next I fired up **Magic** Online and see how the deck played. I made a few immediate changes before I began, removing the Spectral Lynx for more counterspells. With the Lynx gone, I was now afraid of stranding the Repulses, so they went too. I upped the Chainer's Edicts to 4, filled in the last Fact or Fiction, and added a Dismantling Blow so that I would have some enchantment/artifact removal. Finally, because I couldn't think of anything else right away, I added another land.

The deck has more problems. Not surprising since that is often why people send me decks. Some of the new problems that I noticed were that the deck needed another Desolation Angel and another Wrath of God. These are 2 rares, but I really didn't want to increase the rare count. Sometimes I would get the 1st Angel and need to cast the 2nd, but didn't have enough land now. Finally, sometimes the deck was just too slow. This was especially true when I ran into Braids decks. 

Now that I had identified the problems, it was time to find solutions. 

### The Philosophical Change: Tempo

In the old days of control decks, they had fewer counterspells and supplemented these with good removal spells in case something bad hit the board. These decks could often decide not to counter a spell, instead choosing to remove the permanent it created later. As time went on, three big things happened. First, we lost the dual lands in Standard. Second, nonbasic lands became more vulnerable because of Wastelands. Third, Force of Will gave blue mages a sense that they could always counter a spell, even if they were tapped out. The normal strategy of "just counter the important stuff" became "let's just counter everything". 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/12/3a12c80ee6f3d6a8124dbef0789a1e918377f5dc221c39224bdddc479ab8ec2a.jpg)

However, our deck had all the colors needed to behave in the old, "just counter important stuff strategy". Now, the submission mentions that it wanted to be the old style, but the large number of counterspells in the deck was preventing this from becoming a reality. In addition, many of those counters were substandard, and could easily be replaced with high-quality removal spells. We can also look at possibly removing threats from the opponent's hand with discard before he gets a chance to put them on the board. This will also help in control match-ups. I earmarked the Memory Lapses and the Syncopates for elimination, and went back to my other problems to see what I could add. Duress was immediately obvious, and 4 went in. I still had 4 more slots to fill.

Also during this time, I dropped the number of pain lands from 7 to 3, replacing them with dual lands that come into play tapped instead.

### More Angels, but How?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/10/61/1061310db264f2e91d0de77619e0304b04bbf7e506ed79eba2f5369e477a9c1d.jpg)

The deck really needed more threats. Often it just couldn't find a Desolation Angel in time. It was also getting run over by creatures way too often. As much as I really wanted to add the 3rd Angel and the 4th Wrath of God, I couldn't since the owner may not have these cards available. There are a few other ways of making cards show up more frequently. First, you can reduce the size of the deck. Since our deck is already the minimum possible size, this didn't apply to us. Second, we can use deck manipulation. However, this is a control deck, which generally needs card advantage to work, and card manipulation often comes as card disadvantage. Something we definitely want to stay away from. Third, we can simply draw more cards. Control decks in general need to find some method of card advantage. While we had Fact or Fiction, Chainer's Edict and Wrath of God to produce card advantage, I personally love drawing cards. I chose to add Concentrate to increase my card drawing even more, and get to the spells I needed sooner. The change in tempo philosophy above allowed me to feel comfortable with adding another sorcery.

### Now Let's Increase the Speed

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/db/fadb007f49f8d7c758d8dd90f4574d67b9725d82c7062d3422b29474c90d403f.jpg)

The deck was pretty sluggish to begin with, and the addition of four more four casting cost sorceries was not helping any. Worse, in control on control matches, they could afford to just let me cast my Concentrate, and then watch me discard the extra cards because I had too many. The solution to this problem took a while, but was again made possible by the philosophy change of counter only when necessary. I removed 6 basic lands and added 6 Diamonds, 2 of each color. This solved a lot of problems. Suddenly, the deck could cast Concentrate, Wrath of God, and Fact or Fiction a turn earlier. In addition, since you had often cast the Diamond, or later in the game, would play a land and a diamond, your ability to use the card advantage in control versus control match up became substantially better. They were also very effective when the Angel destroyed all the land, since it left me with a good base of mana to start over with. In doing so, it also solved the Angel's Innocent Blood problem as now I could just counter the Blood. While Braids was still annoying, the Diamond acceleration often meant I had four mana anyway, and with my card advantage going, I could draw enough cards to deal with her. 

### The Sideboard

![](https://i.ormos.uk/89/04/89047557a171c61dab80b064eb80ea73da9d59470be39fd2f32e91ba0680c2ce.jpg)

With the main deck now performing well, it was time to turn my attention to the sideboard. My testing had shown that I was now vulnerable to discard, and I wanted something to bring in against it. I choose 2 Dodecapods, for their ability to really punish an opponent caught unaware. While these often were just cannon fodder for the creature removal in their hands, sometimes I had enough counter magic to make them stick. Also, I found them very useful to cast just before the Angel so that I can sac them to Innocent Blood instead. Finally, they were quite effective after the Yawgmoth's Agenda hit the board as simply another creature, since my opponent would have already used the Edict to get them in the graveyard the first time. 

*   I added 2 copies of Engineered Plague to the sideboard. This is currently one of my favorite sideboard cards for the environment, and with just one colored mana symbol in it's casting cost, it easily makes the cut.
*   I dropped the number of Deflections to one since it is a very situational card.
*   I added a second Dismantling Blow to deal with enchantments and artifacts. Since this is a Friday Night **Magic** deck, I want to be able to deal with artifacts just in case. Also, the mana acceleration of the Diamonds really helps the deck gain card advantage off of it. In a real pinch, you can use it on one of your own Diamonds.
*   After a few more, minor changes, the sideboard was complete.

### Summary

This deck has come a long way from its origins. The deck plays faster and more proactive. We closed a few of its big problem areas. Also, the limitation that we could not add rares turned into a benefit as the card drawing that we added to the deck was a very valuable find. While the deck does still bog down at times, begin unable to find the Angel, it almost always draws enough resources to live that long. However, there is one card that I would still change if I had access to rares. I would replace the main deck Dismantling Blow with a Vindicate.
