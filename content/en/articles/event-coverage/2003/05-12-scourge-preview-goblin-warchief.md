Fast, straightforward, and efficient, Suq'Ata Lancer was the first and, for a long time, the best, example of the 2/2 red haste creature for three mana. Top players took it early from the first *Visions* drafts, and that most efficient and aggressive of flanking Knights became a mainstay in Constructed decks with commensurate immediacy. Though thought of primarily as a tool for b-r and monored beatdown decks, Suq'Ata Lancer made appearances in both Darwin Kastle's u-r deck from Pro Tour-Paris and the Top 8 deck that cemented Paul McCabe's Player of the Year title at the 1997 World Championships. Even after the release of *Tempest* and the coming of two-power creatures for 1 mana rather than 3, Suq'Ata Lancer continued to appear in successful monored decks, such as Andrew Pacifico's 1998 U.S. Nationals build, and even penetrated the Extended environment, finding a home in the dominant w-r-based PT Jank decks of the late 1990s.

The next interpretation of the “2/2 red creature for 3 mana” model was Wild Colos out of *Urza's Destiny*. Wild Colos (alternately Raging Cougar or the Standard-legal Goblin Chariot) was not quite as wild as Suq'Ata Lancer, and never caught on in Constructed **Magic**. While Wild Colos did the same amount of damage as Suq’Ata Lancer and was exactly its equal on defense, it lacked the flanking ability to take out a 3/3 defender or mow down untold numbers of 1-toughness blocking creatures.

Future interpretations, like Lava Runner for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) instead of ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), had significant improvements; in the Opposition-crazy Worlds 2001 metagame, Lava Runner was among the tools that brought Mike Turian an individual Pro Tour Top 8. That being said, Lava Runner never rose above Tier Three status as Constructed card. It had specific applications in the context of a particular environment, but was never a staple card or the keystone of any archetype.

It seemed that Suq'Ata Lancer was a hair too good for ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), but that Grey Ogre creatures with haste alone would never be played in serious Constructed deck. Adding an additional relevant ability or so would complicate the mana cost to ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), and with the next implementation, might involve additional costs for the additional abilities. Its discard ability random, Hell-Bent Raider could never be considered a realistic discard outlet, despite the fact that red received arguably the best madness cards in its parent set, *Torment*; in any case, protection from white was far from relevant in the black and blue days of *Odyssey* Block. While first strike definitely made Hell-Bent Raider a more efficient combat creature, and certainly a better potential defender, it still didn’t have the flanking edge that made Suq’Ata Lancer such a ferocious attacker on turn three; it could not so much as penetrate a turn-2 Aquamoeba. While a significant improvement over its *Seventh Edition* and *Urza’s Destiny* cousins, while loaded with extras, Hell-Bent Raider nevertheless lacked the relevancy in its abilities that made Suq'Ata Lancer, or even Lava Runner, a Constructed-quality choice.

With the current attempt, we have a creature that I believe will finally do justice to the legacy of Suq'Ata Lancer:

Go ahead and pick up your jaw, Dan Paskins.

Goblin Warchief will be to Goblin beatdown decks what Wild Mongrel has become to u-g madness decks: no matter what customizations occur, the tuned models will almost always have four copies of this creature. It has haste all by itself and will many times be able to swing in unopposed on turn three. On turn four, if you miss your land drop, Goblin Warchief will nevertheless allow you to play, *and attack with*, Goblin Goon! For years, players have been trying to fill the sparkly shoes of Ball Lightning... Skizzik and Blistering Firecat have come close, but enhanced by the Warchief, Goblin Goon may in fact exceed its predecessor as the "3 mana" 6-power haste creature for red.

But what if you *do* make your fourth land drop? Even if for some reason your goblin deck made no pre-Warchief plays, you will still be able to play both Goblin Goon and Sparksmith on turn four, pick off a 3/3 blocker and attack for six, if not eight, damage.

A recent strategy to come out of *Onslaught* Block Contructed has been to play Skirk Prospector on turn 1 and then sacrifice it on turn three for the "creature enchantment" effect of bringing out Clickslither a turn more quickly than usual. Skirk Prospector may be even better suited to playing Goblin Warchief on turn 2. The investment of one card essentially turns Goblin Warchief into a Grizzly Bears with haste and after that, sets up Goblin Goon or multiple 2-power Goblins with the speed of a Ruby Medallion and the ferocity of a Fervor.

For all the amazing things we can say about Goblin Warchief, the card is not without its limitations. It has to be played in a base red, if not monored, beatdown deck. Where other red beatdown decks have played many creature types, from Wizards to Kavu to Cats, the Goblin Warchief must lead a force composed primarily, if not entirely, of goblins in order to reap its specific speed enhancements.

Goblin Warchief will undoubtedly increase the popularity of the Standard goblin deck. While goblins have had to play second fiddle to Wild Mongrel and Elephant Guide over the past few months, increased haste access and the ability to pick off 6/6 Wurm tokens in the blink of an eye should increase the incentive to play this archetype. In *Onslaught* block, goblins, already one of the strongest archetypes, will receive a much-needed boost in order to stay competitive against the current number one deck, Astral Slide. It will be interesting to see if a faster goblin deck will be able to take the lead over Astral Slide, or if *Scourge's* additions to that archetype will allow it to stay in PT Champion form over the summer PTQs.
