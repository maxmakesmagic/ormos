Juzam Djinn is the stuff of legends. Since the *Arabian Nights* set was released, Juzam Djinn has been one of the fiercest creatures to have graced peoples' play mats. There are generally only three things that I would think about when my opponent would play a swamp against me in the old days. One of them was "please don't cast a Hypnotic Specter". Another was "please don't cast Hymn to Tourach". The last one, of course, was "please don't cast Juzam Djinn". A whopping 5/5 for ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif), his only drawback is that he deals a point of damage to his controller every upkeep. Not a bad price to pay for that much beatdown. That is why many players find black to be so appealing. After sitting on the wrong side of the table, it feels very good to see the worried look on your opponent's face when they are forced to find an answer to whatever overpowered threat you manage to cast.

Since the Juzam's introduction, black has become known for having powerful creatures with drawbacks that attempt to balance them out. Phyrexian Scuta from *Planeshift* is one such creature, similar in many ways to the Juzam Djinn. For ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif), Phyrexian Scuta is either a 3/3 creature or a 5/5 that requires the caster to pay 3 life in addition to the traditional casting cost. This zombie made an appearance in aggressive black-red decks in Standard and it was only held in check by the very successful red-green Fires of Yavimaya decks. Another seemingly overpowered creature was Phyrexian Negator from *Urza's Destiny*, a 5/5 trampler for only ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif). Its drawback is quite severe, however, as its controller has to sacrifice a permanent for every point of damage that is dealt to the Phyrexian Negator. Even so, Phyrexian Negator was a mainstay of suicide black in Extended and also does quite well in Classic, as you aren't very likely to run into direct damage and blocking creatures. Finally, its important not to forget the little people who helped make suicide decks so powerful, like Sarcomancy from *Tempest* and Carnophage from *Exodus*, both 2/2's for ![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) with the potential to deal their controller a point of damage each upkeep. All of these creatures, in some way or another, were created with Juzam Djinn in mind. They are creatures with more power and toughness than is usual for the casting cost, with a drawback that attempts to balance it out, not always successfully.

I find that when most **Magic** players start reminiscing about the old days, they begin longing to play with those cards that they are no longer legal in Standard. Some of them, while powerful in the Standard or Extended decks of the period, just aren't quite good enough to justify playing in Classic. So what is a nostalgic **Magic** player to do?

Luckily, even Wizards of the Coast's R&D gets nostalgic for a time when things were simpler, when Juzams and Erhnams ruled the earth and Serra Angels and Shivan Dragons ruled the sky. Apparently, even they can't help tinkering with their old favorites and including similar creatures in the new sets, not to mention outright reprinting them (as is the case with Erhnam Djinn, Serra Angel, and Shivan Dragon). So, for all the Juzam Djinn-lovers out there, *Onslaught* brings us the Grinning Demon.

For two black and two generic mana (sound familiar?), you don't just get a 5/5 creature - Grinning Demon is a whopping 6/6 for four mana and you don't even have to work to put it in the graveyard like a Roar of the Wurm. At first glance, its drawback seems severe: Grinning Demon's controller loses 2 life at the beginning of his or her upkeep. If I recall, though, people used to think that the upkeeps of Juzam and Serendib Efreet were too much and at first glance, they believed that Phyrexian Negator was simply awful. Time has proven that if you make something big enough and cheap enough, no drawback will keep it from being useful.

A 6/6 creature that inflicts double the pain as its 5/5 ancestor isn't the whole story however. Grinning Demon is equipped with a brand new mechanic called morph, which comes in particularly handy with this fellow. For the price of ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif), a player can play Grinning Demon face down as a 2/2 colorless creature. Later on, the controller can play the morph cost of ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) to flip it over, turning it into the 6/6 demon that it is. Surprise! Not only will it give a player something to cast if his or her hand is chock full of demons and he or she has no three casting cost spells, but it can come in handy in the late game as well. Wasn't it the worst when you drew that Juzam Djinn and then looked at your life total to see that you were at too low of life to cast it? This way, even if you are at 2 life, you can make use of the Grinning Demon as a 2/2 creature. Granted, it is no 6/6, but it's not sitting in your hand, either. Not only that, but during that final attack phase, you can pay the morph cost and get in one last beat with your 6/6 Grinning Demon while still hopefully cheating death.

By the time Grinning Demon is legal for play in the Standard tournament environment, *Invasion*, *Planeshift*, and *Apocalypse* will be on the way out. That means no more Nightscape Familiar, a troublesome regenerator that speeds up the Psychatog decks, whose ability to block Grinning Demon endlessly would wipe the grin right off of its face. Not only that, but the extra point of power and toughness that Grinning Demon has on Juzam Djinn makes it that much better for Standard, where 6/6 wurm tokens are the norm.

Will Grinning Demon help bring black beatdown out of its slump? No one can say for sure, but I for one will be excited to get my hands on some so that I can give it a try.
