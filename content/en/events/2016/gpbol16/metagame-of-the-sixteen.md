So if the choices of these 16 veterans are any indication, there are two best decks: White-Blue Eldrazi and Abzan Company. But one is still on top. As Raphaël Lévy told me: "When the best deck plays [Rest in Peace](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rest+in+Peace) in the sideboard, and the second-best deck relies on creatures like [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) and [Eternal Witness](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eternal+Witness), you need a very good reason to not play the best deck."

But beyond the Eldrazi decks and Abzan Company, there are plenty of interesting one-of choices as well. Players are standing up to the Eldrazi villain by playing sweepers like [Living End](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Living+End), by copying them with [Phantasmal Image](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Phantasmal+Image), by making even more gigantic creatures with [Daybreak Coronet](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Daybreak+Coronet), or by pushing little infectors through with [Distortion Strike](https://web.archive.org/web/20160305000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Distortion+Strike). The hopes of beating Eldrazi are still alive.
