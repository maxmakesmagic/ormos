I would like to start out by thanking all the people that sent in decks for me to look at. Wizards of the Coast was nice enough to bundle a bunch of them into a Word file and send it off to me. This file was over 3 megabytes in size. I opened it up and found out the file was 977 pages long and contained exactly 977 decks. Some decks covered multiple pages with lots of interesting comments to read, while other people were considerably less than a page because they gave comments and forgot to post the actual deck. I strongly recommend that you take a couple minutes to review your submission before sending it to me. It is next to impossible to fix 56 card decks. 0 card decks are even harder!

This week, I took a look at some of the monoblack control decks sent in. There were quite a few of them, all varied. Some additional people sent in black control decks that splashed white for Death Grasp and Gerrard's Verdict. Monoblack Control was the powerhouse deck back in the days of Necropotence, and this deck has a lot in common with its ancestors. The common themes between them being card drawing (Necropotence vs. Phyrexian Arena), cheap creature removal, Drain Life and discard. Creatures were optional, with early Necro decks having them, and later having few, if any. Here then, is one of the submissions. 

### Comments

From what you can see, it's a non-creature black deck that keeps your opponent's creatures at bay with removal spells, takes out their graveyard when its near or just past threshold with Haunting Echoes, and wins the game with very big drain spells. I would like some comments on how to improve it especially against cards like Compost and Circle of Protection: Black. Thanks for the help.

In the past, I have done the Deck Doctor booth at the Pro Tour for Wizards many times. When you do it at a table, the best you can do is take a look at a deck, tell people your gut feelings, and go on. However, being at home, and having a long deadline before having to complete my analysis, I decided to take a different approach. I loaded up **Magic** Online, hunted around for the cards I needed to complete the deck, and tried it out. I then played with the deck until I was reasonably happy with the performance. Lets go over my changes.

### Land

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8e/52/8e52d014a831eb808eedea12f9b4dc64e43bdc9e516db01eb6d0d78ccb8b9951.jpg)

Throughout my history on the Pro Tour, and my association with Scott Johns and Zvi Mowshowitz, there is one thing that has always been constant. I like to shave the mana as tight as possible. It has become the running joke between Zvi and myself that whenever I want to fit something into one of Zvi's decks, I simply cut a land. Then I look for something else to add, so I have an excuse to cut another land. I like keeping land as tight as possible. However, this deck is not a deck to skimp on the land with! At first, I brought it up to 24 lands, then 25. The land count finally settled at 26. Among the lands I added were the rest of the Cabal Coffers, a very powerful card for fueling large Soul Burns, something this deck really wants to do. For a while, I was concerned about the loss of swamps for the Corrupts, but in the end, the benefits for Soul Burn were too much to be ignored.

### Discard

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/12/3a12c80ee6f3d6a8124dbef0789a1e918377f5dc221c39224bdddc479ab8ec2a.jpg)

I like targeted discard, but you can have too much of a good thing. In the case of discard, the problem comes when your opponent already has threats on the board, and is busy killing you with them, while you are drawing more discard spells. An additional minor problem is that it is very annoying to have to make somebody discard a flashback, or worse, madness card. I pulled the 4 Addles. To go along with this, I found I was waiting longer to cast Haunting Echoes, as I was putting a few less cards in their graveyard. I also cut an Echo.

### Tutors

![](https://i.ormos.uk/20/f8/20f8a1ba7bd6ba94d0c1e599bd58351a1f66b45e50879dfe994b3353ab673bb6.jpg)

With the additional mana, I can now cast Diabolic Tutor and then cast the spell I just retrieved the same turn. This increased ability to use Diabolic Tutor caused me to increase the number to four. Now that I had 4 Tutors, I wanted to be able to get certain spells that were very strong in the right situation. These cards were Mind Sludge and Engineered Plague. I eventually became so happy with the Plague that I added a second. One of the biggest threats against the deck early on was Ichorid. With the addition of the Plagues I had an answer. Opposition decks also became much less problematic.

### Card Advantage

It is important to note that I consider Haunting Echoes to be a card advantage engine. While you don't technically draw extra cards, they draw fewer business spells, sometimes significantly fewer. This effect is really quite staggering, and took a long time for me personally to come to grips with just how strong the card is while testing for PT-Osaka. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1f/fb/1ffb1acb5b37655f20b3b1d3270186e555dc1f9b492990c4c1cffd9564140b46.jpg)

Two of the card advantage spells in this deck seemed to be at odds with each other. Phyrexian Arena gives me plenty of cards to cast, and Yawgmoth's Agenda gives me even more cards, but lets me use less of them. While I am very fond of both cards, one of them had to go. I decided that this deck really is based around the Arena. I took out the 2 Agendas, and put in the 4th Arena. Phyrexian Arena is the card that I really want to get down, so I cut the Agenda. Boy was I wrong. This is where the fact that I can playtest it really paid off. My decision basically violated the Tutor effect mentioned above. I continually found that I had a tutor in my hand and wanted to go get the Agenda, usually because I was too low in life to risk the Arena. However, there is another card drawer that can do amazing things IF you have a source of large quantities of mana. This is one thing that a monoblack deck has no problems with these days. That card is Planar Portal. Watching this card in action really impressed me. The first thing you go get is often a Cabal Coffers, which pretty much pays for it's activation from then on.

### Summing up the main deck

All the above modifications left me with a 62-card deck. It was time for a tough choice on what must go. Eventually, I chose to lose an Innocent Blood and a Corrupt. I chose to lose the Innocent Blood because the deck had increased its anti-creature bias and it was time to give a little of that bias back. The Corrupt went because I often had problems with drawing too many really high casting cost spells early when I am unable to cast them. The addition of the Planar Portal made it worse, but also gave me another way to get one should I need it. Here then, is the final deck list:

**Land**  
 4 Cabal Coffers  
 22 Swamp

**Spells**  
 4 Duress  
 4 Chainer's Edict  
 3 Innocent Blood  
 3 Mutilate  
 3 Phyrexian Arena  
 2 Haunting Echoes  
 4 Diabolic Tutor  
 3 Corrupt  
 3 Soul Burn  
 1 Yawgmoth's Agenda  
 2 Engineered Plague  
 1 Planar Portal  
 1 Mind Sludge

### On to the Sideboard

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/90/02/900258a4c708d5cb707c77dbe5392169b8e241f04336661f984de5bc9362047c.jpg"><br><i>Really, what is your opponent going to do about this?</i>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

In the comments section of the submission, it is noted that the deck has problems after sideboarding with Compost. It is a testament to just how good Compost is when the best cards you can get to fight it are main deck, and you still have problems. Yawgmoth's Agenda is a fine answer to Compost, but they tend to have answers to it after game 1. You can fight it with discard, but the games are going to last quite a long time, and eventually they will top deck it and you will lose. Another good way to fight it is with kill spells that don't go to the graveyard. Engineered Plague is quite effective here. However, the most interesting method was actually in the sideboard of a different submission. This individual noticed that with all the mana you have available, Aladdin's Ring is quite viable. Now you have a way to keep almost every creature off the board without putting cards in your graveyard. Eventually, it also kills the opponent.

Another problem was Circle of Protection: Black (CoP). While playing, I didn't find CoP to be a very big problem once I changed my strategy for winning the game. Against people playing white, I planned to be able to run them out of cards. In order to do this, I must never cast Phyrexian Arena since it will both kill me and run me out of cards first. Just wait a little while for the graveyard to grow, then Haunting Echoes them. After this, you just need to wait them out. Obviously, this won't work if your white players are running Millstone. However, the Aladdin's Ring I put in the sideboard for the Compost players came in very handy here, preventing me from having to deck people too often.

Keeping with the creatureless theme, I removed the Mortivores from the sideboard. While 1 might still be a good idea, I didn't want a conflict between him and Innocent Blood, and I wanted the space. Here is the final sideboard:

1 Aladdin's Ring  
 2 Engineered Plague  
 2 Yawgmoth's Agenda  
 1 Mind Sludge  
 1 Persecute  
 1 Phyrexian Arena  
 3 Skeletal Scrying  
 4 Slay

### Summary

This deck was quite fun to play around with. If you liked Control Necro near the end of Necropotence's Standard days, then you should enjoy playing it. I know I certainly did. I would also like to thank the many denizens of the **Magic: the Gathering** Online beta community, upon many of whom I unleashed some pretty awful versions of this deck before I came up with the polished version.
