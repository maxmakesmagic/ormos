Welcome again to the Days of Future Future, where I share some of the decks we created in our Future Future League and give you some insights into what we were thinking when trying to balance Standard.

As usual, I want to add this disclaimer that many of the cards in the decks you will see are different from the final version that saw print—in some cases, the versions in our decks were more powerful, in some cases, they were less powerful.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/images/cardart/cardart_chordofcalling.jpg)

[Chord of Calling](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chord+of+Calling) | Art by [Karl Kopinski](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&artist=%5B%22Karl%20Kopinski%22%5D)

These decks represent months of fine tuning for sets, where cards are constantly in flux and may not look what you are used to when seeing competitive decklists. It doesn't always mean that the decks weren't good; often it just means that our metagame was different or the cards inside them functioned differently. Our goal is to test everything to try and catch things we might not eyeball as being too strong, since those are often the cards we end up most regretting in the future.

With that, let's get started.

The original version of [Dictate of Heliod](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dictate+of+Heliod) was just as a double [Crusade](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crusade) instead of a double [Glorious Anthem](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glorious+Anthem), but at four mana.

This is a pretty quick and dirty decklist, generated in an attempt to see just how busted the card was. The answer was, unsurprisingly, very. It turns out you don't need to force people to play a lot of white creatures in the same way that [Master of Waves](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Master+of+Waves) forces people to play with a lot of [Judge's Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Judge%27s+Familiar)s and [Cloudfin Raptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudfin+Raptor)s.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Dictate%20of%20Heliod)](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dictate%20of%20Heliod)

Generally, when testing out cards, we try to figure out what kind of decks we would expect to see them in, in the real world. In the real world, at the time *Journey into Nyx* was in development, there were a lot of [Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary) decks, and so we spent a lot of time playing with that card, seeing just how far we could push the boundary on our aggro decks.



The earliest version of [Sage of Hours](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage+of+Hours) was a lot stronger than the final version, and decks like this quickly led to what was basically an infinite number of turns being taken. Or, at least enough that the opponent was dead. We weakened the card a bit to make sure that actually taking that many turns would at least be a worthy challenge.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sage%20of%20Hours)](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%20of%20Hours)

Planeswalkers are some of the hardest cards to get right, so we spend a lot of time tweaking their numbers and trying out new abilities before we find the ones that end up getting printed on their cards.

At the time of building this, the Jund decks were doing pretty well in real-life Standard, so I built a deck to see how they would translate into our FFL. The final version looks somewhat similar to the Jund Monsters list in current Standard, but was a bit less polished.

We also take the time to look at some of the more fringy decks, in this case a white-blue [Trading Post](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Trading+Post) deck that was similar to a deck Andrew Cuneo had been playing in (then) current Standard. The question for us was whether or not that deck still worked, and what (if anything) we could learn from it.

Gavin Verhey, well-known ReConstructed columnist, is a big fan of taking pretty fringy combo decks that he gets in his column and trying them out with new cards. In this case, we realized there was an infinite combo with [Archangel of Thune](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Archangel+of+Thune), [Horizon Chimera](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horizon+Chimera), and [Fathom Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fathom+Mage). Since we were putting a powerful tutor into the format ([Chord of Calling](https://web.archive.org/web/20140801000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chord+of+Calling)), we wanted to see what that would do to the deck.
