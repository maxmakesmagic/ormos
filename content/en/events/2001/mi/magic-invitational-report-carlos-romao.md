Another advantage brought to me by my World Championship was my invite. This was another dream come true. This year, the Invitational would be played on **Magic Online**. This was a first and I had the pleasure of being one of the sixteen lucky ones who got to try it out.

Diego Ostrovich and I got to Seattle together and as soon as we dropped our bags in the hotel, we went out to find a place to eat. We poor Latins picked the wrong side of the street. We chose to go right and, hours later, were informed that all the good restaurants were on the left. Downtown Seattle is very nice. There are a lot of stores and, most importantly, a wide variety of restaurants.

### Day 1

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>Gary Wise convinced me to order some chocolate cake dessert called Volcano…</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

We played a draft and went out to dinner at, I think, a typical Texan restaurant. For the first time in years of traveling, I had real food instead of sandwiches and stuff like that. I had more than enough bread and the appetizers were very good. The main course was great, with a huge chunk of meat and a baked potato. Mmm. My biggest fear coming into the Invitational was, because I hardly knew anyone besides Ostrovich, that I would feel out of place. Yet, from the start, everyone was very friendly. A big thanks to the other Invitationalists for making me feel like a part of the group.

### Day 2

This was the tour day. We woke up early and started things with a guided tour through WotC’s headquarters. Mark Rosewater, a great host throughout the trip, was our guide. Dakkon Blackblade welcomed us into the building. I always imagined the building to be smaller. It was nice to see where the game I play is developed.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/mi02/597.jpg)

Right after the tour, we hit downtown Seattle. It’s a great place, with a very diversified shopping market, even if I didn’t buy anything. We had lunch in a mall and I started the countdown to the eagerly awaited Invitational dinner. I’m not really into seafood, but as soon as I saw the menu, I knew there were plenty of other options.

We sat around outside, with the Puget Sound as the background. When we finally went inside the restaurant, they divided us up into four groups. Gary Wise convinced me to order some chocolate cake dessert called Volcano (or something like that).

As you might have noticed, I’m not a small guy and eating is, without any doubt, something I enjoy. But the cake scared me. It was very, very good but no matter how hard I tried, I couldn’t finish it all. This is always my favorite part of any U.S. restaurant: dessert. You always find something huge, with chocolate. 

### Day 3

Finally, the big day arrived. Ladies and gentlemen, the Invitational was about to begin. Again, we were divided in two groups for two rooms. Room A was closer to the entrance and only had the computers. Room B had a perfect location. Or, should I say, a delicious location? It was right next to the biggest treasure in the building: food! Is it just me or does this whole report sounds like a big sugar overdose? That room had donuts, Oreos, drinks and all sorts of food.

Two-Headed Giant – I thought this would be a boring format, mostly because I hadn’t quite figured out how it worked. As soon as the games started, I changed my mind. As “**Magic** for fun”, it’s quite good. The rounds were decided in a single game. Ostrovich and I paid for that in the first round, when both of us had mana trouble. Ostrovich was jinxed all day. When he teamed up with Gary Wise, he had to take a double mulligan.

The results were:  
 Carlo Romao/Diego Ostrovich 0-1 Gary Wise/Eivind Nitter  
 Carlos Romao/Gary Wise 1-0 Diego Ostrovich/Eivind Nitter  
 Carlos Romao/Eivind Nitter 1-0 Diego Ostrovich/Gary Wise.

Online Extended – As always, I chose to play ‘Tog. I wanted it as close to the Psychatog deck I played at Worlds as possible. I added Smother and Polluted Delta to smooth a few things over. The deck deserved a farewell and I tried to do it in style. The Invitational gave me my first ‘Tog loss in a very long time, as Brian Davis was quick to remind everyone after our round. Pure evil.

Carlos Romao 2-0 David Humpherys  
 Carlos Romao 1-2 Brian Davis  
 Carlos Romao 2-0 Gabriel Nassif

This adds up to a 4-2 record at the end of the first day. I was extremely happy with my results, especially since I was in a tournament with such good players. Every day, the staff took us out to lunch and dinner, at some great restaurants. I have to stop talking about the food! As I was saying, I had a great first day and, for a change, we played a few drafts afterwards.

### Day 4

At **Magic** tournaments I can one thing that I can never, ever do in my everyday life: wake up early. When it comes to **Magic**, I always wake up with energy, completely ready to go. Back in Brazil, when I have to get up for college, I can hardly drag myself out of bed.

Auction of the People: Seventeen decks for sixteen players. Some decks were highly coveted. Everyone wanted one of those, so I was wondering if I wouldn’t be better off with one of the less-desirable ones. I ended up with the Z deck (at six cards, thirteen life), which was basically a red-black zombie deck (including the Pyre Zombies, which I really liked in this format).

My starting life total turned out to be a major handicap against Itaru Ishida. He could kill me in a single turn and that was exactly what he did in the first game. He Cauldron Danced and we shuffled up for the second game.

Carlos Romao 1-2 Itaru Ishida  
 Carlos Romao 0-2 Dave Price  
 Carlos Romao 2-1 Tomi Walamies

### Rochester Draft

Warming up for Chicago with the very best? I’m not a big Rochester fan. In fact, I’m more of a booster draft junkie. At the end, I had what I thought was a good red-black deck. It had most of the removal spells you can think of and enough flying creatures (which I think is key in this format). I expected a 2-1 at least. I made a major mistake in a game against Jon Finkel that cost me the game. I’m not sure I could have won it, but I could have tried, at least.

Carlos Romao 1-2 Jon Finkel  
 Carlos Romao 2-0 Chris Pikula  
 Carlos Romao 2-0 Alex Shvartsman

So I ended the second day with seven victories, which meant I was in tied for fifth with Finkel and Kibler. I was still happy with my result, especially because I still had a shot at the finals. It would take a miracle, but it COULD happen. At the end of the day, we headed out to another restaurant (there I go again talking about food) and then we played a few drafts.

I was still undecided about what to play. I had a very strange Enchantress deck or a deck revolving around Braids. I’m a big fan of the card but I have never had a chance to play with it. So, this was the right moment to do it. The deck was built in less than thirty minutes, since I had thought about the deck for quite a while.

### Day 5

To keep my dream of making the finals alive, I would have to ace this last day. I played some games against Ostrovich and immediately lost my optimism. Unfortunately, it was too late to change. All that was left to do was sit through the games and take the punishment.

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I would like to issue my public apologies to Rachel [Rosewater]…</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I ended up beating Kibler because I got very lucky. As you can imagine, I don’t really advise you to play my deck at any sort of tournament. This is what happens when you don’t play ‘Togs. I should have added them; they surely wouldn’t be in the way. I’m sorry ‘Tog, I won’t betray you again.

Carlos Romao 1-2 Jens Thoren  
 Carlos Romao 2-1 Brian Kibler  
 Carlos Romao 0-2 Olivier Ruel (someday I’ll win)

After all the emotional highs of the Invitational, the big party was about to begin. Mark Rosewater hosted the wrap party at his home. Again, my first thought was food! During the party, we played a few drafts and I would like to issue my public apologies to Rachel, Mark’s daughter. Not only did we steal her space to draft, but she was forced to see my horrible draft. She was quite shocked by this and strongly disagreed with all my picks. I’m sorry Rachel, I’ll try to do better next time. Jokes aside, I would like to thank Mark Rosewater and his family for the great party. Thank you. 

It seemed like a dream: **Magic** every day and all the food I could eat. Everything was just perfect. But all good things must come to an end: on Sunday, the Invitational got a champion (congratulations Jens Thoren) and we all started packing up.

I really like this tournament, or should I say the party? Congratulations and thank you to all the players and the staff. I hope I have to write another report next year! A big final thank you to all of you who stuck through my report, I hope you enjoyed it.

Just one more thing, I would like to thank Rui Oliveira. Muito Obrigado Rui.
