The feature match area was silent as the players waited on the signal to begin Round 16, the final Swiss round of Pro Tour *Battle for Zendikar.* For some of these players, this would be the last round of the tournament. For a fortunate few, it would simply be the last round of the day before they returned on Sunday for the Top 8 and a chance at championship glory.

It was a familiar feeling for Yuta Takahashi. He made the Top 8 of Pro Tour San Diego in 2007, where he lost in the finals. Also the veteran of nine Grand Prix Top 8 appearances (with three wins), he was well accustomed to the pressure of the final round under the lights with a Sunday Pro Tour spot on the line.

Ryoichi Tamada was no stranger himself. Though he didn't have the extensive resume of Takahashi, he has two Grand Prix Top 8 appearances and was potentially just one win away from adding a Pro Tour Top 8 to that list.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/events/ptbfz/ptbfz_r16_takahashi_tamada.jpg)  
*Two countrymen faced an elimination match with the Top 8 hanging in the balance. Would it be the seasoned veteran Yuta Takahashi, or Ryoichi Tamada, one of Japan's 2015 World* Magic *Cup team members?*

Both entered the tournament with known quantities, with Takahashi on Abzan and Tamada on Jeskai, but both also brought their own take on the deck. For Takahashi, that meant [Hangarback Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hangarback+Walker) in Abzan to go along with the usual suspects of [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) and [Gideon, Ally of Zendikar](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gideon%2C+Ally+of+Zendikar).

While many players in the room opted to splash black in their Jeskai decks, Tamada decided to keep things simple. He opted for just two off-color lands: a [Cinder Glade](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cinder+Glade) and a [Canopy Vista](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Canopy+Vista), and those only to spread out the targets for his fetch lands. Keeping his mana simple and free of a splash meant that there was more room for [Hangarback Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hangarback+Walker) and [Jeskai Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jeskai+Charm), with four of each finding their way into the deck.

# The Games

Takahashi was fast out of the gates, landing [Warden of the First Tree](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warden+of+the+First+Tree) on the opening turn and following it up with [Anafenza, the Foremost](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anafenza%2C+the+Foremost). It was a fearsome opener, and could have taken over quickly if not for a [Valorous Stance](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Valorous+Stance) from Tamada, who led with a [Hangarback Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hangarback+Walker) and began growing the construct while taking some pressure from the Warden and tokens courtesy of Takahashi's [Gideon, Ally of Zendikar](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gideon%2C+Ally+of+Zendikar).

Tamada found [Jeskai Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jeskai+Charm) to rid himself of Gideon, but that left him short on answers when [Wingmate Roc](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingmate+Roc) arrived with a friend on the next turn. The sudden air force gave Takahashi the advantage, and when a second pair of birds reinforced the first on the next turn, Tamada found himself in a desperate situation.

Men in desperate situations try desperate things, and for Tamada it was an all-out attack with his team, including the 5/5 [Hangarback Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hangarback+Walker). When Takahashi opted to take the damage from the Hangarback rather than trade with it and leave the tokens behind, Tamada picked up his cards with a small laugh and sent the match into the next game.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/events/ptbfz/ptbfz_r16_takahashi.jpg)  
*Takahashi was no stranger to high pressure situations.*

A flurry of removal on both sides was the story of the second game. [Silkwrap](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silkwrap) for both parties removed troublesome [Hangarback Walker](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hangarback+Walker)s, and when the dust settled, each player had a pair of cards in hand but no board. With a [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) off the top of his deck, Takahashi took a peek at Tamada's hand, seeing a pair of [Fiery Impulse](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiery+Impulse)s but no threat. That changed in a hurry when Gideon arrived in Tamada's draw step, and the Planeswalker ended the game with an army of allies a few turns later.

Things started off poorly for Takahashi in Game 3, as he was forced to mulligan and didn't have a source of green mana until the third turn, while Tamada led with [Jace, Vryn's Prodigy](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Vryn%27s+Prodigy). Still, the former Pro Tour Top 8 competitor wasn't going down easily, and he strung together [Abzan Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abzan+Charm) into [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino) into [Tasigur, the Golden Fang](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tasigur%2C+the+Golden+Fang). It a steady stream of high-impact cards that pulled him right back into the game and forced Tamada to find an answer.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/events/ptbfz/ptbfz_r16_tamada.jpg)  
*Tamada was looking for his big break on the global stage.*

He did exactly that, dispatching the Rhino with [Valorous Stance](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Valorous+Stance) and then keeping the banana king off the field thanks to [Jeskai Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20151018000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jeskai+Charm) and then replaying the Jace-fueled Charm. When Gideon joined his Planeswalker comrade on the battlefield, Takahashi extended his hand, conceding the match to Tamada and giving him a shot at the Top 8.
