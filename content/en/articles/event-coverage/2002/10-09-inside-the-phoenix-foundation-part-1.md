Going into PT Boston, Marco, Dirk and I had quite high expectations. We won the last two major team events (Pro Tour-New York and the Masters in Tokyo) and I was part of the German national team that won the team competition at Worlds this year, largely thanks to the preparation we did for Boston. To be honest, our practice for both New York and Tokyo was pretty poor as we didn't do too many drafts before either event. Even though we still haven't lost a sanctioned Team Rochester, we went 5-0-2 at the PT with one draw against Car Acrobatic Team (Cuneo/Forsythe/Johnson) happening only after we needed 1 point to make Top 4. We were up one game in each match when the Americans agreed to take the draw. The second was one round later when we were already a lock for Top 8. I won the deciding match and offered a draw which Cornelissen/Froehlich/Boeken declined before the match. We then went on to win the Masters and I guess it is fair to claim that we were 10-0 in Team Rochester going into Boston.

This time, the format seemed to be a lot trickier then last year and, as Dirk could take time off work, we decided to go to Atlanta, where Dirk went to school for one year, one week earlier to do some gaming. We talked to Rudy Edwards on IRC and he said that his team (Joe Crosby and Matt Ranks) would be available.

Before we left, we did a few drafts with Iwan Tan, Andreas Kruschel and Jan Holland. My strategy for Worlds was to go black-x in seat A, blue-x in B and white-x in C. We agreed that green-blue was the best color combination for seat B pretty soon afterwards. The problem was that we did not like black-red in A. It pretty much only worked if the other team had either black-blue or a really slow deck. We thought that most players would go for black-green. Unfortunately the mirror match is pretty awful. You basically play Portal as your deck consists of creatures and sorcery speed removal without too many tricks. We wanted to try black-white, but didn't find the time to do so before we flew to Atlanta. Marco was pretty happy drafting a very junky white-red deck in seat C. Cards like Suntail Hawk and Tireless Tribe seemed to be quite important for his drafts. It was stuff I would never really want to have in any of our decks, but Marco kept winning with it. After all, that is the most important thing.

Unfortunately we weren't the only ones that arrived in the state of Georgia that weekend. Hurricane Isidor came with us and the sunny weather Atlanta enjoyed the weeks before we arrived was gone and it rained most of the time. While we originally planned to do some sightseeing, the weather foiled those plans. We went out to various excellent places for dinner, played soccer in the afternoon when possible and played Magic the rest of the time. Fortunately there were plenty of gamers around. The first weekend was not all that exciting as the Southeast Invitational took place and Rudy's team along with Brian Kibler, Sol Malka and Antonino de Rosa were all busy drafting Unglued, playing extended Highlander and 5-Color. Thomas Pannell was in town to do some coverage and visit some friends. He drafted against us twice and utterly destroyed us both times by going black in C and blue-white in A, probably the only thing you really don't want to do in this format. It worked out anyway and we tried to figure out for a couple of days if there was actually a good reason why we lost. We couldn't come up with anything and went back to our black-white, green-blue and red-white configuration. Starting Monday, Rudy, Joe, Matt and Brooks, who replaced Matt when he had to work, had lots of time and the gaming started. We did quite a few drafts and Joe desperately tried to switch colors to make his and Matt's matchups favorable by going black-blue in B and white-blue or green-x in C. It just didn't work. When I eventually crushed his four Cabal Torturer blue-black deck with my blue-green deck that splashed a Psychotic Haze as the only way to deal with the Torturers, he gave in. We pretty much reached the conclusion that the way to go was to fix green-blue in B and white-red in C and change that only if bombs were opened in unfortunate seats. The A player was the only one who switched between black with white, green or red. Black-white seemed to be the optimal configuration against both black-red and black-green.

The only obstacle left was the team sealed on Day 1. While I believe that building decks is quite skill intensive, there is still an annoying random factor: the matchups. When Team X sweeps Team Y 3-0, the result can often be reversed when you just rotate the players in one team by one seat. What happens is this: Team A has their white-blue against the opposing green-red deck, their black-blue against the other white-blue and their red-green against the other black-blue. This means Team A has three very favorable matchups but if Team B would rotate decks, all of a sudden the same matchups would happen, but all in favor of Team B. The key to win in this format is to build the decks in a way that they don't have 'can't win' matchups. For example, it is a very good idea to avoid blue-white if you don't have at least a Psionic Gift, a Stupefying Touch and one or two bounce spells to deal with Cabal Torturer, Chainflinger and Nantuko Disciple at least temporarily. It is also possible to construct a green-red deck that has an okay or even good matchup against white-blue. All you really need are Nantuko Disciples, Chainflingers, a Krosan Archer or his pal Anurid Swarmsnapper and a Demoralize or Flash of Defiance in the sideboard. All of a sudden, the matchup against your archenemy isn't all that unfavorable any more. If your card pool doesn't allow you to make your decks workable in their nightmare matchups, it is often a good idea to build unusual archetypes. Black-red, green-blue and blue-white work quite well too. The only deck we really avoided for sealed was red-white. While a janky version does quite well in draft, it never came together for us in sealed. Your 1/1 fliers just don't cut it against really good decks and that is what team sealed decks are usually like. To make red-white work in sealed, you need several bombs that just win the game. But if you have those cards, it is usually better to put them in other color combinations that don't need as many of them to win.

### The Tournament

We flew to Boston on Thursday, registered, went out for dinner and went straight to sleep as we felt quite well prepared and didn't want to do any last minute drafts on the sight that would give other guys a hint at our strategy.

Friday morning, we learned that instead of the seven rounds we played last year on Day 1, there would be only six this time. The number of teams was pretty much the same, which meant that the cut to Top 4 was most likely 9-2. The six rounds on Day 1 were split into three different sealed decks to minimize the luck factor. Our first set of cards wasn't spectacular, but definitely better then average. I got a nearly monogreen deck with really good creatures, Squirrel Nest, Acorn Harvest, Grizzly Fate and Rites of Initiation as a way to win in case the opponent could stall the ground. The other three red cards I played were Ember Beast, Swelter and Arcane Teachings. Dirk had a good blue-white deck that missed a 3/3 flier but was very solid otherwise. Marco got the black-red deck that lacked some quality creatures other then his Laquatus's Champion, but had excellent removal.

In round 1 we met team Angstschreeuw featuring Bram Snepvangers, Menno Dolstra and Jos Schreurs from the Netherlands. The matchups seemed to be okay. Dirk played a white-blue mirror match, got smashed by multiple 3-power fliers in game one but screwed Menno in game 2 and came back after not playing a land on turn 3 in game three to take the match. Bram, in the meantime, demolished me with his black-blue deck in a matchup that should have been in my favor. Unfortunately, I didn't manage to cast a spell before turn 4 in game 1 and turn 3 in game 2 and Bram raced me with a Balshan Collaborator in the first game and nailed me with Upheaval/Psychatog in game 2. Marco was up a game in his match against Jos's green-red deck but lost both games 2 and 3 to bad judgment calls.  
 0-1

Frown. Not exactly the way we wanted to start the tournament.

Our opponents in round 2 weren't very happy to see us when we got to the table. Team Unemployed (Charles Horn/Joe van Sickle/Brendan Bullock) had a worse card pool and we smashed them. My opponent was white-blue but he couldn't handle my token generators in both games. In game 2 he actually had a Kirtar's Wrath to clear the board, but I was holding Grizzly Fate so that didn't really matter.  
 1-1

Then we got our second card pool. Those cards were the worst rubbish I have ever seen when it comes to team sealed. We tried really hard but we couldn't even come up with two decks that would be good enough to take a match from an average deck. In the end, Marco got a green-red deck that splashed for two Thaumatogs and had Kamahl as his only real chance to win, Dirk ended up with a blue-white that scooped to a single Aven Windreader as two 2/2 fliers and a Scalpelexis were the only ways to win and I ended up with a black-blue deck that did have some good removal with double Ghastly Demise, a Crippling Fatigue and a Faceless Butcher, but my creature base was awful at best. I had two Zombie Assassins and a Face of Fear maindeck and Thought Eater and Aven Fisher were my air force.

Kyle Rose and his team the 'Jokas' were our Feature Match in round 3. We got ridiculously lucky and had all the perfect matchups. Marco and his g-r deck faced black and blue cards, Dirk got to play against green-red and I met Kyle with white-blue. Unfortunately Dirk forgot one basic thing in Magic - to play lands - and lost his match rather quickly. I took game 1 from Kyle but had no chance in game 2 as his superior card quality stomped me. I lucked out game 3 after Kyle drew approximately 100 lands and an Aquamoeba, which wasn't quite good enough despite attacking for three starting with turn 3. Marco's opponent forced a third game but fell to the green-red beats and we took the match.  
 2-1

Next up was YourMoveGames.com (Chris DeRoche/Jacob Rabinowitz/Paul Rietzl). The whole match started with a deck check and frowns on both sides. Marco got a game loss for registering a swamp instead of the one plains he played and Dirk's opponent got a pretty random game loss that would be pretty difficult to explain, but, to me, it didn't seem like the penalty was fair. It turned out that it didn't matter at all as Dirk played the w-u mirror and while he played crappy cards, his opponent opened both games with turn 3 Shieldmage Advocate, turn 4 Battle Screech and followed that up with Ambassador Laquatus later on, a quite a good card to have in a w-u mirror. Marco mulliganed to five in the one game he played and his opponent showed him Psychatog, Concentrate, Demise, Fatigue and a 3/3 flier. That was that. I had won game 1 against my green-red opponent but he realized in game 2 that a turn 4 Phantom Centaur is pretty much an automatic win and so he played that guy on turn 4 in the third game as well and we got swept.  
 2-2

Alright, this seemed to be rather bad for us. 4-2 was the record we needed to advance to Day 2 and that meant we had to win out. We were in a rather bad mood when we sat down to build our final sealed decks. But when we saw the cards, we were in good spirits again. Our pool could easily be described as very, very good. I got a nearly monogreen deck again but this time I had Basking Rootwalla, Wild Mongrel, Call of the Herd, Beast Attack, 2 Elephant Ambush, Narcissism, Erhnam Djinn, Possessed Centaur and a splash for Aether Burst, Careful Study, Mental Note and the most insane card in green-blue, Wonder. Thanks to drafting green-blue a lot, I am quite experienced with that archetype and I told Dirk and Marco that in the following two rounds, only one of them had to win. Dirk ended up with white-blue again but this time he had 3-power fliers along with some bounce and generally good cards. Marco had the black-red removal deck again and he splashed for Deep Analysis and Balshan Collaborator. Blue in every deck is my definition of a good card pool.

From now on, it was do or die in order to advance to Day 2 and as our tiebreakers were absolutely horrible we had to go 5-0 on Day 2 to reach the Top 4 and then another 2-0 to repeat our win from last year. Marco joked that that meant we had only 9 rounds to go.

Satoshi Nakamura, Takashi Niwa and Kenta Suzuki of Team No Yaburon were blocking our way first. Our card pool was a lot better though and I won my match against Takashi in roughly 10 minutes while we spent most of that time shuffling. Her mana draws were less than optimal and in game 2 I had turn 1 Rootwalla, turn 2 Mongrel, turn 3 Avenger with a Wonder that I pitched to the Mongrel in the graveyard as well. Marco was down a game but came back to win the match before Dirk, who was up a game in his match, could even finish his 2nd game.  
 3-2. 1 down, 8 left.

Our final Day 1 opponents were Tired Lost Souls. We sat down with Illuminati (Mowshowitz/Gary/Shvartsman) in the Feature Match area with both teams who Top 4'ed last year needing a final round win to get to the draft tables on Day 2. Dirk, once again, had this bad habit of not playing lands and lost his match in a couple of minutes. Marco, on the other hand, demolished his opponent with his splashed Collaborator almost as fast and it came down to me. I had won game 1 against my green-red opponent and my curve in game 2 was turn 2 Bear, turn 3 Call, turn 4 flashback, turn 5 Beast Attack, turn 6 flashback, turn 7 Elephant Ambush and Phantom Tiger, turn 8 flashback the Ambush. At this point, most people would think it was over against green-red but somehow my opponent managed to stay in the game. When he attacked with Ember Beast and Phantom Centaur, Muscle Burst, Sudden Strength and Sonic Seizure all of a sudden wrecked my team and we were back in a stalemate. I drew Wonder but had to play it to have enough blockers. Eventually I drew my second Ambush, started to attack with the Wonder and when I drew my Possessed Centaur, I dropped it together with the Ernie I was holding for a few turns and he had a one turn window to draw a burn spell to finish me off. That didn't happen and we advanced to Day 2.  
 4-2, 2 down, 7 left.

The remaining seven matches are going to be in part 2 which should be up within the next week.
