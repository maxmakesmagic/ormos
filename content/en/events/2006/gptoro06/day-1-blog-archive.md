Hoaen is a sure fire bet to money at Limited Pro Tours - which he recently did in Prague - and won the last North American limited Grand Prix in Richmond. Hoaen has become something of a Limited guru and there is even talk of him flying out to Japan to mentor Kenji Tsumura in the finer points of Time Spiral draft in the days leading up to Kobe.</p>&#10;      <p>If he's good enough for the reigning Player of the Year …</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Powerful rares, I guess,&quot; chuckled Richie when I asked him what he looks for when opening a Sealed Deck. Other than a <a>Brightflame</a> the rare slots in his deck were quite unspectular - although that was not his main concern. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;The first thing I noticed was that there was only one double land which is pretty important in this format. The lands are much more important than the signets because they give you a free card.&quot; Still, he felt he could make something happen. &quot;It doesn't seem overwhelming but it looks like it has potential. While there are no good rares the green and the blue look deep althought there doesn't seem to be much else.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>His blue and green looked especially promising in Ravnica as he flipped past a number of solid cards - <a>Compulsive Research</a>, <a>Snapping Drake</a>, <a>Belltower Sphinx</a> -- but the boosters offered little to help out in either of those colors. It was obviously vexing for the Canadian who was building a Sealed deck for the first time in this format.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I have not played with <i>Dissension</i> in Sealed yet. They didn't have this format on the Beta - they offered double <i>Dissension</i>,&quot; laughed Hoaen as he double checked for mana fixers - maybe a <a>Vesper Ghoul</a> had slipped into the chaff pile. The only problem was that even if he had the good mana fixing there was barely anything he wanted to play.</p>&#10;      <p>I asked him about the X-spell sitting among his chaff and he made it clear that is where it was going to stay. &quot;I don't like <a>Brightflame</a> very much… never have…never will.&quot; </p>&#10;      <p>Right from the start he knew he would be running a blue-green base with splashes in as many as all three of the remaining colors - there was a <a>Faith's Fetters</a>, <a>Ogre Savant</a>, and an ample supply of black cards to choose from. While he only had one bounce land - a Golgari Rotfarm - he did have a handful of Signets, Terrion, and <a>Utopia Sprawl</a> to try and make it all happen. The key for Richie was to have his splashes accessorize an otherwise straightforward two color deck in as unobtrusive a fashion as possible. </p>&#10;      <p>Green-blue had some solid creatures - <a>Snapping Drake</a>, <a>Belltower Sphinx</a>, <a>Bramble Elemental</a>, <a>Ghor-Clan Savage</a>, etc. - to go along with some reasonable spells. It looked like he would certainly be splashing black for <a>Seal of Doom</a>, <a>Consult the Necrosages</a>, and possibly <a>Shambling Shell</a> and <a>Dimir Doppelganger</a>. Surprisingly he left <a>Brainspoil</a>, <a>Stinkweed Imp</a>, and <a>Darkblast</a> in his non-playable pile with little to zero consideration given to any of them. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I am just going to be splashing black,&quot; he explained as he gestured to the double black barrier in the upper right hand corner of the <a>Brainspoil</a>. &quot;<a>Stinkweed Imp</a> in only really good on turn three and…&quot; he made a dismissive gesture in the direction of the <a>Darkblast</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>With the base of the deck settled he looked for some marginal cards that could pad his base colors. He laid <a>Vedalken Plotter</a> with his three drops after a moment's consideration. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I guess Plotter is good in this format - now that people know the bounce lands are good.&quot; It was also made better by the fact that Richie - thanks to his three byes -- would not be playing until round four - when all the good decks would certainly have plenty of Karoo-iffic targets. </p>&#10;      <p>In the end he left the red <a>Ogre Savant</a> riding the pine and went blue-green with a smidgeon on black and just a whiff of white for the <a>Faith's Fetters</a>. He went right up to the time limit trying to figure out his mana - <a>Utopia Sprawl</a> was giving him fits. If he could play the Wild Growth update it would be his most flexible fixer but with two of his other fixers - signet and the Rotfarm - in green he wanted to shave a couple of <a>Forest</a>s.          </div>&#10;            </div>&#10;            <br>&#10;          </div>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Richie's thought that the deck would be more than enough to get him over the Day One hump to the drafting he prefers to be doing. &quot;I will be pretty disappointed if I take more than one loss. Come to think of it I am pretty disappointed whenever I take any loss.&quot;</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, June 3: 1:18 p.m. - Foreign Relations</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Coming into this weekend I was certainly jealous of my counterparts at the other GPs who were going to have an opportunity to cover the players in Kuala Lumpur and Torino. Don't get me wrong; there is no shortage of top notch talent around here from Rich Hoaen to Antonino DeRosa to Mark Herberholz to Billy Moreno. It is just that I cover these guys all the time when I do a domestic GP. While I was looking forward to the short trip (more on that later) I was craving a little bit of strange. </p>&#10;      <p>I was pretty happy when I saw Julien Nuijten, Frank Karsten, Jelger Wiergesma, and Rogier Mattien queuing up last night for an early registration. Things were definitely looking up although it was not quite on the same par with seeing the Japanese stroll through the door in Seattle. (I wish I could say &quot;for the first time&quot; but I missed their exciting debut in Boston) The Dutch have made a habit of traveling to North American GPs in the last year. </p>&#10;      <p>I was able to capture the buzz I was seeking when none other than Anton Jonsson strolled up behind them along with Johan Sedegphour. This was the first time either of these Swedish superstars had traveled to a North American event. Considering both players' prowess with 40-card builds it was even more exciting - although it was 60-card decks that brought them across the Atlantic. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Me and Johan are teaming with Rich Hoaen for the team Pro Tour,&quot; explained Anton Jonsson. &quot;He wanted us to come here. After this weekend we are going to Eric Froelich's house to test for the PT. It makes sense because it doesn't cost us much more - and its fun to try an American GP for a change.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;North American GP!&quot; corrected Hoaen.</p>&#10;      <p>While Anton was hoping to find some block Constructed tech in the coming weeks he already had a little trickery up his sleeve for this event.
