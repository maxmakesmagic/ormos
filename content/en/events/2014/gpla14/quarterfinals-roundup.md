These are the players who battled through 15 rounds of Swiss to end up on top of the Standings. They have earned a qualification to Pro Tour Fate Reforged, but now it's time for them to turn their attention towards the trophy. Here's a brief rundown of what happened in the quarters.

Tamada Ryoichi (Abzan Aggro) - Denis Ulanov (Rabble Red)

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Tamada.jpg)

*Tamada Ryoichi* 

  

In game one, Ulanov played [Foundry Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foundry+Street+Denizen) on turn one, [Foundry Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foundry+Street+Denizen) and [Firedrinker Satyr](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firedrinker+Satyr) on turn two, and [Goblin Rabblemaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rabblemaster) on turn three. With his back up the wall against such an amazing start, Tamada tried to claw back, but was unable to do so. "He plays 11 tap-lands, which really hurt him that game," Ulanov commented afterwards. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Ulanov.jpg)

*Denis Ulanov* 

  

Game two was all about mulligans. Ulanov had a tough call when he looked at four land and three spells. [Flood](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flood)ing out is a real risk with such a hand, but Ulanov still kept because his deck doesn't contain that many lands. Tamada, meanwhile, kept a hand with two [Temple of Silence](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temple+of+Silence) and two [Drown in Sorrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drown+in+Sorrow), hoping that there would be a third land near the top of his deck somewhere. The way it worked out was that Ulanov drew a few spells and curved out nicely, while fortune did not shine on Tamada: He was unable to find a third land in time, and fell to an onslaught of small red creatures. 

**Denis Ulanov defeats Tamada Ryoichi 2-0!** 

**Christopher Goldsmith (Abzan Midrange) - Daniel Scheid (GR Monsters)** 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Scheid.jpg)

*Daniel Scheid* 

  

Game one was over in lightning quick fashion. Scheid ramped into a turn-three [Xenagos, the Reveler](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Xenagos%2C+the+Reveler) on the play and followed it up with [Sarkhan, the Dragonspeaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sarkhan%2C+the+Dragonspeaker). "Too much heat," Goldsmith remarked as he packed up his cards. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Goldsmith.jpg)

*Christopher Goldsmith* 

  

In game two, both players had [Sylvan Caryatid](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sylvan+Caryatid) and [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix) early on, so it turned into a lengthy mid-range slugfest. Realizing that flyers are good against Courser and Caryatid, Scheid took to the skies with [Stormbreath Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stormbreath+Dragon) and [Sarkhan, the Dragonspeaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sarkhan%2C+the+Dragonspeaker), but Goldsmith would have none of that and promptly took them out with [Silence the Believers](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silence+the+Believers) and [Abzan Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abzan+Charm). Afterwards, more haymakers came down as players smashed big spell after big spell into each other, but after the dust settled, Scheid had [Xenagos, the Reveler](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Xenagos%2C+the+Reveler) and [Hornet Queen](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hornet+Queen) left standing, and eventually cast Crater's Claws for the win. 

**Daniel Scheid defeats Christopher Goldsmith 2-0!** 

**Brad Nelson (Mardu Midrange) - Carlo Falcis (Abzan Midrange)** 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Falcis.jpg)

*Carlo Falcis* 

  

The first two games were quick. In game one, Falcis had a great draw, curving creatures into [Wingmate Roc](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingmate+Roc), and Nelson was unable to answer them. In game two, Falcis had to mulligan down to five and didn't stand a chance, especially since Nelson killed off all of Falcis' creatures right away to prevent a [Wingmate Roc](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingmate+Roc) trigger. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Brad%20Nelson.jpg)

*Brad Nelson* 

  

The third game was closer, with the two players trading resources one-for-one for a while and going toe-to-toe. The key moment, as Falcis told me afterwards, was that he was able to take out both of Nelson's [End Hostilities](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=End+Hostilities) with back-to-back [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize). As a result, Falcis no longer had to worry about a sweeper, and he was able to close out the game with Rakasha Deathdealer and [Wingmate Roc](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wingmate+Roc). 

**Carlo Falcis defeats Brad Nelson 2-1!** 

**Eric Pei (Boss Sligh) - Isaac Sears (Abzan Aggro)** 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Sears.jpg)

*Isaac Sears* 

  

Early on in game one, Pei seemed to have the upper hand as he amassed an army of red weenies with [Akroan Crusader](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Akroan+Crusader) and [Hordeling Outburst](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hordeling+Outburst). Sears had bigger creatures, but they were far fewer in number, and it looked like he wouldn't be able to line up enough blockers. And then [Sorin, Solemn Visitor](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sorin%2C+Solemn+Visitor) came down. In a single attack, Sears gained 25 life, and turned around the game completely. Although it took a while for him to actually win, the game was single-handedly decided by Sorin. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpla14/QF%20-%20Pei.jpg)

*Eric Pei* 

  

Games two and three were anticlimactic. Pei's deck, which is filled with cheap one-drops, can operate perfectly very well off of just two lands. Sears' deck, however, does not. After sideboard in particular, Abzan relies heavily on [Drown in Sorrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20141019000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drown+in+Sorrow) to contain the red swarm, and Sears was unable to find a third land in both games. 

**Eric Pei defeats Isaac Sears 2-0!**
