The Daily *Magic* Update is a roundup of everything *Magic* you should know on May 17, 2016. Today's Update is brought to you by Modern, the format de weekend at [Grand Prix Los Angeles](/en/events/2016/gpla16/fact-sheet-grand-prix-los-angeles-2016) (I'll be there!) and [Grand Prix Charlotte](/en/events/2016/gpcha16/grand-prix-charlotte-2016) (I won't be there!).

# Today's Must

1.  [How About That Modern?](http://www.starcitygames.com/article/32932_How-About-That-Modern.html) | StarCityGames.com | Ross Merriam

With not one but two Modern Grand Prix this weekend (Los Angeles and Charlotte, natch), a lot of writers are turning their attention to the most modern of non-rotating formats. In this piece, Ross Merriam attempts to piece together the craziness that is Modern right now, where up is down, top is bottom, and somehow [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) isn't the best card in the format. I don't get it, either.

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Modern Death and Taxes](/en/events/2016/modern-constructed-league-2016-05-16/modern-constructed-league)

Let's stick with Modern here and check out a deck that's definitely flying under the radar, but one that I know I've lost to plenty enough to respect it—Death and Taxes. Long a Legacy staple, it's an archetype that uses disruptive creatures to keep opponents on the back foot long enough to kill them with said creatures. [Thalia, Guardian of Thraben](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thalia%2C+Guardian+of+Thraben) is a mainstay of the archetype, as is [Flickerwisp](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flickerwisp). Beyond that, [Tidehollow Sculler](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tidehollow+Sculler) is discard on a 2/2 body, and [Leonin Arbiter](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Leonin+Arbiter) punishes fetch lands—which basically everyone else plays.

But the real twist to this deck is the Eldrazi component. Four [Eldrazi Temple](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eldrazi+Temple)s help to power out [Wasteland Strangler](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wasteland+Strangler), [Thought-Knot Seer](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thought-Knot+Seer), and [Eldrazi Displacer](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eldrazi+Displacer), and add a ton of synergy to the deck. Imaging casting [Tidehollow Sculler](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tidehollow+Sculler) on turn two and following it up with a [Wasteland Strangler](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wasteland+Strangler) to kill a creature (binning the card you exile with [Tidehollow Sculler](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tidehollow+Sculler)), and then casting [Flickerwisp](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flickerwisp) on the Sculler to take another card? Or on the Strangler after casting another Sculler?

Now picture doing all of that repeatedly with [Eldrazi Displacer](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eldrazi+Displacer).

Add in [Æther Vial](https://web.archive.org/web/20160517000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86ther+Vial) to power out multiple disruptive creatures per turn, and you have a pretty scary deck ready to annoy opponents everywhere.
