Sometimes, it's a tough rap being one of the best in the game. Masticore, a creature that would get picked ahead of virtually any creature in the game, got demolished in last week's poll. Masticore is an awesome creature, as readers pointed out about a hundred times, fully capably of dominating a board and singlehandedly winning a game. So why did it get crushed so badly in the presence of Morphling? Readers cited the fact that Masticore has a very serious downside as a major weakness of the card. While some would argue that Morphling has drawbacks as well (in that if you don't keep mana open, it isn't exactly impressive), Masticore's penalty is very in-your-face. Losing that card every turn can be compensated for, but it definitely hurts. And it hurts in a very palpable way. If your opponent manages to destroy your Morphling, he or she has probably either broken even or given up card disadvantage to do it. With Masticore, it is much easier to break even or get ahead, thanks to the upkeep. Additionally, readers factored in that you must play a Masticore-centric game once it's in play, due both to the discard and the incredible mana investment needed to make it run, while Morphling is more flexible. 

All right, Masticore fans, you're getting a second chance in this week's poll. I received many emails that spoke with yearning for the time period right after the bannings of combo winter, when decks like Angry Hermit ruled the day. So, Masticore gets its second chance to become an All-Time MVP. Its competition, though not Morphling-level, is still formidable. Deranged Hermit was one of the most-feared cards of the day, with Gaea's Cradle making its somewhat prohibitive echo cost a lot more affordable. And, as Mark Rosewater is fond of pointing out, people like squirrels. So expect the Hermit to snag some votes on that alone. Last up is Phyrexian Negator. Though it mainly came from the sideboard to combat combo decks like Yawgmoth's Bargain and control decks like accelerate blue, it was every bit as feared. Often, those decks could not survive a turn one Dark Ritual/Negator. Which will it be? Vote for your favorite at the end of the article.

…And I can do anything. So goes the R.E.M. song, anyway. It's no accident that Morphling's nickname is “Superman”. More so than any other creature in the game, Morphling is Superman. With unprecedented mutability of its already decent 3/3 body, able to give itself +1/-1 or -1/+1, the Morphling is able to be a potent 5/1 attacker or a 3/X monster blocker. If this is all Morphling did for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif), it would be better than many comparable creatures, but this ability is just scratching the surface of what makes it great. In addition, Morphling has a built-in Jump mechanic, meaning that it can take that that 5/1 body to the air, making it a very dangerous attacker. The Jump also allows Morphling to dodge selective mass removal spells, such as Earthquake and, usually, Hurricane. On top of that, Morphling can untap at will, multiple times, meaning that fun decks could put a good tap enchantment on it (like Hermetic Study) for kicks, while the rest of us could always untap our monstrous creature to do some blocking duty, even as it was scoring the lethal attacks. ON TOP OF THAT, Morphling could also become untargetable, ensuring that he didn't get bumped off by random good cards like Swords to Plowshares or a heaping pile of burn spells. Now, these are all fairly powerful abilities on their own. When you consider that each of them only costs ![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) or ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif) to activate and all came on the same card, it's hard to beat that.

These abilities, taken together, give Morphling an amount of power and flexibility rarely seen on a card. You have a 5/1 flying attacker. You have a 3/X blocker. You have a creature that can do double duty, attacking and blocking on the same turn as one of the best attackers in the game and one of the best blockers in the game. You have a creature that can dodge most conventional removal spells which, incidentally, is blue, meaning that there are plenty of counterspells to fall back on when Wrath of God or Diabolic Edict shows up to put the nix on Mr. Superman. It's the complete package. It's your offense and your defense. It has evasion and is untargetable. All that for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif). Think about that next time you see an Air Elemental. 

One of the little fun things about Morphling is the picture. The two identical creatures fit the flavor of its shapeshifter type, but which one of these guys is the Morphling and which one is the creature it is copying? Take a look at the picture:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/81/04/81041084416a9f33b05dfe4dc3e72b4a1e587fc44ef09fd21a17c47de7d89782.jpg)

Give up? Well, it's the one with the tail, of course! As such, we may never know what a Morphling actually looks like. What a shame…

### When It Was Legal…

Ironically, when Morphling was introduced, it was completely unplayable. For months, the tournament scene across all formats was dominated by the incredibly broken cards of the Saga Block (Artifacts Cycle). In those days, you could pull off turn 2 (and occasionally turn 1) wins in Standard, something that had never been possible before in the format. Tolarian Academy was followed by Memory Jar, which was followed by Yawgmoth's Bargain and then Replenish. In between were about five other amazing decks. Finally, with many, many bannings, the formats returned to something akin to normalcy. When the combo cards from the Saga Block were banned, the almost explicit power of the creatures came to light. Masticore, Morphling, Phyrexian Negator, Rofellos, Llanowar Emissary, Priest of Titania, Academy Rector and others began their domination of Standard. But Morphling was destined to move into more powerful formats. Its first foray was in Extended, where it became the lynch pin in Bob Maher's powerful Maher Oath deck.

Bob's deck has a lot of action going on between Enlightened Tutor search, Oaths, Sylvan Library/Abundance, blue search and countermagic. But when it comes time to end the game, it's a Morphling he wants to Oath up. Oath decks are excellent showcases of the most powerful creatures in the environment. Because Oathing a creature out is free, you can concentrate on playing the best creatures for the format. In this Extended metagame, Spike Feeder was important against Sligh-style decks, Spike Weaver was strong against creature assaults, like those from Hatred-based decks and Countersliver. But the best finisher was definitely the Morphling. With it being free, Maher always had plenty of mana to power it up and, if the unfortunate did happen, Oath ensured that the Morphling would always be right back for round 2.

Accelerated blue, which rose to popularity when *Mercadian Masques* rotated the *Tempest* Block out of Standard, used Morphling to great effect. Utilizing Grim Monolith to full potence (as Mana Vault had been used in years past), this deck could rush out any number of powerful threats, from Masticore to Palinchron. Imagine blue with Dark Rituals – accelerated blue is as close to that as it will ever be. Able to cast six-mana spells on turn three, with cycling, Powder Kegs and countermagic to help with sloppy draws, Morphling and Masticore could debut early, dominate the board and win before an opponent could even set up his or her game plan. Treachery was another powerhouse in this deck, often stealing an opposing Masticore or other big-gun creature. The overall strategy allowed accelerated blue to muscle past other decks; something blue is characteristically not that good at doing. Morphling was the key player in the deck. While Masticore could control the board and sometimes Palinchron and Treachery would just win, it was Morphling who could always be counted on to save the day.

### Why Was It Great?

Morphling was, in one of the most powerful and competitive environments, the cream of the crop. To compete in a metagame with a creature that could gun down virtually anything with enough mana, with 5/5 tramplers that were attacking on turn two and cute little guys who could go get powerful combo cards, not to mention the combo decks that pervaded the game, took something special. Morphling was definitely special. Over the next couple of years, Morphling made formerly powerful creatures seem completely obsolete in every format. In Type I, where people used to use Serra Angels and Serendib Efreets as their high-end attackers, Morphling has taken over as the premiere high-end finisher. Virtually any time you are looking at an environment with Morphling legal, if you want a large, flying way to end the game, you aren't looking any further than Superman. Compare Morphling to the average five casting cost flier. There are the classics: Air Elemental, Sengir Vampire and Serra Angel. None of these have abilities anywhere near the power level of Morphling. Nor do any of the other five casting cost fliers since. And it's no trick to understand why – there simply is no reason for a card to be so powerful. 

> *“There was no creature in **Magic** that had as great an impact on all of the tournament formats as Morphling did."* -John Tizon>

### The Big Picture

**Morphling** has no true successor – it is an immortal king of **Magic**. It is unlikely that we will ever see a genuine reinterpretation of Morphling that is anywhere near the same power level. The strength of the card is such that a straight reprint of it would throw everything out of whack. It is on a level that **Magic** cards rarely attain – and, many people feel, shouldn't attain at all. The closest thing we've seen to Morphling in recent days was the *Scourge* enchantment Pemmin's Aura. The Aura takes a creature of your choice and turns it into a Morphling. This can be unimpressive, in the case of small creatures, or very, very imposing. If you've ever seen someone Aura up an Exalted Angel, you'll quickly learn how severely Morphling was “handicapped” by its 3/3 stature. A 4/5 is a much, much bigger Morphling. Of course, Pemmin's Aura is an anagram of “I am Superman”, an obvious homage to its inspiration.

There's a reason that new players gawk the first time they see a Morphling. There has been nothing like it in years. No creature is so complete a package that only a few cards can stop it. Because of this, Morphling creates all sorts of problems. Any metagame has to be prepared to beat Morphlings, which involves either global sweeper cards, sacrifice cards or dramatic beatdown. Modern Standard decks (with the possible exception of Astral Slide, which has the Angel-Slide combo and monoblack control, with access to Innocent Bloods and Chainer's Edicts) would be hard-pressed to come up with good answers to Morphling played in the right deck.
