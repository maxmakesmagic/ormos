**The Players**

This has been an excellent couple of weeks for Adrian Sullivan. After falling just short of making the Top 8 at the StarCityGames Invitational this past weekend, Sullivan shot out to a perfect 9-0 record on Day 1 here in Chicago. Things were dicey to open the day, as Sullivan lost two of the first rounds he played today before eventually rallying to make it into the Top 8. Standing in the way of Sullivan's shot at redemption is Steve Wise of Omaha, NE. Wise has a Grand Prix Top 16 on his resume in the past, and he was the last undefeated player here at Grand Prix Chicago.

**The Decks**

Both Sullivan and Wise are running the WU Control deck that has supplanted Esper Control as the dominant control deck in Standard. It is a pure control deck, relying on blue counterspells to keep things from hitting the board and white to clear away the permanents that have. Between [Supreme Verdict](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Supreme+Verdict), [Detention Sphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Detention+Sphere), and a slew of other potential removal spells, WU Control is able to buy enough time for cards like [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) to find the Planeswalker threats: [Jace, Architect of Thought](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Architect+of+Thought), and [Elspeth, Sun's Champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elspeth%2C+Sun%27s+Champion). For those long mirror matches, such as this one, [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality) works especially hard, giving the deck that is able to activate it inevitability in this grinding matchup.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpchi14/qf_game2.jpg)

 

The players have opted for a few key distinctions. Wise opted for Font of Fortuntes where Sullivan chose [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination). Sullivan's list seems especially good in this mirror match, with [Planar Cleansing](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Planar+Cleansing) in the maindeck for dealing with those pesky Planeswalkers and a more varied suite of cards to deal with opposing threats.

 

**The Games**

The first game of this match was significantly quicker than expected, as Wise jumped out to an early advantage with [Jace, Architect of Thought](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Architect+of+Thought). While he was using his powerful Planeswalker to draw cards, Sullivan was trying to make up ground with [Divination](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Divination), which slowly put him behind. Wise's advantage spiraled out of control when Jace revealed [Ætherling](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86therling) and [Elspeth, Sun's Champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elspeth%2C+Sun%27s+Champion). Wise's weapon of choice was the Planeswalker of legend, and Elspeth and her troops made short work of Sullivan in a fairly uneventful first game.

Almost as if to make up for the relative disappointment of the first game, the second game showcased the true awesomeness of a WU mirror match. Sullivan spent the early turns of the game under assault by [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) and a [Fiendslayer Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiendslayer+Paladin) from Wise's sideboard. Matters appeared worse when Wise was also the first to resolve and activate [Jace, Architect of Thought](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Architect+of+Thought). This kept his hand full of permission, which he used to prevent Sullivan from drawing cards with [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation). The Jace also provided Wise with an Elspeth. The game appeared set up to end just as the last one had, with Wise protected behind a wall of planeswalking allies.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpchi14/qf_wise.jpg)

*Steve Wise*

 

And then the game turned with an innocuous flip of a card. Sullivan resolved a [Jace, Memory Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Memory+Adept), and used him to mill the top card of Wise's deck, knocking out the one [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality) he had at his disposal. From this point on, still relatively early into the game, Sullivan committed to using every resource at his disposal to stay alive long enough to deck Wise. Wise began to attack Jace with [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s and [Fiendslayer Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fiendslayer+Paladin), but Sullivan had [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s of his own to block. While Sullivan was trying his best to defend, Wise was continuing to draw the best cards in his deck, cashing in [Jace, Architect of Thought](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Architect+of+Thought), for a pair of [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation)s that Sullivan was more than happy for him to have. Sullivan kept his own hand filled with [Opportunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opportunity), managing to stay one step ahead of Wise's offense.

 

Wise was perilously close to finishing the game out, winning the game 41-6 thanks to his repeated attacks with [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s and the Paladin. When he made the move to close things out with an [Archangel of Thune](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Archangel+of+Thune) and [Elspeth, Sun's Champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elspeth%2C+Sun%27s+Champion), Sullivan made his move. Wise went for the kill with a [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) that would have lethally pumped his team, but Sullivan won the counter war that eventually tapped Wise out. This let Sullivan, down to 3, to untap and cast [Planar Cleansing](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Planar+Cleansing), completely resetting the board.

With the board clear, Sullivan managed to start gaining the lead with a [Jace, Architect of Thought](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Architect+of+Thought). Jace's card advantage allowed Sullivan to drain Wise's hand before refilling his deck with his own [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality). Wise was effectively finished. He tried to find something to finish things off, but his [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) only hastened his demise. With only four cards in his deck, none of which were actually capable of winning the game, Wise conceded.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gpchi14/qf_sullivan.jpg)

*Adrian Sullivan*

 

For the last game, Wise was forced to mulligan a mana-light hand. Still, he landed a first-turn [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality), clearly not going to lose for the same reason as in the previous game. He also held an excellent way to reverse his mulligan, with two copies of [Font of Fortunes](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Font+of+Fortunes) and a [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation) in his opening hand. This game played out in true control-mirror fashion, with players playing lands and slinging [Sphinx's Revelation](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx%27s+Revelation)s back and forth until they had built up a reasonable advantage. Wise was the first on the board, using [Elspeth, Sun's Champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elspeth%2C+Sun%27s+Champion), to begin making an army. After taking one hit from her Soldiers, Sullivan used [Quicken](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Quicken) to cast an end-of-turn [Planar Cleansing](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Planar+Cleansing). A small counter war ensued, and Sullivan came out the victor. Soon thereafter, he resolved his own Planeswalker: [Jace, Architect of Thought](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Architect+of+Thought).

 

Jace gave Sullivan the choice of his finisher, and he selected a single [Ætherling](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86therling) over the Elspeth and Verdict he was offered alongside it. A few turns later, he found a reasonable spot to cast the [Ætherling](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86therling), and the game ended more or less on the spot. Wise tried to cast his own [Ætherling](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=%C3%86therling) a couple turns later to at least race, but a [Syncopate](https://web.archive.org/web/20140623000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Syncopate) countered it, handing Sullivan a ticket to the Semifinals.

**Adrian Sullivan defeated Steve Wise 2-1**
