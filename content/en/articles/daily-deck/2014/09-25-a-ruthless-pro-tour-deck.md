*Khans of Tarkir* is coming! Three-color wedges are about to make their way into Standard and every other Constructed format, but by no means will they be new to tournament *Magic*. This week, we will look at Pro Tour–winning decks that have been based in one of the wedge clans from *Khans of Tarkir*.

Today's deck is from Justin Gary, who won [Pro Tour Houston in 2002](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Article.aspx?x=sideboard/events/pthou02). If you recognize Justin's name, it is probably because he is on the Pro Tour Hall of Fame ballot or because you noticed the designer credit on [Spirit Bonds](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spirit+Bonds) from *Magic 2015*.

Justin's deck is based around one of the most powerful cards ever, [Oath of Druids](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oath+of+Druids). If your opponent so much as decided to play a creature, Justin's battlefield might shortly contain an [Oath of Druids](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oath+of+Druids) and a [Cognivore](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cognivore), backed by a huge graveyard. If the opponent was not playing creatures, Justin's deck played like a classic blue deck full of counterspells and card drawing. In fact, the only removal spell in Justin's main deck was [Pernicious Deed](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pernicious+Deed), because [Oath of Druids](https://web.archive.org/web/20140925000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oath+of+Druids) made it such a bad idea to play creatures.
