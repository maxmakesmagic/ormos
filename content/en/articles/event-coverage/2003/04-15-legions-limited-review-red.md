This is the fourth installment in my series analyzing the cards of *Legions* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the third installment [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/2003/04-08-legions-limited-review-green).

### Red

Red sucks! Okay, that’s not entirely true. You still get two packs of Sparksmiths and Shocks and Skills and Solar Blasts and and and… but now that the third pack doesn’t provide more of the same, red brings with it some dashed expectations. Namely, there isn’t enough to go around anymore. Not if everyone’s trying to play it.

There are good red cards in *Legions*: Skirk Marauder is among the top five commons in the set, there are multiple cards with the word “dragon” in the creature type slot, not to mention other powerful rares, a couple of cool uncommons and a “kill spell” like Crested Craghorn in the common slots, but the sheer number of players who will go red because of the enticements *Onslaught* offers just means that in Legions, your other color had better be plentiful.

Of course, there are still benefits. Red partners very well with any of the other four colors, as long as you draft with the understanding it should be looked upon as the support to your other color’s base. And of course, by starting down that road, you do still have two shots at the *Onslaught* goods. Just understand that you’ll want to have most of your cards in hand 33 picks into the draft; if you’re rely on red after that, you’ll be duelling with scraps.

**Imperial Hellkite**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not a lot to argue about when it comes to 6/6 flyers for seven with additional abilities. The Hellkite is big. It’s big enough to get past Needleshot Gourna, big enough to survive any non-banish effect creature kill the environment can provide and big enough to finish your opponent off rather quickly. There aren’t a lot of non-rare cards in the format I’d consider taking over it that don’t start with *T* and end with *imberwatch Elf*.

**Rockshard Elemental**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It’s always frustrated me when a new keyword ability is introduced with as small a sampling as double strike has been, but the Elemental makes it pretty obvious why that’s necessary. Nearly impossible to stop once face-up in play, the Elemental brings with it a lack of usual drawbacks in high-cost bombs in that it can come into play on turn three. Especially good with creature boosters that will affect both damage steps.

**Lavaborn Muse**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 At 3/3 for four mana, the Muse is going to be better than solid anyways, but the ability kicks in an awful lot of the time, either by hastening the damage race or forcing your opponent to hold cards in hand while you play yours. Either way, it’s going to be really difficult for your opponent to keep up.

**Killmouth Dragon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I know it says Dragon, but unless you have a few of them, it’s a 5/5 for eight mana and while yes, it does fly, the casting cost is a lot of drawback for a generic 5/5 flyer. That’s not to say it isn’t powerful, just know that you’re going to end up holding it in your hand at times when another dork would do the trick.

**Goblin Goon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Simple math really: 6 power + 6 toughness = a lot of body for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif). A ridiculous amount actually. Yes, the Goon requires a certain amount of resource management to be truly effective and can prove a bit of a dud at times when you’re drawing first, but if you’re playing first, this thing is the wrecking ball and almost impossible to come back from.

**Gempalm Incinerator**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Is there any better feeling in this environment than killing a Sparksmith on your opponent’s turn two by cycling the Incinerator? Yes, there are situations in which you’ll be forced to cycle with no effect or worse, play the Incinerator as a bad Nosy Goblin, but more often than not, it will read “kill a small creature, draw a card”. I’d play that for two mana.

**Skirk Marauder**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The number four common in the format (after Timberwatch Elf, Echo Tracer and Skinthinner), the Marauder gives you just about everything you could want in a weenie: low casting cost, potential for card advantage, creature kill, its go it all. Sure, as a 2/1, it’s especially susceptible to creature kill, but once it’s 2/1, it has already done its job. Especially nice with Gempalm Incinerator or Sparksmith.

**Clickslither**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not quite as good as it is in Constructed, it’s not for a lack of trying. The realities of Limited play’s limited card pools is that any card with three colored mana in the casting cost is going to be difficult to summon and, at the same time, you can’t be too picky about the creatures you use, meaning you may not get all the goblins you’d like in order to optimize this thing. Of course, I’m just nitpicking. It’s still a very good card.

**Goblin Dynamo**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Dynamo or Marauder? I’ve been asked that question more than once, and while the Dynamo is a really good late game card, it costs seven mana and needs to stick around a turn before becoming a threat. Yes, once that happens, your opponent will be hard-pressed to win at times, but the same can be said for a lot of seven casting cost cards and the Marauder is too strong too early to be considered the weaker of the two.

**Frenetic Raptor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I like this card a lot, not only because it’s big and bad, but also because its casting cost makes it potentially splashable, especially in those decks lacking beasts. At 6/6 for ![5 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/33/3c/333cbd82f68b5e306003b2955e517a94ffa7a3ea03087365c10afad540856dce.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), you’re obviously getting a pretty solid deal if your deck is looking for attackers, but be careful: you probably won’t want to be rating it this high if you have a bunch of beasties already.

**Crested Craghorn**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I’ve heard this referred to as *Legions’s* answer to Pinpoint Avalanche, but I don’t think it compares favorably. First strike, any kind of burn or a ping effect completely neutralize the Craghorn, not to mention Whipcorder, Choking Tethers, Whipgrass Entangler… the list goes on. That said, the Craghorn occasionally can hit more than once and removes in a set that doesn’t remove, so you’ve got to at least give it some respect.

**Magma Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As with Lavaborn Muse, the first thing you’re getting here is a non-green 3/3 for four, a pretty big deal in this format. Unfortunately, the Sliver’s is a combat-oriented ability that requires it to be untapped to use, meaning more often than not it won’t come into play, seeing as to how red’s an aggressive color. If you’re blue-red though, thanks largely to illusions, this guy’s value will go up a fair bit.

**Goblin Clearcutter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The only reason it’s this low on the list is because you don’t always play green. When you do, it moves way up, giving you the mana boost you need to activate Invokers while casting other spells that same turn, get out fatties three turns before your opponent and muster too much power for your opponent to deal with. When that’s all done, it’s still a 3/3 for 4. Pretty good deal.

**Skirk Outrider**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Obviously the value of this card is conditional upon your deck. It’s ranked here for those decks where you’ll have them in abundance, with it not receiving a higher placement because just having beasts in your deck isn’t always enough. If you’re sporting a deck without beasts, it’s completely unplayable.

**Flamewave Invoker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 One of the reasons Sparksmith got even better with *Legions* is the abundance of playable goblins the set provides. The Invoker is one such goblin, but it’s far from being just an extra guy for your ‘Smith to be effective. If you can get the game to the late stages, you win. That’s how the card reads. Seems like a pretty good deal considering it can also function as a simple 2/2 for three in the early goings.

**Ridgetop Raptor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 My feelings on this little guy are mixed. The pluses are the fact he stops morphs cold, does some pretty sick things with cards that pump a creature’s power and hits pretty hard for a four-casting cost creature. The minuses are the fact it only carries with it a toughness of one, which makes it WAY too fragile for my tastes. I’m not a huge fan of 2/1s for two for the same reason and when you double that investment, it makes for a pretty tough pill to swallow. Use your judgment. If you have pumpers, take it a bit higher. If you don’t, a little lower.

**Skirk Alarmist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Each set brings with it one quirky rare card that I attach myself to and this is my pick for *Legions*. Not only does the Alarmist come down turn two for a color with very few quality two-drops, but it can do some really obscene things with otherwise-mediocre cards. 

Voidmage Apprentice

and Bloodstoke Howler come to mind off the top of my head, and I haven’t even mentioned how ridiculous it can be with

Echo Tracer

**Blade Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Almost everyone else would put the Sliver ahead of

Skirk Alarmist

, and with good reason. 3/2 for three is good deal in and of itself: throw in the fact you can draft other slivers and illusions with which you can share the bonus and you have a nice, offensive card. Just be careful when playing it later on against blue decks. The help you’re giving opposing slivers can really come back to haunt you.

**Unstable Hulk**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Nine times out of ten, this is a 2/2 for three generic mana. The rest of the time though, it can be a game winner, throwing off opposing calculations enough to win you the game. That said, you have to be very careful with it: it doesn’t receive the de-morphing bonus until the ability resolves from the stack, meaning your opponent can kill it while it’s 2/2 and still get their free turn. Try to save it until they’re tapped out.

**Skirk Drill Sergeant**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 See? 2/1 for two mana and it even has a special ability, yet it only rates this high. Yes, the Drill Sergeant is playable, but its ability is seldom beneficiary, often asking three mana from you to give your opponent some information. In other words, this is a two-power creature for two mana and that’s all it brings to the table, meaning the only times it will be good are on turn two or on turn three after casting Sparksmith.

**Shaleskin Plower**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As far a morphs go, there are plenty that are better. The Plower’s morph cost is excessive to say the least, with an ability that, later in the game, has virtually no effect and a body that won’t survive morph-on-morph combat. That said, its land destruction ability can prove far more valuable in Sealed Deck, where mana bases are shakier than in draft and it does capably fill the four mana slot in a tribe that has trouble doing so. You can do better, but it’s often at least playable.

**Macetail Hystrodon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Possessed of the same problems as Hundroog (namely too much mana to cast or too much mana to cycle), the Hystrodon can nevertheless hit pretty hard if you actually succeed in getting it into play. 4/4 first strikers have been tough to stop since draft began. Don’t see why anything should change now.

**Warbreak Trumpeter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The poor man’s Broodhatch Nantuko, the Trumpeter can leave a bunch of guys in its wake once morph-on-morph combat has taken care of the host body, but it often takes a long time for that ability to manifest itself. It provides a body for Sparksmith though and can always be that sacrificial first morph, so it’s far from unplayable.

**Goblin Grappler**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Virtually unplayable on its own merits, the Goblin and provoke elements of its person make it maindeck-able, provided that you have the right cards to make it strong. Sparksmith, Crown of Fury, Improvised Armor… these cards can make the Grappler good. Without them though, he’s anything but.

**Bloodstoke Howler**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Never a must-have, the Howler is playable by virtue of its “off-color morph” status, but there are other little things that make it better than some others. Its status as a beast can help with amplify and other tribal effects, its casting cost makes it a great Erratic Explosion result. I wouldn’t expect to flip it too often, but you could do worse.

**Hunter Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A lot like Goblin Grappler in its benefits and drawbacks, the Sliver brings with it the additional pluses and minuses of sliverdom, namely, your other slivers get provoke, but so do your opponent’s. This should usually work in your favor if you have others as they’ll get to attack with the newfound ability, but again, unless you have augmentation, this guy should stay in the board.

**Goblin Lookout**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 You need a lot of goblins to have this ability prove useful, and even then you’re likely trading two of yours for one of theirs. I wouldn’t bother trying.

**Goblin Firebug**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Two mana 2/2s are great, but the whole reason is because of their ability to get you tempo advantage, precisely what you’ll lose when the ‘Bug dies. It’s just not playable at any time.
