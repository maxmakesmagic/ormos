### White

![](https://i.ormos.uk/72/0d/720dd4136eed65a758395a04ab83b84b706f7c92e356c1d27c343344cd877a01.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/36/c9/36c98249db09f470bc069fe123e73c4ba54a32b69f2b2fb393d3a017a455a7ad.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/55/ae/55ae4d4b8e47cce38d47cfe537f4cd748acaafbe30fbad2e6f8f8d8b08dd8103.jpg)

One of Rob Alexander’s early concepts was of an expanse of salt flats. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a5/b5/a5b5c0e5281d347967dd2664323b7406c8ba0ba9e044da839656ab3332c6036e.jpg)

Score. I loved the idea, as did Cranford, and we ran with it. It was textural, and stark, but still seemed ‘white.’ We added the idea that these salt plains were bi-leveled, and that White-alligned peoples would seek refuge from the blowing salt storms under the surface (remember, lower is safer). Buildings and architecture would be reduced to crumbled skeletons of structural rebar and would have a ‘watermark’ of crusted salt accumulated around their base. This treatment could be applied to any place that needed to show up on a card, to show that recognizable place in a state of stylish ruin. The people themselves would wrap their feet to disperse their weight as they trek across the precarious top crust, and would shield their faces from the biting sting of the blowing salt. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/25/6b/256b4ec7fcfd74291943b7aeaf19ba391b990f32871f0a1de52d91d3f9b0fb0a.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f0/8e/f08e373feaf7a55371d25648f692948f5df8db66ff2243195163beac13d2d7ac.jpg)

Weapons and armor would be reinforced with leather to slow their erosion from the salt-blast of the winds.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/45/db/45dba35fd7c9c7655054e54dc0d16541d5be5d390c74b5a5bbaf667e85f3273b.jpg)
