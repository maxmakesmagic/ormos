This is the fifth installment of my series analyzing the cards of *Odyssey* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the rest of the series [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### Green

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6f/d7/6fd7071710dce1c801f9658701fc2ba0f9a72c38a913713dc54a1cd728a49b5d.jpg)

Historically, the green mage has always gotten the short end of the stick. Because of its ability to diversify and speed up your mana, green seemed to lose out, forced to watch as the other mages got all the creature kill and evasion and rare bombs. "Green has no rare bombs" complained the green mage. Fortunately, R&D, without breaking any of their templating rules, found a way to even the score in *Odyssey*:

Make a ton of green common cards, and make them all amazing.

*Odyssey's* green offers as strong a creature selection as any one color I can remember in any one set. There's the best bear of all time, the two casting cost 4/4, the living Giant Growth, the unblockable 3/4, the instant creature... the list goes on and on. Sure, there was an obvious drop off in *Torment*, but in sealed deck tournaments, there are still just as many green players as black ones, because when you get that sealed deck, there are just too many good green creatures to ignore.

Green works with any color but white for my money, forming an aggressive deck with red, a threshold driven deck with blue or a strong offensive arsenal with black. Green is also more capable of supporting a third color than any of the other colors. Sure, there's still a shortage of rare bombs and sure, there's still no evasion, but hey green mage, stop complaining: 2/2 flyers usually won't stop an army of 3/3s, and if they do, then they have to deal with your big guys. 

### The Cards

**Bearscape**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There are a lot of factors to weigh when determining the strength of Bearscape in your deck, and the ability to get cards into your graveyard and the need to maintain threshold are primary among them. Blue-green is the most capable deck at getting cards into the graveyard, so Bearscape's value should escalate in that color combo, while the opposite seems true of green-black, where your black cards will constantly deplete your graveyard. This is a potential game breaker, but make sure yours is the right deck for it before picking it high. A 4th - 6th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e3/fb/e3fbdaf25944e0676e5e3c5555dbe52e25fd75dadf6dfefeaaacbfba54e1b510.jpg)**Beast Attack**  
 To all you Overrun supporters out there, I have some sad news for you: yours is not even the best green uncommon in the set: Beast Attack is numero uno in my book. Bringing with it the same hard to use casting cost as the aforementioned sorcery, if Beast Attack were a mere sorcery that allowed you to eventually bring two 4/4 creatures into play, it would be merely incredible, but that its an instant often allows you to surprise your opponent with a mid-combat blocker, at which point the sheer card advantage involved becomes ludicrous. If you're at the mid point of the draft and aren't green, don't even think about the switch, especially considering *Torment's* anemic offerings, but if you're still in pack one and you see Beast Attack, I can think of very few reasons not to take it. A 1st pick.

**Call of the Herd**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
Beast Attack may be the best uncommon, but Call of the Herd gets my vote by a very slim margin for best green card in *Odyssey*. Obviously strong in that it dominated Extended late last year, these days Call of the Herd's speed make it the better of the two cards to have in your opening draw, while the Attack's potency makes it the better of the two later on. Of course, if you're offered the choice between the two, it's likely that the contents of your draft will decide which one you'll want for your deck, but as I place paramount importance on the limited early game and good mana, Call gets my nod over Attack, Overrun and all other comers. A 1st pick.

**Cartographer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As coming into play abilities go, Cartographer's is a little boring, often producing irrelevant card advantage or, worse yet, none at all when the need for a warm body becomes paramount. In a set with relatively few three drops, the Cartographer will suffice when you need a creature, especially in green-blue, where you're likely to have lands in your graveyard, but don't pick it highly: you never really want this in your deck. An 8th - 10th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c1/ba/c1bac84c650a69747a4ca1911626fb86495e330316b83ab0996c7d0c5ecf3297.jpg)**Chatter of the Squirrel**  
 Man, was I wrong about this one. If I'd completed this series earlier, you would have all been told that Chatter was a mediocre card that would squeak its way into red-green and blue-green main decks. Now, I'm telling you that it's one of the most important cards in the format, providing almost entirely on its own the foundation for the best red-green deck (and maybe the best non-black deck) in the format: Chatter/Rites. With black mages all over the world stocking up on unmatchable power, the red-green mage must win via speed, and there is no better card in *Odyssey* to have in your opening hand for this deck than Chatter. Don't pick it too high if you're not playing the aggressive red-green, but if you are, pick it right behind Ember Beast, Mongrel, Flame Burst and Firebolt. A 3rd - 5th pick in red-green, 7th-9th elsewhere.

**Chlorophant**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Obviously, the main obstacle here is Chlorophant's casting cost. Green is best suited to being the base color for a deck, providing fat creatures around which you can draft a second color for removal, bounce or the like, so you'll often end up playing nine or ten forests anyway. If that's the case, this thing can get very big, very fast, and while I wouldn't take it over something more solid or consistent like Elephant Ambush, I would be happy to have this as a potential late game threat. Don't take it too high, there will usually only be one other player who can consistently cast it early, but if you get it seventh or eighth, you've got a bargain. A 5th - 7th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/30/cc/30cc9d324cb33ceb21c2ebdbf69f61d5d07b9a023dc37e376dd7068e058c69dd.jpg)**Crashing Centaur**  
 The only complimentary color I like this card with is blue, simply because blue will get you to threshold faster than the others. Unless you have Bloodcurdler or Millikin, this fatty is going to stay 3/4 for a long time, and ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) mana is a lot to pay for that kind of beef. A great target for Seton's Desire if you can see threshold on the horizon. A 4th - 6th pick. 

**Deep Reconnaissance**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I find myself preferring Rites of Spring to Reconnaissance for three reasons: 1) Rites of Spring cures the mana screw, while Reconnaissance sits in your hand with two lands in play. 2) Rites of Spring does a faster, more efficient job of thinning out your library. 3) Rites of Spring will often get you half way to threshold on turn two. The Reconnaissance is playable when you desperately need a mana fixer, but I wouldn't play it otherwise. A 10th - 12th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e8/81/e881cf5f0674488901a8051f716fad1b6e1077f812b96ec5c4aeb087e3755e7d.jpg)**Diligent Farmhand**  
 I find myself preferring Diligent Farmhand to Rites of Spring for twenty-five-hundred reasons: This card is so good. Providing a one-drop, preventing mana screw, providing the option for your deck's color diversification, boosting your Muscle Burst... the list goes on and on. I seem to pick this little guy higher than anyone (I'm a big fan of temporal advantage), so you may want to take this advice in stride, but if you get two, you'll have a good deck: the splash becomes pretty easy when you only need one basic land to do it. A 4th - 6th pick (though I go 3rd -5th)

**Druid Lyrist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When I first saw this card, I thought it was pretty cool that there was a second Lyrist option, but then I realized Elvish Lyrist wasn't a very good Limited card. Same goes for the Druid, which has relatively few applicable targets in this environment. A solid sideboard card, if your Overrun/Rites of Initiation deck is starving for one-casting cost creatures or your three Reckless Charges need quick targets, this could be the critter for you. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Druid's Call**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I thought I might end up liking this card at first, but I discovered the hard way it wasn't really up to doing what I wanted. What I wanted was to create an untargetable creature or a thousand squirrel tokens: what I got was an excuse for my opponent to kill, bounce, Kirtar's Desire or Repel the target of the spell. Might be viable with Overrun or the like, but more often than not, you'll regret having it in your deck. An 11th - 13th pick.

**Elephant Ambush**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This was the card that everyone was raving about in the beginning, but surprisingly, the Ambush has found itself declining in value when compared to a number of green's other commons. Everyone knows what two open forests and two other lands on turn four means and frankly, eight mana is a lot to pay to turn this into card advantage. Don't get me wrong, the Ambush is a very solid spell, but I'll take Springing Tiger over it almost every time. A 3rd - 5th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8a/0f/8a0fa2e664b681840e3f8ee6b488c592e18a2c141a3bbb5709214472c63ab3eb.jpg)**Gorilla Titan**  
 In a roundabout way, the Titan got better with the introduction of *Torment* into the *Odyssey* Limited environment. While blue loads the graveyard and white and red don't really affect it one way or another, black drains the graveyard in an effective fashion, with cards like Organ Grinder and Painbringer keeping things barren. At 4/4 trample for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif), the Titan is very big and very good regardless, but anything that can keep it 8/8 into the midgame makes for a noteworthy bonus. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Ground Seal**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I like Ground Seal more than most, but that may just be because it's more effective than it seems at first glance. For two mana, you effectively cycle a card, while ensuring that opposing players can't abuse cards like Scrivener, Gravedigger and Morgue Theft. As an additional bonus, cards that target your graveyard like Gravegouger and Crypt Creeper are no longer an issue with your flashback and threshold cards. Obviously you won't pick it over anything that will have a more substantial impact on the game, but this is probably better than a Grey Ogre in most decks. An 8th - 10th pick.

**Holistic Wisdom**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In Constructed formats where you can pick and choose the contents of your deck, this card can be very strong. In Limited formats where beggars can't be choosers however, it's very difficult to coordinate a deck that can really abuse the Wisdom. If you have a number of fragile bombs like Kamahl, Holistic Wisdom can be worth playing if you have no other recursion, but often it will be too ineffective for the wait it forces on you. An 8th-10th pick.

**Howling Gale**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 An interesting trick, the Gale probably won't find itself effective enough in this environment to warrant main deck play at all times. That said, if your deck is aggressive and you're short on cards, there are worse things to play than a card that deals two damage to your opponent while offering hopes of dead flying things. An 8th - 10th pick.

**Ivy Elemental**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 One of the stronger green rares in the set, the Elemental is usually a 3/3 for four or 4/4 for five, but the fact it offers up the hope of a mid game 7/7 or 8/8 to break the game open is what makes it better than most green fatties. I would place Gorilla Titan ahead of the Elemental, but I put the Elemental ahead of Rabid Elephant. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Krosan Archer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card can give the blue mage fits. Traditionally, there's always been one green creature that has fit this description in any given Limited environment, but unlike most of them, the Archer can be effective on offense as well as offering a way to stop almost any flyer your opponent tries to use to get to you through the skies. Because of its defensive nature, I'd recommend you draft it a little higher in your blue-green decks then your red-green ones. A 4th - 6th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f0/6f/f06f741c679b825158fa8b3199018db4c7cdd56e285a9a7e8e49da57c1436e78.jpg)**Krosan Avenger**  
 Either way you cut it, this is a great creature. Falling into the hard-to-fill three casting cost slot, the Avenger is either a three-powered trampler for your more aggressive decks (especially good with Reckless Charge) or a three-power regenerator in those decks that are geared towards achieving threshold early (Especially good with Seton's Desire). Either way, I recommend you pick it higher than your better 3/3s for four, because you'll have no problems finding other four casting cost creatures for your deck. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Krosan Beast**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When I saw this card get taken over Gorilla Titan and Firebolt in the Top 8 of Pro Tour-San Diego, I was mortified. Yes, the Beast can be an 8/8 for four mana, especially in those blue-green threshold decks with Rites of Spring, Think Tank, Compulsion and the like, but more often than not, it's going to be a 1/1 for four that needs four more cards in the 'yard to become something more substantial. Don't get me wrong, it's obviously very good in the right deck, but more often than not, your green decks won't qualify. A 6th - 8th pick.

**Leaf Dancer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 As much as any other creature in *Odyssey*, Leaf Dancer's stock plummeted in draft with *Torment's* introduction to the format. Before the black set came into its own, green was probably the most played color in the set, with most tables featuring four green mages, making the Dancer a very potent offensive weapon half the time. Now, with Green's weak showing in *Torment*, we're down to two or three Green players per table. If you have Leaf Dancer in your deck, you'll be one of them. It's seldom more than a 2/2. A 7th - 9th pick, a bit better in sealed. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b2/a0/b2a0974d2ecda7f4facc9d1bb77903595dfa9bd42f6df36151127bfe9da750ad.jpg)**Metamorphic Wurm**  
 While Krosan Beast can be bigger for less mana, this is definitely more my type of card. The Wurm offers solid dividends if picked early on regardless of your deck's ability to attain threshold quickly, while once threshold is attained, it's an absolute monster. You have to be careful with how many five casting cost creatures you're playing in your deck, but this one is definitely worth the wait. A 3rd - 5th pick, goes up a little with each graveyard filler you have.

**Moment's Peace**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 How far Fog has come. Once an unplayable card in Limited except as a sideboard card against aggressive decks, the Fog has since gone through many forms in R&D's constant attempts to make it more useful. The latest of these forms, Moment's Peace, has accomplished the task to some degree, not always warranting main deck use, but definitely finding itself a niche. Moment's Peace is best in conjunction with blue cards like Cephalid Looter and Compulsion that will allow you to take advantage of the flashback without costing you a card and worst in aggressive decks that shouldn't be thinking defensively. Don't pick it too early as there is such a thing as too many for your deck, but try to get at least one regardless of your color combo. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Muscle Burst**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Your opponent attacks with three 2/2s on turn five, with your just-cast Mystic Zealot looking pretty strong. You're already at fourteen, so you've got to block, and you both know it. When you finally assign the Zealot to block, your opponent just smiles, because it's pretty obvious you're as good as dead. They tap two mana, Muscle Burst their blocked creature, your Zealot dies, they play a fifth land and tap out making yet another dork. If this were Muscle Burst's only function, it would be very, very good. Fortunately, it's a lot more versatile than that. A 3rd - 5th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/80/b9/80b949f6e4824ed5f10132f382440e6caf03cc39ab0389e9c33327c29ffbee42.jpg)**Nantuko Disciple**  
 Now take the above example, remove the surprise, have the effect cost one and make it usable once every turn. As good as the Burst is, the Disciple is better, functioning brilliantly in both control and aggression based decks. I'll take Wild Mongrel over it every time, but I think the Disciple is up there with Werebear and Rabid Elephant in the fight for the 'second best green common in Odyssey' label. A 2nd - 4th pick 

**Nantuko Elder**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If I were a Nantuko, I'd rather stay inexperienced and naïve. The Elder, while providing a very respectable mana boost in the mid-game, starts accelerating your mana on turn four, when you'd normally have the four mana you need to start casting the bulk of your spells. It provides nice insurance against mana screw and allows for the fast casting of cards like Crashing Centaur and Stone-Tongue Basilisk, but with a 1/2 body for three mana, I just don't feel like the Elder does enough consistently to warrant normal main deck use. a 9th - 11th pick.

**Nantuko Mentor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Mentor, a fragile and costly card, makes up for those attributes with the fact it can dominate the table if your opponent can't kill it quickly. 'Attack with Roar of the Wurm token, Muscle Burst it, activate Mentor, take eighteen' has happened more than once, but the situation doesn't need to be this drastic for the Mentor to work its magic. Just use it with Krosan Avenger and you'll be in pretty good shape. A 3rd - 5th pick

**Nantuko Shrine**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The **Magic** Colony broke this card, but you'll have to draft sixteen to twenty Eggs. A 15th pick.

**New Frontiers**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I've seen this card function very well in draft with Thaumatog and Time Stretch, but unless the circumstances are very unusual, I'd suggest not playing it in your decks. If you get it late pack one though, do keep your eyes open for ways to exploit its potential. A 10th - 12th pick

**Nimble Mongoose**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I love this card in Constructed, but in Limited, it just doesn't do enough in the early game, and later on its untargetability can actually be a hindrance (of sorts) as your opponents will invariably have creatures that are large enough to stop it and can be secure in the knowledge you won't have tricks to boost it with. You can't Charge it, Burst it, Embolden it or what not and in an aggressive green deck that may make even Woodland Druid preferable. Okay, maybe not the Druid, but anything else. A 10th - 12th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0f/03/0f031a16236e426a0317ff09e228444d9854cf06ba9538e81c684fcc4eefd38b.jpg)**Overrun**  
 The simple fact about Overrun is that it's often the only card that can win you the game when it does. You've all been there, in control of the table, thinking nothing can stop you, after all, the guy on the other side of the table only has a bunch of Squirrels. Then the guy taps five lands and the match is over, and long after he's left the table, you're still sitting there, desleeving, shaking your head and cursing the fates. No other card in the format does this so often, but hey, maybe they won't draw it next time. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Piper's Melody**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The bad news is this used to be a 4/3 creature. The good news is that at least it helps you lose threshold. A 15th pick.

**Primal Frenzy**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 "We can't make every card good" - Randy Buehler. A 15th pick

![](https://i.ormos.uk/79/ab/79aba4e5352dc0160c0e5ad19ba1b78d9c2fd8037e0e8370521e3fa9c59edb07.jpg)**Rabid Elephant**  
 When you pay five mana for a creature, you'd better get a decent package out of your investment, and in the case of Rabid Elephant you definitely get what you pay for. 3/4 would feel a little small if not for the fact this thing is virtually unblockable, either getting through unscathed or killing the unfortunate placed in the beast's path. Especially good with Seton's Desire in this, an environment virtually devoid of regenerators. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Refresh**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If you like wiping out opposing armies with a sweep of your hand, portraying Timmy, Power Gamer with every deck you build or draft, this isn't the card for you. If however you need another card for your deck and relish the opportunity to get some card advantage out of subtle play, I'd recommend Refresh. Good against burn, Ghastly Demise and combat damage, Refresh is a versatile cantrip that can be burnt off to prevent damage or draw a card. Don't draft it too high, but play it if you get it. A 6th - 8th pick.

**Rites of Spring**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When you draft on **Magic** Online, your account keeps track of every card you've ever drafted, meaning that over a long period of time, you can figure out which cards you value more than others by the number you happen to have collected. I have more Rites of Spring than any other card. Whether you're building up your graveyard, diversifying your mana or just trying to thin your deck out, Rites of Spring will do it all, all in a neat little two-mana package. You'll get the Rites late, which is one reason I like it so much, and when you do, I'd start looking for cards to splash. A 7th - 9th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ef/3a/ef3aacde0c53dc2658bceb93cdb3b8742c6761fa3c2997859c71bf0927ede392.jpg)**Roar of the Wurm**  
 Right up there with Beast Attack, Call of the Herd and Overrun among green's best cards, the Roar has one major advantage over the other three in that it's the best of the bunch as a splash card. Every color is capable of discarding now and the mana is very easy, meaning it may be the best splash card in the set. Especially good with Last Rites, like Call and Attack, Roar lost a small step with the introduction of Faceless Butcher to the format, but I'd still treat it in the way that it's accustomed to: as a bomb. A 1st - 2nd pick. 

**Seton, Krosan Protector**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not quite as easy to splash as the Roar, once Seton is in play he doesn't seem to do quite as much. Unless you manage to draft monogreen, you're going to have a lot of trouble casting this little legend, and while the same holds true for Beast Attack and Overrun, they make the wait a lot more worthwhile. A 10th - 12th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/07/bd/07bd2374a325c6bb39858193bfdaabb88720cbb6ea0af392005c7132d6cb0547.jpg)**Seton's Desire**  
 Opinions seem to vary a lot on this card. Some pros treat Seton's Desire as decidedly mediocre, while others draft it high regardless of color compliments and the like. For me, this card is best in blue-green, where the fast acquisition of threshold will make it decidedly strong, while I think it's pretty bad in black-green, where the graveyard is always empty. It will usually be good enough for your deck, but the other contents of the deck will really decide how high you should pick it. A 4th - 6th pick

**Simplify**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It's very seldom I side this in because I try to make sure I have targeted enchantment kill and don't particularly enjoy effects I can't control. However, if you're desperate to get rid of Narcissism, Squirrel Nest or the like, you've got to do what you've got to do. A 12th - 14th pick.

**Skyshooter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Two casting cost creatures are definitely at a premium in this day in age, which is the main reason this card is remotely playable. A good sideboard card for control-oriented decks, the Shooter becomes a viable main deck option if you have a lot of spells that boost your creatures up when you get them in play. Don't pick it high, but those red-green decks need something to Reckless Charge. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Spellbane Centaur**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 For the mana involved, this card is very strong. Easy to cast in a tough to fill mana slot, the Centaur provides a very solid 3/2 body while additionally providing a powerhouse ability against half the players at the table. Offering potential as a sideboard splash if you end up outside of green, blue mages will convulse like Donnie Gallitz when it hits play. Don't make the same mistake Donnie did though: keep your lighter in your pocket and remember that cards like Psionic Gift grant the creature the ability instead of acting as a blue source. A 2nd - 4th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/63/af/63afe402947a2bb7c59333de0428edfe52e266c1379e16adef3b5f68e68e0c0e.jpg)**Springing Tiger**  
 The best of the *Odyssey* 3/3s for four, the Tiger comes out nice and easy, requiring only one green mana, then becomes a force later on when you have threshold. A good card regardless of your ability to get seven in the 'yard, the Tiger's stock goes up with each graveyard filler you obtain. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Squirrel Mob**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 For a long time, the Mob went vastly underdrafted, but with its emergence in the Osaka metagame, I doubt that will happen too much more. Especially good with Acorn Harvest and Squirrel Nest, it works out just fine with Chatter of the Squirrel also, regardless of who casts it. Remember: the Mob is a Squirrel also, so if you manage to get two, they'll help one another out a lot. A 5th - 7th pick, but its stock can rise with Chatters in your deck.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6e/ca/6ecab2258035d473a572637fa7e4caf0448db2dcaef1ea343778693d259967b2.jpg)**Squirrel Nest**  
 Probably green's sixth best card (Call, Attack, Overrun, Roar, Wild Mongrel), there are definitely times I'd take the Nest over some of the others. Great with Rites of Initiation or Overrun, the Nest is good enough that it makes a card like Krosan Restorer pretty playable. Don't rely on the Nest too much, because it becomes less than great when you keep making Squirrels to prevent three points of attacker damage, but understand when you draft it you're getting a card that can win the game on its own. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Still Life**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I HATE this card. I'm sorry, but the simple fact is that it costs ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) to attack with this 4/3 ONCE, and that's just ridiculous. In addition, it's a creature that can be killed by Aven Cloudchaser, usually can't block the turn it comes out and still dies to Fiery Temper after all that. Do yourself a favor: if you think this is the best card left in the pack, pass it. Maybe the other green mage will play it. A 9th - 11th pick.

**Stone-Tongue Basilisk**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This big puppy sure does cost a lot. Yes it's large, and tough and possessed of strong pre- and post-threshold abilities, but in order to take advantage of all that, you need to get it into play, and at ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif), that's pretty hard to do. The Basilisk is definitely worth playing if you have it, but I'd recommend taking green's stronger commons over it more often than not. A 4th - 6th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d2/5b/d25b485c6f118e761925b3a8b0a1aaa7d8f3437f1b2c4d923432b02ea4aca5ab.jpg)**Sylvan Might**  
 Man, this card is good. Casting it the first time is usually better than Muscle Burst in that your creature will live, theirs will die and they'll manage to take some trample damage in the process. That you get to flash it back as a reasonable cost is just unfair. If I'm already in green, there's very little I'll pick over it... after all, cheap card advantage cards that deal damage to your opponent on top of everything else are just too few and far between to pass up. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Terravore**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In any non-blue deck, Terravore is pretty much awful, because black, white and red have a lot of difficulty getting land into the graveyard. Sure, those colors could side in this card against Looter-laden blue decks, but really even that doesn't seem like a tactically sound decision. If you have multiple ways of getting cards from your hand to your graveyard without losing card advantage in the process, Terravore can be good enough to play, but don't pick it too high: if you can play it, no one else can. A 9th - 11th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/34/b0/34b0d2eb8fbb74a013c09c80a951d1ddde85b934e8f9d41c9ab12bcea3ecb57a.jpg)**Twigwalker**  
 One of the best three-drops in the format, Twigwalker offers you everything Symbiosis once did, but the instant version couldn't attack or block. The best use for this card is to play it on turn five or so as you're about to attack with multiple creatures so you can activate it the turn it comes into play. This will throw off your opponent's math, often to the point of no return. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Verdant Succession**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This is a lot of fun in Constructed, but in Limited you just won't get enough copies of your assorted green creatures, especially now that we're using two sets. A 12th - 14th pick.

**Vivify**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A neat little trick, Vivify is costly for its effect, but versatile nonetheless. The best thing about this card is the surprise value involved. I'm sure a lot of you reading this have lost a good board control creature that was trying to attack for one into no blockers to the Vivify, and there's no wonder: its commonality makes it very hard to account for. Throw in the ability to do three points of 'haste' damage and the fact you can cycle it for three mana and you have a nice little card that does a good job of filling your deck out when you need card twenty-three. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Werebear**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I love this guy, but I'm probably preaching to assembled worshippers. Werebear does everything right: accelerates your mana, takes care of mana difficulties and eventually attacks with force. I wasn't shocked at all when the 1/1 started showing up in Extended, so you shouldn't be too surprised when I tell you that aside from Wild Mongrel, I can see myself taking this over any common. A 1st - 3rd pick in blue-green, 2nd - 4th everywhere else. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a4/66/a4662692f3afa80eeff7f490ee71b304fa3ed34e3ba37073f1d18a90fdcef4f8.jpg)**Wild Mongrel**  
 For my money, the second best common in the set after Cephalid Looter, I'm more regularly a blue mage then a green one, and those green mages out there might say I'm crazy for not putting it number one. The Mongrel does everything from attack to stop opposing attackers to save itself from Banish effects to provide an outlet for threshold and madness to kill Finkel to... ah screw it, you know what it does. Brian Kibler was probably right when he called this the best bear ever. A 1st - 3rd pick.

**Woodland Druid**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If your opponent is playing the 1/1 red-green Rites of Initiation deck, this can actually be an annoying sideboard option. Other than that though, you won't find it does enough. A 12th - 14th pick.

**Zoologist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Disappointingly, this guy didn't end up as good as I'd hoped. I figured that, sure, it was a 1/2 for four and sure, the activation cost a lot, but the dividends were limitless: free flashback cards, threshold activation, mid-combat blockers... just seemed like a lot of upside. Unfortunately, in the end, the negatives outweighed the positives. Yes, you can play the card, but most of the time you'll regret it. A 9th - 11th pick.
