## GRAND PRIX BRISBANE 2017

Top 8 Coverage

Oliver Oks has long been a contender in the APAC *Magic* scene, but has never quite converted any of his solid Grand Prix finishes to a win, until today. Oks piloted the Lantern Control deck he's been practicing with for the last year perfectly through the Swiss rounds to finish second behind No. 11 Lee Shi Tian. Oks first dispatched Sean Hume's Black-Green Tron deck in the Quarterfinals before moving on to beat Timothy Cheng's Living End deck in the Semis, earning him a spot in the Finals where he would face New Zealand's Zen Takahashi. 

Working with Team MTG Mint Card, Takahashi and Lee Shi Tian were both playing near identical Dredge decks that saw them squaring off in the opposite Semifinals bracket. Takahashi managed to beat his mentor in 3 games before making a valiant effort to rush under Oks' Ensnaring Bridges, but just couldn't put the match away as Oks tactfully targeted Takahashi's cards with Grafdigger's Cage and a Lost Legacy on his Ancient Grudges, allowing Oks to win the match 2-1 to become the Grand Prix Brisbane Champion.

Of note this weekend was just how diverse the Modern Metagame was, With 7 distinct archetypes in the Top 8, and many more in the Top 32, the format is certainly very vibrant and alive, and lured out nearly a thousand players to compete this weekend, over doubling the attendance at the last Grand Prix held in Brisbane in 2013.

The Top 8 will all see each other at Pro Tour *Amonkhet* in Nashville on 12-14 May, and we hope you'll join us in cheering them on. Until then, Grand Prix Vancouver is still underway on the other side of the World, and there are a bunch more Grand Prix events between now and then. Some of them may even be near you!

Day 2 Coverage

For the second morning in a row, Queensland has greeted us with what can only be described as “almost cool weather” as we get Day 2 of Grand Prix Brisbane underway. Yesterday we had 966 plucky players shuffling up to play some Modern, but we’ve since narrowed the field through the tried and true scientific process of “good ol’ fashioned biffo”\* to have 285 players competing today.

Leading the field at 9-0 are Japan’s Akito Shinoda, Taufik Indrakesuma from Indonesia, and No. 11 Lee Shi Tian from Hong Kong. Snapping at their heels like one of the many freshwater crocodile no doubt lurking beneath the surface of the nearby Brisbane River (because Australia\*\*) are many noted names like Zen Takahashi, Anthony Lee, Kelvin Chew, Huang Hao-Shan, David Mines, Matt Rogers, Maitland Cameron, Tomoharu Saito, and Jason Chung.

The metagame has given every indication of being more diverse than it’s been in a long time, and we’ll be keeping an eye on it as we get nearer the Top 8 this afternoon, bringing you deck spotlights and the Top 32 decklists at the end of the day.

*\*Note: there was no actual biffo had at Grand Prix Brisbane this weekend. Not even any fisticuffs.*

*\*\*Note: there aren’t actually any crocodiles in the Brisbane CBD. None that you can see, anyway.*

Day 1 Coverage

A record-breaking 966 players rocked up – as we say in Australia – to Grand Prix Brisbane this morning, more than doubling the record set here back in 2013. After nine brutal rounds of Modern warfare, only three players remained undefeated. Japan’s Akito Shinoda, Taufik Indrakesuma from Indonesia, and No. 11 Lee Shi Tian from Hong Kong.

What’s been very noticeable so far this weekend is just how diverse the metagame appears to be. The Grand Prix Trials run yesterday saw ten different archetypes across thirteen decks, and coming into the last round of Day 1 today, there were five different decks between the six players vying to remain undefeated, and another seven different decks again between the ten players right behind them.

Also making the cut to 285 players for Day 2 are Zen Takahashi, Anthony Lee, Kelvin Chew, John-Paul Kelly, James Zhang, Jay Kinkade, Timothy Nolan, Huang Hao-Shan, Luke Mulcahy, David Mines, Matt Rogers, Maitland Cameron, Tomoharu Saito, and Jason Chung to name a few. Okay, I named more than a few there, but they’re all good players, Brent!

Join us tomorrow as the players continue to battle towards the Top 8. We’ll endeavour to bring you more deck spotlights as well as the Top 32 decklists at the end of the event. Is that what you want? You know it is. We all know it is.
