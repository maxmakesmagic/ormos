### Card Vote #6 and #7

This week we've got double votes for card vs. card and the artist sketches. 

Click here when you're ready for your first vote.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/features/10e_week6_choices_rvw3pnaqq.jpg)  
  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/00/5e/005ed9efe23a25041a84773b44b186615dccaf08a848fc4c8557ef36731b4072.jpg)

*Comments from Matt Place*

> Crucible of Worlds is a great base set rare artifact because it features both straightforward and concealed applications. At first glance most players see that it is great defense against land destruction, and more advanced players may notice combos like two uncounterable damage every turn with Barbarian Ring. Being a card that combines obvious and not-so-obvious uses is what makes it a great choice for *Tenth Edition*. 
> 
> I love rare artifacts that a new player can initially play for its clear uses while then discovering other synergies that reveal the depth and fun of **Magic**. For me that card was Winter Orb. It was one of the first rares I saw when I was brand new to **Magic**. At first I though it would be good in my weenie deck since I didn’t need much mana, and it worked fairly well. But when I first discovered the combo of Icy Manipulator and Winter Orb, I had a blast annoying my friends to no end! (Yes I was a mean and heartless **Magic** player.) The revelation that **Magic** had secret combos that I could be the first of my friends to find is what made me fall in love with the game!

*Comments from Aaron Forsythe*

> When **magicthegathering.com** first launched "You Make The Card" [back in 2002](/en/articles/feature/2002/01-16-you-make-the-card), I was the one managing the website side of things, and I remember there being a great deal of electricity surrounding the card and the process of creating it. The results of that initial experiment--Forgotten Ancient--never really made the big-time in Constructed formats, but that doesn't mean the card wasn't a huge success. In fact, it embodies the characteristic I most associate with a successful **Magic** card--fun. Everyone has tried a deck with this guy, or at the very least, been on the wrong end of a "Mr. Babycakes" gone haywire or heard a story from a friend of such an occurrence. And in multiplayer games, the card is about as hilariously absurd as anything we'd print.
> 
> Give in to your fun side--you made it, you enjoy playing with it--vote Forgotten Ancient!

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4c/93/4c93368f511b6a3a4b2fae7592d268d96c0823fe7db1f64dd51bc90afb7d9fb2.jpg)

*Comments from Matt Place*

> There is nothing more fun in **Magic** than beating a player with his own cards. Fellow blue mages all over the world are nodding their head in agreement with me right now. Spelljack is the ultimate “anything you try to do will go very very badly for you!” spell. **Magic** has had a lot of big spells in Standard in recent history, like Tooth and Nail or the *Kamigawa* Dragons. Spelljack existed in Standard with decks like *Onslaught* Goblins and so it saw only a limited amount of play. Had Spelljack come out a year or two later, we may have discovered the true power of this card. With that in mind, I think *Tenth Edition* would be a great chance to see what this card is really capable of.

*Comments from Aaron Forsythe*

> Three little words. Three tiny, harmless little words--that's all it takes to communicate one of the most jaw-dropping card abilities we've ever dared to print. End. The. Turn. Part Counterspell, part Stifle, part Fog, part Time Walk, Time Stop does it all. Of all of our attempts to make blue feel like the "time matters" color, Time Stop does it best of all, manipulating the temporal fabric in tricky and unexpected ways. Don't let that brick of reminder text deter you... vote for the card that wowed you in *Champions*, Time Stop.
