This is the sixth and final installment of my series reviewing the cards of *Judgment* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the rest of the series [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### Gold and Land

There are only four gold cards and three land cards in this set and comparing them to one another seems a little silly in that they're all rare or uncommon, but the pattern has been set in the rest of the series so we'll continue as is. What isn't so silly is the way they establish a new archetype, because they're all white-green cards, showing not only the strength of those two colors, but also the bias R&D showed towards them when designing the set.

While I haven't been a fan of drafting white-green in *Odyssey* Block up until now, the gold cards show us in blatant terms that this is now going to be a deck to be reckoned with. The incredible creature selection the colors provide in *Judgment* sets a strong base for an aggressive deck that can use cards like Shelter, Guided Strike, Sudden Strength, Serene Sunset and Strength of Isolation to maintain the stranglehold once it's been established by superior mana curve utilization. Get used to it folks: green-white is here for the duration.

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/43/2d/432d4e04319105e039322b14e4e32d7648cd3477ae99f236ae4a4eaa8326786d.jpg)**Anurid Brushhopper**  
 This thing is incredible. 3/3 for three mana has always been considered very good, so when you add on a point of toughness and throw in an ability that helps with threshold, helps with madness and leaves the Brushhopper virtually invulnerable, you have a powerhouse that you'd take very few white or green cards over. Granted, there may not be any other green-white players on the table, but players of either or will be willing to splash the other, so don't take it for granted because of its gold border.

**Phantom Nishoba**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I put the Brushhopper first because of its speed, but don't think for a second that doesn't mean this guy is absolutely insane. The Nishoba is close to invincible, absolutely massive and turns the damage race on its tail end, giving you seven, six, or five points of life every time it attacks. Obviously, its casting cost is something of an issue, but the moment it hits play, your opponent either deals with it or starts to lose horribly.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cd/2b/cd2b62e428fd33f69481f640d7c9b9d3b3ca2e9b0127dd35238a4ca0e38f60c5.jpg)**Mirari's Wake**  
 Regardless of the colors of your deck outside of white and green, giving all your creatures +1+1 in the colors that sport Acorn Harvest, Grizzly Fate and Battle Screech is worthwhile enough to play the Wake, but the real bonus comes in the form of the mana boost it gives you, allowing you to splash double casting cost cards more easily and potentially use some of the set's unrealistically costed spells. Want to cast Crush of Wurms or Time Stretch? Here's the method to your madness. 

**Nantuko Monastery**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Zero casting cost 4/4 first strikers don't come around every day, but the fact the Monastery can't be activated before threshold means it isn't quite as good as a five casting cost variety would be. One benefit of this card though is the fact that if you're only playing two colors, you may be able to afford to play the Monastery instead of a color-producing land, and that means you'll have an extra threat in your deck where your opponent has their eighth forest. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/8b/58/8b58cdf17a2d0032ed820f641e4031508fc450c69fbc2b03066c91d7e089b83b.jpg)**Krosan Verge**  
 Looking to splash? I've got a great way for you to do it. Krosan Verge provides you card advantage and mana stability while putting a card in your graveyard. All this at the cost of having it produce colorless mana instead of the white or green you might want it to provide otherwise. That isn't much of a drawback in the face of the upside.

**Riftstone Portal**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Portal shouldn't gain automatic admission to your deck, but if you're splashing either color and have a lot of discard methods in your deck, it may be viable. 

**Hunting Grounds**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While the option of playing creatures without possibility of having them countered seems like a good one, it definitely doesn't warrant main deck play, and its seldom you'll want to bring it in from the board. Could be a cool way to circumvent Spelljack if your opponent has a couple though...

### Final Thoughts

With the series done, you now have the building blocks around which to do your initial draft experiments, but like me, now you have to actually start drafting. Try out combos, try to find archetypes and see if you can't break the format with unusual choices of mediocre cards that work well enough together to change their individual value. It's those players who innovate in this way that gain the early advantage on the opposition, and in this game, every advantage helps.

I'm confident enough in the writing I've done here to say that most of it is right, but I've already started finding errors in white, so use this series as a loose basis and don't be afraid to stray from the path that's been laid out here. If you want to know the specific changes I'd make, keep your eyes on the *Sideboard* over the next couple of weeks: As I learn, I'll share my findings with you, and together we'll figure this format out. Hope you enjoyed this!
