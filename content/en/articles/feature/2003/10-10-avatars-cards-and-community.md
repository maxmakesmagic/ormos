### Dan Get's A Little Rant On

* * *

### The Normal Stuff

Well, we almost got the **1700 Room** up and working correctly. However, there are a number of players who can't get back into the room after they leave it -- no matter what their ratings are. If you're one of those players, send an email to custserv@wizards.com letting them know your player name and that you're having this problem.

*Eighth Edition* redemption began this week and the last round of Royal Assassin avatars went out. I guess I offended some folks last week when I asked them to have some patience on the avatars. I didn't mean that you hadn't already waited a long time; just that Customer Service needed time to collect the last names and check the information. My apologies if it sounded otherwise.

### Welcome To My Block List

**Warning:** Long rant ahead. If you'd rather skip to what I know you're all waiting for, [click here](#skip).

In the past, I've been a fairly vocal proponent of penalizing players for rude behavior in ***Magic** Online.* I'd like the game to be a place anyone can come to play without worrying about running into people who don't care if they ruin someone else's experience.

While I still want ***Magic** Online* to be that place, I was recently involved in a situation that made me rethink my opinions on community management. While we still need administrators who can kick players for truly abusive behavior, it's up to the community to police itself on a day-to-day basis.

This past weekend, I was in a private chat room where one player made a rude, inappropriate comment directed to another player. Especially because the statement easily fell into the realm of harassment, I told the player it was a pretty uncool thing to say. His reply?

"Why don't you report me?"

I was stunned. I'm sitting there with a Wizards tag next to my name and this is the response I get? It's not like I even really need a screenshot to send into magicconduct@wizards.com. Who did this person think he was? I'll admit it. Had I the powers of an administrator, I'd have bounced the person for an hour or so just to make my point.

However, instead of flying off the handle, I thought about the situation for a few minutes. I may have been *in* the chat room, but the comment wasn't directed towards me. I'm not even a member of a group likely to take offense to the comment. If I were an everyday ***Magic** Online* player, what would my options be? It's not like I somehow deserve better treatment than other players just because I work here.

That's what stopped me. Sure, I work at Wizards and could probably get this player temporarily booted for harassment. But if I weren't, what would my best course of action be?

Well, I could go to **Magic** Conduct with the situation. But what, really, is that going to gain? As I stated above, I wasn't even the target of the comment, so it's not really harassing behavior in my eyes -- inconsiderate, but not harassing. So, while the player on the receiving end might have reason to report this, I didn't really have anything to stand on. After all, if it doesn't offend the target enough for a report, maybe I'm just overreacting.

Besides, it was a private chat room, not one of the public rooms. I, for one, think that players in a private chat room are responsible for their own behavior. (Being obnoxious in a public room, however, deserves getting booted.) If I'm the only person in the room offended by a statement, then maybe it's not the place for me to be. If I'm offending everyone else in the room, I fully expect the other players to tell me so and ask me to clean my act up or leave.

So I took this to one more level of removal: If I was at a club with a group of people, and one person made a rude comment to someone else that I took exception to, what would I do?

First, I'd let that person know I thought his behavior was inappropriate. He may not have realized he was being offensive -- what bothers some people might not bother others. This is especially common in such a diverse population as we have on ***Magic** Online.* I like to give people the benefit of the doubt and, for the most part, I'm not usually disappointed.

But what if the person doesn't care? Well, then I obviously have no reason to associate with the person. The rest of the group may even tell me that I'm being overly sensitive, which gives me a chance to reevaluate my own behavior.

If I don't like what someone in the group is talking about, I wouldn't call the owner of the club and ask her to remove the person -- that's a bit extreme. I do, however, have the option of ignoring the person, as does every other person in the group. And, if most of the group comes down on someone for her behavior, the situation usually resolves itself.

Bringing this back to ***Magic** Online,* sure I'd like to never have to deal with people who are inconsiderate and rude. (Then again, I'd also like to find a parking space near the doors at IKEA on a weekend -- possible, but unlikely.) But my definition of inappropriate behavior may be different than yours. Unless a player goes completely over the line or continues to harass me after I've made it clear how I feel, there's no reason to "take it to the authorities."

What did I finally decide? I blocked him.

Since we all have our own tolerances for what we'll put up with, we all have different people we wouldn't want to deal with. The **Block user** command allows us to clean up our own corner of the world -- whatever our reason for doing so.

In my case, this is someone who, through his own statements, told me that he'd say whatever he wants to no matter how anyone feels about it. I don't get much time to actually play ***Magic** Online,* so I don't want to waste time dealing with that kind of behavior. Now I don't have to.
