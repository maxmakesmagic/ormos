Modern has changed quite a bit since Pro Tour *Fate Reforged*. The metagame that we saw at Grand Prix Charlotte last weekend was markedly different from the one we had at the Pro Tour, and Grand Prix Copenhagen may have more developments in store for us. In this article, I'll go over the main recent trends and give an early glimpse into the pros' deck choices.

# Trend 1: Kolaghan's Command Causes Colorshift

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Kolaghan%27s+Command)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kolaghan%27s+Command)

  
*Although [Dromoka's Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dromoka%27s+Command) and [Atarka's Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Atarka%27s+Command) have also found their way into various Modern decks, Kolaghan is the Dragonlord whose Command has had the largest impact.*

The most popular deck at Grand Prix Charlotte, at 12% of the [Day 2 metagame](/en/events/2015/gpcha15/day-2-archetype-breakdown) was Grixis Twin, and there were various Grixis Delver and Grixis Control decks running around the top tables as well. Contrast that with the [Day 2 metagame](/en/events/2015/ptfrf/day-two-modern-metagame-breakdown) at the Pro Tour, where almost all of the Splinter Twin and Delver players were straight up Blue/Red, and most of the control players were on Jeskai.

A key reason for this recent move towards Grixis is that [Kolaghan's Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kolaghan%27s+Command) was printed in *Dragons of Tarkir*. The instant provides an easy two-for-one, is particularly good against Affinity, and synergizes nicely with Snapcaster Mage. As Patrick Chapin said before finishing ninth with his [take on Grixis Control](/en/events/2015/gpcha15/controlling-modern-with-patrick-chapin): "[Kolaghan's Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kolaghan%27s+Command) is literally, single-handedly the thing that took Grixis up to being one of the defining decks of the format."

Likewise, midrange players have also picked up [Kolaghan's Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kolaghan%27s+Command). [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) and [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek) are as popular as always, but nowadays they are more frequently accompanied by [Kolaghan's Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kolaghan%27s+Command) than by [Lingering Souls](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lingering+Souls). If we turn to the numbers and compare the metagame at the Pro Tour in February to the one in Charlotte last weekend, we see that Abzan has gone down from over 20% to just 5.5%, whereas Jund has gone from 2.7% to 10.3%. That's a clear color shift, and it affects how some of the games play out. Those [Amulet of Vigor](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Amulet+of+Vigor)s are not safe anymore, for instance.

# Trend 2: The Birth of Collected Company Decks

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Collected+Company)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Collected+Company)

  
*In Michael Malone's winning Elf deck from Grand Prix Charlotte, [Collected Company](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Collected+Company) will hit 1.94 creatures on average.*

[Collected Company](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Collected+Company) is another new addition to the format from *Dragons of Tarkir*. In the right deck, it hits two creatures most of the time, providing a lot of value at instant speed. It even helps assembling various creature-based combos such as [Viscera Seer](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Viscera+Seer) plus [Melira, Sylvok Outcast](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Melira%2C+Sylvok+Outcast) plus [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks).

Looking at Grand Prix Charlotte last weekend, Abzan Company, Elves Company, and Zoo Company were only 3%, 1%, and 1% of the [Day 2 metagame](/en/events/2015/gpcha15/day-2-archetype-breakdown) respectively, but all of these decks did very well: Paul Rietzl finished in tenth place with Zoo Company, Ian Bosley finished sixth with Abzan Company, and Michael Malone won the event with Elves Company. Decklists can be found [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20220926070103/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/gpcha15/top-8-decklists2015-06-13) and [here](/en/events/2015/gpcha15/archetype-exemplars-top-decks-of-grand-prix-charlotte), if you're curious. With such a good showing, I wouldn't be surprised if we see even more [Collected Company](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Collected+Company) decks this weekend.

# Trend 3: Affinity, Burn, Infect, R/G Tron, and Others Still Around

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Cranial+Plating)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cranial+Plating)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Lava+Spike)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lava+Spike)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Glistener+Elf)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glistener+Elf)

  
*Some things never change.*

New cards come and go, but certain decks always stick around. We already touched on Splinter Twin and Jund earlier in Trend 1, but in addition to those decks you can expect to face Affinity, Burn, Infect, and R/G Tron once in any 15-round event. It's been that way pretty much ever since the inception of the Modern format, and it's no different today.

At slightly lower popularity levels, you have Amulet/Bloom, Merfolk, Zoo, and many, many others lingering around as well. Hey, "no change" can be called a trend as well, right?

# Trend 4: A Resurgence in Offbeat Combo

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Goryo%27s+Vengeance)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goryo%27s+Vengeance)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ad+Nauseam)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ad+Nauseam)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ensnaring+Bridge)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ensnaring+Bridge)

  
*Modern has always had its share of bizarre combo decks (from [Living End](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Living+End) to [Scapeshift](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scapeshift) to Storm) but it never fails to disappoint with even wackier strategies.*

In Charlotte, we suddenly saw an [Ad Nauseam](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ad+Nauseam)/[Angel's Grace](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel%27s+Grace) deck featuring [Spoils of the Vault](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spoils+of+the+Vault) and a [Goryo's Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goryo%27s+Vengeance)/[Griselbrand](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Griselbrand) deck with [Nourishing Shoal](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nourishing+Shoal) in the [Top 8](https://web.archive.org/web/20220926070103/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/gpcha15/top-8-decklists2015-06-13). Many of their opponents probably had no clue what was going on! There was also a crazy [Lantern of Insight](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lantern+of+Insight)/[Codex Shredder](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Codex+Shredder) deck that aimed to lock out the game with [Ensnaring Bridge](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ensnaring+Bridge) in the [Top 16](/en/events/2015/gpcha15/archetype-exemplars-top-decks-of-grand-prix-charlotte).

Of those three, the [Goryo's Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goryo%27s+Vengeance) deck called "Grishoalbrand" generated the most excitement. Last night, questions like "How good is that deck?" or "Do you think it is going to be popular at the Grand Prix?" or "What does it even do?" were abuzz. As for that last one: It plans to (1) get a [Griselbrand](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Griselbrand) onto the battlefield via [Goryo's Vengeance](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goryo%27s+Vengeance) or [Through the Breach](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Through+the+Breach), (2) chain [Nourishing Shoals](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nourishing+Shoals) and draw cards with the Demon until another [Through the Breach](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Through+the+Breach) is cast with the help of several [Simian Spirit Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Simian+Spirit+Guide)s and [Desperate Ritual](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Desperate+Ritual)s which then puts [Borborygmos Enraged](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Borborygmos+Enraged) on the table, (3) ping your opponent to death with lands. The key innovation is [Nourishing Shoal](https://web.archive.org/web/20150620000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nourishing+Shoal), which allows to deck to go off at instant speed and do all kinds of splice-onto-arcane tricks.
