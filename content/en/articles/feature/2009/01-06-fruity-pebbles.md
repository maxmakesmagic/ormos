As one of the early combo decks to meet with Pro Tour success, Fruit Pebbles was innovative for a host of reasons. It allowed players to "go infinite." And of course, and perhaps most importantly, it set the stage for naming combo decks after cereals. The combo was simple: play [Shield Sphere](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shield+Sphere) to survive the early turns. The play [Goblin Bombardment](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Bombardment), followed by [Enduring Renewal](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enduring+Renewal). The Renewal allowed you to return Shield Sphere to your hand when it died, [Goblin Bombardment](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Bombardment) allowed you to sacrifice the 0/6 to sting your opponent for 1, and the Sphere's zero-casting cost allowed you to play it without spending any mana. Fruity Pebbles was a powerhouse for years, but was eventually tamed by cards like [Aura of Silence](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aura+of+Silence), [Wasteland](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wasteland), and [Hymn to Tourach](https://web.archive.org/web/20090106000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hymn+to+Tourach).
