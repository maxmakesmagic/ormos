## GRAND PRIX PITTSBURG 2013

![](https://i.ormos.uk/16/0c/160c7d265c90ce37f6a809b2bb5115dfa838b25ff87558a5e2f805a3fe4a2471.jpg)

One of the Brockafellers has just made a comeback at Grand Prix Pittsburgh! Brock Parker has returned from years past to show the **Magic** world that the greats aren't ready to retire just yet. After earning his first Grand Prix Top 8 since 2003 in Columbus, Parker crafted the ultimate Orzhov deck, and it was through the Orzhov's extort mechanic that he won his final match against Alec Nezin by extorting all 20 points of life from his opponent in the final game!

This win at Grand Prix Pittsburgh is Parker's second Grand Prix victory, added to an impressive resume that includes six Grand Prix Top 8s and a victory at Pro Tour Boston in 2003, where he took down the tournament with teammates William Jensen and Matt Linde.

With *Gatecrash* explored inside and out, and Orzhov shining with the brutally powerful extort mechanic, players can look forward to how the *Return to Ravnica* Block will change as *Dragon's Maze* approaches. Which guild will rise above the rest as we approach Pro Tour *Dragon's Maze*?

But that's the future. For now, it's time to enjoy the moments that have taken place this weekend. Congratulations to Brock Parker, Grand Prix Pittsburgh champion!

## Sunday, 8:30 p.m. – Drafting in the Top 8 with Eric Froehlich

*by Mike Rosenberg*

 

Eric Froehlich, professional poker player and ChannelFireball team member, has been on fire since *Gatecrash* has been legal. While his lifetime record was already very impressive, his record since February includes a Top 4 finish at Pro Tour *Gatecrash* and a Top 4 finish last week at Grand Prix San Diego. This week, he's been all Boros all the time in his previous two drafts, where he managed to earn a 2-1 record in each draft with decks that he considered mediocre.

However, the one thing that has eluded him in his seven Grand Prix Top 8 finishes before this is a win. With a solid run all weekend in both the Sealed and Booster Draft rounds, Froehlich is intent to earn his first Grand Prix victory. And, given his run for a slot on the United States team at the World **Magic** Cup based on his pro points, it seems fitting to focus on his choices in this Top 8 draft.

![Skyknight Legionnaire](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Skyknight+Legionnaire)

Froehlich started off the draft with [Skyknight Legionnaire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skyknight+Legionnaire) over [Mystic Genesis](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mystic+Genesis) and [Slaughterhorn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Slaughterhorn), a clear nod to the power of a hasty 2/2 flier for the Boros Legion. His next pick after that though showed his true preference for the draft, if it can come together: [Orzhov Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Orzhov+Charm), a card he took over [Arrows of Justice](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arrows+of+Justice) and [Slaughterhorn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Slaughterhorn).

However, the third pack had nothing exciting outside of [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers), which would go nicely with Froehlich's first pick. [Skyknight Legionnaire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skyknight+Legionnaire) fourth pick was also a clear signal: Boros was open. It was time to red-white cards.

That signal, by the way? It wasn't necessarily the only one. Froehlich was visibly frustrated when a fifth pick [Grisly Spectacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grisly+Spectacle) was staring back at him. However, it shared a pack with [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite), a card that looked like it would be a fine inclusion in an already good looking Boros deck. Despite having an interest in Orzhov, Froehlich stayed on target, taking the one mana creature over the powerful removal spell.

[Court Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Street+Denizen) was the only real notable card to come after that, though [Millennial Gargoyle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Millennial+Gargoyle) and the expensive [Debtor's Pulpit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Debtor%27s+Pulpit) could potentially make the cut. Froehlich, having made his guild choice, made sure to ship reasonable but beatable cards such as [Syndicate Enforcer](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Syndicate+Enforcer) late as a signal, intent on making sure the player to his left gets the memo that he should be Orzhov or Dimir.

The second pack, however, offered little encouragement, as Froehlich took a [Hellraiser Goblin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellraiser+Goblin) first pick while passing [Bomber Corps](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bomber+Corps), [Mindeye Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindeye+Drake), [Ground Assault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ground+Assault), and [Executioner's Swing](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Executioner%27s+Swing). The second pick brought with it another [Court Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Street+Denizen), followed by [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers), [Madcap Skills](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Madcap+Skills), and [Martial Glory](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Martial+Glory). [Scorchwalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scorchwalker) gave Froehlich a combat trick or a potential 5/1, which he favored over [Burning-Tree Emissary](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burning-Tree+Emissary) in the same pack. [Bomber Corps](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bomber+Corps) late gave him another two mana creature, but his two drop slot was looking a little light. Surely Boros was open, and he could pick up some more two mana creatures in the third pack.

Right?

Well...not exactly.

The third pack yielded a first pick [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite), the second pick gave him [Holy Mantle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Holy+Mantle), and then a tough decision came in the third pack. He had to choose between [Armored Transport](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armored+Transport), which would go really well with his two [Madcap Skills](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Madcap+Skills) that he picked up in the second pack, or [Massive Raid](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Massive+Raid), which would give Froehlich his first removal spell. He opted to choose [Massive Raid](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Massive+Raid), but another came later, making him regret having to choose the removal earlier in the pack. No other two drops came, although he did find an [Angelic Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Edict) which could give him outs to potentially large threats.

After the match, Froehlich explained his frustration when he was making some of his picks.

"The guild that was open was the guild that I wanted to draft," said Froehlich. He had a clear preference for Orzhov, especially since he believe [Grisly Spectacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grisly+Spectacle) is one of the best cards that you can take, with only a few cards and rares being better than it.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/85/f6/85f68e979baeb5503732a576016d8a08b6692ac5b90e2e13d44f9eca296e876b.jpg)  
*Eric Froehlich's been all Boros all the time today, and while his Top 8 deck has a few weak points, it is the best one he's drafted this weekend.* 

With three copies of [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite), two [Skyknight Legionnaire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skyknight+Legionnaire)s, and two [Madcap Skills](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Madcap+Skills), Froehlich's got the potential for some very fast starts. However, his lack of two mana creatures could hurt, with only two copies of [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers) and [Bomber Corps](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bomber+Corps) at his disposal. He settled with seventeen lands, forgoing his [Boros Guildgate](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Guildgate)s in favor of always making a first-turn play. The Guildgates were regrettable choices, he noted, as he never wants them when he could play a turn one creature, and could have picked a potentially sideboard-worthy [Zarichi Tiger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zarichi+Tiger) over one of the picks.

However, given the capabilities of his deck, it's possible that Froehlich's concerns and missed opportunities may not be a worry in the long run. One mana creatures that can attack as 3/3s can be rough when backed up with the right draws, and Froehlich noted that this was the best Boros deck he was able to draft all day.

Will it serve him well in the remaining rounds of this tournament? We'll see soon!

## Quarterfinals – Eric Froehlich (Boros) vs. Matthew Falcioni (Dimir splashing Green)

*by Jacob Van Lunen*

 

### Game 1

Falcioni finished higher in the standings, giving him the opportunity to play first. Froehlich led things off with [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite) on the first turn. Falcioni didn't have a two drop and started getting hit by Froehlich's Elite. Froehlich quickly established a scary board with [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers). Falcioni tried to regain some control of the vehicle with corpse blockade, but [Skyknight Legionnaire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skyknight+Legionnaire) came down for Froehlich and Falcioni felt the wrath of an optimal Boros draw; Falcioni blocked the [Halberdier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Halberdier)s, but took five anyway.

Falcioni pondered his options. It was going to be difficult to overcome the near-perfect Boros draw from Froehlich. [Duskmantle Guildmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duskmantle+Guildmage) came down and he passed the turn. Froehlich elected to just attack for two in the air with his [Skyknight Legionnaire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skyknight+Legionnaire) before passing the turn with Falcioni at 12.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ad/d4/add409f3c76a01e569ad014bdd2b1fee43fb413d137f9311dac528b40009ffc1.jpg)  
*Matthew Falcioni* 

Under a lot of pressure, Falcioni needed to find an answer because his current hand didn't have the necessary tools to beat Froehlich's board; Falcioni cast [Urban Evolution](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Urban+Evolution), played an extra land, and passed the turn back to Froehlich.

Eric wasted no time sending his whole team into the red zone. Duskmantle guildmage jumped in front of [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite), and [Corpse Blockade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corpse+Blockade) got in the way of [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers). [Corpse Blockade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corpse+Blockade) was activated to kill the [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers), but Eric had another [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite) and [Zarichi Tiger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zarichi+Tiger) to further press his advantage.

Falcioni tried to stabilize with [Balustrade Spy](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Balustrade+Spy), but [Act of Treason](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Act+of+Treason) from Froehlich was good enough to end this lightning fast game.

Eric Froehlich 1 - Matthew Falcioni 0

![](https://i.ormos.uk/54/7b/547bd03c91faba58a5f7d83e078e9257fbf2cbd356a6c433e7d12b9410ee293d.jpg)  
*Eric Froehlich* 

### Game 2

Falcioni made a friendly gesture by offering Froehlich pretzels between games. Froehlich politely declined as they presented decks. Falcioni chose to mulligan and Froehlich decided to keep his opening seven.

Froehlich got off to a quick start with [Boros Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Elite) again. His deck is packing 3 copies of the powerhouse one drop, so it's not unreasonable or strange to see it on the first turn from him, especially on the draw. [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers) further advanced Eric's board presence, Falcioni tried to get back into the game with [Gateway Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gateway+Shade), but [Hellraiser Goblin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hellraiser+Goblin) came down on Froehlich's side and left Falciona on the backfoot against multiple Battalion triggers. [Bomber Corps](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bomber+Corps) was next and Falcioni was in a pretty awkward spot when he was forced to block Froehlich's [Wojek Halberdiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wojek+Halberdiers) with his [Gateway Shade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gateway+Shade). Froehlich's next attack was good enough to end the game.

**Eric Froehlich 2 - Matthew Falcioni 0**

## Quarterfinals – Brock Parker vs. Michael Derczo

*by Frank Karsten*

 

This quarterfinals pitted together Michael Derczo, in his first Grand Prix Top 8, and Brock Parker, a member of the of the Pro Tour Boston 2003 winning team. Parker drafted an excellent Orzhov deck with a ton of extort creatures. Derczo's draft didn't go as well as he'd hoped.

Derczo was one of four (!) players who had dabbled into Gruul cards. To be fair, there were only two actual Gruul drafters at the table (which might already be more than that guild can support), but two other drafters had snagged up [Clan Defiance](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Clan+Defiance) and [Zhur-Taa Swine](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zhur-Taa+Swine) as well for a splash. Accordingly, Derczo had to fill out his deck not with broken mythic rares, but with 3 [Armored Transport](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armored+Transport) and an [Ivy Lane Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ivy+Lane+Denizen) that had only four other green creatures to trigger off. Not exactly ideal.

### Game 1

Parker got to play first and wasted no time shipping his 7 cards. Derczo thought on his opening hand of 5 lands, [Ivy Lane Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ivy+Lane+Denizen) and [Armored Transport](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armored+Transport) for a while, before deciding to take a mulligan. After that, the game got underway with both players down to 6 cards.

Parker was the first to get into the red zone, sending in his [Gutter Skulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gutter+Skulk). ## Semifinals – Eric Froehlich vs. Alec Nezin

*by Mike Rosenberg*

 

Eric Froehlich blasted past his Quarterfinals opponent, Matthew Falcioni, in two quick games, showing off just how fast his deck can be. His opponent in the Semifinals, however, was Alec Nezin, who had drafted a brutal Simic deck splashing a little red for some bloodrush action. The creatures in Nezin's deck were big, and could give the smaller power of Froehlich's Boros creatures some fits.

However, we are talking about Froehlich, who had worked through harder circumstances. Could his Boros creature blast through Nezin's evolving creatures, or would green and blue giants triumph?

### Game 1

Nezin led with Simic mana but no action until turn three when he played [Crocanura](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crocanura). Froehlich's first play was [Court Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Court+Street+Denizen), which was locked down by [Hands of Binding](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hands+of+Binding). ## Finals – Alec Nezin vs. Brock Parker

*by Nate Price*

 

It has been a very long time since I've seen Brock Parker's name in type during the Top 8 of a Grand Prix, nearly a decade, in fact. Way back in 2004, Parker was eliminated in the Quarterfinals of Grand Prix Boston, six months after he and his teammates Matt Linde and William "Huey" Jensen took down the title at Pro Tour Boston. Now, nearly a decade later, he has made his made his way back to the top. One man stood between him and his first individual title: Alec Nezin, who was using this Grand Prix as his prerelease, and crushing along the way.

One of the most interesting things about this finals match was the fact that all five colors of **Magic** were represented between the two decks. While red is a minor splash for Nezin, the [Zhur-Taa Swine](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zhur-Taa+Swine) he is splashing for was instrumental in his reaching the finals, allowing him to win crucial games over both Chase Kovac in the Quarterfinals and Eric Froehlich in the Semis. In addition to representing all colors, this was a match between the darling of the *Gatecrash* Limited environment, Orzhov, against the highly maligned Simic. While opinions on Simic have certainly improved over the previous month, players still seemed to avoid blue cards unless they were also black, and green cards unless the were also red. And likely white.

In any case, the early leanings on this match seemed in Parker's favor. His heavy-extort deck was very well-positioned against Nezin's more defensively-oriented Simic deck. His ceratures certainly had the ability to get out of hand, with a fair amount of evolve, including two [Elusive Krasis](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elusive+Krasis), but Parker's ability to grind out life totals with extort and a reasonable amount of removal seemed better suited to win in a long match.

Parker was the first on the board with a [Gutter Skulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gutter+Skulk). Nezin immediately slammed a roadblock in the way, one of his two Frilled Occuluses. Occuli. Whatever. In any case, the 1/3 was a big barrier for the Rat to get through, and Parker just left it home on the following turn. Willing to leave a window open, Nezin decided to attack with the Occulus. Parker put his Skulk in the way, stone-faced, daring Nezin to pump it.

"Okay," Nezin said, content for the two creatures to bounce. After combat, he passed the turn to Parker with three lands untapped.

Parker was a bit slow on his draw, but Nezin wasn't punishing him any. A [Last Thoughts](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Last+Thoughts) was enough to draw one card, but he still wasn't ready to get through with his Occulus, leaving it home on defense. When Parker played a [Guardian of the Gateless](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Guardian+of+the+Gateless), his first play since the turn-two Rat, Nezin finally put the Occulus to work, pumping it and killing the Angel with a [Pit Fight](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pit+Fight). After another empty turn from the seemingly mana-screwed Parker, Nezin made a [Deathcult Rogue](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathcult+Rogue).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5e/93/5e935c32e74b952509cb6a1557af39889689c3fd2e0624c5306466aad74a0e91.jpg)  
*Alec Nezin* 

Parker found a third spell on the following turn, but his [Millennial Gargoyle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Millennial+Gargoyle) was in no position to do any attacking just yet. Parker was first going to have to get things a bit more under control First, he used an [Executioner's Swing](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Executioner%27s+Swing) to kill the [Deathcult Rogue](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathcult+Rogue) after getting hit for two. The following turn brought an [Angelic Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Edict) fot the Occulus. Nezin was without a board.

It was merely a stalling tactic, however, as Nezin had no board presence to back it up.

The next couple turns brought a gradual rebuilding for both players. Parker makde a [Dutiful Thrull](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dutiful+Thrull), [Basilica Guards](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Basilica+Guards), and [Knight Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+Watch) to fill his board. His found a second Krasis soon after, as well as a [Mindeye Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindeye+Drake) that might as well have been unblockable for the time being. Parker's only air creature was the lonely [Millennial Gargoyle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Millennial+Gargoyle). A [Zarichi Tiger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zarichi+Tiger) from Parker topped things off, resulting in an incredibly cluttered board.

As Parker's life dipped to 10, things swung incredibly in his favor. [Angelic Skirmisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Skirmisher) gave his team lifelink, allowing the Milennial Gargoyle to swing over for a four-point life swing. Alec Nezin 1 - Brock Parker 1

Parker started off conspicuously with three [Plains](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plains), leading into a Basilica Guard. A second Guard on the following turn off of those same three [Plains](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plains) gave a clear indication of what his hand looked like. Nezin began to build his board with a [Crocanura](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crocanura) and a [Scab-Clan Charger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scab-Clan+Charger), but they were not the best form of offense against a team of 1/4s.

Parker found his [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) on the following turn, enabling a [Gutter Skulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gutter+Skulk) and a pair of extort triggers. It also allowed him to try a [Wight of Precinct Six](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wight+of+Precinct+Six) on the following turn, but a [Spell Rupture](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Rupture) kept it from the table.

While Parker's offense came mostly through extort at this point, Nezin wasn't amassing much of an offense either. His fifth turn brought an [Urban Evolution](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Urban+Evolution), drawing three cards and putting a pair of lands into play. This allowed him to cast [Prime Speaker Zegana](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Prime+Speaker+Zegana) for much less than seven cards on the following turn. After that, he found himself his first source of offense, an [Elusive Krasis](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elusive+Krasis), but Parker was starting with a big head start.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ac/33/ac33676b282cd585d5fdd42727fa4310ad41671fd1ef07ba535f0e0958741634.jpg)  
*Brock Parker* 

Parker had driven his life up to 26 casting a [Dutiful Thrull](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dutiful+Thrull) before Nezin began to attack. The Krasis grew to a 2/6 thanks to a [Shambleshark](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shambleshark) and a [Mindeye Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindeye+Drake), giving him a steady stream of damage. Still holding four cards in his hand, Nezin looked to have turned a corner. PArker wasn't that far out of it, though. With a grip full of cards himself, he surely had an answer or two for Nezin's attempts to get at his life total.

With a peel of a second [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) from his deck, Parker found what he had been looking for: a way to cast his [Angelic Skirmisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Skirmisher). While a 2/5 [Crocanura](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crocanura) and [Mindeye Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindeye+Drake) prevented the Skirmisher from attacking, Parker would have a nearly unlimited capability to increase his life total. This was increasingly true as Parker continued to add creatures to his board, beginning with two Knight tokens from a [Knight Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+Watch).

Nezin found yet another [Crocanura](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crocanura), setting up that impressive defensive front his deck was capable of. While Parker wasn't able to get through with his creatures, extort gave him all the reach he needed. A [Vizkopa Confessor](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vizkopa+Confessor) gave him access to a third extort trigger, as well as knocking a [Scatter Arc](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scatter+Arc) from a hand of two lands and another [Scab-Clan Charger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scab-Clan+Charger). This cost him four life, dropping 20 to Nezin's 10.

Nezin began to send more attackers. An [Adaptive Snapjaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adaptive+Snapjaw) further evolved the [Elusive Krasis](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elusive+Krasis), and the 3/7 joined a [Mindeye Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindeye+Drake) int he red zone. Parker dropped to 15, still a far cry from being in danger. When he cleared out Nezin's hand with a [Purge the Profane](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Purge+the+Profane) on the following turn, leaving him the Charger as his only card, he swung the life totals to 20-7.

![Angelic Edict](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Angelic+Edict)

Nezin added a [Last Thoughts](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Last+Thoughts) to his Krasis, giving him a stream of cards in addition to damage, but his window to actually kill Parker before the extort triggers became lethal was growing short. The same two creatures again attacked Parker to 15, and Nezin passed the turn with a pair of cards in hand and a boatload of mana available.

Once again, as in the first game, the board had become clogged with creatures far more sutied for protecting rather than attacking. Parker had managed to do all sixteen damage thus far to Nezin with only extort triggers, and things got tense as he closed in on the home stretch. When he drew, tapped his eight lands, and aimed an [Angelic Edict](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Edict) at the Krasis, the tension seemed to drain away. His victory seemed almost assured.

With one lat-ditch effort, Nezin sent every creature but his two [Crocanura](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crocanura). [Angelic Skirmisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Skirmisher) gave Parker's team first strike, and he thought with his head in his hand as he lined up his blockers, leaving only the [Mindeye Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mindeye+Drake) free. After the dust cleared, Parker had lost his [Thrull](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thrull), while Nezin lost his [Snap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snap)jaw and the Charger he had been holding most of the game. Parker made a [Treasury Thrull](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasury+Thrull) on his turn, using two more extort triggers to regain the life he had lost while simultaneously putting Nezin one turn from dead.

Nezin tried yet again, this time bringing the whole team. Parker's squad once again gained first strike, and he lined up his blockers to once again only let the Drake through. This gave each blocker a single attacker. Again, only the Drake hit home. Parker had a slew of options of how to finish things off, from returning a creature with [Treasury Thrull](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treasury+Thrull) to extorting off of a spell in his hand. His final decision was to attack, [Arrows of Justice](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arrows+of+Justice) his own creature, and extort for the final points of Nezin's life. Parker had managed to take down the final game of the match taking every point of life with extort.

"No byes. A GP Winner with no byes," Dave Williams cheered as Parker stood up from the table.

"He played, like, 31 matches this weekend," Eric Froehlich smiled with a shake of his head.

It took every single round of play for Parker to reach his title, but the reward was well worth it as he walked off, surrounded by friends, to pick up his first individual Grand Prix trophy.

## Top 5 Cards of Grand Prix Pittsburgh

*by Jacob Van Lunen*

 

 

![Angelic Skirmisher](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Angelic+Skirmisher)

 

### 5\. [Angelic Skirmisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Skirmisher)

[Angelic Skirmisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angelic+Skirmisher) is one of the best six drops available for Orzhov and Boros Limited decks. Brock Parker's Orzhov deck was able to ride the Angel to victory in the finals where its trigger gained him well over ten life despite it being permanently tapped down by [Hands of Binding](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hands+of+Binding). The ability to grant his entire team First Strike on his opponent's turn allowed him to draw the game out long enough to eventually close the game with a few evasive bodies and extort triggers.

  
  
  
  
  
  

 

![Prime Speaker Zegana](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Prime+Speaker+Zegana)

 

### 4\. [Prime Speaker Zegana](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Prime+Speaker+Zegana)

Alec Nezin's Simic deck made excellent use of [Prime Speaker Zegana](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Prime+Speaker+Zegana) throughout the top 8. In the finals, a [Sylvan Primordial](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sylvan+Primordial) into [Prime Speaker Zegana](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Prime+Speaker+Zegana) allowed Nezin to draw a back breaking seven cards.

  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  

 

![Grisly Spectacle](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Grisly+Spectacle)

  
  
  
  
  

### 3\. [Grisly Spectacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grisly+Spectacle)

Virtually every pro agreed when asked what their common of choice for *Gatecrash* Limited was. [Grisly Spectacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grisly+Spectacle) is an all-encompassing instant-speed spot removal spell that increases the power level of other Black commons and uncommons like [Death's Approach](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Death%27s+Approach) and [Wight of Precinct Six](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wight+of+Precinct+Six). The survey is in and [Grisly Spectacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grisly+Spectacle) is about as premium as it gets in *Gatecrash* Limited.

  
  

 

![Burst of Strength](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Burst+of+Strength)

 

### 2\. [Burst of Strength](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burst+of+Strength)

The underplayed Green instant was seen swinging games throughout the weekend. Alec Nezin used the card to great effect in his top 8 Simic draft deck. A timely [Burst of Strength](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burst+of+Strength) on [Adaptive Snapjaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adaptive+Snapjaw) in the Semifinals both protected him from Eric Froehlich's would-be lethal attack and gave him exactly lethal damage the following turn.

  
  
  
  
  
  

 

![Zarichi Tiger](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Zarichi+Tiger)

 

### 1\. [Zarichi Tiger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zarichi+Tiger)

It's easy to dismiss [Zarichi Tiger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zarichi+Tiger) as an unplayable, but the card made the cut in some of the most successful decks in this 1626 person event. Brock Parker's top 8 Orzhov deck used [Zarichi Tiger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220526074516/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zarichi+Tiger) to bait removal spells and win seemingly unwinnable races throughout the top 8.
