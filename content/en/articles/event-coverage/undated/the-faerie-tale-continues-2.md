![](https://i.ormos.uk/90/3f/903f6d91851e61602291c2666fac2abe41a43616dd8632eb2f1260f932b32c8b.jpg) Yuuta Takahashi and the Fae, it’s a match that can’t be beat. Quite literally, it turns out. After taking home the title in Shizuoka earlier this year, Takahashi has once again come out on top, with what practically amounts to the same deck. Showing an incredible aptitude with the archetype, he bested Katsuya Ueda in the mirror to take the quarterfinals, Masaya Tanahashi with Kithkin, also known as The Other best Deck in the semifinals, and Takayuki Takagi and his Doran, the Siege Tower deck in the finals. Congratulations to Yuuta Takahashi, the first back to back Japanese Grand Prix Champion!

Meanwhile, the Summer of Magic is set to continue. Next stop, Denver, Colorado August 9-10. Will Faeries continue to take home titles, or will one of the many other decks, as we’ve seen here in Kobe this weekend, step up to take down the Fae? Keep it locked here at magicthegathering.com and we’ll keep you in the know.

## Round 10: The Best Skizzik Ever - Shuuhei Nakamura vs Shingo Kurihara

*by Tim Willoughby*

Every now and then a feature match slips through the cracks. Sometimes, while trying to find a really interesting match to cover at speed around the time that pairings are posted, you just miss the big match between two name players. This can be a little awkward to say the least. There was no way that I was missing this one though. I’ve been waiting for a good opportunity to feature Shuuhei Nakamura’s deck since round 4 yesterday. Dubbed ‘the greedy deck’ it is yet another original creation to come from Nakamura who has been having a wickedly good season, and is currently pushing to become Player of the Year.

Nakamura won the die roll and elected to play. He had a dangerous looking turn two [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) to his opponent’s [Bloom Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloom+Tender), but followed up with [Silvergill Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvergill+Adept), revealing [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret). This was not your average faeries deck.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/91/e1/91e1a3ff823c2bfa048c8a2ea0ab233d6cdb95078acc522b622e973678516f8e.jpg)*Kurihara comes out swinging!*Kurihara wasn’t trying anything cute or clever. His elemental beatdown deck, as designed by Tomoharu Saito does have some tricks, but let there be no doubt, it is basically a deck that makes massive monsters and swings. He cast Chamelion Colossus and passed. Nakamura cracked in in the air with a Faerie Rogue, and took a hit back from the Colossus. He had mana up ready for something, but allowed [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) to kill his Adept, before playing [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) on an evoked [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter), bouncing that Colossus.

Shuuhei seemed content to let [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) be his offence, keeping his mana up during Kurihara’s turns. He had another [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) for that Colossus when Kurihara tried to replay it, and attacked to make the life totals 12 each before playing [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret), leaving two mana up.

Kurihara was unafraid of counters from Nakamura, playing [Horde of Notions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horde+of+Notions) and smiling a little upon it’s resolution. His [Bloom Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloom+Tender) then tapped for WUBRG to allow [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus) to come back.

A pained expression came across Shuuhei’s face. This could be tough.

As it turned out though, Nakamura’s draw step held the answer, in [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation), to take [Horde of Notions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horde+of+Notions).

After a little thought, Kurihara slammed a [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout) onto the table. If it resolved, that would be the ball game...

Nakamura scooped up his cards.

Shuuhei Nakamura 0 – 1 Shingo Kurihara

During sideboarding, Kurihara complained that he didn’t know what to take out or put in... not so surprising against the hybrid deck of Nakamura’s. Tempted to put in all 15, then take out 15 at random, he eventually settled on an ideal configuration, declaring proudly that he’d done some pretty weird sideboarding. Shuuhei was all smiles. Going first for game 2, his changes had been quick, and appeared powerful against elementals – including a set of [Puppeteer Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Puppeteer+Clique)s which would surely have plenty of targets.

On the play, Nakamura kept a five land hand, and had to show it off on Kurihara’s very first turn, when [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) came along, taking [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret), leaving just [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation) as a spell in his hand. There were two [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s there, so it was certainly far from bad news.

The bad news was for Kuriahara. When he played [Bloom Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloom+Tender), he found that Nakamura had topdecked a [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke), which along with [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) even drew Nakamura a card. Nakamura, short on counterspells, went on the offensive. With his land heavy draw, [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) looked quite the threat. Kurihara had another [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize), which took that [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation), and revealed nothing but lands beyond that.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/50/f5/50f5a332897fc5b58155354027926fab66ab4f774904c4e1c86a973ab7c5e663.jpg)*Nakamura carefully plans his next move.*Somehow, Nakamura remained the beatdown in what had started quite a strange game. His [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) rumbled in for five, taking Kurihara to 9. Shingo struck back though, with a [Horde of Notions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horde+of+Notions) who off a [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider) that had come the turn before. This alone looked quite the threat, as it could get back [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) at any time, assuming that Kurihara could find the five mana he needded.

At the end of Nakamura’s turn, this is exactly what Kurihara did. The ‘best [Skizzik](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skizzik) ever’ as described by Tomoharu Saito tried it’s best to off [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity), but a [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) countered the spell, and bounced the legend.

It came straight back down again, and attacked Nakamura to 5. The legend on its own looked likely to be able to do the full 20. If ever there was a time for thinking for Nakamura it was now. His back against the wall, he did the maths of how a big [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) attack should go. He had 9 damage to do, but there were two blockers up to his potential two attackers. [Horde of Notions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horde+of+Notions) and [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider) vs [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) and [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) didn’t excite Nakamura too much. After much thought, he ran in with just [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity), taking out [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider). he then played [Puppeteer Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Puppeteer+Clique), removing that [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider) from the game.

The persist faerie would be a troublesome blocker for that [Horde of Notions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horde+of+Notions), or a powerful attacker given that [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity). Unfortunately for Nakamura though, there was an on board trick that would work for Kurihara. [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) on his own [Horde of Notions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Horde+of+Notions) would allow sufficient trample damage through. Would Kurihara spot it?

Unfortunately for Nakamura, he did.

Shingo Kurihara defeats Shuuhei Nakamura 2-0!

  

## Round 10: Shhh Happens - Katsuya Ueda versus Tsuyoshi Ikeda

*By Ray “blisterguy” Walkinshaw*

As day one came to a close, three undefeated players remained. While Masayasu Tanahashi was paired down to play off against one of the 8-1’s, Katsuya Ueda and Tsuyoshi Ikeda dutifully took up residence on table one. Of course, we were having none of that, and herded them towards the feature match area.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/be/c2/bec2e5f331cc2534b19785706ca2395a4f706ae82f69518d39d2d7cbf2af7736.jpg)*Ueda takes control of the situation*On paper, Ikeda definitely has the better matchup here, with his Red deck being one of the better options for taking down the ever present and always strong Faerie decks, much like the one Ueda is running. He even got off to a roaring start with a six on the die to play first, leading with an [Intimidator Initiate](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Intimidator+Initiate) and a [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider). The ‘Braider fell to a [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke) from Ueda however, before a [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) came into play. If Ueda was to win any games against Red, this was just the kind of start he needed. The [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) gave Ueda control of the game, and allowed him to sit back on his mana, leaving himself able to counter any serious threats from Ikeda. A [Vexing Shusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vexing+Shusher) went down to a [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion), and when Ueda had the [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) for a [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger), Ikeda moved for his sideboard to end Game 1.

Both players stalled on two lands briefly in Game 2, each chuckling as Ikeda made small men and Ueda killed them and dropped a [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom). Once both players had doubled up to four lands each, Ikeda made an [Ashenmoor Gouger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ashenmoor+Gouger) and Ueda Championed his [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) to a [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique). A [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) beefed up Ikeda’s small men, and a [Tarfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarfire) helped kill of the Clique, only to see second one come down and a [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) bounce the Gouger. A [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) paired up with a [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke) to kill the Gouger when it returned, but Ikeda found and played a [Vexing Shusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vexing+Shusher), that allowed him to topdeck a [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) and play it unfettered. Ueda counted up the number of +1/+1 counters on Ikeda’s creatures and with an impending attack step, scooped up his cards.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/07/31/0731a2ceb70c5972597e79987edb923a27aef1342eaa9e444ad1e47959178de9.jpg)*Ikeda stacks his draws with Elemental Harbinger*Both players started a little slower in Game 3, with no turn one drop for Ikeda, and Ueda’s [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) not arriving until turn three. Ikeda played a [Vexing Shusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vexing+Shusher), Harbinger’d up another [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) and deployed a [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider). Although Ueda was going to soon have an Army of Faeries to defend him, he wasn’t going to be able to stop Ikeda from practically dropping his hand into play the over the next two turns. Sure enough, down came another Shusher and the Forger pushed past a [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite), which was at least able to trade with an incoming [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider). Meanwhile, Ueda dropped dangerously low on life. With two Shushers already in play, Ikeda flashed Ueda a hand full of [Flame Javelin](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Javelin)s, [Flame Jab](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Jab)s, acetylene torches and a single box of matches. The Faerie player hurriedly packed up his cards with a laugh.

Tsuyoshi Ikeda defeats Katsuya Ueda 2-1

  

## Round 11: Keeping the Streak Alive - Masayasu Tanahashi vs Tsuyoshi Ikeda

*By Tim Willoughby*

This would be it – following the showdown in round 11, there would be just one undefeated player left at Grand Prix Kobe. On one side of the table, Masayasu Tanahashi, playing a Kithkin deck greatly bolstered by the presence of the one man battle, [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny). Opposing him, the finely chapeau-ed Tsuyoshi Ikeda, with the Red Shaman deck designed by Tsuyoshi Fujita, containing such *Eventide* hits as [Flame Jab](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Jab) and a set of Thunderbluffs, which are capable of dealing a huge amount of damage if left unopposed.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/83/6f/836ff09a7e5b3d32c5443b0485836fde288d4ea7b150be511769c43b9c616a10.jpg)*Tanahashi deals a beating.*Tanahashi won the roll and led with Goldemeadow Stalwart. Ikeda had [Flamekin Harbinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flamekin+Harbinger) for [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider), and had to look on as the beats began as early as turn two. He used a [Tarfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarfire) on [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) before it could get active, but continued to take beats from that turn one 2/2, and could only look on as [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession) filled up Tanahashi’s board.

[Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider) and another Harbinger came down from Ikeda, the latter fetching [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) – the elemental that cold spell a huge amount of damage. That damage would need to be quick coming, as Ikeda dropped to 9 on attacks. Another [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) briefly graced the board with its presence, only for [Tarfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarfire) to finish it off before it could get out of hand.

[Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) powered up Team Red, and [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) did much the same for... oh wait, most of Tanahashi’s Team White were Spirits, and as such unconcerned with the Cenn, Wizened or otherwise. Tanahashi attacked Ikeda down to 6 with his creatures, cast [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny), and prepared for a Big Red [Assault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Assault).

Ikeda cocked his hat to a jaunty angle, and had a look at the situation. There were 3 blockers up, and he had 13 damage to deal. He couldn’t find that damage. Instead, he cast a Thunderbluff and passed. Tanahashi took the opportunity to get in for 3 in the air, played a [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) and another [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny). The game seemed to be slipping from Tanahashi’s grasp. While the board was locked up on the ground, in the air, those Spirit tokens were going unopposed. It was after much though that Ikeda ruefully used a [Flame Javelin](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Javelin) on one of them just to keep himself from dying. When [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger) showed up for Tanahashi, that was enough.

Masayasu Tanahashi 1 – 0 Tsuyoshi Ikeda

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5d/d4/5dd4805a46c5e72d7678fdbcfdc2f0b5ccc7c1f1bad40bbb73f67639066f7379.jpg)*Ikeda returns the beating!*Ikeda led off with a [Flamekin Harbinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flamekin+Harbinger), going straight for [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger). Tanahashi, off a mulligan, had [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) revealing [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn). Not the worst mulligan then. The flying start from the red deck was going to be tough to beat though, as back to back [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider)s were followed by [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger).

When another [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) came the following turn, Tanahashi was taking 8 damage on the swings *before* the declare blockers step. With just 2 creatures on the board, he scooped up his cards. This was not a fight the Kithkin could win.

Masayasu Tanahashi 1 – 1 Tsuyoshi Ikeda

Game 3 started with a [Burrenton Forge-Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burrenton+Forge-Tender) from Tanahashi, who had no immediate follow-up. Ikeda had a turn two [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider), but winced a little when he saw a [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession) – his bane from game 1. He had an Ashling, the Pilgrim, but would have to get rid of [Burrenton Forge-Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burrenton+Forge-Tender) before he could do much with her.

[Ajani Goldmane](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Goldmane) from Tanahashi made the ‘getting rid of Forge-Tender’ plan look rather tough, as the planeswalker effectively shortened the clock on Ikeda by a number of turns, letting the white weenie team bulk up pretty effectively. With a small smile, Tanahashi played [Moonglove Extract](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Moonglove+Extract). He immediately killed the protection from red kithkin. All that was left to worry about were all those Spirits, and the Planeswalker leading them.

As it turns out there was nothing that Ikeda could do. His [Ember Gale](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ember+Gale) would no longer be good enough. Another set of pumps and attacks was enough to finish things.

Masayasu Tanahashi defeats Tsuyoshi Ikeda 2-1, advancing to 11-0 in the tournament!

## Round 12: Shuuhei Nakamura versus Takakuyi Takagi

*by Ray “blisterguy” Walkinshaw*

Takakuyi Takagi got off to a strong start with his [Wren’s Run Vanquisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wren%E2%80%99s+Run+Vanquisher) charging in and backed up by a Wilt-Leaf Cavalier. Shuuhei Nakamura’s [Greed](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Greed)y Three Color Merfolk deck looked like it might run into a spot of difficulty, until of course, a [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation) took the Cavalier under its wing. A [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret) powered up a [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing) from Nakamura to take down a [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus), while a [Wolf-Skull Shaman](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wolf-Skull+Shaman) began to fill out Takagi’s side of the table. The Cavalier was doing a great job of holding them back however, while Nakamura was taking every opportunity to send his Sower in for two. [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) stopped a [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) from reversing the tide, but this left room for a [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout) to clear out everything but the Cavalier, who had now returned to its rightful owner. Nakamura recovered with a [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey), and had another [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) to not only stop Takagi’s [Profane Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Profane+Command), but bounce the Cavalier as well. [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) joined the Reejerey, and together with a [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) they began to attack to even up the life totals. With no apparent answer to the Entity, elected to Takagi scoop up his cards.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/0b/c2/0bc2fbf85059d7c1d02f4595d9dfde4c79ee886c2392082cfae32ef55d3c82b8.jpg)*Takagi watches as Game 1 slips away from him*When a [Treefolk Harbinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treefolk+Harbinger) tutored up a [Doran, the Siege Tower](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doran%2C+the+Siege+Tower) on turn two, and Nakamura missed his third land drop, everybody knew we’d soon be shuffling up for Game 3. I wish I could say more, but repeatedly taking five to the face doesn’t leave much room for literary expression.

The third game looked much better for Nakamura, who had the [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) for Takagi’s turn two [Wren’s Run Vanquisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wren%E2%80%99s+Run+Vanquisher), and a [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) to fill out his hand. Another [Vanquish](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vanquish)er came down and Nakamura again got a little light fingered, stealing it with a [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation). Takagi busted that plan right open with a simple [Profane Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Profane+Command) for two. Killing the Sower and getting back the recently demised [Vanquish](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vanquish)er. Nakamura tried to rally with a [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret) and a [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey), but again Takagi decimated the board, this time with a [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) on the Reejerey, the [Vanquish](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vanquish)ers now finishing off Jace. Nakamura was unable to find a suitable way to stop Takagi’s three powered attackers, and things looked especially dire when a Doran showed up and Nakamura’s only reply was to cycle a [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing) on it.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1e/cb/1ecb6bb1410da38f1e0caaceee30f8063f99ed2a7346adcd2a1ac3889223abdd.jpg)*Nakamura pondering his play*Nakamura played a [Secluded Glen](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Secluded+Glen) untapped, revealing another [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation), and started thinking about how best to play his turn. Could he turn the game around and steal it out from under Takagi’s nose? He played another [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret), and stole the Doran with the Sower for the low, low price of UU. Doran happened to be Takagi’s only uptapped creature, so Nakamura animated two of his [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s and swung in with the lands, the original Banneret and the [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey) to halve Takagi’s health in one swoop. However, Takagi just untapped and took back the Legendary Treefolk with the help of a [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) and swung back to drop his opponent to a mere three life. Nakamura was once again outnumbered and outgunned. He drew a [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) off the top and played it for a single White mana, decided very quickly that he was in no position to attack again and shipped the turn. Takagi untapped and dropped a profane Command on the table. Nakamura could only break out his now nearly trademark grin and shuffle up his cards.

Takakuyi Takagi defeats Shuuhei Nakamura 2-1

  

## Blog: 12:35pm - Wary of the Heat

*by Tim Willoughby*

Mad dogs and Englishmen, it has been suggested, go out in the midday sun. Alas, by this reasoning, it was inevitable that I would end up getting surprisingly hot for a feature this weekend. It has been suggested that there might be such thing as a Kobe Summer Diet. Eat as much as you want – if you walk outside for an hour or two each day, you’ll sweat it all off.

Today I decided to find out how foils stand up to the crazy humidity if left to their own devices in the sun. After a trip to the dealers, for the princely sum of 50 Yen I had my companion, one [Norin the Wary](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Norin+the+Wary). My cardboard companion started out pretty flat, but all it took was a moderate amount of time in the heat, and he was developing curves like Oprah on the off-season. ## Feature: Deck Tech with Shuuhei Nakamura

*by Tim Willoughby*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a9/4f/a94ff5ae4516025376514cbe997eac42e961c4e23fdb06f4d71e4afeee061110.jpg)Shuuhei Nakamura is one of those players who I see everywhere, because essentially, if there is a premier event happening, he *will* be there. For GP Kobe, he seemed particularly excited about his deck, and judging by his performance thus far, this feeling is entirely justified.

Designed by Yuuya Watanabe, the PWC Special (named after the Planeswalker’s Cup, a big tournament run in Tokyo of which Yuuya is the current champion) could equally easily be described as ‘The Greedy Man Special’.

Originally designed as a deck to make the most of the interaction between [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) and [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity), this deck, which has been played by Shuuhei, Yuuya and Katsuhiro Mori among others, has changed quite a bit since its inception. Where initially it was black/white, it was with the inclusion of merfolk that it really started to tick. In many respects it plays like a Merfolk/Faeries hybrid deck, but with it’s very own additional twists. In the main deck, the only card that uses white mana is [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity). Out of the sideboard though, there is access to [Wispmare](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wispmare) and [Hallowed Burial](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hallowed+Burial). Mori even went as far as having an [Arbiter of Knollridge](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arbiter+of+Knollridge) in there in order to be able to fight burn more effectively!

It seems fitting that as the hybrid block completes, we start finding hybrids of existing archetypes. This merfolk/faerie mix might be one to look out for at PTQs to come.

## Round 15: White Lightning - Shuu Komuro vs Masayasu Tanahashi

*by Tim Willoughby*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fc/12/fc1254d8ea7fc75352baf72887d3af84fbc32a32739499fcd3e69a5c7cdd517c.jpg)*The winner here goes on...*And so it comes down to the final round before top eight. For most there is still some work to do. For many, the need is to both win, and then get a little lucky. For Shuu Komuro it is very simple. A win and they are in.

Komuro, the first Japanese player to win a limited Pro Tour, winced as he had to mulligan his opener. He didn’t have a play for the first two turns beyond attacking with his [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault). Tanahashi, meanwhile, had a turn two [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn). Just a turn later Komuro had one of his own, but was trumped by a turn three that saw another Cenn from Tanahashi, and a [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny). Ajani made things thoroughly unfair. He attacked with his 3 creatures, all of which were 4/4s thanks to Ajani, and vigilant ones at that.

Komuro’s head was in his hands. He had to let both his [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s die to be able to block both [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn), and was faced with the concerning prospect of that [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) getting huge. As it happens, this never came to be though. When Tanahashi played a [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger) the following turn, that was enough to convince Komuro that it was time to scoop it up.

Shuu Komuro 0 – 1 Masayasu Tanahashi

For Game 2 it was Tanahashi’s turn to mulligan. He still had a turn one [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart), and a second, along with [Windbrisk Heights](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windbrisk+Heights) on turn two. Komuro was forced to laugh as he saw [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) join the other side of the board, while he had still yet to make a play.

Tanahashi attacked with his team, and revealed a [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn). There was little fighting back from that. Komuro quickly drew, and scooped up his cards. While it was the Kithkin’s day, it was not the day for *his* Kithkin.

Masayasu Tanahashi wins 2 – 0, locking up his place in the top 8!

## Feature: Ask the Artists

*by Tim Willoughby*

Here in Kobe Japan we are lucky enough to have not one but two eminent **Magic** artists who from the very start of the day are busy doing sketches and signings for an ever-increasing queue.

Both Jim Murray and Chippy Dugan originally hail from the United Kingdom, but somewhat bafflingly, they have travelled to Kobe from Montreal and Melbourne respectively. Any jet-lag is well behind them now though, and the queuing masses have to be thankful, as they dutifully wait for their cards to be signed.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2e/de/2ede92debfa528f10f5b46dc6c7951f2c558bf58832724622e5572aa23c8c2ab.jpg)*Jim Murray*Jim originally started in fantasy artwork illustrating British comic *2000AD*, including Judge Dredd and Slaine, before transitioning onto Batman. This, if nothing else, makes him unassailably cool in my book. For those really on the ball, you might even spot some of his work in the hit British TV show *Spaced* by the team that made *Sean of the Dead.* 

Jim’s first **Magic** cards were in *Darksteel*, and included the powerhouse 7/10 [Sundering Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sundering+Titan). Following up with [Razormane Masticore](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Razormane+Masticore) (now back in 10th Edition). More recently, the dynamic duo of [Wolf-Skull Shaman](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wolf-Skull+Shaman) and [Bramblewood Paragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bramblewood+Paragon) have been terrorising standard, as has [Reveillark](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reveillark), all on its lonesome.

[Reveillark](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reveillark) is the card that Jim has been signing the most all weekend, and he seemed pretty pleased to find out that it has it’s very own deck. Not a player himself, Jim has to manage his jobs of freelance illustration and concept art for a computer games company against raising a family. Unfortunately this hasn’t left him a great deal of time for learning to play just yet.

That said, Jim is loving the travel that comes with being a bit of **Magic** celebrity. The trip to Pro Tour London wasn’t all that far for him back when he did his first signing, but since then he’s been at World in New York, and now for the first time, he’s had a chance to visit Japan. On the first night, Jim might have been a little hesitant about some of the raw fish on offer, but by Saturday night he was wolfing it down like an old pro.

All of Jim’s work, which is primarily character based, to suit his comicbook background, is painstakingly painted onto canvas, meaning that he has a wide array of original artwork on sale this weekend. He’s already sold the [Gaea’s Herald](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gaea%E2%80%99s+Herald) original art, and there are many other incredible originals on offer. Oh but for the money with which to buy them.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f2/2d/f22dea48d6a2cf4231a436d05aff03df0c62f7ed88231da47cb7341db062a498.jpg)*Chippy Dugan*Chippy Dugan, whose Scottish accent probably sounds a little out of place in Australia where he currently resides, got into illustration through being a gamer. Playing RPGs while growing up, there was a natural transition for him into doing fantasy illustration. In fact now, he is involved with the artwork for the new 4th edition of Dungeons and Dragons, along with his long term relationship with **Magic**.

Chippy had his first cards way back in Mirage, with Tainted Specter, Haunting Apparition, and Cycle of Life, a rare example of cards with faeries in before their recent resurgence. His latest hot numbers include [Bloom Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloom+Tender) and [Overbeing of Myth](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overbeing+of+Myth).

While back in the day, Chippy worked in paint, but now he is exclusively digital in his artwork. This doesn’t necessarily change his creative process in a big way, but means that he can easier create exactly the image he wants when he’s settled on it. Everything is still ‘painted’ but by being done on computer, control of colour is made that much easier, and the process generally flows for him more easily.

Chippy has worked on a large variety of cards over the years, including various lands, which is something that Jim is less inclined to move into. “Jeremy \[Jarvis, the WoTC art director for Magic\], is very good at putting people where they do their best work when it comes to picking artists for cards.” Chippy was happy to say, and following his initial leanings towards black cards, he has now branched out across the whole spectrum.

Normally the artists receive a fairly brief brief of what they should be painting, with a general description, along with some background, and details like the colour of the card that affects the palette that they might choose. This leaves quite a lot of room for creativity, and each artist will do a few concepts before sending through just the one sketch that is how they see the card.

“You don’t want to send more than one sketch, you just want to send what you want to draw” quipped Jim. He had a fairly bizarre brief when it came to [Reveillark](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reveillark) which is a picture of... well, the brief more or less describes what it is. A lot of wings with some shining gleaming eggs in the middle is one of the tougher images to easily conceptualise, but Jim has done a sterling job.

Between them, Jim and Chippy have been really busy this weekend, and judging from how much they seem to have enjoyed themselves, it seems likely that we will see them again at an event sometime very soon.

  

## Yuuta Takahashi vs Katsuya Ueda

*by Tim Willoughby*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/54/7a/547a6c375f15530d2d47483f5db8f3aada62738a342452e827edea37be9dce2a.jpg)The quarterfinals of Grand Prix Kobe suddenly saw a crowd descend on the feature match area. Now there was only one place where the action would happen, and everyone was keen to get the best spot. With four great matches to choose from, I elected to get my seat in on the action looking at Takayuki Takagi and Hiroshi Yosida. It was no coincidence that from here I had a clear view of what GP Shizuoka winner Yuuta Takahashi was up to. The crowd favourite to win the whole show, Takahashi had a faeries mirror in the quarterfinals. He has plent y of practice with them though, having had to win one in Shizuoka against Olivier Ruel to win his last title.

Yosida watched his opponent mulligan, and kept a strong hand himself. On the draw, he saw an early [Treefolk Harbinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Treefolk+Harbinger) to fetch [Murmuring Bosk](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Murmuring+Bosk) on the other side of the board, but was happy not to find a Doran, The Siege Tower, immediately after.

The red deck powered out first a [Stigma Lasher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stigma+Lasher), and then [Boggart Ram-Gang](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boggart+Ram-Gang). The Ram-Gang got hit by [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion), but the Lasher gradually withered through that Harbinger. Takagi was on the ropes.

Takagi drew for his turn, and thought. Serious throughout the match, this was the most grave situation he had been in at any point. Was there a way out? Even the Changeling token from [Crib Swap](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crib+Swap) made his life harder, as a [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout) would still leave lethal damage on the board. Takagi evoked [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) to kill [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique). He attacked with [Wren’s Run Vanquisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wren%E2%80%99s+Run+Vanquisher), and played a [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128110754/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus), getting ready for the swings back. The air force took him to 4.

Takagi needed to draw more gas to get back in the game, but really, by this point there wasn’t enough gas left to get him there. He drew his card and extended his hand. It was all over.

Congratulations to Yuuta Takahashi, Grand Prix Kobe 2008 champion!
