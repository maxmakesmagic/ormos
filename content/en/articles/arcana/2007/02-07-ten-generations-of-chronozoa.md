You first saw [Chronozoa](https://web.archive.org/web/20070207000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chronozoa) as Mark Rosewater’s [example](/en/articles/makingmagic/2007/01-15-utter-chaos) of the vanishing mechanic during *Planar Chaos* previews. Its similarity to a single-celled eukaryote is significant—if left unchecked, its growth potential is exponential. But what does exponential growth mean, really? If you had plenty of turns for [Chronozoa](https://web.archive.org/web/20070207000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chronozoa) to do its thing, what would it look like?

At this point we run out of pixels to reasonably display any more [Chronozoa](https://web.archive.org/web/20070207000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chronozoa) in a typical browser window, but that doesn’t mean [Chronozoa](https://web.archive.org/web/20070207000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chronozoa) can’t keep going! 2^10 [Chronozoa](https://web.archive.org/web/20070207000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chronozoa) would be 1024 of the flying 3/3s, and so on.
