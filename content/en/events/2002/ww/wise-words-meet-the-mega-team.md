It’s pretty amazing, the difference a week can make in your life. It was just about this time seven days ago that I was cruising towards the U.S. border, ready for change, something different, a temporary sating of my obviously nomadic spirit, but now I’m back in Toronto, sorting through red tape and apartment hunting.

Despite this hiccup, it looks like I’ll be staying with the CMU-TOGIT mega team for a while. It’s a huge group, which I’m not entirely accustomed to, but if nothing else, I’m curious to see if that much manpower (the mailing list has more than twenty established players on it) can produce goods sufficient enough to overcome the disadvantage of expecting at least a match per Pro Tour where I’ll be playing a team mate. If nothing else, I’ll be making frequent road trips to Pittsburgh and occasionally New Jersey, so at the least I’ll be playing more live **Magic** than I have over the last six months. This is a good thing.

The team is a little ridiculous. It has only one member outside the U.S. and that’s strictly because of U.S. Immigration, with a ludicrous twenty other core members (that’s members of its three-day old list) and several others who will work with the team when qualification occasionally arises. In case you haven’t realized it, it’s something of a social experiment. Can almost two dozen gamers, each participating in a game in which one-on-one competition is at the forefront, survive one another long enough to reap the benefits of their collective expertise? Must see TV…

There are obvious flaws in the concept. In addition to the aforementioned “I got paired against a team mate again” syndrome facing team members, with as many people as this list has, the possibility of a tech leak is staggering, while team familiarity, an easy gap to bridge in a smaller group, is questionable at best. The following is from a Pro Tour chat room conversation:

\[Jensen\] WHO  
 \[Jensen\] IS  
 \[Jensen\] SOTTO?  
 \[GaryW\] Huey: Paul Sottosanti, PT regular, Turian/Harvey Housemate and your team mate.

This came out of a moment of confusion, true, but with members in Philadelphia, Oakland, Maryland, Albany, Atlanta, all over Florida, Pittsburgh, New Jersey and Toronto, you have to expect that even with six PT stops a year and handfuls of U.S. GPs, there will be members of this group who don’t know one another from kids at the local card store.

Did I mention there’s no structured leadership? Team debates are going to get fierce…

For all the negatives though, there is a lot of positive, enough to leave the negative behind in the dust. There’s a lot of talent on this team, enough for deck-building, theory, drafting and play-technique to all be more than amply covered. There are full-time players who will bridge the electronic gap with internet play and multi-hour drives. There’s a fresh, unusual attitude (at least in PT circles) that says friendship outweighs all other criteria when team decisions are made and of course, there’s the realization that in Venice, while the group lay splintered, its members took three of the top four spots. 

What follows is a look at the members of the mailing list, their relationships to one another and how it all holds together. Some names you’ll know well, some you’ll become familiar with over the next few months:

**Jordan Berkowitz** – For months, the kid was pestering me to put him in Wise Words, showering me with such gems as “I’ve qualified five times!” Well, that’s kid stuff on the Pro Tour, but going undefeated on Day 1 of Venice and finishing in the Top 4 are bona-fide mention-worthy accomplishments. Jordan’s day often starts with drafting, followed by drafting and a little more drafting, suggesting the Top 4 finish came in his weaker format, so Yokohama could be big. He worked well with TOGIT for Venice, paving the pay for his continued membership.

**Joe Crosby** – Regardless of Ken Krouner’s penchant for hyperbole, Crosby does have a strong enough grasp on the theoretical aspects of the game to translate into some of the finer Limited theory works this website has seen in recent years. Crosby’s only recently started taking the game seriously, and it’s started showing in his results, which include a 10th place finish this year in Chicago. In part, it was his connection with Krouner that led to his original teaming with CMU.

**Andrew Cuneo** – Mostly retired, Cuneo still contemplates the game, just electing to forgo traveling for it. A member of Car Acrobatic Team, the only two-time day 3 finisher without a German on it, Cuneo’s true forte is deck building, which he apparently does in his sleep.

**Antonino DeRosa** – Speaking of which, this may be the PT’s great unheralded deck building hope. Antonino told the world that Deep Analysis was the card for Osaka back when everyone else was running Breakthough and Compulsion and if that weren’t enough, he pieced together the beasts deck that Berkowitz played in Venice, which many experts were calling the best in the field. Long a friend of Matt Rubin’s, when Rubin moved to Florida, the team up became a natural.

**Morgan Douglass** – Owner of American GP Top 8 finishes in both Constructed and Limited this year, Douglass is still looking for that breakout performance on the Tour. A constant presence online, one would think the opportunity to be on a PT team for the first time can only help.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptbos02/774.jpg)**Gerard Fabiano** – Emotional, expressive, unusual, uncanny. That doesn’t even begin to describe this guy. A member of *Slay, Pillage, Gerard*, the team that finished fourth in Boston this year, GFabs practices far from conventional methods to his madness, but he makes them work. One of the central members of TOGIT.

**Adam Fischer** – The man is a living, breathing database. Pursued for his workhorse attitude and affinity for numbers by some of the PT’s finest, Adam chose to stay with the CMU guys out of friendship. He’s still just getting his feet wet as far as PT play goes, but he’s been around for a while. You’ve probably seen his work on Star City.

**Eugene Harvey** – The U.S. National Champ, Harvey was probably the key to this team coming together, in that he spends most of his time in Pittsburgh while coming from New Jersey. This arrangement led to a constant swapping of ideas between TOGIT and CMU, leading to a number of friendships being formed and eventually, this team. He’s also one of its finest players, of whom it’s been said that his presence skews testing results because he’s too good.

**Adam Horvath** – Just a rookie, Horvath teamed with Pat Sullivan and Craig Krempals to finish 9th at Boston this year. He’s part of the TOGIT core.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/usnat02/827.jpg)**Bill Jensen** – Definitely viable to argue he’s the best player on this list. Jensen’s long friendship with Krouner and his new one with Berkowitz (his frequent draft partner provided the political clout here while his play skill did the rest of the talking. Jensen has Top 8’s in each of the last two PTs, and is playing far more regularly now than he has since his first Sunday finish at 1999 PT-London.

**Paul Jordan** – becoming known as the self-effacing “other guy who writes for Togit.com”, Paul is one of the game’s good guys. A high finisher at each of the last two team PTs, it’s tough to cheer for the man when you know another loss will mean another report…

**Craig Krempals** – The leader in this year’s Rookie of the Year race, Krempals made Top 8 at GP-Philly and came 9th in Boston before finishing 40th at Chicago. The man can draft.

**Ken Krouner** – Writer-, gamer- and gossiper-extraordinaire, KK has been one of the more influential forces in this team’s forming. Working with CMU for the better part of a year, Krouner brought in the Crosby/Ranks team and played a large part in Huey’s membership. With a Top 8 at Worlds and strong GP finishes this year, he’s not a bad spellslinger either…

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcle02/920.jpg)**Osyp Lebedowicz** – If you didn’t know him a month ago, you know him now. Osyp burst on the scene last season with three strong finishes to start his career including a Top 8 finish at Osaka, but it was only when he started to consider leaving the game behind that he managed to win himself a Pro Tour. Likely the most influential member of TOGIT, he may also have been the team’s biggest advocate for unification. 

**Matt Ranks** – Ranks has been around forever, but he waited until his little brother made the U.S. team to finally start putting some numbers up himself. Coming into the CMU fold with Crosby, the Atlanta resident finally had his breakthrough in Houston, where he finished 11th.

**Matt Rubin** – The third Floridian on the list (alone with De Rosa and Crosby), Rubin spent a good portion of his life in New Jersey, where the TOGIT connection was a natural. His strongest finish to date was a Top 8 at 2002 U.S. Nationals, but it was a 28th place finish in Venice that finally got him on the gravy train for the first time.

**Jon Sonne** – Slow…. silent… deadly. One of the “more methodical” players on the Tour, Sonne’s strongest finish came in Boston where along with Fabiano and Scott McCord, he finished fourth as part of *Slay, Pillage, Gerard*, a year after coming sixth with McCord and (the recently retired) Eric Ziegler. Sonne also managed to finish 20th in Osaka despite a concession and a match loss. He’s a New Jersey original.

**Paul Sottosant**i – Sotto, who you probably know from his articles on the official ***Magic** Online* web page or the dilemma series with Ken Krouner on Star City, occupies that little house on Hobart Street along with Harvey, Turian and the recently retired Andy Johnson. Paul’s Top 32 in Chicago was made all the more spectacular by the fact that he had no invitation before the weekend, winning the last chance qualifier at 6 AM. A member of CMU for almost two years, one has to think he’d have followed up in Venice if he’s bothered to renew his passport before the trip…

**Pat Sullivan** – Half a year ago, I was Shocking his creature while he sat at two life, now we’re team mates. Who’d have thought? Sullivan, as mentioned, has long been teaming with Craig Krempals and Adam Horvath, the strength of their bond proving evident in their 5th place finish at GP-New Jersey last year. He also happens to be part-owner of TOGIT. His membership on the team was not debated.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpphi02/a895.jpg)**Mike Turian** – The mighty CMU Potato, Mike is the last original member. Owner of a Team PT win in 2000 and a Worlds Top 8 later that season, Mike’s gravy train membership has been a foregone conclusion for a long time. Considered by many the finest Team Rochester drafter in the world, Turian’s pretty good on his own too.

**Gary Wise** – …is beat. That’s about it for now. Have a good week.
