<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 10:10 a.m. - Round 8: Dave 'The Great White Hope' Grant vs. Brad Barkley</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Tim Willoughby</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>&quot;I don't want to be known as Dave Grant… I'm The Great White Hope, or GWH&quot; remarked GWH as he shuffled up for round 8. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gbnat07/great_white_hype.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Dave Grant</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>When 'The Hope' was last in the coverage, he had just drafted a red green deck which he deemed a little bit ropey, in spite of a pair of <a>Sprout Swarm</a>s. Four rounds later, he was 4-0 in the draft and sat at a very exciting 6-1. His opponent Brad Barkley is one of the best players in Scotland right now stood right near the top of the standings also, and with a pretty hot Red/White/Black deck to match The Great White Hope's Red Black monster.</p>&#10;      <p>'The Brad Man' had to mulligan on the draw, but was happy with his six, and mirrored GWH's <a>Swamp</a> <a>Mountain</a> start, though without either the <a>Rathi Trapper</a> or the <a>Skirk Shaman</a> to come from the Englishman.</p>&#10;      <p>Brad had a <a>Blazing Blade Askari</a>, which got his by <a>Sudden Shock</a>, but followed up with a <a>Fomori Nomad</a>, to hold off attacks for a turn or two. Instead, a <a>Mana Skimmer</a> came down for Granty, who had a great target for it's ability, in the form of a lone <a>Plains</a> on the other side of the board. </p>&#10;      <p>The Nomad got tapped, and while <a>Mana Skimmer</a> got hit by a <a>Ghostfire</a>, <a>Skirk Shaman</a> got stuck in for two. <a>Empty the Warrens</a> for four goblins followed, and it was all over to Brad. All that came in reply was a <a>Melancholy</a> from Brad, on the Trapper, but it wasn't enough to stop the hordes of rampaging red monsters, and he succumbed on the next attack step.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 2</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>While sideboarding the Great White Hope confided to his opponent that he had brought in a bomb. <a>Gibbering Descent</a> was coming in… and it seems likely that even with the warning, it would prove something of a surprise.</p>&#10;      <p>Even after a mulligan to a one land hand, the Great White Hope was ready to show the world how it's done, ripping consecutive lands in the first couple of turns, having a <a>Cradle to Grave</a> to kill a <a>Grinning Ignus</a>, and a <a>Basal Sliver</a> up the following turn. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Flamecore Elemental</a> from Barclay looked like it could be a problem, but possibly not as much as the <a>Jaya Ballard, Task Mage</a> which Granty dropped into play. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;She's quite hot isn't she?&quot; suggested GWH as he discarded a <a>Swamp</a> to use as an <a>Incinerate</a> on the Elemental at the first opportunity. He followed up with <a>Midnight Charm</a>, to kill the 5/4. Jaya soon died to a <a>Ghostfire</a>, but she'd done her job, and Dave was still in the race, though when his <a>Basal Sliver</a> found itself facing down <a>Shivan Sand-Mage</a> and <a>Emberwilde Augur</a>, things started to look a little dicey. </p>&#10;      <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gbnat07/dave_grant_v_bradley_barclay.jpg">&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Basal Sliver</a> blocked the Augur, and a <a>Brute Force</a> kept the Great White dream alive. A <a>Ghostfire</a> meant that briefly <a>Basal Sliver</a> was the only creature in play. <a>Corpulent Corpse</a> soon came in for Barclay, and got to smash in unimpeded. The Hope crashed back with his sliver and a <a>Henchfiend of Ukor</a>, taking the life totals to 11-10 in Barclay's favour. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Melancholy</a> ensured that Dave was not too bothered about paying his echo cost, and the Henchfiend went to the graveyard. At just 7 after another swing, Dave thought carefully about his turn. He played a land and loudly declared that he couldn't believe he was being beaten by a <a>Corpulent Corpse</a>. Another <a>Emberwilde Augur</a> came down for Barclay, looking very dangerous with Granty on just 4. </p>&#10;      <p>He drew for the turn, and played a <a>Chromatic Star</a>, <a>Death Rattle</a> on the Augur and <a>Empty the Warrens</a> for 6. Grant had had the <a>Death Rattle</a> for some time, but knowing that his opponent had an Oros, he had been hesitant to play it until he really had to. Barclay declined to swing the following turn, to stay alive from the fresh hordes on the Great White Hope's side of the board. </p>&#10;      <p>On the very last turn, Barclay drew the <a>Plains</a> for Oros but it was two late. Oros' ability only deals damage to non-Great White Hope players.</p>&#10;      <p>The Great White Hope wins 2-0!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 11:20 a.m. - Round 9: Simon O'Keeffe vs. Craig 'Prof' Jones</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Tim Willoughby</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>A few years ago, after a <b>Magic</b> tournament, Simon O'Keeffe and I came up with a plan. It was a good plan, involving personalised deckchairs, a treasure chest, and some beer. That plan never quite came together, but Simon's plan of being awesome at <b>Magic</b> has been going quite a bit better. Prof has been riding high on the Pro Tour following his now legendary <a>Lightning Helix</a> in Honolulu, and is enjoying fair success thus far at the Great Britain nationals, after an unceremonious exit last year.</p>&#10;      <p>Craig led off with <a>Swamp</a>s and <a>Forest</a>s, and was soon followed by Simon. O'Keeffe had both <a>Spinneret Sliver</a> and a morph to the lone morph from Jones, and took the early initiative with his morph battle with an attack. Both morphs turned out to be <a>Gathan Raiders</a>, but Craig had a <a>Muck Drubb</a> to play via madness for his, which seemed to trump Simon's play. A <a>Feebleness</a> meant the Raiders were not trading though, so it was a tough call as to who was ahead in the game. </p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Nessian Courser" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Nessian%20Courser&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Prof tried to jump in front with <a>Vhati il-Dal</a>, to make combat thoroughly awkward for his opponent, and seemed to have succeeded when Simon stumbled for a play. Craig tried to push the tempo with <a>Sporoloth Ancient</a>, but it immediately died to <a>Cradle to Grave</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>With Vhati on the ground, Simon went with a new strategy.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;To the air!&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Giant Dustwasp</a> joined the menagerie of 3/3s in play, but Simon could never effectively get much damage in, while <a>Muck Drubb</a> was giving him a bit of a beating, and <a>Citanul Woodreaders</a> was keeping the good professor in the gas. </p>&#10;      <p>Simon played a <a>Kavu Primarch</a> with kicker, to give himself a good answer to Vhati. The counters would always apply after power/toughness changing effects, so Craig would not be able to completely stop the monster with just a simple activation of his legend. </p>&#10;      <p>Craig played a <a>Nessian Courser</a>, which meant that Simon would be left attacking for a lowly one in the air each turn, when Craig dropped the bomb - <a>Sprout Swarm</a>. For this play, Craig Jones would be playing the part of the evil genius, and his army of Saproling henchmen (via a stack of Pro Player cards) in a one man show receiving rave reviews from everyone but his opponent. </p>&#10;      <p>A <a>Phantasmagorian</a> from Simon got into play pretty happily, and Craig spent the next few turns generating an army of little Craigs. Pretty soon it was time for attack of the clones, where a bajillion little Craigs got there and brought their master victory.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 2 </b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Simon had a mulligan for the second game, and dropped on life early on to a <a>Greenseeker</a> who was getting busy with attacks on the second turn from Craig. Simon had a morph for turn 3, but it appeared to be trumped by a <a>Nessian Courser</a> from Craig. O'Keeffe attacked regardless, and had the <a>Might of Old Krosa</a> to kill it off after blocks.</p>&#10;      <p>Craig played <a>Vhati il-Dal</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;You're an evil evil man&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I try my best&quot; replied Craig, dressed all in black. </p>&#10;      <p>He had a <a>Harmonize</a> the following turn, and things looked a little grim for Simon. A <a>Pit Keeper</a> with nothing to fetch back would not be a great answer to Vhati, but Simon hoped that a kickered <a>Kavu Primarch</a> would. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Big Game Hunter</a> said no. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Muck Drubb</a> got put into play via madness the next turn, and it seemed that it was only a matter of time before Prof finished off Simon, who had somehow ended up Green/Black in spite of being fed by Craig in the draft. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Deadwood Treefolk</a> fetched back both <a>Big Game Hunter</a> (who had died in combat) and <a>Nessian Courser</a>, when it briefly saw play before being hit by <a>Cradle to Grave</a>. Craig's card advantage engine was firing all cylinders. </p>&#10;      <p>While Craig was refilling his board, all Simon could do was play another kicked <a>Kavu Primarch</a>, who again died to <a>Big Game Hunter</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Craig swung in, and it was all over. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, 12:18 p.m. - Round 10: Craig Stevenson vs. Stuart Wright</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Tim Willoughby</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gbnat07/craig_drafting.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Craig Stevenson</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Going into round 10, we have what is a match that could eliminate either player from contention for top eight. Both Stuart Wright and Craig Stevenson, editor of Starcitygames.com, made top eight of English Nationals last year, with Craig walking away with the title of English National Champion a year ago. </p>&#10;      <p>Craig had had a slightly awkward blue/green draft coming into this match, where he didn't get any kind of green hookup in pack 2 at all, in spite of cutting it pretty heavily. His blue is strong though, including a first pick Draining Whelk, and while he is a little short on quality monsters, he does have the puncher's chance of a Sprout Swarm. After 4-0 in the last draft, both Craig and Stuart were 0-2 coming into this match, and keen to break their streak. </p>&#10;      <p>Craig was a little short on green mana early on, with his Shaper Parasite being bounced by Sting<i>Scourge</i>r in the early game not helping things. He replayed a morph to block the goblin with, and revealed it to be a Fathom Seer, drawing out of his mana issues in one respect, but being left without a great many lands in play. </p>&#10;      <p>When Shaper Parasite came back into play, it got hit by Sunlance. Stuart then played a Goblin Skycutter, an Intervention Pact (to no good effect), and an Empty the Warrens for eight tokens. </p>&#10;      <p>He remembered to pay for his Pact the following turn, and swung with his team, losing his Skycutter to Sprout Swarm, and the token it produced, but dropping Craig to 12. The Mox Radio team members both had fair looking decks, and Craig was now fighting back, with an Evolution Charm to get back Shaper Parasite.</p>&#10;      <p>His time was a lot shorter than he knew though. The Parasite was dealt with again, by an Arc Blade this time, and all of a sudden, The Champ was a little short on blockers. A few swings from Stuart and it was all over. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 2</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>While sideboarding, Craig looked to the motivational note that he'd received to Johnny Chapman the round before.
