![Anthony Alongi's Multi-Lab](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/75/fa75c03f23ba1d0dbe09246affbc4fd646c6c07222ef2989d507d279b97a5c8e.jpg)

I think I need to be firmer when I respond to those emails that don't follow the Multi-Lab guidelines. A lot of incomplete requests are making it hard for me to get to the readers who have done their homework. When you make a Multi-Lab request, please make sure 

*   you provide a decklist;
*   it's for a multiplayer format;
*   you tell me, briefly, how you believe it works;
*   you share a bit about what your group tends to play; and
*   there's detail on what your deck struggles against.

Those last two are really important, and the vast majority of you are just asking to "make a deck" or "make it better." Sorry, that won't work! I can't just "make it better." You need to do *some* of the hard work of trying the deck out, failing, and analyzing what's going wrong. More importantly, I have no idea what you're up against. Every group is different, and it would be dishonest of me to give you advice that I hope will work, instead of more informed advice that I'm fairly certain will show results. That means we both have to work harder. If you're not interested in that, Multi-Lab isn't for you. 

But most of you *are* interested in Multi-Lab, and I have to say that the gems are worth the effort. I know that there are still many nifty ideas sitting in my Inbox that I haven't even gotten to yet; but after reading and replying to the first couple hundred, I've already found four or five requests that I think show some real creativity and interesting strategic decisions. 

If you're considering a Multi-Lab submission, please read these carefully. Each contains the elements I'm looking for. Beyond that, they're interesting case studies in decks that might smash one group, but fail in another. And beyond that, they're just plain fun to look at.

Here are some additional, public comments. I think decks that run low on creatures (less than ten) are even riskier than decks that run *no* creatures. Creatureless decks, after all, consciously accept their risk, and make up for it in massive removal or incredible focus on achieving a combo. But I think most decks that end up with less than ten creatures do so because of "mission drift" -- there's some cool stuff the builder wants to do, and it happens to be sorceries and instants. So he or she builds the deck with few creatures as it is, and then adds a few more instants and sorceries because he or she wants to deal with X problem. The deckbuilder doesn't recheck the creature count, and after a month or two of this, the deck has, like, a Thornscape Apprentice and that's it. 

Heath isn't all the way down that road, but I worry about this deck, even if it is winning right now. 

I don't know if the *Legions* set will get us all back in the practice of using more creatures than spells, but in general, that's a good guideline. Creatures stay on the board and prevent massive amounts of damage. When you think about it, if you're in a five-player game and your four opponents each have two Grizzly Bears, having even the threat of blockers can save you up to 16 damage in between turns. Don't ignore obvious solutions to tricky problems.

Hey there, YOU. 

Thanks for laying it all out so nicely! 

Of course, you know that the problem with black is its inability to deal with enchantments and artifacts, so I'm not surprised you're having the trouble you are. Fortunately, there are ways around it. You said you're okay picking up rares, so get ready! 

 ![Mutilate](https://i.ormos.uk/0a/5c/0a5cc4e9f71125d215553e9391a5f93cd42742abac37539378ae53bd6b017736.jpg) 

For protection from white creatures, Mutilate is the way to go. This will kill your Zombies as well, but that's what the Dredging (or Unholy Grotto, or Unearth, or Haunted Crossroads) is for. Bulk up on Noxious Ghouls and Frightshroud Couriers, as well: these will help you defeat those pesky white decks. 

For artifacts or enchantments, you're going to need to find (nonblack) creatures that can get around the condition in question. Example: Light of Day. Wish you had a nonblack creature? You'll need to borrow one. Best way for a Zombie deck to do this is Gravespawn Sovereign. This will require you dedicating more slots to creatures (Zombies, of course). 

If you're dying to Lavaborn Muse, you're not killing it enough (ha ha). Get rid of one of those spells and focus: 4x Terror, 4x Dark Banishing, 4x Pestilence, whatever. This is not a deck that should be losing to creatures (unless the creature is Akroma . . . then you extend your hand and congratulate your opponent!). 

I agree that the Whispering Shade and Murkdiver are probably not worth their costs. Swap 'em out for things that are three mana or less. My favorites are Withered Wretch, Carnophage (you don't *have* to pay the life if you're in a fix), and Zombie Trailblazer. The Trailblazer is particularly nice because you can occasionally colorscrew someone during his or her upkeep. Find cards like this that give you extra ways to win -- monoblack needs additional dimensions, wherever it can find them! 

I hope this is helpful to you. Thanks again for writing in! 

-- AA

More extra comments. I often can't tell if readers are using lots of ones and twos of cards because they're creative souls just trying out lots of different ideas, or because they're new and they haven't learned the trick of four copies = consistency yet. I think this was one of those cases where the player can ramp to four copies without much trouble. (After all, our deckbuilder said rares weren't a problem, which usually indicates a deep collection.) 

This deck is also interesting because monoblack is, on its own, an intriguing choice. Without the safety net of Nevinyrral's Disk, artifacts and enchantments are just something you're going to have to accept. Discard is an option, but I think that would have forced this deck to do too many things. I like that Zombies have gotten strong enough, through cards like Gravespawn Sovereign and Zombie Trailblazer, to offer creative solutions to old problems. That, and sheer volume, is what's going to keep Zombies in a high tier of creature-type decks. 

Lastly, I didn't criticize Dead Ringers, even though I don't like it, because I don't have proof that it doesn't work in this player's group. (And no, the fact that I hate trying to find two creatures of exactly the same color does not constitute "proof"!) For all I know, this player is waxing pairs of Questing Phelddagrifs and Lightning Angels like nobody's business. Since it's at three copies, the deckbuilder obviously likes the card and finds value in playing it. I'll try to respect choices like that, though I'll certainly point out if it looks like they're part of the problem.

Great request, and an intriguing deck situation! Let me tell you what I think, and you tell me if I'm insane. 

Red-black, generally, has an uphill climb in multiplayer. Both colors specialize in exhausting resources early to get a temporary gain. Your deck has a lot of that, and so you're running into a structural wall. 

That doesn't mean we can't get around it, a tiny bit.

Additional notes: Single copies of cards generally aren't cool -- unless you're talking about a stellar rare that you really, really wanna use! So I'm not touching his array of different Dragons. I didn't mention it to Alex the first time, but I'm thinking that in the *Onslaught* age, Tsabo's Decree is probably a superior choice to Void. Even at six mana, its instant speed and hand punishment is absolutely crippling. A bit nasty, since it focuses on a player kinda hard, but the perfect answer to all those Sliver decks that are reemerging, no?
