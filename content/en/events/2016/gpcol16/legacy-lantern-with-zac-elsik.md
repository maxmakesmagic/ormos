Zac Elsik made the world of Magic go crazy last year when he won Grand Prix Oklahoma City with a Modern deck built around [Lantern of Insight](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lantern+of+Insight). The deck milled opponents' relevant cards while controlling the board with broad brush answers like [Ensnaring Bridge](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ensnaring+Bridge).

This weekend, Elsik decided to build a Legacy version of the Lantern Control deck. With the massive available cardpool in Legacy, there were a lot of new toys for Elsik to play with.

I found Elsik about to go to time against a Jund opponent that had disrupted him with discard, [Abrupt Decay](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abrupt+Decay), and [Ancient Grudge](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancient+Grudge). Things were looking grim. Then, seemingly out of nowhere, Elsik cast [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) in response to [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) to find [Helm of Obedience](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Helm+of+Obedience) which, in conjunction with the [Rest in Peace](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rest+in+Peace) he had in play, meant that he was able to immediately exile his opponent's library and win the match.

Afterward, I asked Elsik how he went about building this new version of the Lantern Control deck.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2016/events/gpcol16/gpcol16_lantern_elsik.jpg)  
*Zac Elsik*

“There are a lot of powerful Modern decks that end up leaking into Legacy like Jund and Eldrazi. I felt like Lantern Control got enough from the bigger cardpool that it could compete in this format. I started with a Blue-Black-Green version of the deck, but I wasn't impressed. Soon after, I changed to White-Blue and shifted the focus of the deck to [Energy Field](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Energy+Field) and [Rest in Peace](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rest+in+Peace). [Unexpectedly Absent](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unexpectedly+Absent) essentially functions as a two mana [Vindicate](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vindicate); I put the card on top of my opponent's library and mill it away.”

Elsik mentioned one particularly awesome interaction that came up.

“Sometimes I know what I'm exiling with [Pyxis of Pandemonium](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyxis+of+Pandemonium). Sometimes everything is face down. I've had games where I fetched for my seventh land and realized that I was missing some unexpected number of [Rest in Peace](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rest+in+Peace) and [Energy Field](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Energy+Field) from my deck. This meant that I was able to activate the big ability on [Pyxis of Pandemonium](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyxis+of+Pandemonium) and ensure survival.

The deck still plays [Ensnaring Bridge](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ensnaring+Bridge), and with [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) it's easy to find whatever you're missing for a given situation.

I wanted to know what Elsik was hoping to play against this weekend.

“I thought Eldrazi would be a good matchup, but they keep playing [Thorn of Amethyst](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thorn+of+Amethyst) on the first turn. In general, fair decks seem like pretty good matchups for the deck.”

It's also worth noting that the combination of [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) and four copies of [Rest in Peace](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rest+in+Peace) mean that Elsik is a big favorite against graveyard focused decks like [Dredge](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dredge).

I asked which decks seemed like difficult matchups.

“Storm was really bad; my only chance against them is to use [Enlightened Tutor](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Enlightened+Tutor) to find [Nevermore](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nevermore). Jund has a lot of interaction; [Abrupt Decay](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abrupt+Decay) decks are usually tough,” he smiled, having just beaten a Jund opponent.

I wanted to know if there was anything particularly excited about the sideboard.

“Peacekeeper has been incredible. Opponents are sideboarding out all of their removal and I can just stick a [Peacekeeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peacekeeper) and ride it to victory.”

Elsik is currently 8-3 as we enter the 12th round of swiss here at Grand Prix Columbus. Is Legacy Lantern a deck we should be examining more closely? It seems very powerful and versatile while gaining a lot of consistency with cards like [Field of Dreams](https://web.archive.org/web/20160612000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Field+of+Dreams) to make draws more consistent.
