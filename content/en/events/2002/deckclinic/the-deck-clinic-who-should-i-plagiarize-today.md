![](https://i.ormos.uk/a6/6e/a66ee9a134bab3220a5302f3cd755e015a13050e8d13ea42a1ae98a6ededad16.jpg)Plagiarize recursion is a deck design I have been fascinated with since talking to Your Move Games member Chad Ellis in Osaka. They had a deck which could recursively cast Plagiarize, forever locking the opponent out of the game. There are quite a few different combos with Plagiarize that are available, and they had a lot of them. However, the deck was deemed too slow for tournament play, and thus dropped from development. I decided to scan through all the entries and see what Plagiarize decks had been sent to me. Here is the one I selected to work on.

### Comments

![](https://i.ormos.uk/58/ff/58ffcd372d295d1ce5b25f434dcaa9940cbf2503f62e824f3b0a1bec5b720693.jpg)The obvious combo here is Holistic Wisdom and Plagiarize, which essentially creates a "lock" in which your opponent should never draw a card again if the engine is running correctly. The deck wins by dropping Ordered Migration or by recurring Fire/Ice, the former being the more likely win condition. The other important combo involves Plagiarism and Cephalid Coliseum which is essentially three extra draws for you while your opponent still must discard three cards. The deck totally abuses Holistic Wisdom, often chucking lands to obtain Coliseum recursion, or chucking extra wisdoms to gain back destroyed Collective Restraints, instants for Plagiarize, and Rampant Growths for more Migrations. The deck matches up very well with beatdown and black or black/x decks, but decks packing lots of counters seem to give it trouble. I think the deck is very strong in the environment, and stands as a tier 1.5 deck or so right now. It's a rogue deck right now, but I think with some more tweaks, it can be a more mainstream deck. Heavy control decks seem to be the biggest barrier to this, hence the heavy anti-control sideboard. Nevertheless, please feel free to tweak the deck if you deem necessary, and let me know how it goes!

Thanks,  
 Phil

This deck looks like quite a reasonable design and a great starting place. I would play with the mana base a little, but first it is time to try it out. Well, after playing the deck for while, you could say that the deck and I did not bond very well. I had a lot of different failures, most of them after sideboarding. The deck was having a big problem with aggressive decks once they brought in enchantment removal. Here is what I learned:

*   The Plagiarize engine is great so long as they aren't winning. If they have threats on the board, and you need to get rid of them, or you just die to the threats.
*   The Plagiarize engine dies out. Eventually you don't draw an instant and they get to draw a card. With only 2 Ordered Migrations, this happens before you draw the Migration too often.
*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/6f/66/6f6669b8c22e590527016a4f33cf1b129b979a8d234cbd0a074c29dc6577556e.jpg)You never kill with Fire/Ice. You can't keep up Plagiarize and Fire/Ice. You simply just don't draw enough instants. Somebody killed my plains and then removed it from the game turn 4, eliminating my ability to play Migration. I had the entire game to try to kill him with Fire/Ice. I decked myself trying. Not being able to use the Plagiarize lock since I needed a certain amount of instants for the Fire/Ice kill, he overloaded the Fire/Ice with annoying little creatures that it didn't matter if they attacked. Very unpleasant, but also very enlightening.
*   I dislike 5-color decks with two main colors, both using search to find the other mana type. I much prefer a green mana base with only as much of the other colors as I need. I like to use Rampant Growth and Harrow to get my mana.
*   Sure, you can search a lot, but if you don't draw the Collective Restraint early, it is all over.
*   I kill myself on the damage from the Cephalid Coliseums too often.
*   ![](https://i.ormos.uk/a8/de/a8deed9db4077fbec6fccf3fb7b347de3d82b776d1e72c5d9337edff899f0bb4.jpg)It is way too tempting to start holding back on the Plagiarize for the Coliseum trick. It is better to just fire it off early.
*   Boy is it funny when they put down Braids, so you cast Ordered Migration for 5! (This was a good thing!)
*   Collective Restraint can be a huge liability after sideboard against colors with enchantment removal.
*   The Plagiarize and Cephalid Coliseum combo isn't that common, and often isn't as great as it seems. It happens late due to needing threshold, when their hand is usually empty anyways.

### Fixing the Mana, My Way

![](https://i.ormos.uk/34/d4/34d4a55b1e2e81fe3508873a6659ef1a6896aa5975f8b1f7c3e2bab47f22996e.jpg)Ever since my success at PT-Barcelona, I have used a very specific mana ratio when building both Constructed and Limited 5-color decks. I always start off base green. The number of non-forests is determined in the following manner: You look at what is the most mana of a particular color that you would want on any turn, and add that many of the appropriate basic lands to the deck. Then, all the rest of the lands will be forests. If the number of forests is not at least 14, there will be problems. Here is how you determine the effective number of other (non-forest) basic lands. For your secondary land type, take the number of that land in the deck and add the number of mana fixers (Like Harrow, Rampant Growth and Lay of the Land). After this, you do the same, but you really only get to add half the mana fixers. The mana ratio of the basic lands that I am going to move this deck to is going to be: 14 forests, 1 plains, 3 islands, 1 swamp, 1 mountain. I want to be able to cast a spell and be able to counter up to twice in case I get into a control match. I also want to be able to just cast my spells and have counters for the opponent's turn. This boosts my island count to 3. However, no other color needs more than 1 mana a turn, so they only receive one slot each. In addition, I will run 4 Rampant Growths and 4 Harrows. This gives me 14 forests, effectively 11 islands as my second color. After this, I have 5 of each other land type. Normally, I want at least 12 of a land type, but the Coliseums will do in a pinch. While this is only an approximation, it has served very well for me in the past. Although I have counted 1 Coliseum as an island, I consider that the rest are there more for the combo than for mana.

### Adjusting the Spell Ratios

Ok, having just done major work on my land count, all the cheap blue search spells are no longer very tempting. I can't use them until I have a lot of mana, so I will replace them with more expensive and effective search cards. The first one is easy: Worldly Council. At 2 mana, the potential ability to look at many more cards is worth paying the extra. Next is one of the biggest card-drawers in Standard right now: Allied Strategies. I often find when I cast this that I must discard, but that is fine, as even discarding, the quality of cards in my hand has become stronger. 

Now that I have much more effective search, albeit slower, it is time to readjust my spell base a little. First off, I draw way too many Holistic Wisdoms for my liking, so I cut one of these. Next, I also cut a Cephalid Coliseum. Since I didn't count these in my mana sources, I don't have to go back and readjust.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/67/b0/67b0f197a1cfd94f0b315b401e6ea2a7e7e3ddf48ad79b17a1a8071bc72c11cd.jpg)I want another Counterspell, but since I can't really afford ![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif), I chose Dromar's Charm. This also gives me the ability to gain life if I really need it against burn decks. I should stress that I am not fond of spells that only gain life. However, spells that have this option can be very nice. Dromar's Charm one of the best there is.

Next, Fire/Ice just isn't getting the job done. Sure, it can go 2 for 1 against creatures. But really, if you don't draw the Collective Restraint, you can't keep enough creatures off the board. I cut all 4. I then added 1 Tribal Flames, as Tribal Flames does much more damage, and is a reasonable finisher, still being very cheap, while still buying me time early if necessary. I also put in a Death Grasp, which makes another nice finisher, and again helps the deck against burn. A minor factor here is that I want a few different kill methods to help out against Haunting Echoes.

Finally, I upped the count of Ordered Migrations to 4. I want to be able to find them to kill my opponent more often. I also want more threats against control decks. Finally, they help buy time against a creature rush if I can't find the Collective Restraint.

### On to the Sideboard

![](https://i.ormos.uk/af/17/af174cdd51a8786f663fd647a31c9c8b49979630d9cf20dc73f9014abbddea97.jpg)While the main deck felt much more comfortable, I was almost always terrified of what would happen after sideboard. People would go after the Collective Restraints, and I would constantly crumble. The original deck had Moment's Peace in the sideboard, which would help out a lot in these situations. However, adding them to the deck was always a chore, as you had to make some nasty decisions as to what to remove. I decided that I wanted the ability to perform a switch, and remove all the Restraints. This would leave my opponents with Disenchants in their hand either looking at no targets, or having to decide if they should hit the Holistic Wisdom, or wait for a Restraint. The cards I would bring in needed to be able to do the job of Collective Restraint, which was to prevent my opponent from attacking. The first one of these was easy, Dromar, the Banisher. Unfortunately, as a legend, I didn't want 4. Rith, the Awakener has an effect very similar, as she can create a bunch of tokens to chump block with. I decided upon 2 of each, and moved on.

Disrupt is a fine spell early, but gets much worse as time goes on. Due to the change in mana, this is no longer an effective answer. My choices against control seemed to narrow to Duress and Global Ruin. Duress would be my first choice if I could have 4, but space is going to be tight in the sideboard, so I chose 2 Global Ruins. I also want something to help against Opposition decks, and for that I added 2 Aura Blasts. While this deck accelerates to the 6 mana required for Dismantling Blow quite easily, I want to be able to have counter backup, so the blue makes that unusable.

### Summary

While I really liked the idea of making a Plagiarize deck, unfortunately, this deck often seems to win in spite of the Plagiarize combo, not because of it. While I wish this wasn't the case, the final deck is still interesting enough that I thought you would like to see it.
