What would you say if I told you there's an exciting, very accessible format that you may have never heard of? That it has a deep card pool, but yet some of the top cards include [Spire Golem](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spire+Golem) and [Loyal Cathar](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Loyal+Cathar)? And that it's hidden right under most **Magic** players' noses, nestled in **Magic Online**?

Well, such a format actually exists and is definitely a lot of fun! Welcome to Pauper.

![](http://archive.wizards.com/mtg/images/daily/mm/mm56_ponderSlice.jpg)

If you've never played **Magic Online** Pauper before, the rules are simple: any card is legal... as long as it was released on **Magic Online** somewhere as a common. (And isn't on [the short banned list](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/tcg/resources.aspx?x=magic/rules/Pauper).) Let's look at a few example cards:

[Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm)? Yes! It's common.

[Arid Mesa](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Arid+Mesa)? No! It's rare.

[Strategic Planning](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Strategic+Planning)? Yes! Although uncommon in face-to-face **Magic**, it is common in the **Magic Online**–only *Master's Edition III*.

[Red Elemental Blast](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Red+Elemental+Blast)? No! Although common in *Revised*, it's uncommon in *Master's Edition IV*, the only place where it shows up online. (Although [Pyroblast](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pyroblast) is certainly legal.)

[Serrated Arrows](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serrated+Arrows)? Yes! Although *Homelands* isn't on **Magic Online**, and it was timeshifted rarity in *Time Spiral*, [Serrated Arrows](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serrated+Arrows) appeared in *Duel Decks: Garruk vs. Liliana* as a common!

As you might be able to tell, since **Magic Online** has released older cards in different ways (namely, *Master's Edition*), Pauper has a bit of a unique face. It's a format we've come to consider when we work on Duel Decks or similar unique products, to help change up the format just like how we change up Standard. (Or, as the case may sometimes be, make sure something problematic *isn't* printed at common so that it doesn't hurt the format.)

And *Vintage Masters*? Oh yeah, we definitely did that for *Vintage Masters*. I'd go as far to say that *Vintage Masters* may end up having one of the most wide-reaching effects on Pauper of any set we've recently released.

And if you're not sure if you're willing to believe that, well, then just check out these two old buddies.

### Madman With a Deckbox

"Have I gone mad?"  
 "I'm afraid so. You're entirely bonkers. But I'll tell you a secret. All the best people are."  
 —The Mad Hatter, asking Alice a question, *Alice in Wonderland*

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/rc/2014/rc_wk19_299_madhatter.jpg)

  
*Original art by John Tenniel; modifications by Bonnie Bruenderman*

* * *

In a format full of fair(ish) common creatures, there aren't too many huge threats running around. (Which, by the way, is part of what makes [Serrated Arrows](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Serrated+Arrows) a potent weapon against some decks.) But in a land of [Ravenous Rats](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravenous+Rats) and [Spire Golem](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spire+Golem)s, a 4/4 is king.

And a 4/4 you only have to pay ![2 Mana](http://archive.wizards.com/images/Symbols/Symbol_2_mana.gif)![Green Mana](http://archive.wizards.com/images/Symbols/green_mana.gif) for? Even more frightening.

But let me back up. If you've never played or really interacted with a madness deck before—and it's such a niche ability that I can't really expect you to—I want to run you through the basic idea. Madness has traditionally been a pretty tempo-based deck, centered in blue and green. Because madness both lets you pay less mana for your spells *and* play them at instant speed (provided you can discard them at instant speed) you can operate your aggressive strategy at times other than what your opponent might be used to.

What are the key madness supporters to make madness work? Oh, just some cards like [Wild Mongrel](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Mongrel), [Aquamoeba](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aquamoeba), [Merfolk Looter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Looter), and [Careful Study](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Careful+Study). You know. *All commons*.

In fact, with this addition, nearly all of the cards to play the old Standard and Block Constructed GU Madness cards are now legal in Pauper. GU Madness was a Pro Tour–winning deck!

An important thing to understand about madness in Pauper is that [Basking Rootwalla](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Basking+Rootwalla) *is already a playable card*. Some aggressive decks just play it, ye olde 1/1 pumper for ![Green Mana](http://archive.wizards.com/images/Symbols/green_mana.gif). But in a deck like this, where you can consistently cast it for free, it's going to be even stronger.

### But Wait, There's More!

Madness isn't the only deck that's going to be given some new cards by way of *Vintage Masters*. While madness has the potential to spawn an entirely new archetype, plenty of other decks are going to pick up a card or two. And there's one card in particular that's going to slot right into one of the top decks in Pauper right now.

If you love Goblins, then get ready to start playing with this guy!

![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Goblin%20General&options=)

While he showed up in *Masters Edition IV* as an uncommon, he's back in black (that is, the common symbol) in **Vintage Masters**!

The red deck in Pauper primarily takes the shape of Goblins. While it's not particularly innovative, [it makes sense](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/magazine/article.aspx?x=mtg/daily/feature/299): Goblins appear in most blocks, and they do tend to show up at common a lot. And with a super-low curve using cards like [Foundry Street Denizen](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Foundry+Street+Denizen) and [Goblin Cohort](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Cohort), they can pack a punch quickly!

In Pauper, Goblins have mostly had to rely on cards like [Goblin Sledder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Sledder) and [Goblin Bushwhacker](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Bushwhacker) to use as a makeshift "lord"... but now with the General in the mix, they're going to hit harder than ever.

Full of super-cheap guys and ways to pump them, this deck isn't the card-advantage-heavy [Goblin Ringleader](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Ringleader) deck you're used to. I don't even think [Skirk Prospector](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skirk+Prospector) is that exciting, since you're just going to sacrifice a cheap Goblin to power into another cheap Goblin, mostly. This deck so demands having creatures that it's probably the closest I've ever come to cutting [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt) in a Pauper red deck!

Interestingly, [Sparksmith](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sparksmith) isn't quite as strong in a deck that just wants to flood the board with one-mana Goblins, when all you want to ping are 1- and 2- toughness creatures... but it's still strong enough that I wanted to include four of it. [Dragon Fodder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Fodder) would be a four-of if [Goblin Cohort](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Cohort) and [Mogg Conscripts](https://web.archive.org/web/20140513000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mogg+Conscripts) didn't require an actual creature spell to be cast.

If you're looking to get into Pauper, this is a great place to start full of easily acquired cards. Give it a try!

Considering all three of these cards are commons, that means—yep, that's right—that Madness and Goblins are both common archetypes you could draft in *Vintage Masters*.
