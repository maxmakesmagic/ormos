Akroma, Angel of Wrath is the perfect Timmy card. If this statement confuses you, I suggest checking out [Mark Rosewater's recent refresher on player types](/en/articles/makingmagic/2006/03-20-timmy-johnny-and-spike-revisited-3). 

I mean, Akroma clearly isn't a Johnny card. I asked Mark Gottlieb and Chris Millar if they wanted to write a three-author, House-of-Cards-reunion type article on Akroma for today's Feature Article. Gottlieb responded with something along the lines of “Bah. No time. Akroma's boring. She just attacks and blocks. No.” Chris was game, but none of his deck ideas actually included Akroma. In other words, Akroma, Angel of Wrath isn't a very House of Cards card. Johnnies can get inspired about anything, but Akroma inherently stifles a lot of their creative impulses.

What this means for today is that you shouldn't expect to be blown away by creative, off-the-wall decks. Millar's got some good stuff cooked up for you on Wednesday, but today is suspiciously free of wackiness. This doesn't mean the decks will be boring, only that they won't be particularly clever. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5c/cc/5cccc1ede95fc26aca67144eb1ccfebbaae3c63f2b3352380a76578f01c18f91.jpg)

What about those Spikes over there in the corner, sharpening their horns? Spikes like Akroma's ability to take over games, but they see the casting cost and chuckle derisively. An eight mana creature and it doesn't read, “You win the game?” No thanks. I asked Mike Flores if Akroma had appeared in any serious tournament decks back in her day, and he responded that she was once hot in *Onslaught* Block Constructed, and then was either replaced by Decree of Justice pretty much across the board or was used as a reanimation target, but little more. That was a long time ago. It seems that Akroma is only interesting to Spike if a) she is the most dominant, hard-to-deal with creature available in a format, and b) this format has some fast, efficient way to circumvent her casting cost. As you can tell, these two criteria have rarely occurred at the same time.

For today's purposes, Akroma's non-Spikiness means that the decks in this article won't win you any tournaments. Anyone familiar with my writing and play style probably figured this out already, though. I'm a casual gamer guy, and I'm never one to dole out tournament advice. That's why we have Mike Flores, and I'm sure he'll say something techy or insightful on Thursday. My decks today might win, but it will be on a nighttime dining room table, not a Saturday afternoon DCI table. 

Alright, so this all sounds a bit depressing. Why the heck should you press on, especially if you happen to be primarily a Johnny or Spike player? To answer this question, I think it's important to figure out why Akroma won the recent [“You Decide!” voting](/en/articles/feature/2006/04-04-you-decided).

Put simply, Akroma, Angel of Wrath is fun. In fact, she's überfun. She's the kind of card that makes your eyes light up when you first see her, elbowing your friends in the ribs hooting, “That is *ridiculous*! I want four!” Sure she costs eight mana, but when Akroma hits the table, it's usually with a windmill slam and a “Booya!” When you or one of your friends is playing a deck featuring Akroma, you are almost guaranteed an evening of memorable stories. She elicits visceral feelings when in play. She is a Power Gamer poster child - she's beefy, she takes over combat, and she has, like, every ability you would ever want on a creature to smash face. Plus - and these two aspects help her popularity immensely - she's an Angel and a hottie to boot. Hottie Angels are like gold with young men.

Which is all to say that today is an Ode to Fun. Timmies should rejoice, though Akroma isn't just for the Timmies out there. I'd like to think that Akroma brings out that spark of Timmy in everyone. You might think I'm overstating my point, but all you have to do is look at the “You Decide!” voting for validation. Akroma makes people happy. If today's article is at all successful, you'll smile sometime between now and the end. The decks may not be crazy or competitive, but they make for good stories and conversation over chips and soda. 

### Akroma And You

As far as I can tell, featuring Akroma (and by “featuring,” I mean using more than two copies) in a deck has three distinct implications. These aren't rules, per se. That is, *every* Akroma-based deck won't share these three features *every* time. **Magic** is a dynamic game with thousands of cards, and deckbuilders are a clever people. Playing the averages, though, you should expect that if you use multiple copies of Akroma, your deck is probably conforming to these standards.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6c/4f/6c4f57a0353f67665f5a67cf259155d305653278888d0325eee76cbc4ea9cbfe.jpg)

First, it's likely that your Akroma deck is going to have alternate ways of getting creatures into play besides simply casting them. Hell's Caretaker, Sneak Attack, Shifty Doppelganger, Elvish Piper, and Breath of Life will often sit beside Akroma and a host of other fattie fattersons like Verdant Force, Scion of Darkness, and Darksteel Colossus. The reason is that most decks can't wait until turn 8 to cast their feature spell. A 6/6 flying, first-striking, trampling, protection from Black and Red Angel with haste and vigilance is cool later in the game, but it's even cooler on the fourth or fifth turn. As I said, the easier it is to get a fattie into play during the first four turns, the more likely Spike will consider playing Akroma. 

The second implication to deckbuilding is that your Akroma deck is probably mana heavy. Most fattie-oriented decks would prefer to not rely purely on their alternative ways of getting creature into play. Indeed, some of these alternative ways - Tooth and Nail and Myojin of Life's Web come to mind - cost as much or more than Akroma herself. For these reasons, expect artifact mana like Marble Diamond, mana creatures like Birds of Paradise, and land-accelerators like Harrow, to be Akroma's constant companions. 

A corollary here is that if your Akroma deck can't reasonably cast eight-mana spells, your deck usually has some other way to use cards trapped in hand. Compulsion, Kindle the Carnage, Peace of Mind, or *Saviors of Kamigawa*'s wisdom mechanic are all perfect rationale for using Akroma in a deck without access to heavy mana. 

The third and final implication for deckbuilding is that your Akroma deck is unlikely to be budget-friendly. This is partly because fattie decks tend to use big, splashy cards and big, splashy cards tend to be rare. It's also partly because if you happen to own multiple copies of Akroma - a sought-after rare from *Legions* - you can probably afford other good, older cards. Most of today's decks are a real eyesore for Building On A Budget fans.

I'm not saying that Akroma decks *must* be budget unfriendly, of course, but they probably are. As a challenge, I tried assuming that I was a player coveting my three copies of Akroma and who otherwise only had access to commons and uncommons available online. I was surprised to find that my Budget Akroma deck tore up the Casual Decks room of ***Magic** Online* for a full day, so it can be done, but this sort of deck is by far the exception when it comes to Akroma.

The idea is to apply early pressure (against slower decks) or play defense (against faster decks) with hard-to-handle creatures until Akroma can win the game. Mask of Memory serves as a way of dumping Akroma into my graveyard to be revived by Breath of Life, but I've just as often won by hard casting my Angel of Wrath. Like all Akroma decks, it's a blast to play, and like all Akroma decks I'll list today, I've spent enough time playing it to consider it a solid starting point for exploration. Take these ideas and run with them until your Timmy heart can beat no faster.

Anyway, those are the three implications of Akroma that I see: You'll want ways to sneak her into play, you'll want ways to cast or use her when you can't sneak her into play, and you'll want a fat wallet. Notice that I'm not saying an implication is that you'll want Plains in your deck. It's perfectly acceptable to build a dedicated Akroma deck without a single basic Plains.

One final question you'll want to ask yourself when featuring Akroma, Angel of Wrath in your deck: Why Akroma? If you can cheat impressive creatures into play, why not feature the aforementioned Darksteel Colossus, Verdant Force, or Scion of Darkness? If what you need is an Angel, why not use Angel of Despair, Iridescent Angel, Platinum Angel, or Silver Seraph? Most recently, you need to justify the old school panache of Akroma over Simic Sky Swallower, which is similar in stature but far easier to cast (especially since it's Green). Since we're all playing for fun here, your answer can be as easy as “I like Angel Hotties,” “All of those abilities make me happy,” “The art is killer,” or “I own four of them.” She's a Power Gamer's fantasy, so your logic doesn't need to be airtight. I ask only so you can have an answer when others see your deck in action.

That's what happens to decks that feature Akroma - now it's time to start generating examples. Whenever I build decks around a single card, I always look at what each color has to offer that card. Below are my brainstormed ideas, although these ideas aren't meant to be exhaustive. The point here is to give your Timmy urges a kick in the pants, leading to what I hope are several nights of casual gameplaying fun. Especially when it comes to “fun” decks, my ways of building around Akroma might be vastly different from yours. Speak up on the Message Boards if you have new ideas, other decks with Akroma in them, or just reflections based on my reflections. As I said, let your Timmy wander free.

### White's Wrath

If Black is the first color that comes to mind when making an Akroma deck, White is the second. ### Red's Wrath

The remaining three colors - Red, Blue, and Green - involve some head-scratching to make a deck featuring Akroma. Red has a few tricks for getting big fatties into play, the most famous of which is Sneak Attack. Sneak Attack decks play like pure combo decks, looking to deal twenty damage in one glorious turn. Red's other ways of, uh, sneaking creatures into play aren't quite so spectacular, nor are they plentiful. Zirilan of the Claw is fun, but doesn't help an Akroma deck. Same goes for Stronghold Gambit. Warp World is too hard to control and not great when using legendary permanents. Search for Survivors is a possibility, but difficult to build around. That leaves Through the Breach as one of Red's only decent ways to get Akroma, Angel of Wrath into play for less than eight mana. Through the Breach is only going to give you one shot to smash face with Akroma, but there's always graveyard reanimation in White or Black to help out, as well as other Red cards like Bloodshot Cyclops and Stalking Vengeance to provide that “combolicious” taste.

The other fun things about Red Akroma decks is that Akroma has protection from Red. This means that global damage effects like Wildfire, Inferno, and Bloodfire Colossus leave Akroma alone whilst they sweep away the other creatures on the board. This is true for Black's global damage effects like Pestilence too, but the really spectacular “BOOM!” cards are going to be in Red. 

Speaking of BOOM!, Red has a type of card that is unusually fun with Akroma. Undying Flames, Erratic Explosion, Kindle the Carnage, Torrent of Fire, and, yes, Kaboom! turn Akroma from six damage a turn into eight (or fourteen, in the case of Torrent of Fire). Talk about a windmill slam. The question again is why you're choosing Akroma over, say, Autochthon Wurm, Draco, or Iname as One, but hey... Burning Angels is a fun idea with thematic appeal.

I originally tried a Red/White Bloodfire Colossus/Akroma deck that used Through the Breach, Breath of Life, and lots of board-sweepers. After no less than a dozen iterations of the deck, I couldn't find something I liked or that was in any way consistent. In a moment of weakness, then, I reached for Black to help me out. It's a cop-out, I realize, but at least I get to post a deck in the Red section. If you have other, better, Red deck ideas with Akroma, please post them on the Message Boards.

### Blue's Wrath

![](https://i.ormos.uk/56/6d/566d63adfe1b0ccf4c854351e8a5531f8f7802b5effe0106dec00a8ae627336d.jpg)

In contrast to the previous three colors, Blue pretty much stinks at putting any creatures from a graveyard into play. As I mentioned before, it has traditionally been a support card for reanimator decks because of its ability to draw lots of cards and discard them into the graveyard, but there aren't a lot of Mono-blue Reanimator decks running around (though I'm sure someone has tried it with Volrath's Shapeshifter and Dreams of the Dead). Instead, Blue has the tricksy ability to summon creatures of all shape and size from either your hand - a la Shifty Doppelganger, Metathran Aerostat, and Aetherplasm - or your library - a la Polymorph and Riptide Shapeshifter. Not surprisingly, Blue is where Johnnies get most excited about Akroma decks, since it involves them doing the unexpected. One of my favorite deck foundations for any fattie involves Green token generators and Polymorph with only one *huge* creature in the deck. Another tried and true trick is Riptide Shapeshifter, which allows for a “creature-type toolbox” that can be great fun to build and play. 

Rather than go Blue/Green and flirt with Simic Sky Swallower, I decided to try Blue/White. The reason was partly because I wanted to sometimes cast Akroma if I could, but mostly it was because of a new Blue way to sneak fatties into play: Aethermage's Touch. The deck doesn't take full advantage of the Touch without Sensei's Divining Top, but Aven Fateshaper works pretty well and there are enough creatures that I almost never “miss” with it. The addition of Azorius Guildmage pushed the deck from silly to scary, and the result is a deck so fun that it's almost guaranteed to be the one I pick up after this week. 

Also, in case you missed it: The Shapeshifter toolbox allows me to call “Angel” for Akroma, “Drone” for Hoverguard Sweepers, “Bird” for Aven Fateshaper, or “Vedalken” for Azorius Guildmage. Wheeee!

### Green's Wrath

At last we come to Green. On the surface, Green looks like Akroma's best friend. It can sneak creatures into play with cards like Elvish Piper, Oath of Druids, Call of the Wild, Animal Magnetism, Tooth and Nail, Gamekeeper, Defense of the Heart, Game Preserve, Zoologist... The list goes on and on. Green is also **Magic's** premier mana-producing color. Any deck with Sakura-Tribe Elder, Harrow, Heartbeat of Spring, Explosive Vegetation, and Kodama's Reach can reach eight mana in no time. Green can even tutor for Akroma via Worldly Tutor, Time of Need, Living Wish, Fierce Empath, and the like. It doesn't require much imagination to figure out how Green can throw an Akroma, Angel of Wrath onto the table and start attacking.

Here's the problem: Green is also **Magic's** fattie color. Akroma is impressive, sure, but how often can I justify it in a deck over something Greener, like Thorn Elemental, Darksteel Colossus, Penumbra Wurm, Plated Slagwurm, or Verdant Force? It's no accident that Timmy, Power Gamer from *Unglued* is a Green card. Maybe, as I said earlier, you just like Angels and so Green is a means to an end. But if your eyes light up when Akroma hits the table, it's probably the case that you're just as happy with Green monstrosities. As a result, I've personally found Green to be one of the harder colors to center around Akroma. Each time I made a Mono-Green deck, I could easily see replacing Akroma with a card like Phantom Nishoba, Kodama of the North Tree, or the aforementioned Simic Sky Swallower. Hm.

I also tried a bunch of Green/White decks, but they all felt forced. Instead, I decided to try out a card from *Guildpact* that's intrigued me: Killer Instinct. Killer Instinct is slightly redundant with Akroma because she already has Haste, but I found my Instinct deck was able to use a bunch of Green fatties while still justifying Akroma's inclusion. I also discovered the fun wonder of Gleancrawler and Protean Hulk with Killer Instinct, especially together. Anyway, here's the deck:

### But Wait... There's More

As I said, when I personally approach a single card and consider decks around it, I rotate though the five colors to see what each has to offer my feature card. Speaking of theme decks, I would feel remiss if I signed off today without a nod to Akroma theme decks. I'm wandering into Johnny territory here, but Akroma is ripe for decks that look nonsensical until you realize they represent Akroma the novel character, not Akroma the can of buttkicking glory. 

The trick to theme decks is building a deck that actually has some value during a game in addition to matching whatever theme it's trying to promote. Some people abandon playability completely and are theme purists when it comes to theme decks. Not me. I like to see the theme emerge, almost like a story, while the deck itself is clicking along mechanically. It's much more of an art than a science, but theme decks were what first drew me into Constructed **Magic**, and may be why I'm so preoccupied with [preconstructed decks](https://web.archive.org/web/20070225134513/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/authorarchive&author=JayMoldenhauer-Salazar).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/6b/fa6b991c0551d5cfc62a019d6dc9fe537014f8e786a002914f9e4919e224d50c.jpg)

For my Akroma theme deck, I decided to avoid the whole Karona, False God plot event, and instead focused on Akroma the angelic avenger who hates Phage. You don't need to read the novels to pick up on Akroma the Angel and her personality - it's all right there in the flavor text, card names, and art.

Here's what a Gatherer search helped me discover:

Cards with Akroma in the name: Akroma, Angel of Wrath, Akroma's Blessing, Akroma's Devoted, and Akroma's Vengeance.

Cards with Akroma in their flavor text (not including those above): Lowland Tracker, Pacifism, Righteous Cause, Infest, and Windborn Muse.

Card with Akroma in its art (not including those above): Convalescent Care. (Pacifism is a real gem here, though I already mentioned it)

I also decided that Decree of Justice is an Akroma card, partly from the name, partly from the mechanic, and partly from the art.

Can I build a deck with this pile of cards as a starting point? Infest is probably out, which is fine, since its flavor text is more about Akroma commenting on Phage's ickiness than a true Akroma card. Otherwise the cards hang together pretty well. As usual, you get stinkers like Convalescent Care, Lowland Tracker, and Righteous Cause included for the sole reason that they fit the theme, but there's plenty of power in there as well thanks to Akroma, her Vengeance, and her Decrees of Justice. 

There's also the odd problem that Akroma's Devoted likes Clerics, which describes neither Lowland Tracker nor Windborn Muse. Because of the Devoted, I included Blessed Orator, Doubtless One, and Noble Templar into my deck, figuring that if they didn't add explicitly to the theme, they at least didn't detract from it. In my mind, Doubtless One is Akroma's avatar, which is why there are only three copies of Akroma and one copy of Doubtless One (hey... it's my deck. I can make weird choices like that). ### Thoughts of Vengeance

After eight decks (not including my numerous failed experiments) and over one hundred and fifty games (not including games with said experiments) with Akroma, Angel of Wrath... What have I learned? 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/68/87/6887c7c5c1432fee6f1fa4293a33bcdbca05354a45f414d1c57b27e274759085.jpg)

First - and maybe I just need to find these things out for myself - Akroma is really, really hard to handle once she's in play. Black decks suddenly find all of their vaunted creature removal useless except Cruel Edict and Chainer's Edict. Red curls into a little ball and feebly throws burn at my head because it can't do a thing against my flier. Green dies to first-strike, or ends up using multiple creature-pumper cards to kill Akroma in combat before I revive her for more fun. White and Blue are about the only colors with reliable answers to her, White because of its answers like Wing Shards, Wrath of God, and Faith's Fetters, Blue because of countermagic and bounce. If you have a creature that can completely invalidate three colors' worth of answers, though, you've definitely got a bad-ass creature.

A corollary to this first point is that it's really cool to play a fattie with so many abilities all at once. Sometimes it's the flying that is the back-breaker, but just as often it's the first strike, protection, trample, or vigilance. All together, you can almost see opponents' hopes wilt and die as their eyes dart from their hand to the Hottie Angel smacking them with a big sword.

Second, Akroma really is a feel-good card. Unlike, say, Umezawa's Jitte, no one ridiculed me in the Casual Decks room for playing Akroma. When she hit the table and swung the game in my favor, no one whined that I was “playing cheap” or relying on “cheese” for my victory. I received complaints about Reya Dawnbringer and Darksteel Colossus, but never Akroma... Even when I put her into play on the fourth turn. Of my scores of games, one person conceded early against my budget deck saying “I don't play weenie decks,” and another blocked me after I scored a lucky win via Erratic Explosion and Draco. Other than that, I experienced only friendly banter, lots of compliments on my cool decks, and good-natured wins and losses. Keep in mind too that I wasn't using a “name” account, so people didn't know it was me.

Third, Gottlieb was right... Akroma is pretty much all about attacking and blocking. She's not like Morphling, a creature stacked with abilities that surprises you with its supremacy. All of Akroma's abilities are combat abilities, and all are meant to help her smash as much face as possible. This means that there is no “timing” involved with when Akroma hits the table, and very little strategic thinking. The sooner she's in play the better, because she needs to hit an opponent three or more times to finish the game. The only cards that have true “synergy” with Akroma are those cards that help get her into play or otherwise slant combat in your favor. The more you love creature combat and sky-shattering fatties, the more Akroma is your best friend.

As I said at the beginning of today's article... Rejoice! It's Akroma Week! You picked her as the Queen of Legends in the “You Decide!” voting, now you get a whole week of articles celebrating her Angel Hotness. It would be highly appropriate to invite your buddies over for a night of **Magic**, especially if you're looking to unleash your holy wrath upon them. It's only a week... I don't see any harm in letting your inner Timmy out on good behavior.
