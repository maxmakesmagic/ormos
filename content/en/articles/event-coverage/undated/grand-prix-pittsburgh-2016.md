## GRAND PRIX PITTSBURGH 2016

TOP 8 COVERAGE

It was a crazy weekend in Pittsburgh. After an almost storybook weekend for Pascal Maynard— who looked to clinch Platinum with a win—he was taken down by Evan Petre, who grabbed the Finals right out from under the Canadian. Petre was hoisted to the rafters by his friends, and gave Maynard something to work for in São Paulo next weekend.

Third-ranked Steve Rubin also put up a strong Top 8 finish. For Rubin, it's a fitting last *Shadows over Innistrad* Standard Grand Prix. He all-but defined the last three months of the formatafter winning with Green-White Tokens at the Pro Tour. Tokens were good to him again this weekend, but not quite as good as they were to Petre.

And the race for Grand Prix Master is still up for grabs. Both Brian Braun-Duin here and Tomoharu Saito in Taipei, earned two points. Which means they are *still* tied for the lead. They will both be at Grand Prix São Paulo next weekend. It might all come down to Brazil.

From all of us in Pennsylvania, another congrats to Evan Petre, and have a great night!

DAY 2 COVERAGE

After Day 1, only six players left with nine wins to their names. (1) Seth Manfield, (3) Steve Rubin, Daniel Weiser, Eric Flickinger, Michael Derczo, and Connor Bowman all navigated *Shadows over Innistrad* Standard to a perfect record on the first day. Now they're looking to bring it home.

But notable players like Shuhei Nakamura, Dan Lanthier, Jarvis Yu, and Ben Friedman only have one loss apiece, and will be looking to snipe those players off their privileged positions.

In the race for Grand Prix Master, Brian Braun-Duin enters the second day at 7-2. He is still in the running for Top 8. With Tomoharu Saito's two points in Taipei, BBD still looks good to catch him. But again, we'll see if Seth Manfield just wrecks everyone.

The metagame story of the weekend in the last Standard Grand Prix is: Green, and White. The top tables are littered with the colors, as the format has evolved to revolve around Steve Rubin's Pro Tour–winning Green-White Tokens deck. But soon, Emrakul cometh. To shake the very foundations of our waking lives. Will the decks at the top survive the onslaught?

The Grand Prix Pittsburgh Top 8 is only five rounds away. It'll be here before you know it.

DAY 1 COVERAGE

After nine rounds of *Shadows over Innistrad*Standard, (1) Seth Manfield, (3) Steve Rubin, Daniel Weiser, Eric Flickinger, Michael Derczo, and Connor Bowman are jointly lords of the mountain. All are 9-0 and are looking to keep a big goose egg in the loss bracket again tomorrow. This goes double for Weiser, who two weeks ago finished the first day of Grand Prix Columbus in the same position, but was unable to translate it to a Top 8.

There are some 8-1s still tightly chasing them. Shuhei Nakamura, Dan Lanthier, Jarvis Yu, and Ben Friedman—with his wonderful 4 1/2-Color Dragon deck—are all at one loss so far. There's still a lot more *Magic* to come.

And the race for Grand Prix Master is also very much alive. Though Brian Braun-Duin started 7-0, he lost his last two rounds and now sits just one match about Tomoharu Saito's record after day one at Grand Prix Taipei. Though still live for Top 8, unlike Saito.

But maybe that's all moot, because if Seth Manfield just wins yet another Grand Prix—which is clearly an emerging trend—then maybe he'll clinch Master despite his best intentions. #WorldChampProblems.

We'll be back tomorrow for the exciting conclusion of the *Shadows over Innistrad* Grand Prix circuit! See you then.
