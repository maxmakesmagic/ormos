In the wake of the bannings that followed Pro Tour Philadelphia, Words offers the *Magic* community and hungry-for-tech PTQ players their first look at high-level Modern play. With [Cloudpost](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudpost) and Infect decks gone and Blue-Red decks severely crippled, Worlds would set the new metagame that will almost certainly define the first weeks of the Modern PTQ season.

With that in mind, we looked at what the Top 20 tables were playing in the first round of Modern on Day 3. While we'll have a more robust look at the metagame later in the day, this first look holds a number of surprises that are likely to ripple through Modern.

First, the archetypes:

Almost certain to be the breakout deck of the tournament is Blue Zoo, an evolution of the CounterCat deck played by a number of pros at Pro Tour Philadelphia. Blue Zoo differs in that it has adapted [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage) which has given it a number of new toys to snap back, not the least of which is [Tribal Flames](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tribal+Flames). But [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt) and [Lightning Helix](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Helix) can give the deck an incredible amount of reach, and the ability to play spells like [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate) and [Bant Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bant+Charm) gives the deck a dimension other Zoo decks don't have.

*Ten you?*

 

[Splinter Twin](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter+Twin), as expected, stayed strong since it leaned on its [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder)s and [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain)s much less than decks like Ascension and Storm did. Most players seem to have simply slotted in [Sleight of Hand](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sleight+of+Hand) and kept right on rolling.

Another surprising deck that can't be ignored is the Four-Color Control. With [Sakura-Tribe Elder](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sakura-Tribe+Elder) and fetchlands finding Ravnica shocklands, the control deck utilizes discard for early control and [Gifts Ungiven](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gifts+Ungiven) to find its long-game [Punishing Fire](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Punishing+Fire)s and other goodies. The four players playing it seemed likely to have worked together, and several very big names are piloting this list, including a former Player of the Year.

Another deck making a surprise appearance in the hands of the tournament's top players is [Ad Nauseam](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ad+Nauseam), which tries to set up [Ad Nauseam](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ad+Nauseam) and [Angel's Grace](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel%27s+Grace), letting a player draw their entire deck. The kill condition is usually [Conflagrate](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Conflagrate), but when you draw your whole deck there are a number of ways to do it.

 

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ad%20Nauseam)](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ad%20Nauseam) [![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Angel%27s%20Grace)](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Angel%27s%20Grace)

*When you can't lose for going to 0, there's nothing stopping you from drawing your whole deck with [Ad Nauseam.](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ad+Nauseam.)*

 

The [Mystical Teachings](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mystical+Teachings) decks were all different and likely unrelated. One was pure Esper Control, one had [Gifts Ungiven](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gifts+Ungiven), and one appeared to be a supplement to a [Punishing Fire](https://web.archive.org/web/20111119000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Punishing+Fire)s plan.

Stay tuned throughout the day for more on several of these decks, as well as a look at the Modern metagame as a whole.
