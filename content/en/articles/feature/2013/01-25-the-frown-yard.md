Yesterday, [I looked at](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/kill-it-fire-2013-01-24) one of the few decks in Standard that's always the aggressor. Even after sideboarding, it had no way to slow a game down and take control—just more aggressive creatures and ways to push them through. Today, I'm going to look at the other end of the spectrum: a control players' control deck. This is for the **Magic** player who doesn't really have anywhere else to be and is looking to play as much match per round as possible. 

![Nephalia Drownyard](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Nephalia+Drownyard)

Nick Spagnolo is certainly a control player's control player, and he can always be counted on to show up with a blue deck that's looking to play some serious games of **Magic**. He was playing [Elixir of Immortality](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elixir+of+Immortality) well before it was cool. 

At the StarCityGames.com Invitational in Las Angeles, Nick's creatureless control deck could win a game with tokens made by [Lingering Souls](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lingering+Souls) or [Sorin, Lord of Innistrad](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sorin%2C+Lord+of+Innistrad), but that's definitely Plan B. Most games, he won by running his opponent out of cards (called "milling," after Alpha's [Millstone](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Millstone)) using his four [Nephalia Drownyard](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nephalia+Drownyard)s and his [Jace, Memory Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20130125000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+Memory+Adept). 

His entire deck is based around buying time to do that, with a few counterspells, several ways to kill all of his opponent's creatures, and a massive number—sixteen—of cards that can essentially draw more cards.

If you're looking to tap more lands than anyone else in the tournament, this is the deck for you, as it excels at tapping all of its mana on each of the many, many turns it will take every game.
