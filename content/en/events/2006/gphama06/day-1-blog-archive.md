I guess having done the artwork for seven of the must-have dual lands in the block will do that (and rumor has it that he did the last three as well, surprise surprise!). This weekend marked his second trip to Japan, and the players here are ecstatic, lining up 30 deep just for a few doodles from his Sharpie. I figured that I might as well get paid to learn.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <i>What are your favorite cards that you've worked on?</i>&#10;        <br>&#10;        <b>RA:</b> It's just too hard to pick one… I really like the new cycle of dual lands, I think those turned out really well. From the older days I thought Spectral Cloak was good and I like Underground Sea quite a bit as well. </p>&#10;      <p>I have to admit that I also quite like <a>Exalted Angel</a> (featured as one of the recent judge promos). It took me ten years to get my first angel, but I did a pretty good job when I finally got one.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <i>You've had a sizeable influence in setting the style for recent Magic sets…</i>&#10;        <br>&#10;        <b>RA:</b> Yeah, I've been on the style guide teams for Kamigawa, Ravnica, and Time Spiral. Last Emperor Jin Okamoto claims he's retired, but got the call and rallied behind the Fireball banner yet again with shop owners Jun Nobushita and Fukuoka's Tsuyoshi Ikeda. Backing up Tokyo's hard working deckbuilder were 2004 standout Ryou Ogura and Takuya Oosawa, a name you should be hearing more of this year. All six have worked with each other over numerous occasions, so they came to the table in good spirits.</p>&#10;      <p>Nobushita's discard-heavy Hand in Hand Orzhov fared poorly in the first match against Ishida. He tried to put <a>Ravenous Rats</a> and <a>Dark Confidant</a> on the table, but Ishida's Izzet <a>Magnivore</a> build kept threw <a>Stone Rain</a>s, <a>Electrolyze</a>s, and <a>Boomerang</a>s to keep the Orzhov army away.
