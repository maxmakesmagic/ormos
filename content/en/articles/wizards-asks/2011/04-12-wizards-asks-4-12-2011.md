*How do you decide on your basic land mix in Sealed Decks?* 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e0/0c/e00c2900e4532fed44efdbc0223f486ee1518c0cf594bb289867319eeda0f043.jpg)

**Kelly Digges:** The rule of thumb that most people seem to follow is to total up all your mana symbols, figure out what ratio each color makes up, and arrange your basics in a similar ratio. This is a good starting point that will seldom lead you astray—but I like overthinking things, so I've got a few extra tricks up my sleeve.

A friend of mine (who was better at **Magic** than I was) once told me that what you really need to ask yourself are two questions: *What's the minimum number of this basic land I need to run to cast my spells of this color when I want to?* and *What's the maximum number of this basic land I can play and still be able to cast my other spells?*

In answering these questions, I think about how many mana-fixers I have (such as Rampant Growth or Prophetic Prism) and what color(s), if any, I need to cast them. I think about not only how many mana symbols I have, but how expensive the spells they appear on are. If I need one red mana early for a lot of my spells but two green mana late for many others, I'll weight Mountains and Forests more evenly than I would based on the ratios.

Occasionally these questions lead me to some odd places, but those odd places usually work out well for me. Probably the strangest result was a *Scars of Mirrodin* Sealed Deck that was nearly mono-artifact, with something like six mana Myr. I ran fifteen lands, and nine of them were Swamps, so I'd be sure of getting maximum value out of Carnifex Demon—*my only black card*. The other six lands were two three-land splashes for my three or four other colored cards, aided and abetted by all those Myr.
