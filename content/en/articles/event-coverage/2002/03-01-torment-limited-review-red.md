This is the third installment in my series analyzing the cards of Torment for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the second installment [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/02-26-torment-limited-review-blue).

### RED

In a set where white and green have seen their quality diminish both in the numbers and in strength, and everyone and their sister wants to be the black mage, you'd think there would be more metagame demand to be the red player, but that just isn't the case. Black's popular partner has become blue, and white seems to be the metagame choice, leaving red... well, to be forgotten.

Best as a partner to either black or green, the partner color will determine the style in which your red decks should be drafted. With red's creatures coming up lacking, it's best as a support color whose efficient burn can offer some control. If drafting red with green, you'll want to draft an aggressive deck for which card advantage is irrelevant in the face of doing massive amounts of damage as quickly as possible. If you draft red with black, it's more likely you'll want to use the massive amounts of kill available in both colors to control the table until finishing your opponent off is convenient.

Red does have some benefits. You get the best common kill spell in *Torment* in the form of Fiery Temper, the best card in the set in Violent Eruption and enough other kill to fill your deck box without having to fight too hard for any of it. Red may not be worth fighting for, but if no one else wants it, you'd be dumb to pass it up.

### The Cards

**Accelerate**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I haven't seen this card played too much, but I think it's definitely passable in the red-green squirrel deck. Getting a card into your graveyard without costing you a card, Accelerate can also deal a few points of damage on your behalf by actually doing what it's supposed to do, all the while throwing off opposing math by providing an attacker your opponent couldn't account for. If nothing else, you can use it on your opponent's turn two creature... on their end step. A 7th - 9th pick. 

**Balthor the Stout**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 With just five Barbarians in *Torment* and three in *Odyssey*, there won't be too many opportunities to put Balthor's strength into use, but that doesn't mean it isn't strength. The abilities this legend possesses are definitely potent, and when you have barbarians, he's going to be more than worthwhile to play, but if you don't have any, I'd recommend heavily against playing him: as if a 2/2 body for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) isn't bad enough, he'll pump up opposing Barbarians. Find another Grey Ogre. A 5th - 11th pick, depending on how many Barbarians you have. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b0/08/b008a7ed6204b9c69b5be2179878c99ae134b5ac4f330b569d0bad54d3a057e0.jpg)**Barbarian Outcast**  
 This card I like, because it has a very specific niche, namely red-black beat down, which isn't very popular, and it's only good in the confines of that niche. In English, I love the Outcast because it's a very playable card that you'll get to draft late as you'll often be the only person playing both colors. Obviously, there are consistency issues as it's only 'strong' on turn two, but these are the risks you take. A 5th -7th pick, but you'll get them a lot later. 

**Crackling Club**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A marginal card that can usually determine whether you have a good deck or not. If you're playing the Club, you have a low maintenance, low versatility card that may prove next to useless against some decks, suggesting your deck isn't quite as good as you want it to be. The Club will be ok, potentially dealing a point or two of damage before being expunged (hopefully), but it's really not exciting enough to want in your main deck. A 7th - 9th pick.

**Crazed Firecat**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 For seven mana, you'd better get a heck of a lot, and you'd better be sure of it. Seven mana for a 4/4 creature is unacceptable, and while the cat can obviously get a lot bigger (I read one tournament report where it went to 15/15), I like to know what I'm getting. If I'm going to risk holding onto a creature I can't cast while facing a lethal attack, that creature had better be dominant when it hits the table. A 4/4 just doesn't do it for me. An 8th - 10th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/89/6e/896e40220d4c736b8fff690bde80e19d6f649e13d59f1431ac443525270b09f1.jpg)**Devastating Dreams**  
 I'm not a huge fan of the Dreams cards, but this one sure packs a punch. Played with some fat green guys, the Dreams can not only wipe out opposing forces while leaving one or two of your guys standing, but also could prevent your opponent from casting the replacement troops needed for survival. Because of the low casting cost, the Dreams allows you to float mana from destroyed lands and cast creatures after the fact, and as such, should more often then not directly lead to victory. I like that quality in a card. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Enslaved Dwarf**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Dwarf could well contribute to a red-black beatdown deck as a one mana creature that offers the opportunity for synergy and the chance to deal a couple of points of damage at the beginning of the game. They'll come late and shouldn't be in most decks, but they may find their niche. A 9th - 11th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/dc/b4/dcb41a2de60b08bbca5b28d17b7bb034595c4ce2f07b224f3a8602fdb8fb9a75.jpg)**Fiery Temper**  
 Red's best common, the Temper may be the best common card in the set, though it probably takes a close second to Faceless Butcher. Three mana for a three damage instant is a pretty fair deal in the creature kill department, and this spell would be one of the best commons in the set without the madness factored in, but it is that factor which makes this card so strong, regenerating a creature, drawing a card or boosting power or toughness while taking out its target, all at a cost of one mana. It's just too good to not give it the respect it deserves. A 1st - 3rd pick. 

**Flaming Gambit**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I really seem to like this card more than most. At the speed of an instant, you get to deal X damage twice, and while your opponent will obviously get to make most of the choices, most of the time it will either kill two creatures or, if you've managed to clear the table, kill one opponent. The down side is that if they get a protection from red creature into play, this card is dead, but hey, you can always try to bluff your way through, right? A 6th - 8th pick.

**Flash of Defiance**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A solid sideboard card, the problem with the Flash is that it is very seldom that your opponent will have only white or green blockers, meaning that there will be other obstacles to overcome. If your deck races, side it in against those decks with high numbers in the green/white creature departments, but don't rely on it too heavily. An 8th - 10th pick. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4b/f7/4bf75e9a23c699596e49a035dc5352ff7b7412325d36dd2f752bbeaf32096c21.jpg)**Grim Lavamancer**  
 You can't ask for much more for one mana, can you? A first turn play that can get a couple of points of damage in while your opponent gets their mana on the table, the Lavamancer really shines later on, dominating smaller creatures by trading an already used resource, graveyard cards, for them. The Lavamancer is one of the best first turn plays in recent Limited history. If your deck doesn't need threshold and is low on flashback, it's value becomes even more increased. As it is, it's going to be ridiculously good regardless. A 2nd - 4th pick

**Hell-Bent Raider**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Grey Ogres are unexciting, but they sure packed a decent amount of power into this one. Haste, first strike and the ability to grant itself protection from white, make the Raider a very potent creature for the cost involved. Yes, it would be a lot better if the cost only required one red mana, but hey, what can you do? As is, you'll have to be happy with three special abilities for one low price. Good deal. A 3rd - 5th pick.

**Kamahl's Sledge**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Everything I said about Crazed Firecat goes double here. While it has a passable effect, the Sledge costs way too much for what it does. On the bright side, it seems like by the time you reach seven mana, you should have or be close to threshold. Still doesn't take the sting of holding the Sledge as you die, does it? A 10th - 12th pick.

**Longhorn Firebeast**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not much of a control card, in decks like red-green beatdown, where you're taking the sligh approach of doing as much damage as you can with each card, this guy becomes playable. Frankly, in an aggressive deck that gets going faster than your opponents, a 3/2 creature and five points of damage are both good options for three mana, and while your opponent will get to choose the less detrimental, the less detrimental should do the trick. I wouldn't play this outside of red-green or a really aggressive red-black, but it is viable. A 7th - 9th pick. 

**Overmaster**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While Overmaster seems to have more practical applications in Constructed, it's always going to be playable in Limited too. Any spell that cycles for one mana in the threshold environment should be good enough to make your deck, and as an added bonus, you can make sure that important instant can't be countered by those annoying blue mages. Better than a Grey Ogre, be careful about playing it over the 2/2s for three of the world if your creature count is low. A 6th - 8th pick.

**Pardic Arsonist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 They're going to be calling this thing the new Flametongue for the next year and a half, and while it obviously isn't quite as good as the original, the Arsonist can put a pretty big hurting on your opponent. The one problem with this card is that red is not very good at achieving threshold, in that it not only has very few ways to get cards in the graveyard, but also is the least synergetic color with blue. That said, at worst the Arsonist provides a 3/3 body for four mana, and in non-green *Odyssey* block, that's a pretty good deal. A 2nd - 4th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/78/e9/78e920c36bf7392567f28084853fb5b327a7943060d002036beff1520af2aac1.jpg)**Pardic Collaborator**  
 One of the benefits of being the black player nowadays is that there are red and blue cards that players of those colors who aren't playing black won't want. Heading the list of these cards is Pardic Collaborator, a 2/2 first striker in the hands of the non-black mage, it's barely playable in that situation, but backed by a few swamps, it becomes a powerhouse, and one you may get as late as sixth or seventh if you happen to be the only red-black player at the table. I wouldn't pass it in the hopes of getting it back ninth or tenth, but don't be too shocked if you get this guy relatively late. A 2nd - 4th pick.

**Pardic Lancer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Five mana won't always buy you what it used to. Granted, the threat of +1/+0 and first strike is an annoying one, but the cost is a random discard, and that will throw the controller's desire to use this ability too often. Easy to kill, not especially large, for five mana you really should get more. Save those parts of your mana curve for something more powerful. An 8th - 10th pick

**Petradon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Well, this guy is big if nothing else. The Petradon's land removal shouldn't be too relevant by the time you get to eight mana, but against three-color decks it may remove an entire color. Aside from that though, you do get a very large creature that will kill in three turns as often as not, but a lack of evasion makes this a questionable purchase for eight mana. If you're sure your deck can get to that point, don't be afraid to play this beast, but I wouldn't pick it too high. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Petravark**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I like this card a lot more than I originally thought I would. Petravark takes a land out, removing it from the game until it dies (and if this occurs in an exchange, it will likely be worth while), providing you with one turn worth of temporal advantage as your opponent has less mana with which to establish themselves. Less effective later in the game, it's the early game that matters, so this little beater maintains a decent rating. A 4th - 6th pick.

**Pitchstone Wall**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Do you realize how big 2/5 is for three mana? I don't care if this thing doesn't attack, the Wall completely stops an opposing ground offense, allowing you in the process to save your creature kill for those annoying evasion creatures. The Wall's additional ability is seldom going to be used, as most of the discarding done in this format is done to benefit the discarder, but it gives you an extra option with Looters and the like, and offers protection from Last Rites just in case. A 4th - 6th pick.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7e/48/7e48e51766344a73b2692334f1fd60483511138dc306ae317ff5cc81759c3edb.jpg)**Possessed Barbarian**  
 Like all the other possessed creatures, the Barbarian is great. A 3/3 for four mana, the Barbarian only gets better with threshold, killing opposing red creatures and becoming a ridiculous 4/4 first striker for four mana. There's really not a lot of negative to this card. Just pick it, and play it, and most of the time you should be very glad you did. A 1st - 4th pick.

**Pyromania**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I guess you could say red got the second shortest stick on this one. While Pyromania is definitely more playable than Mortiphobia, it just doesn't compare with Narcissism, Compulsion or even Hypochondria. Still, it gives you an outlet for threshold/madness while offering you a recurring damage source that will take out those annoying one-toughness critters, and while the discard is random, you can get yourself to a point where the only cards you have in hand are there to be discarded anyways. A 5th - 7th pick. 

**Skullscorch**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There are no one or two line paragraphs in red thus far. Finally, there's a card that's pointless enough in Limited to do one. A 15th pick.

**Sonic Seizure**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While this is no Lightning Bolt, one mana for three points is still a great deal. If you're going to play with a card that assures you card disadvantage, it might as well involve a lot of power and a very little mana, and the Seizure fits that bill. Don't play more than two as they'll just get discarded to one another, but at the same time, don't be afraid to play them main deck. A 5th - 7th pick.

**Temporary Insanity**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Do you guys remember Ray of Command? I sure do. You'd be sitting there, feeling really good about how the game was going with your four creatures to one advantage, then you'd attack and suddenly, your opponent would tap four mana, three of your guys would die and your opponent would only lose two life in the exchange. Now, if you play red, you can be on the other side of the table. A 2nd - 4th pick, only because red has trouble accumulating graveyard cards.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/63/7c/637c398447f85d22a94efeffa3c0f2d738fdb5f71d2e2a9202cc23fbac442432.jpg)**Violent Eruption**  
 Ladies and gentlemen, may I introduce the best card in *Torment* for Limited play. There are a lot of ways to discard in *Odyssey* block, and that means that the casting cost of this card more often than not will be ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif). I don't care if you use the madness to attain card advantage or not, because when you pay three mana for four points of damage split however you want, you win. Pick it. A lot. A 1st pick.
