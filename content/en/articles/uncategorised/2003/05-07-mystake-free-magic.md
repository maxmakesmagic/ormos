### Tips On Avoiding Costly Errors

* * *

What is the biggest difference between a solid, knowledgeable player and a great player? The answer is one mistake per game. While a **Magic** game is always filled with uncertainty due to card draws, drafting order, random sealed decks, or unexpected meteors landing in the middle of your game table, the top-notch players always seem to overcome these obstacles with error-free, precision play.

It's easy for us pedestrian players to whine about our horrible luck, mana screw, or inability to topdeck the right card, but the players that win, and win often, beat us with similar decks in Constructed, and sometimes inferior decks in Limited.

One article won't make you a Pro player. Heck, I'm writing it and I'm not that good (my personal online rating is about 1670), but if you actually pay attention to the tips here and use them, I can promise you'll win a few extra games. These tips are presented for ***Magic** Online* but may be applicable to physical **Magic** games as well.

### Missing Card Text

How many games have you lost because you weren't paying attention to the card text? In ***Magic** Online,* you're faced with a challenge you don't have to worry about with physical cards--missing text.

Depending on your **Display** settings, it's possible that you don't see all of a card's text. Avarax is a good example. It has haste--clearly visible at the top of the card--but you can also give it +1/+0 by paying ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif).

It's easy to miss this when attacking or defending, since it isn't visible on the card unless you point at the card to show it in the large card view. If you aren't paying attention, or don't remember the ability, you might not successfully kill off your opponent's Barkhide Mauler or hit for the extra point needed for victory. If you don't know a card inside and out, take an extra moment to point at the card to reveal all the text.

### Distractions

In a live tournament setting, chances are you'll play your entire match without any serious distractions. Certainly a friend might wander up and say hello, or a judge might be looking over your shoulder, but for the most part it's just you and your opponent. (Of course, your opponent's personal hygiene might be distracting enough.) Online, however, you may have to deal with your roommate, pets, phone, parents, doorbell, kid sister, the pizza delivery guy--you get the idea.

The great thing about ***Magic** Online* is your ability to play in a completely casual environment of your choice, but it's this feature that often poses problems. When all it takes is one misclick or bad play to lose a game, even the smallest distraction can cost you a win. If the phone rings or some other distraction looms, rather than rush your play, let your opponent know you need a moment or two while you deal with the distraction. Come back to the game and refocus. It may save you from a costly mistake.

### Time Waits For No One

***Magic** Online* brings you something you don't have in physical **Magic** games: a clock. In a Sanctioned game you have thirty minutes to finish, while League games give you a generous forty-five minutes. If your clock runs out, you lose. No grace period, no cookies, no writing home to mother. When your clock runs out, the ballgame is over. Keep an eye on your timer. Generally speaking, if you're playing, and not trying to cook dinner and entertain your girlfriend at the same time, you'll never have to worry about your clock.

If you're running some ability-intensive control deck, constant clicking and tapping will eat up your clock. Be aware of this and don't doddle. Know your deck ahead of time and, if you have a chatty opponent, just politely wish him or her a good game and let your opponent know you need to pay attention to your deck and won't be chatting much.

### Lag And The Internet

Chances of a live tournament ever getting cancelled due to a faulty phone line, Internet issue, or server problem are pretty slim. Any of these could sabotage your online game, though.

If your ISP is plagued with terrible lag or has a habit of disconnecting you for no apparent reason, it would be a good time to stick to casual games. The ***Magic** Online* servers have proven themselves to be as reliable as any, but depending upon circumstances such as massive tournaments or large invitational events, there could be issues. Large events are announced well prior to their dates, however, so it's easy enough to avoid Sanctioned matches during these times.

One last note: if you are disconnected from a Sanctioned tourney and you think it had something to do with an issue on Wizards of the Coast's end, email techsupport@wizards.com with all of the pertinent information, including game numbers, draft numbers, date, and time.

### Timing And The Stack

Always remember: Last In, First Out.

Not understanding exactly how the stack works can cost you games. For example, you're attacking with a 4/1 trampling Branchsnap Lorian and a facedown Patron of the Wild. Your opponent is at 5 life, has an empty hand, and nothing on the board but an untapped Sparksmith. If the Sparksmith blocks the Lorian, your opponent takes 5 damage and loses, so the only safe move is to block the 2/2 morphing creature and hope for the best.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b1/6f/b16fecdbec62bdc20f91b19e56af60ff53eba99246fc4f3dc426fc9c14f8b8bf.jpg)

This is where it's easy to make a timing error. In a hurry to win, you morph the Patron to make your 4/1 trampler a 7/4 fatty for the win. This is a huge mistake. Your opponent taps the Sparksmith in response, pings your 4/1, and destroys your Patron as well, since it's only going to be a 1/1 when damage resolves. Your opponent is down to 4 life, and both of you have an empty board now.

The smart play is to wait and see what your opponent does. The Sparksmith is going to the graveyard no matter what. The only question is whether or not to ping your beast. If your opponent decides not to ping your beast, 4 damage gets through, the Sparksmith goes to the graveyard, and your opponent has to topdeck something to deal with both creatures on the next turn.

If your opponent does ping the Lorian, you morph your Patron, target your beast and win the game. Your effect resolves first, and the beast becomes a hefty 7/4 that will giggle at the 1 point of damage dealt by the Sparksmith.

Again: Last In, First Out.

### *Magic Online* Game Mechanics

Don't dive into Sanctioned games until you know how a **Magic** mechanic works and how to use it in ***Magic** Online.* Mechanics such as madness require a bit of finesse to use, while others such as flashback aren't quite as difficult to figure out.

The best way to be sure you understand how to play a card mechanic is to build a deck then play a solitaire game. Go to any Constructed **Casual Play** room, start a new game, and select **Solitaire** in the **New Game Options** window. This lets you to practice in a game with no opponent.

You'll need to test some things with an opponent, however. A good example would be using provoke on a creature with a tapping ability. If a provoked creature has an ability that requires it to tap, you don't have to block with it. You can either wait until the effect resolves then use the ability or, if you want to use the ability twice, tap the creature, wait for the provoke effect to resolve, then tap the creature again.

### Settings And Stops

Stops tell the ***Magic** Online* game which steps and phases you want the opportunity to do something. Stops and other features you'll need are found on the **Game Play** tab of your **Settings** window. Not setting the right stops for your deck will definitely effect your game play. For example, if you wish to Shock somebody during an opponent's end of turn phase, but don't have the stop set for that phase, the game will simply roll over to the beginning of the next player's turn.

The default settings are usually correct for most players, but some decks require a bit of stop tweaking to maximize your options.

### Pay Attention

Blocking a 5/5 trampler with a 1/1 creature instead of a 4/4, or playing a mountain when you have a Birds of Paradise in hand on turn one are examples of careless play. These types of mistakes seem simple enough to avoid, yet they happen all the time.

Don't hurry. Survey the board and your options before you make your play. The extra 3 points of damage you take, or the inability to cast your morphing creature on turn 2 could easily cost you a game.
