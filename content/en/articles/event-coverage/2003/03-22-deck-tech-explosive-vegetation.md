![Explosive Vegetation](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Explosive+Vegetation)

By now you've heard plenty about the giant monsters. You've seen how eight-mana spells have dominated the environment, and how one gigantic Legend or another seems to be lurking around every corner. Well, whether it's [Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20030322000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon)s or Angels or Elemental Legends doing the beating, each of these decks has one strategy at its core: get to six mana as fast as possible. And one card is central to that strategy: [Explosive Vegetation](https://web.archive.org/web/20030322000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explosive+Vegetation).

Vegetation, or "Veggies" as it's being called here, is the key card in what looks to be a diverse selection of mana acceleration cards. With the ability to go from 4 to 6 land is nice, the added ability of fixing colors is what makes it so amazing. Let's look at four relevant decks.



Mana Fixers: 4 Veggies, 4 [Wirewood Elf](https://web.archive.org/web/20030322000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wirewood+Elf)  
 Jensen's deck ditches the Tuskers for Wirewood Elves. With only two colors, the "tutoring" of the Tusker isn't really needed, and the Elf allows for some truly crazy openings. Imagine turn-2 Elf, turn-3 Veggies, turn-4 [Silvos](https://web.archive.org/web/20030322000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvos) or [Exalted Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20030322000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Exalted+Angel). With so many decks running [Silvos](https://web.archive.org/web/20030322000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvos), the games can often be decided by who gets his in play first, and Jensen's deck seems to have the most consistently fast way of doing so.
