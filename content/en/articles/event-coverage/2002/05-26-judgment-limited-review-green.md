This is the fifth installment in my series reviewing the cards of *Judgment* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the other installments [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### Green

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6f/d7/6fd7071710dce1c801f9658701fc2ba0f9a72c38a913713dc54a1cd728a49b5d.jpg)

Green rules. Sure, in *Torment* it got the short end, but in *Odyssey* it was the powerhouse that had the world buzzing and in *Judgment* it's even better. Be it the power cards, the fatties, the speed or the depth, green pretty much has everything you need in your quest to win games, matches and drafts.

Green's versatility is what makes it the best color in this environment. It diversifies your mana so you can play more colors than the norm. It plays well with any of the other four colors by its side. It provides a better creature base for mana curve and deck coherency purposes than the other four colors and its sideboard options are as strong if not stronger than its counterparts.

The one issue with drafting green is that everyone is going to want to draft green now, and its up to you to get the practice required in to develop that sense that tells you when it's a lost cause. Green decks won't be built on *Torment* pickings, so if you aren't seeing much in *Odyssey*, don't bother: you aren't getting much in *Judgment* either. 

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1a/6b/1a6b91c1e1eb90bafc5b08dad103379e9909f2c9dc2bb76492b21af637a70074.jpg)**Genesis**  
 So let me get this straight: You have a 4/4 for ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif), a pretty solid creature that your opponent needs to deal with. It's capable of returning itself to your hand for three mana during your upkeep, but it often won't as it can return any creature in its stead as long as it stays in the graveyard. That's nuts. A 4/4 that doesn't die should be good enough to win the long game more than not, but there will always be creatures your opponent must deal with. If Genesis returns those creatures to your hand two or three times, there's no way you're losing. Nuts.

**Erhnam Djinn**![](https://i.ormos.uk/76/60/7660cbc3cc3f44c8baebc99338d44c16707f1c478cc58f3dad64e85a54fa3dce.jpg)  
 Speed kills, and this *Arabian Nights* veteran has got speed to burn. Usually, you'd have to pay something like ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) for a 4/5 creature, but this guy comes with a drawback that, while potentially annoying, allowed R&D to give him to us with this kind of a casting cost. Yes, the Erhnam's drawback is annoying, but your opponent will usually have a 1/1 or at worst a 2/2 in play, so it isn't the end of the world. Just keep attacking with the Djinn if you need them to lose the forestwalk: They'll be forced to deal with it soon enough.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b6/39/b63907223958da33a6e43b5afedd1da90f3ba89a47169e611d79112d05b05046.jpg)**Phantom Centaur**  
 What a powerhouse! *Juggernaut* still gets played in Type 1 sometimes and it's a 5/3 for four mana, but it has to attack every turn, dies to Disenchant and Lightning Bolt and doesn't have protection from black. That's a lot of edge to have on a Type 1 card. The Centaur's Phantom ability can be a bonus or a drawback, depending on the situation it sees play in, but this card has so much going for it that I'd be happy to take either. Try enchanting it with a card that gives it +1/+1 or better and see what happens when all of the counters have been removed.

**Elephant Guide**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Probably the best creature enchantment in the environment outside of Persuasion, the thing that makes Elephant Guide so strong is its speed. Turn two Mongrel, turn three Guide, attack for five seems pretty good, doesn't it? That stuff's going to happen in Standard. Then, once (if) they deal with that monster, they'll likely have to deal with the 3/3 Elephant token that emerges from its wake. Yes, smothering enchantments like Kirtar's Desire and Lost in Thought and bounce spells can deal with both the Guide and the creature it enchants, but that's an acceptable price to pay. Pick it high, because non-green mages will be more than happy to splash it if you pass it to them.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/63/7b/637ba52e2fdb42b61bf8f60e585df1be6af4716e95be42e9970bbf3a6ca223f3.jpg)**Grizzly Fate**  
 Any time you can produce four creatures with one card it's generally a good thing, and that's despite the fact those creatures are likely to be 1/1s. Grizzly Fate produces 2/2 creatures at an astonishing rate, with threshold granting you as many as eight guys. That's eight 2/2s. Eight 2/2s. Are you kidding me? Especially cool with Overrun.

**Phantom Nantuko**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Some would rate this a little higher, but in order to activate its ability, you have to leave this Phantom untapped, and that means you have yourself a green wall, at least for a little while. There's no doubting this card is very good, getting a little better every turn it sticks around, but it doesn't have the immediate impact on the game the cards listed above it do, and that's why it doesn't place higher on this list.

**Sudden Strength**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It's a little costly, but the effect is definitely worth it. Sudden Strength serves to either kill a creature, save a creature or deal three damage to your opponent, all the while drawing you a card to replace itself with, and in any of those scenarios it seems more than worthwhile. I may be wrong to rate it this high because of its casting cost (You really want to be casting creatures early on), but this is the kind of card that can win a game.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5b/3e/5b3e7a1bc52a3a6e502d7da708afe19f8031e25c4a37348ddb36b11aca536798.jpg)**Ironshell Beetle**  
 Wow is this card good. I mean, generic 2/2 creatures for two mana are a longed for commodity in this day and age, and at its least that's what the Beetle is, but it can be so much more, protecting your one-toughness creature from one-power creature kill, making your creature bigger just before an attack or boosting your defender's resolve. One of the better tempo cards in the set.

**Nullmage Advocate**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 2/3 for three is a good deal, so why does this card need an excellent activated ability on top of that? More and more, the environment had added important enchantments, and for the (granted) high cost of returning two cards to your opponent's hand from their graveyard, you now have solutions to those enchantments. That cost means you'll have to pick and choose carefully, but there will definitely be times where its activation will be more than worthwhile. A very solid card.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/35/8c/358cf90981b97fafd76840ce3e1173cd422db5c8a9da49e1660816d2cfb6c4dc.jpg)**Brawn**  
 3/3 trample for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) is a good deal. The same without the trample has always been the standard by which other four casting cost green creatures have been measured, and the trample pushes the living incarnation of this card over that edge, but the graveyard ability puts things well beyond. Obviously, once you have Brawn, you'll want to get the biggest guys you can find to do your dirty work for you, but Brawn's pretty big itself, considering your opponent may be forced to try and keep it in play. Remember; if your opponent is low on life and you have discard capability, you should hold on to Brawn and discard it after blockers. Trample's pretty good when your opponent's blocks don't account for it.

**Harvester Druid**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A week ago, I wouldn't have thought this card would have rated this high, but everyone I talked to at the Prerelease agreed that of the green two-casting cost creatures in this set, this had been the best throughout the day. The key to the Harvester is the mana boost it provides, which couples well with the way it helps color diversification make the splashing of double casting cost bombs easy. Remember: the sac lands are capable of producing all five colors.

**Sylvan Safekeeper**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 It took Olle Rade half a decade to design this card for winning the Invitational, but it was worth the wait. For just one mana, you can virtually ensure the safety of every creature you control, while giving yourself an alternative use for those lands you don't really need and an instant threshold generator. Sure, it's just a 1/1 and you really won't be able to afford to protect it in the face of Psionic Gift, Cabal Torturer and the like, but if you deal with those threats, you won't have to deal with any others.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b3/7d/b37d49d57fbf7b4df23f851300034cc656d80ea6ef60aef8f7b609502577232d.jpg)**Anurid Swarmsnapper**  
 Green has traditionally had high-toughness creatures that served as blue's bane by blocking their flyers, and the Swarmsnapper adds to that tradition and then some. With a toughness of four, this Anurid is hard enough for flyers to get through, but it may be its other ability that makes it truly great. If you need to make a massive attack, you can leave the 1/4 back as your only blocker for your opponent's entire army, assuming you have the mana. There will always be green cards in freshly opened *Judgment* packs that you'll want to take over this, but unless you're red-green, I suggest you try hard to get one.

**Forcemage Advocate**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Returning a card from your opponent's graveyard is a pretty steep cost, but the Advocate is more than worth it. Offering some measure of table control, the Advocate's bark is worse than its bite, with the possibility of trading a creature for the land that's being returned usually too bitter for your opponent to actually taste. Throw in the fact this is a 2/1 creature for two mana, a valuable commodity in this set, and you have a very solid card that will have a profound effect on both the early and late game. 

**Thriss, Nantuko Primus**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Well, Thriss will definitely dominate the table, either creating two huge blockers (herself included) or one insane attacker, but for seven mana you have to get a lot, and having her in your opening hand is awful, so she doesn't get placed higher up this list. She would in most sets, but *Judgment* isn't most sets.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/14/67/1467fe9594cd203a117440089d191f2fe52b330fca087928825480eee8e9ecb8.jpg)**Phantom Tiger**  
 That this card is this low on this list is indicative of how strong green is in this set. I've heard the Tiger likened to Penumbra Bobcat, and the comparison isn't unfounded except that it's a little bit bigger when it starts out. The Tiger will be great as long as there are no pingers or four toughness creatures that aren't Angelic Wall in play. Once there are, it won't do too much for you, but if it's part of the swarm it'll still do its job.

**Living Wish**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The only main-deckable Wish card, this one is actually really good, serving as a mana fixer that can become a creature in the late game. For two mana, you can go get any of the five basic land types or some quirky nonbasic you hadn't planned on utilizing that turns out to be good in some particular situation. Once you have your mana though, you don't want to go searching for more land, and with the Wish you don't have to, allowing you to get that off color creature you couldn't splash that you now have a sac land for. Utility is a good thing; this provides it.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/29/33/2933dba5f8531e378ac382379f9388d830e11c11e107ba63575d125df29c5578.jpg)**Serene Sunset**  
 A very cool trick in a set full of cool tricks, the Sunset will absolutely destroy in the green on green match up while serving capably in your non-green match ups. The more combat interaction, the better this will be. Don't pick it over quality creatures unless you have a lot of them already, as without creatures it loses its usefulness, but Serene Sunset will definitely serve its purpose, be it protecting your creatures or preventing damage to yourself.

**Exoskeletal Armor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Hans has been running from the Lhurgoyf for years ("Ach, Hans, run! It's the Lhurgoyf!" - Lhurgoyf's flavor text), but *Odyssey* block really has him in trouble. First R&D creates the 'Vores, forcing players to count the artifacts, enchantments or creatures in all graveyards and now they've done it again with this Lhurgoyf enchantment. Exoskeletal Armor can provide a huge boost for very little mana, but the mana isn't all that relevant, as this card will only become effective in the midgame. Not too shabby, but don't put all of your eggs in this basket: there are a lot of cards your opponent can use to get your creatures out of the graveyard.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/bf/6a/bf6a7c08bfd50e8006b47fef8a75b23c3def76f9c8717c9df35a3f8849e5a7dc.jpg)**Nantuko Tracer**  
 2/1 for two is a commodity the other colors beg for, and while it isn't all that special in green, it's definitely good enough for your deck in an environment where you'll want to get the jump if at all possible. While the Tracer's ability doesn't look like anything to write home about at first glance, it can deprive your opponent of threshold, flashback or an incarnation, and that's pretty good. If you get to the midgame and a 2/1 won't make a dent in your opponent's forces, don't throw it on the table the way you would most 2/1s. Instead, hold it for a couple of turns and see if you can utilize the graveyard ability. 

**Giant Warthog**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I know some would rate this higher, but six mana is one heck of a price tag for any ground creature without activated abilities, so until we see how the environment pans out, this is where the 'hog lands on my list. Don't get me wrong, 5/5 trampling creatures are pretty good once they hit play, but that's the key: you've got to get it into play and then keep it there, and that costs.

**Seedtime**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Powerhouse sideboard cards win games, and I've always believed that gave licence to pick them higher than mediocre main deck cards. Seedtime is just that, a card that will ruin blue mages, essentially reading 'draw a card, untap your lands and creatures and get an extra combat step'. That's a pretty good deal for two mana, isn't it?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/18/34/18347a84dffec8d4fadc4ceac973ad4cd1c6ce1d7a685f0091cf403f85062a00.jpg)**Battlefield Scrounger**  
 I'm not a huge fan of this card if only because there are so many cards in the format you'll want those cards in the graveyard, but if you're in need of fat you can definitely do worse. The cool thing about the Scrounger is that once you have threshold, your opponent will have to play with the assumption you'll active its ability if given reason to, and that means they'll treat it as a 6/6. Now, if you only have 7, 8 or 9 cards in your graveyard, they could force you to either lose the Scrounger or threshold by doing this, but you'll usually gain a favorable advantage in making that kind of exchange. Don't pick this high, but if your deck is a little lean, this can be the fat you're looking for.

**Anurid Barkripper**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Well, the power is there, but the payment is pretty high. In a color where the standard is better than 2/2 for three, the Barkripper is just that, bringing with it a harder-than-normal casting cost. Obviously, later in the game this little guy will become this big guy, paying dividends on your patience, but that won't happen all that quickly unless you're playing blue, so when you draft it, think of it as a generic creature with potential for later bonuses.

**Venomous Vines**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not a great main deck choice as some players won't have anything to destroy, but the Vines will serve you well from the board, killing those creatures enchanted by Psionic Gift and Arcane Teachings, and those lands enhanced by Squirrel Nest and Chamber of Manipulation. Try to grab one late if you can.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/14/a9/14a9d7289b929601ae2d83ba33023ad7c04cb4233c73e8f2eb9fb0c4b5795561.jpg)**Crush of Wurms**  
 Nine mana is a ridiculous amount and don't even get me started on the flashback, but if you build your deck around it with cards like New Frontiers and Mirrari's Wake, the Crush is viable. Good luck finding those rares.

**Canopy Claws**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not the strongest sideboard card ever created, the flashback makes it useful, potentially trading for two opposing flyers. If you're really desperate to kill those Windreaders and Avens, don't be afraid to bring in the Claws.

**Tunneler Wurm**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Sorry guys, but eight mana creatures without any evasion abilities just don't seem worth it to me, but if they were, I'd pay one more mana for the second and third 6/6s.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9d/71/9d71a8931873ebc7ee2aacfec1b677c99a295de28fc7054cc7c5efbe706c0c41.jpg)**Centaur Rootcaster**  
 Well, the ability is a good one, but the problem here is that 2/2's for four mana usually aren't quick enough to get through for damage if your opponent doesn't want them to. If you're really desperate for creatures, I suppose you can try this one, but I don't even like Nantuko Calmer because it's too small for its casting cost, so I find this guy to be really lacking.

**Krosan Wayfarer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 First turn mana boosts are valuable, but they aren't worth card disadvantage in Limited. Don't bother.

**Epic Struggle**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Have three Squirrel Nests, four Acorn Harvests and two Grizzly Fates? Do I have the card for you...

**Krosan Reclamation**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If your opponent has tons of Werebears and Firebolts, you might consider bringing this in. I'd recommend you only do so in the most dire of circumstances.

**Folk Medicine**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 You'd have to be pretty desperate for life gain to side this in. Life Burst isn't playable, and it automatically gives you four life for two mana. Don't bother.
