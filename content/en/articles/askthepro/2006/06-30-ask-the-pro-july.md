Unfortunately, I was at seven and Craig drew <a>Char</a> and <a>Lightning Helix</a> in the last two draws while I had one draw to find removal, <a>Ghost Council of Orzhova</a> or <a>Umezawa's Jitte</a> and another one turn to find a non-<a>Dark Confidant</a> creature. He had about 0.4 percent chance to win that game two turns to the end (I had done the math right after the game), but he kept me away from a final that would have been a pretty good matchup. So I'd say that good play was useless in the end.</p>&#10;<p><table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td><td></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td><td><a><img alt="Reroute" src="https://i.ormos.uk/97/e5/97e56382130d7c1a642f44dda6dcca32c09ae021879839a4f7f52beb7ba0972a.jpg"></a></td><td></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>Against Joseph Crosby is another key game in the next PT, in Prague. I need to go 2-2-1 to make Top 8 at this point. This is a way I can theoretically not win. I'm down a game, I've mulliganed down to five cards and missed my land drop on turns three and four. I'm trying to come back in the game but I can't win in any conventional way. So I just decide to try and make a plan around my <a>Reroute</a> in hand and his <a>Scorched Rusalka</a> on the table, to push him to sacrifice all of his creatures. </p>&#10;<p>The thing is I have to adapt my plan on every turn to find out exactly what I need to do for him to sac his last guys. For this I have to not keep any white open to let him believe that I don't have any possible trick or prevention spell. When I draw <a>Withstand</a>, things get even more complex. Eventually, I manage to <a>Withstand</a> an attack phase tapping out all my white, and then to <a>Reroute</a> his Rusalka. <a>Life</a> totals are then 1-19. I manage to come back, and on additional turn number two, realising I'll win on turn four, he concedes.</p>&#10;<p>About my worst play, it was on my first PT, in Rome in 1998. I was 1-0 in the tournament when I faced this Brazilian guy playing counter sliver. For all the game I'm thinking &quot;please no <a>Worship</a>! Please no <a>Worship</a>! Please no <a>Worship</a>! At the end of the match he asked me:</p>&#10;<p>&quot;Why did you concede Game 1?&quot;</p>&#10;<p>&quot;Well, I had no solution.&quot;</p>&#10;<p>&quot;Maybe, but I had no creature yet.&quot;</p>&#10;<p>&quot;........&quot;</p>&#10;<p>I would have probably lost it anyway, but I just feel like committing suicide whenever this story comes up to my mind.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 13, 2006</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Q</b>: I always think about the real extent of Pro Players' fame, and wonder how it works in their outside <b>Magic</b> lives. For many <b>Magic</b> players (like me), the Ruel brothers, Kenji Tsumura and Raphaël Lévy, among many others, are in a higher &quot;celebrity level&quot; than Madonna or Brad Pitt are for people in general, for example. So I would like to know: with your name known all around the world, even by a small percentage of people, have you already felt like a celebrity in or outside the PT halls? Have you already been recognized as a famous people walking in the street? Have you ever given an autograph in a shopping center, took a picture in a walk in the park, or received any kind of distinguished treatment elsewhere for being a Level 6 player?<br>-- Pablo Iacovazzo <br></p>&#10;<p><b>A:</b> There is the inside and the outside <b>Magic</b>. On the inside, I will sign cards and take pictures at every tournament I attend. Grand Prix-Mexico City is one of my best <b>Magic</b> memories, for I signed about 200 cards (including 20 pieces of Jitte), took about 25 pictures, and I even got to sign two shirts and one SHOE! I think Julien Nuijten signed the other one, or maybe was it Rogier Maaten.</p>&#10;<p>Outside <b>Magic</b>, fame disappears nearly completely. Even if my banker asked me if my brother was playing <b>Magic</b> too for he knew there were two bros doing well in that game, and if my next-door neighbour recognized me, these are exceptions. But my favorite exception occurred last December. While I was waiting for a friend in front of the Fountain St-Michel, in Paris, I was offering some couples to take pictures for them. And then, at some point, I see this guy who is staring at me. I act like I don't care, wait for 20 seconds and look again... still the same stare.</p>&#10;<p><b>Him:</b> Are you playing <b>Magic</b>?<br><b>Me:</b> I am.<br><b>Him:</b> Man, you're Olivier Ruel, right? You just seem to be everywhere!<br> (Then he calls for his girlfriend)<br><b>Him:</b> This is Olivier Ruel!<br><b>Her:</b> (Nearly fascinated) Hey! (she actually meant something like 'good for you' I believe)<br><b>Him:</b> (to her) Can you take a picture of us?</p>&#10;<p>This role reversal was pretty nice.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>July 11, 2006</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Q</b>: Hi, have you ever tried to get your opponent a game loss when you were playing in a sanctioned event? I can imagine that a lot of people do this, but I was just wondering if this happens a lot when Pros play<br>-- Anonymous</p>&#10;<p><b>A:</b> I used to when I started playing. The environment in which I grew up was rotten with unsportsmanlike players and extremely severe judges who would issue game losses for nearly no reason (player B sacrifices <a>Urza's Bauble</a> on his turn, player A doesn't tell his player B to draw at the beginning of his own turn, then asks for a game loss). I was behaving the same until I first qualified for a Pro Tour.</p>&#10;<p>It made me realize the <b>Magic</b> I knew was different from the one I wanted to play. The better my results got, the more I relied on my skills to try and win games. If an opponent is unhappy about his last play I'll let him take it back most of the time now, and I won't call for a judge for my opponent to get a sanction, except for slow playing.</p>&#10;<p>Globally, on the Tour, players are a lot more sportsmanlike, and there are also fewer cheaters than at local events, because in the end skills and testing make the difference.</p>&#10;<p>If you start focusing on the ways you can win without playing, maybe will it work sometimes, but you will lose at least as many games for focusing a little less on the game itself, and moreover, you will hardly get satisfaction from winning like this.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

For instance, Affinity, Goblins or Zoo where pretty obvious builds from the beginning. Pros have more time to invest on playtesting, so they will have time to anticipate those decks and to find the appropriate answer.</p>&#10;<p> Of course, non-Pros can play control and they can play it well, but generally, you can expect an experimented player to be playing control and a newcomer to run aggro.</p>&#10;<p>On local events I plan on beating aggro.</p>&#10;<p>At my Nationals, I try to beat aggro and do well against the more popular control deck.</p>&#10;<p>My plan preparing for a Pro Tour usually is 1) Beat aggro on Day One, 2) Defeat control on Day Two.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
