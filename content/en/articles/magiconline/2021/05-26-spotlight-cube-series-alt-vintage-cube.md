*Alt-Vintage Cube* is available to play on *Magic Online* May 26 through June 2.

* * *

Both Gaby Spartz and I are Cube aficionados, and it's really cool to get an opportunity to have our cube featured on *Magic Online*. We have this cube in paper in Denver and love playing it when we can (more in the coming months than in the last year, I can tell you that). We can't wait to share it with you and would like to give you a little background on what's going in our version of *Vintage Cube*.

Our cube is a Vintage cube constructed under the following tenets:

*   **High synergy** – The cube includes plenty of cards that need other cards to operate at their best. That's not to say that there aren't intrinsically powerful cards, but synergy is a high priority.

*   **High power** – Making big plays is awesome, and so is playing with powerful cards. We've included the best cards in the game and aren't shy about it.

*   **Lean and mean** – You'll find fewer classic five-mana Cube cards and more one- to three-mana plays in this cube. The focus is on cards that do something, and many longstanding cube cards got cut because other cards are just more efficient these days (sorry, [Mirari's Wake](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirari%27s+Wake)).

*   **Fun** – Some notable cuts from the cube ([Fractured Identity](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fractured+Identity), [Bribery](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bribery)) were made because they didn't lead to particularly fun games and weren't that interesting during the draft process. In particular, [Bribery](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bribery) punished decks playing cool creatures, and we chose to leave it out as a result.

The way our cube evolved was just based on what we enjoyed doing. We do think it's important to have a range of archetypes available, such as mono-red and mono-white to help keep people honest, but a lot of the small decisions we came to organically. I still remember when we decided to cut [Bribery](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bribery), after which we realized we could make similar cuts to cards like [Fractured Identity](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fractured+Identity) or [Opposition](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Opposition). I'd never fault you for including those in your cube, but we've had the experience of those cards enough that we wanted to rotate in some new things instead. Let's take a look at some of the archetypes we've made sure to support.

## Reanimator

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Reanimate)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reanimate)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Griselbrand)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Griselbrand)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Animate+Dead)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Animate+Dead)

Reanimator is a Cube staple and is well-supported here. This deck often touches blue for more draw and discard but can operate as mono-black or black-red as needed. Reanimator is the fastest combo deck when it comes together and is good at operating with the lowest amount of resources in particular.

You can mulligan to four or five and still have [Entomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Entomb) and [Animate Dead](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Animate+Dead) by turn two, which makes the deck much more resilient than others. That said, it's also the most reliant on drawing specific combinations, whereas other combo decks can usually make something happen with most combos of cards.

## Mono-Red Aggro

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Goblin+Guide)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Guide)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sulfuric+Vortex)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sulfuric+Vortex)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Shrine+of+Burning+Rage)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shrine+of+Burning+Rage)

The original "Fun Police," mono-red is actually fast enough to keep up in *Vintage Cube*. We've found that land destruction elements are key, with cards like [Wasteland](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wasteland) and [Strip Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Strip+Mine) being very important. I'd like to note that I personally enjoy the challenge of playing against mono-red and have drafted the 17-Mountain deck plenty of times myself.

## Mono-White Aggro

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Armageddon)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armageddon)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Thalia%2C+Guardian+of+Thraben)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thalia%2C+Guardian+of+Thraben)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Stoneforge+Mystic)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoneforge+Mystic)

Mono-white in this cube has an emphasis on taxing effects and early pressure, alongside both [Ravages of War](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ravages+of+War) and [Armageddon](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armageddon). White is another deck that can crush unprepared opponents and is surprisingly able to play long games with the equipment package.

## Green Ramp

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Noble+Hierarch)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Noble+Hierarch)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Natural+Order)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Natural+Order)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Craterhoof+Behemoth)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Craterhoof+Behemoth)

Green ramp decks are always strong in Cube, as one-drop accelerants into powerful plays is quite consistent. This completes the monocolor triad, as red, white, and green are all tier 1 when drafted solo (blue isn't because it's so hard to get that many blue cards, and black isn't because black needs other colors to support it).

The best ramp decks tend to focus on acceleration and cards of mana value 6 or more, mostly skipping the four- to five-mana plays (since those aren't as effective at closing out the game).

## Lands

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Oracle+of+Mul+Daya)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oracle+of+Mul+Daya)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Strip+Mine)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Strip+Mine)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Crucible+of+Worlds)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crucible+of+Worlds)

This is one of the more fun archetypes when it comes together. It uses [Crop Rotation](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crop+Rotation), [Wrenn and Six](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wrenn+and+Six), Crucible, and [Elvish Reclaimer](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elvish+Reclaimer) to get value from [Strip Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Strip+Mine) and [Wasteland](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wasteland), or to assemble the combo of [Dark Depths](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dark+Depths) and [Thespian's Stage](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thespian%27s+Stage).

There are plenty of supporting characters, and this deck has a ton of different ways to go depending on which you get. The rough idea is to combine cards that let you play extra lands with cards that let you play lands from your graveyard or the top of your library and get value from special lands as often as possible. This is my favorite deck to draft in the cube, and it has all the fixings here.

## Five-Color Niv

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Niv-Mizzet+Reborn)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Niv-Mizzet+Reborn)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Omnath%2C+Locus+of+Creation)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Omnath%2C+Locus+of+Creation)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Bring+to+Light)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bring+to+Light)

The five-color Niv deck really alters the drafting experience, and the more cards that do that, the better. This deck is a blast to play and is quite good if you pick up the key cards (chiefly, Niv and Omnath). The idea is to play a ton of gold cards, five-color mana fixing, and payoffs like Niv, Omnath, and [Bring to Light](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bring+to+Light).

Note that [Bring to Light](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bring+to+Light) also has the mini-combo with Valki, and it's great if you can get both cards.

## Artifacts

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Mana+Vault)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Vault)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Mishra%27s+Workshop)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mishra%27s+Workshop)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Urza%2C+Lord+High+Artificer)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Urza%2C+Lord+High+Artificer)

Artifacts, or blue-X, uses all the artifact payoffs and artifact mana to do big things. Urza often carries the deck, and it's the best [Balance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Balance) and [Mishra's Workshop](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mishra%27s+Workshop) deck as well. This is the best big-mana deck outside of mono-green and even gets to take full advantage of cards like [Balance](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Balance), [Upheaval](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Upheaval), and [Time Spiral](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Time+Spiral).

## Blue-Red Combo

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Splinter+Twin)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter+Twin)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Sneak+Attack)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Counterspell)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Counterspell)

There are a bunch of two- and three-card combos in blue-red ([Sneak Attack](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sneak+Attack), [Splinter Twin](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Splinter+Twin), Narset/[Hullbreacher](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hullbreacher) plus draw 7s), and they often play like combo-control alongside the good blue disruption. This is likely the strongest deck overall when it comes together and is in many ways the archetypal *Vintage Cube* deck.

It turns out that if you have good card draw and good counterspells, there is no shortage of ways to finish the job, and compact combos fit nicely into these deck styles.

## Blue-White Blink

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Ephemerate)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ephemerate)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Mulldrifter)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Yorion%2C+Sky+Nomad)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yorion%2C+Sky+Nomad)

Most of these cards are present in the usual MTGO *Vintage Cube*, but we added a few more blink effects and a few more payoffs. Playing and blinking value creatures is a good time, and this deck can land anywhere from aggressive to controlling, depending on the mix of cards.

## Storm

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Lion%27s+Eye+Diamond)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lion%27s+Eye+Diamond)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Brain+Freeze)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brain+Freeze)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Yawgmoth%27s+Will)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Yawgmoth%27s+Will)

Storm is somewhat of a trap when it comes to win percentage, but it is routinely one of the most fun decks to attempt. There is a lot of support for storm here, though the deck still isn't the best. It wouldn't be a cube I had a hand in without a robust storm deck, so just make sure you have good ways to generate mana before attempting this.

## Graveyard and Sacrifice

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Bloodghast)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodghast)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Braids%2C+Cabal+Minion)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Braids%2C+Cabal+Minion)

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Midnight+Reaper)](https://web.archive.org/web/20210526000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Midnight+Reaper)

Black is one of the toughest colors to get to work in *Vintage Cube*. This selection of black cards offers a bit of a graveyard or midrange sacrifice theme, which can do some powerful stuff. It's still not the best deck, but it gives black more of an identity than "splashable removal."

# A Fine *Vintage*

We will also both be streaming it, and you can stop by on Twitch (same handles) this week, and we will undoubtedly be forcing the decks we love. Best of luck if you hop into the queue, and make sure to try out whatever strikes your fancy.
