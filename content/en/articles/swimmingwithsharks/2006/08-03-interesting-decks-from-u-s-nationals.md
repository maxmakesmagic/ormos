In case you didn't know, last weekend several National Championships took place, including Italy and England (where Star City Games Managing Editor Craig Stevenson took top honors), as well as the battle to see who the top player in a little country called the United States of America is.

The big deck of U.S. Nationals was without a doubt Solar Flare or “Japanimator,” a three-color control deck that has been building steam on ***Magic** Online* in recent weeks. Last week here on Swimming With Sharks we focused on the u/W Weenie decks of French Nationals, but at the same time, Solar Flare was posting multiple copies in the Top 8 Down Under, including a first place finish for the Australian Champion. The deck veritably exploded in popularity with maybe two days notice in the United States, and ultimately took two of the three spots on the U.S. National Team, including our eventual Champion, Paul Cheon.

Check out Cheon's deck if you are not familiar with the archetype. Solar Flare sure has six Signets (the Diamonds); it plays like an attrition-control deck, topping off with any number of expensive late game threats (the Dragons). The deck is powerful, but it is difficult to explain exactly why it is effective given the competition... With only four Remands to its list, Solar Flare is a significantly worse counterspell deck than even ‘Tron. It has four copies of Wrath of God, sure, but besides those three Mortifies, Solar Flare isn't really impressing anyone with its removal suite. As for a reanimator deck, the 2-3 Zombifies don't even make a huge difference in most individual games… But there is something about the combination of all these elements alongside the deceptively essential Persecutes (2) that has set the metagame afire. Solar Flare trades well and can usually play trump with Angel of Despair, Meloku, or one of the actual Dragons going long.

Unlike his new teammates, Ben Lundquist did not take a Japanimator deck to U.S. Nationals. To the surprise of, well, absolutely no one, Ben continued his aggressive advocacy of ‘Tron-Wildfire (a deck he cashed with in Honolulu, and used to make Top 4 of Grand Prix--Madison). While many others have adopted Simic Sky Swallower, Ben went old… err… *middle* school with his ‘Tron build, playing essentially a streamlined U/R version that bluffed the Simic Signets (his previous versions played Dimir Signet) and added Spell Snare… but was more-or-less free of recent set influences.

Congratulations to Paul, Ben, and their third, Luis Scott-Vargas, the 2006 U.S. National Team!

This is my favorite deck of the tournament, and you can probably figure out why without my telling you. Aggro-Ideal is exceptionally powerful: It can function as a straightforward creature / board control deck or set up for a Boseiju-driven Enduring Ideal long game. The deck also has a powerful Gifts Univen toolbox that is generally used to set up Life from the Loam tricks (fighting over Miren, getting Boseiju in play, or picking at UrzaTron with multiple Ghost Quarters), but can just get several good cards to overpower beatdown decks… or three trump cards to force the Sensei's Divining Top. You won't find a deck in this format better suited to beating the once ubiquitous Red beatdown cadre of Zoo, Heezy Street, and Sea Stompy.

I hope you enjoyed this look at some of the interesting and different looks from U.S. Nationals 2006. Hopefully there is a cool idea or two in here that you can run with for *Coldsnap* Standard and beyond!
