I've always had a [soft spot for Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20221004224523/https://magic.wizards.com/en/node/502416), and I have gotten good feedback on [the last](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/enchanting-dream-2013-08-22) few [Pauper lists](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/razing-stakes-2013-07-24) I've put up, so when I saw a ten-Forest Elf deck, I knew I had a winner. This is more of a beatdown Elves list than a combo list, but the [Gitaxian Probe](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gitaxian+Probe)s, [Manamorphose](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Manamorphose)s, and ten lands do indicate that a certain combo element is present. 

![Elvish Visionary](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Elvish+Visionary)

Of course, ten lands is a bit of a stretch, since [Land Grant](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Land+Grant) essentially makes it fourteen, but just getting to submit a decklist with ten land is pretty awesome. Between the ten Forests, four [Land Grant](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Land+Grant)s, four [Gitaxian Probe](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gitaxian+Probe)s, and twelve one-drop Elves that add mana, the deck shouldn't have too much trouble operating off one or even zero lands in the opening hand. 

Playing so few mana sources means a high density of spells, and it isn't uncommon for the deck to have a board of five or more Elves within a few short turns. That in turn makes [Timberwatch Elf](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Timberwatch+Elf) a monster, which is the main engine driving the deck. Once Timberwatch taps to give a creature (and by "creature," I mean "Elf") +5/+5, the game is usually over very rapidly. 

Even if Timberwatch is hiding out on the bottom of the deck, between [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor), [Nettle Sentinel](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nettle+Sentinel), and [Multani's Acolyte](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Multani%27s+Acolyte), the deck can easily present enough power to take down the opponent quite easily. On the defensive end, [Essence Warden](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Essence+Warden) protects your life total and [Spidersilk Armor](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spidersilk+Armor) protects your Elves from sweepers, making the deck solid on both defense and offense. 

I would be concerned about combo decks, and the sideboard doesn't really address them, so some way to disrupt the [Ghostly Flicker](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ghostly+Flicker) decks may be worth investigating. I like the anti-beatdown answers and the additional ways to protect your Elves, as well as [Gleeful Sabotage](https://web.archive.org/web/20130830000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gleeful+Sabotage) against Affinity, so most of the sideboard options look sweet.
