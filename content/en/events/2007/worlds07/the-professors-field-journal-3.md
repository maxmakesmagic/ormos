It's never as free as people assume though, as the next seven contained no land. The actual mulligan hand was fine. There was a mulligan on the Dutch side as well.</p>&#10;      <p>So often in Two-Headed Giant if one player's draw slips up the game is over very quickly. In this case Tijs de Kler failed to find a forest. They made a bunch of 1/1s and I got to brutalize them with <a>Battlewand Oak</a>, <a>Battlewand Oak</a> and.... <a>Battlewand Oak</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>You know that scene in the second <i>Lord of the Rings</i> film...</p>&#10;      <p>1-0</p>&#10;      <p>Unfortunately Dan and Marco lost against the Netherlands Team 2, making the overall match result a tie.</p>&#10;      <p>Great Britain 1, the Netherlands 1</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, December 8: 1:01 p.m. – Round 2 vs. the Russian Federation 1</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Ah, as always with the broken deck from hell, it always misfires on you at some point.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        </p><div>&#10;          <div>&#10;            <a>&#10;              <img alt="Hostility" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Hostility&amp;options=">&#10;            </a>&#10;          </div>&#10;        </div>We got off to a flier this game. This time I got to play the reactive role with a hand of <a>Shriekmaw</a>, <a>Nameless Inversion</a> and land. One of the Russian players had a blue-white Merfolk deck but was struggling to find land while the other had a five colour special from what I could see.<p></p>&#10;      <p>Stuart opened with <a>Blind-Spot Giant</a> and then got it online with a <a>Stinkdrinker Daredevil</a> and <a>Avian Changeling</a>. I followed up with a <a>Shriekmaw</a> and Stuart doubled it for a turn with <a>Heat Shimmer</a>. At this point we looked totally in control.</p>&#10;      <p>Unfortunately we then both hit a glut of land while the Russians stabilised with <a>Thorntooth Witch</a> followed by <a>Timber Protector</a> and a ton of card advantage from <a>Mulldrifter</a>s.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Hostility</a> gave us hope of pushing through the last points as we alpha-striked to try and maintain pressure. <a>Hostility</a> fell to <a>Neck Snap</a> and our board position basically crumbled. I continued to draw land and ended the game having drawn five spells to eleven land.</p>&#10;      <p>0-1</p>&#10;      <p>Fortunately GB 2 pulled through and we again split the match 1-1.</p>&#10;      <p>Great Britain 1, Russian Federation 1</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, December 8: 4:05 p.m. – 2HG Round 3 vs. Canada</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>And it's all change as my team mate is now my semifinal victim from the British Nats, Marco Orsini-Jones.</p>&#10;      <p>Two-Headed Giant being what it is means no time to cover our second draft in detail. In summary, Marco OJ has a much better Giants deck than Stuart from last round as he's got <a>Sunrise Sovereign</a> and <a>Thundercloud Shaman</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>This is fortunate as I have a green-black mess splashing blue for <a>Æthersnipe</a> and <a>Mulldrifter</a>. I do have two <a>Dread</a> though.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds07/blog_whisper.jpg">Against Andrew Ting-A-Kee and Mike Arenson we started promisingly, mainly because Andrew fails to make a third land. We're going to let them overextend and Marco's going to wreck their team with <a>Thundercloud Shaman</a>. Meanwhile I'm going to walk up to six mana and make <a>Dread</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>This would require Marco to draw a second <a>Mountain</a> for the <a>Axegrinder Giant</a> and <a>Thundercloud Shaman</a> in hand though.</p>&#10;      <p>I don't really want to make <a>Dread</a> into Arenson's untapped blue mana but a clash reveals the second <a>Dread</a> on top. Obviously if he has countermagic he's going to sit with it open next turn as well, so I send out the first <a>Dread</a> as a tester. Sure enough, he has <a>Faerie Trickery</a>. Even more unfortunately, Ting-A-Kee finds a white source and <a>Oblivion Ring</a>s the second <a>Dread</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>It's fine, sooner or later Marco is going to find a second <a>Mountain</a> and wreck them with <a>Thundercloud Shaman</a>. I mean he's playing ten of them.</p>&#10;      <p>Any draw step now...</p>&#10;      <p>Unload three removal spells, swing with a horde of Elves, cast double <a>Surge of Thoughtweft</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Sigh.</p>&#10;      <p>Gotta love the 2HG format.</p>&#10;      <p>Stuart and Dan overcome a scary team including Rich Hoaen and we end up 1-1 again.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="5"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, December 8: 5:00 p.m. – 2HG Round 4 vs. South Korea</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Marco takes the free mulligan and then takes the real mulligan. Fortunately I'm able to help him out with a <a>Fertile Ground</a> on his land.</p>&#10;      <p>We start off well as Marco starts beating with a <a>Sunrise Sovereign</a>. I get stuck with a <a>Changeling Titan</a> in hand as I can't risk casting it with my single and very vulnerable <a>Woodland Changeling</a> in play. I have <a>Dread</a> though. They kill it with a clever trick in using <a>Wings of Velis Vel</a> to give it all creature types. This sadly includes Treefolk, and <a>Boggart Loggers</a> just love sinking their axes into Treefolk.</p>&#10;      <p>We're still looking good, but then I draw a bunch of land, Marco draws a bunch of land and they come back into the game and beat us.</p>&#10;      <p>Once again I draw more land than spells, and I'm including <a>Fertile Ground</a> amongst the spells.</p>&#10;      <p>Stuart and Dan manage to win, which give us our fourth 1-1. Unfortunately, the rather strange scoring system means that this record is actually worse than if we'd got our 4-4 by going 2-0, 2-0, 0-2, 0-2. Effectively we are actually 1 &amp; 1/3 from 4 rather than 2 from 4.</p>&#10;      <p>No, I don't understand it either.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="6"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, December 8: 7:57 p.m. – Round 5 vs. New Zealand</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>And the musical chairs continue. Now I get to play with Dan Godfrey.</p>&#10;      <p>The draft was a little mixed. At first I wanted to go with the red-white Giants plan again with black-green Treefolk. But then we actually got blue cards for a change. This turned our Giants deck into a blue-red Elemental deck with a slight Giant sub-theme. (The one that comes into play and deals damage to everything that isn't a Giant.)</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        </p><div>&#10;          <div>&#10;            <a>&#10;              <img alt="Profane Command" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Profane%20Command&amp;options=">&#10;            </a>&#10;          </div>&#10;        </div>I kind of picked up some Elves as the Treefolk were a little sparse. My deck isn't that bad. Two <a>Profane Command</a> will do that for you I guess.<p></p>&#10;      <p>This one went long and messy. I made some fatties including Doran, as did Dan, but we couldn't really make much headway against their tappers. Dan got out first one Giant (Hearthcage) and then a second (Axegrinder).
