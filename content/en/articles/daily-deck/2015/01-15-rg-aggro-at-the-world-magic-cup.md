I would like to introduce another deck from the [World *Magic* Cup](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/2014-world-magic-cup).

Today's deck is the RG Aggro piloted by Thiago Saporito from Team Brazil.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Savage+Punch)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Punch)

I recall discussing red-green as variation to the green decks, with a relatively slow game plan. However, Thiago's red-green is nothing like this—100% aggression; no heed to tempo loss because you are always trying to be fastest.

This build has no intention of defending. You could compare it to [Brian Kibler's Temur](/en/articles/daily-deck/2014/10-31-temur-aggro-at-grand-prix-la) without the blue—to minimize mana problems and early board development. The whole idea is to push further and further forward.

Personally, I was impressed the deck did not contain [Goblin Rabblemaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20150115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Rabblemaster). This was not because of the restriction rule of four cards per team. Team Brazil chose not to use a single Rabblemaster.

As I touched on in [my article on Sam's deck](/en/articles/daily-deck/2015/01-13-rw-beatdown-at-the-world-championship), the opportunity to utilize Rabblemaster is decreasing. It seems the trend now is to remove it altogether. I agree with this movement.

However, [Savage Punch](https://web.archive.org/web/20150115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Punch)—or "bear punch"—is too much. Simply going for [Lightning Strike](https://web.archive.org/web/20150115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Strike) would have been much better.
