Representing Brazil was Pedro Motta, scoring the maximum amount of wins with his grandeur-iffic mono-red deck. Opposite Pedro was David Irvine, hailing from Miami, Florida, hoping to topple the giant and score a win with his RGW aggro deck, a popular choice on the weekend.</p>&#10;      <p>Game 1: David started off with a two drop, the growing <a>Kavu Predator</a> off a <a>Forest</a> and <a>Plains</a>. Pedro was already in a bind. His mono-red deck, he explained, had issues with 7/7 trampling creatures on the third turn. A source of red mana and <a>Fiery Justice</a> from Irvine would accomplish exactly that. But did Pedro want to slow down his development on the chance his opponent had <i>both</i> of these? Perhaps he was short on removal or didn't want to live in fear, but Pedro pushed forward with his suspended Gargadon and a morph. David looked pleased but kept it to himself, as he dropped a <a>Grove of the Burnwillows</a> and used the red there to cast his <a>Fiery Justice</a>, making an <i>8/8</i> trampler. Pedro, stoic, nodded his head and adjusted the life totals. Motta had some degree of chance to come back with <a>Greater Gargadon</a>, but it wasn't to be. David followed up with <a>Serra Avenger</a> and a <a>Riftsweeper</a> to shuffle in the Gargadon, and Motta quickly conceded.</p>&#10;      <p>Motta: 0<br> Irvine: 1</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpsf07/World.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Tarmogoyf holds off the world</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>David: &quot;That was…a perfect draw&quot;<br> Pedro: &quot;It happens.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Game 2: Fortunes reverse, and this time it was Pedro who retained dominance. His opponent took a double mulligan, perhaps over-zealous in his goal of getting another perfect hand. Motta kicked off with <a>Keldon Megaliths</a> into morph, morph, morph. David's draw was color heavy and land light, so much so he elected not to sac his Expanse on turn one, forgoing a turn-two drop just so he could draw more land and make a more informed decision. David did find the info he was looking for and fixed up all three colors. He spent his fourth turn casting his first creature, a <a>Serra Avenger</a>, which immediately took <a>Rift Bolt</a>. Again came the morphs. Irvine finally slowed things down with <a>Calciderm</a>, although the 2/2s and his own pain lands made things precarious. Unconcerned, Pedro crashed in. When the dust settled, David had taken 5 from an unblocked unmorphed <a>Gathan Raiders</a>, one <a>Zoetic Cavern</a> was dead from Dead, and the other had turned into a land via combat damage. Irvine started to turn it around with <a>Temporal Isolation</a> on the Raiders, but by this time Megaliths was active. But Future Sight brought in <a>Tarmogoyf</a>, a card that made many mono-white decks G/W (although there is a mono in the top 64), and some say made pure red decks unplayable. Clearly Pedro Motta would have disagreements on the viability of an all-red deck, but it is true the Goyf can be tough. In this final game, things began well for the Brazillian with Dead//Gone on a <a>Riftsweeper</a> and a <a>Keldon Marauders</a> ready to brawl. Yet the lost creature, the split card, and a <a>Terramorphic Expanse</a> gave Irvine a 3/4 <a>Tarmogoyf</a> on the third turn, happy to step in the way of any number of 3/3s. Pedro seemed a bit frustrated, playing a morph and passing back with Keldon Maurauders on defense. Note to readers: <a>Keldon Marauders</a> playing D is not a sustainable strategy.</p>&#10;      <p>David upped the scales with Calicderm and another <a>Riftsweeper</a>. Once the Raiders took a <a>Temporal Isolation</a>, Pedro was drawing dead and had to extend the hand.
