<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, Aug. 19: 11:07 a.m. - A Short History Lesson</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/aug6.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>A watch frozen by the blast</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Hiroshima is known around the world for being the first city subjected to an Atomic Bomb. At the end of World War II, the United States dropped an A-bomb on Hiroshima, and later at Nagasaki, Japan. These bombs were used to seal a Japanese surrender to the Allied forces. The plan was successful, although many people today debate the A-bomb's necessity to the war. Indeed, their existence at all in many country's arsenals is a hotly debated topic. What is the origin of atomic weaponry, and why was Hiroshima the first target?</p>&#10;      <p>The United States began to study the effects of atomic weapons immediately after entering World War II in 1939. That research became the Manhattan Project in 1942, which at its heyday involved over 130,000 people and cost nearly two billion dollars (twenty billion in modern dollars). The first test of the Atomic Bomb was known as the Trinity Test and its success gave the military the go-ahead for the A-Bomb's use against a populated area. Modern political scholars say the bomb was used, in part, to justify the immense cost of its development.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/dome.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>The A-Bomb Dome</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The choice of Hiroshima as the target was fiercely debated among the United States' military leaders. Hiroshima was on this list because of its many army barracks, as well as high amounts of factories used to create arms for the Japanese for World War II. Other potential targets included Kokura, Niigata, and Kyoto. Hiroshima was chosen because it had no allied prisoner of war camps, as well as a unique &quot;T&quot; shaped bridge that made targeting more accurate.</p>&#10;      <p>On August 6 1945 8:15AM, the A-bomb known as &quot;Little Boy&quot; was dropped over Hiroshima. Its detonation occurred about 600 meters above the city, resulting in a blast and shock wave that flattened almost all of the buildings within 2 kilometers of the hypocenter. The exact death toll is difficult to determine, however most estimates put the loss of life between 90,000 and 140,000. Besides the initial concussive force and heat that claimed life, residual radiation caused many deaths of survivors and later the medical and restoration personal. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/cenotaph.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>A cenotaph to the victims of the atomic bomb</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The structure that best withstood the blast is a building is called the Atomic Bomb Dome, kept in its original condition to show visitors the effect of the bomb. The area surrounding the dome is now known as Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park, and contains many shrines to the lives lost, as well as a Memorial Peace Museum. In modern times, Hiroshima is considered a city of peace. Its government and people repeatedly hold demonstrations and sends letters to countries around the world urging disarmament and global unity. </p>&#10;      <p>Information courtesy: Hiroshima Memorial Peace Museum</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, Aug. 19: 1:13 p.m. - Two Masters Build Sealed Decks</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Grand Prix: Hiroshima is not short on pros to observe. For the sealed portion I decided to go with Takuya Oosawa and Julien Nuijten. Takuya won Pro tour: Prague this year, which as you may remember, was all RGD. 17-year old Julien has had success this year as well, but is best known for his status as the 2004 Magic world champion. Interestingly, while both of these players are total masters of high-level competition, their construction styles were totally different.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Takuya Oosawa</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/cc/9f/cc9f4fccc9efe49a179fcd133fce9df88bf1bf340d2235df27a5ba33da1220de.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Two MVPs?</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Takuya started off his building in a very controlled, precise manner. Each card was carefully put into its respective color pile, with nothing being eliminated off the bat. After being able to examine every card as a piece of the whole, Takuya confidently removed Blue. The best card out of Blue was <a>Glimpse the Unthinkable</a>, and the only card that comboed with was <a>Vulturous Zombie</a>. That Plant Zombie certainly doesn't need any help. Takuya did seem a bit annoyed that his only two Signets provided Blue mana. </p>&#10;      <p>Oosawa's cards were extremely synergistic as Red/White or Green/Black combinations. Red and White had speed and efficiency, while Golgari represented the highest quantity of gold cards. Takuya compared piles for a little bit of time until he realized just how much power and combinations existed in his Green/Black cards. <a>Savra, Queen of the Golgari</a> looked pretty nice with <a>Shambling Shell</a> and <a>Entropic Eidolon</a> for example. Julien could only offer his concession when Takuya laughingly showed his building partner those cards. <a>Seize the Soul</a> didn't look bad either.<br><br> Takuya's final decision was whether to add a few Red or White cards to his mostly G/B ones. This process took about half of the remaining ten minutes, to Takuya's consternation. He eventually decided on the Red, due mostly to one stray Signet that enabled. While his White cards could be considered a bit stronger, Takuya knew his deck was good enough to not need extra help. Mana concerns were his priority at this point. Making sure everything was castable, plus his three byes, was the plan to make day two. With bomb rares, synergy, and two on-color bounce lands, Takuya had a lot of reasons to be very happy with his deck.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Julien Nuijten</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Julien's technique was quite a contrast from his neighbor. His style could only be described as frenetic as he cut as many cards as possible on his first pass. Clearly Julien considered sealed deck an exercise in squeezing every inch of power out of a deck. At the end of his eliminations, he had piles of his strongest cards representing every color.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/54/fa5431049fa17a28c3e024a4e4005676e93e8e71dc0f3666fd47ba0c920d2afb.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Julien tries another permutation</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Right away Julien determined Green was going to be a starter, due to creatures like <a>Ursapine</a> and <a>Gruul Guildmage</a>, and very necessary fixing. Black was next to be labeled a staple, for its creature base and very solid removal options. Interestingly, Julien labeled his deck a success even before he was finished, due to the <a>Godless Shrine</a> that &quot;paid for the trip&quot;.</p>&#10;      <p>Despite the presence of a dual land, a bounce land, and two Signets, the mana situation was clearly troubling Mr. Nuijten. Julien would speak to himself about mana curve and mana ratios, inclusions and exclusions, alternating between English and Dutch. Piles would be haphazardly created and destroyed, while Julien tried to find the combination that met his approval. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I don't know if I can play every color. I probably can.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>Time started winding down for Julien, forcing him into heavy decision mode. The White splash of <a>Lightning Helix</a> and <a>Pillory of the Sleepless</a> hit the bench due of mana concerns, a decision that seemed to truly pain the competitor. He resolved the choice by informing me that with his now four color deck, he could get away with 15 lands. Flooding had been another concern for Julien since construction began, and this option eased his mind considerably. With only a few minutes remaining, Julien went with this last model and started scratching out a registered deck. Literally seconds before the deck construction clock hit zero, Julien Nuijten had finished his deck. After his massage, cigars, and catnap, when Julien's three byes run out he'll definitely be ready for action.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, Aug. 19: 2:48 p.m. - You Call That a Knife?</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>One of the absolute joys of traveling around the globe is being able to experience the local culture. When you're among the traveling elite, matchups like this will happen. No one was complaining of course; these guys are professionals. One simply got the sense these two wished they were playing each other a little further down the road.</p>&#10;      <p>Julien's deck construction had been covered previously in this blog. Now it was time to see how his deck fared in the field. Opposing him was Austria's Helmut Summersberger, a player featuring one Pro Tour top 8, three Grand Prix top 8, and two Grand Prix wins. Clearly these players' respect for each other was justified.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/summersburger.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Helmut Summersberger</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Game 1: Helmut started off in a bad way, with two mulligans going first. His five card hand was a <a>Watery Grave</a>-all Green cards special. It did contain <a>Moldervine Cloak</a> however, one card that's well known for stealing wins. Julien kept his initial seven and used his early turns to fix his mana through <a>Simic Signet</a>, <a>Civic Wayfinder</a>, and <a>Terrarion</a>. After missing a land drop, Helmut scraped his way to three mana, but still had no play. Julien continued to fix things with a <a>Gruul Turf</a>, finally playing out a double spelled turn with <a>Torch Drake</a>, <a>Seal of Fire</a> and then <a>Leafdrake Roost</a>. Helmut could only chuckle at his dwindling position but came back well enough with <a>Greater Mossdog</a> and <a>Golgari Rotwurm</a>. Drakes increased and continued to bring Helmut lower in life. Needing to go on the offensive, Helmut Cloaked up <a>Greater Mossdog</a> and swung with his team, hoping for a double block from Julien to garner a little breathing room. The former world champion wasn't having any of that and took his lumps like a man. His return strike brought Helmut down to eight life, and <a>Galvanic Arc</a> + <a>Seal of Fire</a> + <a>Recollect</a> + <a>Seal of Fire</a> earned Julien game 1.</p>&#10;      <p>During sideboard time, Julien pulled out and consider the idea of bringing in his White cards. Presumably, he wanted an answer to <a>Moldervine Cloak</a>, and <a>Pillory of the Sleepless</a> fit the bill nicely. The in/out columns were studied for a couple of minutes, with Julien ultimately deciding to incorporate color number five. Despite the rapid and decisive beating of the first game, Helmut remained confident with his original 40. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/Brambles.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>A strong board position.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Game 2: Helmut was again on the play and kept his initial hand, as did Julien. Things started off much better for the Austrian as his <a>Dryad Sophisticate</a> came down on the second turn. The tide turned very swiftly when Julien flashed a Peel From Reality, removing both Helmut's Pillory and his <a>Siege Wurm</a> from play. That sharp tempo boost gave Julien a lot of time, and he used it to play <a>Bramble Elemental</a> and <a>Strands of Undeath</a>. The Strands was absolutely devastating, giving Julien two tokens to Helmut's loss of two cards. Helmut lost everything but his <a>Guardian of Vitu-Ghazi</a> and an uncastable <a>Borborygmos</a>. Helmut did his best with a topdecked <a>Sporeback Troll</a> leading to a 5/8 Guardian, but the game was definitely slipping away. Julien continued to develop his board and make attacks with <a>Torch Drake</a>. A clutch <a>Stinkweed Imp</a> gave Helmut a little hope, until Julien's <a>Civic Wayfinder</a> found a <a>Plains</a>, allowing Julien's own Pillory. Shota's undefeated status shows he too is a contender for the champion title.
