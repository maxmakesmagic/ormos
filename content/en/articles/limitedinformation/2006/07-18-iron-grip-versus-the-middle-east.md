### Pack 1 (*Ravnica*)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3c/84/3c8469f81756b3089326f188942b3ca900085a3e816e7cfceb6df474c31528e7.jpg)**Pack 1, Pick 1**: Goblin Spelunkers, Farseek, Woodwraith Strangler, Nightguard Patrol, Muddle the Mixture, Goblin Fire Fiend, Sundering Vitae, Leave No Trace, Snapping Drake, Golgari Signet, Viashino Fangtail, Spectral Searchlight, Sunhome Enforcer, Grifter's Blade, Vinelasher Kudzu

My pick:

Things began on a complicated note, with multiple cards at very comparable power levels. After excluding the weakest cards, the choices were reduced to five:

*   Snapping Drake
*   Viashino Fangtail
*   Spectral Searchlight
*   Sunhome Enforcer
*   Vinelasher Kudzu

While I have an awful lot of respect for Sunhome Enforcer, this was not the pack to take it. First of all, it's a two-color card. I like to hold off on making archetype declarations so soon. Even though either half could be splashed in some four-color build, Sunhome Enforcer does require plenty of Red mana to be truly vicious. If we are going dedicated Red, then I'd say the Fangtail is the better card of the two, although it is close. The clincher would be Fangtail needing one less color, and its ability being more relevant in *Ravnica* block. That's not to say Fangtail is the right pick, yet.

Spectral Searchlight would be a very interesting first pick, opening up any and all combinations a drafter could fancy. This early in the draft, it's a very safe pick with some degree of potential. It's a safe pick because it clearly goes into every single final deck. As potential, it can provide an access point to the most powerful cards imaginable, allowing the drafter a lot of flexibility in picks down the road. With all that said though, it's just too weak as a first pick from this particular pack. The Searchlight is a great enabler, but by taking the enabler we're passing cards we'd actually want to be able to cast. It's hardly effective to have all the means to cast cards with nothing to actually play. In addition Searchlight, while a moderate damage source, is really more of an early-to-middle game card. For my first picks, I like to take cards that have impact at all stages of the game. While Civic Wayfinder is best early, a 2/2 and a card draw, even in the late game, makes it a quality first pick. Searchlight just doesn't offer this.

This is also the reason I discarded Vinelasher Kudzu. It's phenomenal early, but gets decidedly weaker as the game goes on. It's also enhanced by plentiful bounce lands, but as yet, we don't know if those are available. Again, it's a fine card, but not quite the best first pick out of this pack.

For me, this pick came down to either Snapping Drake or Viashino Fangtail. In *Ravnica* block, Blue and Red are my two favorite colors. Blue is generally stronger than Red, but like the classic Reese's analogy, they're killer together. Of these two cards, Fangtail is the more powerful card, which makes sense considering it requires more of a color commitment. The decision whether to play it safe or gamble a bit is a classic draft conundrum. I believe, in this block at least, a little risk is a good thing. I wouldn't look down on anyone who took Snapping Drake here, but for me, I like to start things off with power.

**My pick**: Viashino Fangtail

* * *

**Pack 1, Pick 2**: Dogpile, Civic Wayfinder, Votary of the Conclave, Tidewater Minion, Sadistic Augermage, Scatter the Seeds, Shred Memory, Island, Golgari Brownscale, Compulsive Research, Svogthos, the Restless Tomb, Chant of Vitu-Ghazi, Duskmantle, House of Shadow, Autochthon Wurm

My pick:

This one was pretty easy. Civic Wayfinder is a very fine card, but it's no Compulsive Research. I look forward to drafting Izzet whenever possible; this seemed like a good time to start. The presence of another quality Green card in the pack only cemented the decision.

**My pick**: Compulsive Research

* * *

**Pack 1, Pick 3**: Terrarion, Votary of the Conclave, Drift of Phantasms, Incite Hysteria, Scatter the Seeds, Rally the Righteous, Forest, Grayscaled Gharial, Elves of Deep Shadow, Conclave Equenaut, Carven Caryatid, Carrion Howler, Bloodletter Quill

My pick:

Now this pack had some interesting cards, and I note, upon reviewing this draft, that I have may have made an error. Definitely maybe, as it were. I'll just say right now I took the Drift of Phantasms, a card that fits very nicely with control/card advantage decks. Stay alive and ping, stay alive and draw cards; these are all positive formulae. It's just that Scatter the Seeds should have been a stronger consideration. I had, so far, been passed Civic Wayfinder and two Scatters. Is it possible the player on my right is not Green, and is it further possible that could lead into a Simic situation? That's quite possible, and if it's true, that's a good place to be. Izzet is good, IzzeGruuSim is even better. Some believe R/G/U to be the strongest archetype in *Ravnica* block. I don't know if that's true, but it's certainly a contender. 

With the potential to create a strong R/G/U deck - and I'd prefer that over Conclave Equenaut's R/U/W - then I'd say Scatter is the pick. Drift is the safe pick, while Scatter could end up being a complete blank. That's the risk, but I think at this point, it was worth taking. What ended up happening was actually rather ironic, but we'll get to that in a bit. 

**My pick**: Drift of Phantasms

* * *

**Pack 1, Pick 4**: Gaze of the Gorgon, Coalhauler Swine, Benevolent Ancestor, Surveilling Sprite, Necromantic Thirst, Perplex, Dryad's Caress, Brainspoil, Mark of Eviction, Drooling Groodion, Cleansing Beam, Searing Meditation

My pick:

I required a moment's pause, lest mine eyes deceived me, but really, that was a fourth pick Mark of Eviction. People on the backend know the havoc this innocuous-seeming card could create. Briefly, it's cheap as all get out, and continues to fit with the control/card advantage theme we're creating. The fact that it goes oh-so-nice on Izzet's “coming into play” creatures and Simic's grafty dudes is what we call sweet gravy.

**My pick**: Mark of Eviction

* * *

**Pack 1, Pick 5**: Wojek Siren, Convolute, Clinging Darkness, Bramble Elemental, Ordruun Commando, Caregiver, Drake Familiar, Thoughtpicker Witch, Auratouched Mage, Lore Broker, Hunted Dragon

My pick:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2b/f6/2bf6504013d8004b8dd1faf582806cf688d77b1b01098dc3a6c6f297e781a382.jpg)

On color, the only choices are Hunted Dragon and Lore Broker. Lore Broker is pretty weak, fitting well only in a decking strategy, which this deck is certainly not. Hunted Dragon's not bad *per se*, but its role is clearly that of a finisher. This draft is, at the moment, a control deck. That means we want an iron grip on the proceedings. The actual victory condition is almost incidental, be it Wee Dragonauts, Torch Drake, or Rakdos Ickspitters. Hunted Dragon does not let us maintain a firm hold on the game. Rather, it's like pouring hot butter all over the battlefield. A Hunted Dragon causes plenty of movement. 

For the purposes of this pack, it's time to look at a third color. The Brainspoil and Groodion from the previous pack could indicate Black is open, and look, a Clinging Darkness to start down that path. As a cheap defensive card, Clinging Darkness even fits the themes. I still don't like it though; it's a weak card, especially with our other options. Absolver Thrull, their Graft creatures or Azorius First Wing all weaken Cling even further. It's the card to pick if we're dedicated to drafting R/B/U, but at this point, I can't imagine why we'd want to.

This pack has the better options of either Bramble Elemental or Auratouched Mage. Again, this could come down to personal preference; certainly our Mark now and potential Ocular Halo later make both cards good. Auratouched Mage is slightly easier on the mana, and Bramble Elemental is (at this point) more consistently powerful. My decider was that Green seems to be flowing right now, and White is all but dry. Regardless of archetype preferences, we should at least go with the colors that are actually being passed.

**My pick**: Bramble Elemental

* * *

**Pack 1, Pick 6**: Viashino Slasher, Golgari Rot Farm, Fists of Ironwood, War-Torch Goblin, Surge of Zeal, Gate Hound, Boros Signet, Recollect, Wojek Apothecary, Golgari Germination

My pick:

That's a very late Golgari Rot Farm, once again showing Green and Black are under-drafted. While the Rot Farm would be very likely to make the final build, regardless of what we choose for our third color, I don't believe it's the pick here. Instead, since we just finished picking up a Mark of Eviction and Bramble Elemental, Fists of Ironwood is the way to go. It works well with what we have so far and plays very nicely with Graft creatures down the road. 

**My pick**: Fists of Ironwood

* * *

**Pack 1, Pick 7**: Terraformer, Gaze of the Gorgon, War-Torch Goblin, Boros Fury-Shield, Gate Hound, Lurking Informant, Sewerdreg, Mountain, Selesnya Sagittars

My pick:

Which Blue mana fixer will it be? Terraformer is better early as a Gray Ogre with a cheap activation cost. Lurking Informant is better late, where its activation cost won't be so damaging to development. Between Compulsive Research and Bramble Elemental, our deck is looking well suited to the later game. Thus, I went with the Wizard who plays better early. Since we're packing heavy Blue, double-Green, and double-Red, Terraformer's ability could definitely be relevant. 

**My pick**: Terraformer

**Pack 1, Pick 8**: Barbarian Riftcutter, Screeching Griffin, Vedalken Entrancer, Sewerdreg, Smash, Flight of Fancy, Sell-Sword Brute, Leashling

This pack had a number of choices, which is surprising for this late in the draft. Although we only had a single Red card up to this point, Red still seems like the best color to pair with Green and Blue. Consider that while we haven't been passed much Red, the person to our left has received even less. *Guildpact* is the best set for getting Red cards and it's being passed from the player who has essentially received nothing. Pairing the G/U with either White or Black makes no sense from a guild model. While it's possible the deck could end up four colors (Terraformer woot!), G/U/R should definitely be the main focus.

So I consider the Red cards in the pack still in contention. 

Of those, Riftcutter can be removed from consideration. It's not unplayable, but in *Guildpact* alone, Izzet Chronarch, Ghor-Clan Savage, and Streetbreaker Wurm all look much better in the five spot. Sell-Sword Brute is a little more intriguing, less as a damage source, but rather as a way to slow their early rush. Due to far different *Dissension* evaluations from the rest of the table, this ends up being the wrong pick. However, even disregarding what actually happens down the road, I think Brute is the slightly better grab. It gives my mana a little breathing room in regards to Viashino Fangtail, and it could boost up some Bloodthirst down the road. Alas, dreams of the perfect aura deck distracted. This time my pick was FoF.

### Pack 3 (*Dissension*)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/23/7423757fd32cbec88f77267ec7830acacace03a4400b08b88ecb425279809431.jpg)**Pack 3, Pick 1**: Carom, Rakdos Signet, Rakdos Ickspitter, Ogre Gatecrasher, Ocular Halo, Gobhobbler Rats, Delirium Skeins, Sporeback Troll, Valor Made Real, Enigma Eidolon, Shielding Plax, Patagia Viper, Jagged Poppet, Squealing Devil, Cytoplast Root-Kin

My pick:

This pick was by far the most difficult and time-consuming of the draft, although clearly with some of them I should have taken longer. This one went to the flashing red screen, but I simply could not decide between Cytoplast Root-Kin and Patagia Viper. Both are four-power worth of creature with a bonus. The Root-Kin has a little more potential to do obscene things, but the Viper is certainly no slouch. 

Which to pick: ground demon or flyer+? Up to this point I hadn't played with Cytoplast Root-Kin, although the one time it was played against me was absolutely devastating. This guy had out Vigean Hydropon and a bunch of Bloodthirsted men. Cytoplast Root-Kin was a very big nail in the coffin. I had also watched the card do some amazing things at Pro Tour: Charleston. Those experiences colored my view, and ultimately led to the wrong pick. 

First of all, watching a card beat the crap out of somebody is valid, but it can be misleading. Any card has the potential to be a superstar, but that certainly doesn't mean every card is consistently good. Second, seeing a card work in Constructed makes absolutely no sense for draft. Have you watched Early Harvest do good things in *9th Edition* Limited lately? My limited experience with the card was a little more relevant, but even then, it doesn't correlate. My opponent already had Graft creatures and a bunch of Bloodthirst. Graft was still possible, but my Bloodthirst count was a big zero. *Guildpact* is done with; there's no more chance of any Gruul cards. Really, Patagia Viper is the pick here for two reasons. One is that it's far easier on the mana. While Bramble Elemental is going to be played, double Green is definitely going to be a pain. Second, Patagia Viper fits the deck better. The flying is nice, and more tokens work well with the pingers and Dogpile. Adding this up, means Patagia Viper is the right pick. No shame in a 4/4 for four of course, but the Patagia Viper just fits this deck better. Ah, hindsight. 

**My erroneous pick**: Cytoplast Root-Kin

### Round 1 “Ostracize” Limited Rating: 1814. Real name: (see below)

If the readers would oblige me, I'd like to take this time to relate a story that took place a number of years ago. I promise this does relate.

In 1999, two other Minnesota players and I qualified for and attended Pro Tour--London (Urza's Block booster draft). Of the three of us, only Ken Bearl managed to make Day 2. Now while Ken is probably worth his own column on **magicthegathering.com**, he's not the subject of this particular story. If you'd like to learn more about “The Bearl”, [you can click here](/en/events/2005/gpmin05/day-1-blog-archive#7). The information is completely accurate, although I must say that picture makes him look awfully crotchety.

Anyway, Ken was the sole competitor on Day 2. The other Minnesota player that weekend was a man named Jason Webster, a good friend and a fine player. We were cheering on Ken for a while, but after he cast Steam Bath\[sic\] during his opponent's attack step, at the Pro Tour, we decided to go off and fend for ourselves. Luckily for us, there was a Team Sealed event just about to start. Unfortunately we were short one player. We wanted to play as “two men and a bye”, but the tournament official told us, laughingly, that if we were to make the final rounds' draft, we'd certainly need a third person to play with. 

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/WORLDS2000/images/373.jpg"><br><i>Uri Peleg, Baruchi Kimchi and Ayal Hassidim from 2000 World Championship</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

Time was running out on registration; we needed a third ASAP. Looking around the room for someone, anyone really, we saw a very young man looking particularly waif-ish and woebegone. We introduced ourselves and told him of our plight. He told us, in excellent English, that his name was Uri, he was from Israel, and he'd love to join us, although we'd probably need to guide him on deck construction.

So was born the blinding presence of Team Uri. And wouldn't you know it, we opened strong cards and played well together. Uri actually forgot to show up for one of the rounds, earning a sarcastic תודה (“todah”) from us, despite all that, we ended up winning the tournament anyway. We all warmly shook hands and went our separate ways. Jason Webster retired from professional **Magic**, started a family, and bought a store, Dreamers Games. I of course continued to play, and Uri Peleg, perhaps inspired by our victory team victory, went on to win Israeli Nationals. Three times. 

So back in the present, here I am playing Ostracize, A.K.A. Uri Peleg. This is quite an extraordinary set of circumstances. First of all, I'm playing a gentleman from Israel, thousands and thousands of miles away. Perhaps our modern, globalized, internet culture makes that passé, but I'm still impressed. Further, and this is a rarity I assure you, I know this Israeli! There might be three people in Israel whom I could say I've spoken with, and here's one again. And wait, we're interacting over **Magic**, a passion we continue to share, regardless of language or hemisphere. Finally, Uri was a guy I met and played with over *7 years ago*, and he's still playing **Magic**! So am I! This is some game.

**Game 1**: Uri was playing a five color special, with a lot of signets and bounce lands. My little Vedalken Plotter ended up being the MVP this game, stealing a crucial bounce land off Uri's diverse assortment. His draw was awfully slow as well; Utvara Scalper did at least 6 points. The big move from this end was playing Ocular Halo on Bramble Elemental. Not only did that put a couple of tokens into play, but thanks to the Azorius Chancery I stole, I could also give it vigilance! Swift beats commenced.

In the second game, his deck came out far more efficiently. I was stuck on four mana for far too long when I desperately needed five. His Spectral Searchlight, which must have been a heady fifth pick for him, was hurting me and helping Uri perfectly. While I was trying to hold on as best as possible, Uri played Coiling Oracle into Auratouched Mage. The Mage fetched out Moldervine Cloak and things started to look quite dire. An Ogre Savant from Uri sealed up the game.

At this point, I was feeling the pinch of missing out on a Sundering Vitae or Stomp and Howl. These are the exact kinds of decks I like to have a Disenchant-type effect waiting in the sideboard for, but in this draft, that option didn't materialize. The third game had both of us drawing all our colors, which if you're wondering, favors a five color deck over a three. Uri again played the Auratouched Mage. Forced into an unpleasant position, I cast Cackling Flames on the Mage in response to the trigger. That meant that the Cloak would go to his hand instead of in play, barely an answer, but it was all I had available. It did let me attack with some guys, but the real hope was that Uri was simply out of creatures to play. That plan was quickly dashed when Uri played Coiling Oracle into a Dimir House Guard. If you'd like to, take a moment to review in the decklist which cards of mine handle a 5/6 Regenerating Fear creature. That didn't take very long. A last ditch Stormscale Anarch was my last chance, and that chance involved some *very* lucky discards. Alas, Uri had the Seal of Fire and it wrapped up the game and the match. We wished each other good luck in future events and went our separate ways.

While in no way could I be accused of enjoying a loss, this one was merely bittersweet. My tournament was over, but I learned some useful lessons and got to reconnect with an old acquaintance. That's quite a range of experiences over a little online draft.
