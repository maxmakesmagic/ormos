311 people descended on the City of Brotherly Love this weekend, all vying for a piece of the $200,000 prize pool available at Pro Tour Philly 2005. After six rounds of Swiss play, all but 105 players have fallen prey to the experimental triple elimination format, though under the skin style prize system, all but forty players secured at least some payout. Today going into Day 2A, familiar Pro Tour site greets us as the German juggernaut Kai Budde sits atop the standings at six and O thanks to a rogue black white deck played by only two other people in the field, joining Kai at six. Oh, or three white weenie players including Pro Tour Seattle 2004 finalists Ashira Chamorro, Simon Carlson, 20th place at Pro Tour Nagoya 2005 and tabbed by fellow suite Anton Johnson as a player to watch and Top 8 Grand Prix Seattle finisher 16 year old Ryan Kimra, the only undefeated US player in the field. Rounding out the top five is German Andre Mueller playing A4 color control deck. Just behind this top five is a load of talent. Olivier Ruel, Mark Herberholz, Gadiel Slifer, Richie Hohen, Kyoshi Fujita and Gabe Walls joined by top finishing rookie Jim Roy playing three color control and Morgan Douglas piloting a Rogue blue white Ninjas deck. All have high hopes for tomorrow. That's not to say there weren't heavy casualties among the pros, however. Among those on the side winds tomorrow are such notables as Shoe Kamuro, Tim Aiton, Julian Norton, Jeff Cunningham, Justin Gary, Sam Gomersall, John Sunny, Frank Karsten and Tom Johnson, Euron Rami and Jin Okamoto. Of note, while snake based decks made-up almost 25% of the field, they seem to have consistently underperformed. But Fosco cruises 14th place, being the highest finish for a snake based deck. No, this weekend isn't about snakes, it's about ginormous monsters. Dragons, Myosin patrons. If it's an animal and it has a power or a manic cost greater than 5, you can bet that someone's playing it today. Standing their ground against the hordes of pizza over our three flavors of white weenie, joined together by the superpowered umezawa's Jitte, which, once it gets rolling, is nigh impossible to stop. So we'll take the crown this weekend. The monsters, or the little white men with the big bad Jitter or Wokai or another rogue build managed to to rule the day. With only 33 players being more than one match for elimination, it's sure to be an exciting day of magic. Let us know what you think in the forums and stay tuned to magicgathering.com for updates.
