It has been quite a year since Antonino DeRosa had his silly likeness commemorated on a Player Card. Coming in to Toronto he had won the second Grand Prix of his career, became the 2005 U.S. National Champion, finished second at the team competition at Worlds, made his first Pro Tour Top 8 at Prague, way more than doubled his lifetime winnings, and capped it all off with another Grand Prix win this weekend. 

DeRosa defeated his good friend, and occasional teammate Jon Sonne in two quick and dirty games in his ninth appearance in a Grand Prix Top 8. 

There was little time for rejoicing though as everyone had to hurry home and get working on the 60-card Ravnica decks for PT Charleston that was looming in less than two weeks. See y'all then.

     </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Ravnica</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>The first three picks were easy for Jelger: first pick offered him <a>Lightning Helix</a> over <a>Plague Boiler</a>, <a>Skyknight Legionnaire</a> and <a>Conclave Equenaut</a>. Second pick gave him a bomb rare in <a>Cerulean Sphinx</a> and his third pick was <a>Snapping Drake</a> over some mediocre green cards. His fourth pick was a tough choice though: move into black with <a>Brainspoil</a> or <a>Dimir House Guard</a> or stick to his colors with a safe <a>Benevolent Ancestor</a>, also fitting nicely into a deck with already two flying creatures. Fifth pick he once again decided to pick a safe <a>Screeching Griffin</a> rather than a <a>Dimir House Guard</a> or <a>Moonlight Bargain</a>. The sixth pick surprisingly offered him a late <a>Bottled Cloister</a>, and he rounded the pack out with a couple of <a>Terraformer</a>s and a <a>Courier Hawk</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Guildpact</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>The first pack offered another great flying creature in <a>Harrier Griffin</a> over <a>Pyromatics</a>. Second pick brought the choice Guildpact often brings: <a>Steamcore Weird</a>, <a>Ogre Savant</a>, <a>Izzet Chronarch</a> or <a>Blind Hunter</a>. Jelger opted for the Weird but thought about taking Savant for a long time. His next pick showed that Jelger isn't <i>always</i> a greedy man when he took <a>Izzet Signet</a> over <a>Repeal</a>, another Chronarch and <a>Ghost Warden</a>. He still got a <a>Ghost Warden</a> in the next pack, shipping <a>Train of Thought</a>, and in the next pack picked up Guardian's Magemark, ignoring Osiary Thrull and <a>Streetbreaker Wurm</a>. The black cards kept on coming as the next pack contained <a>Exhumer Thrull</a> and Teysa, but Jelger realized that it was too late to pick up black cards and picked another <a>Ghost Warden</a>. <a>Bloodscale Prowler</a>, <a>Thunderheads</a>, <a>Train of Thought</a> and <a>Withstand</a> rounded out a reasonable second booster.</p>&#10;      <h3>Dissension</h3>&#10;      <p>The last booster came up, and Jelger's deck wasn't looking like it might win him the Grand Prix yet. He was in a good position for the final booster though: John Fiorillo on his right and Antonino De Rosa on John's right both didn't plan on picking any Azorius cards.</p>&#10;      <p>The first pick Jelger took <a>Plumes of Peace</a> over <a>Ocular Halo</a> and Soulsworn Journey, and second pick he passed the same two cards and the white Eidolon for <a>Court Hussar</a>. Another Plumes was a fine third pick, but the fourth and fifth picks were amazing for Jelger: two <a>Pride of the Clouds</a>! After that, he got passed an unexciting booster and then a very late <a>Azorius Herald</a>, confirming once again that there weren't many Azorius drafters at the table. <a>Beacon Hawk</a>, Prahv, <a>Spell Snare</a> and a very late white Eidolon rounded out a terrific pack for Jelger.</p>&#10;
