![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a678.jpg)Ostensibly, this is Olivier Ruel's Pro Tour. The "curse" that comes with finishing last at the Magic Invitational ensures that that player will win a Pro Tour in the coming year. Ruel welcomed that ignominy, and now it seems to be paying dividends.

Also reaching for that brass ring is Your Move Games's Rob Dougherty. Dougherty's been on a bit of a constructed tear recently. He made Top 8 at Chicago last year with Fires, then only missed Top 8 at New Orleans by losing his last match to teammate Dave Humpherys. Add to that his performance this weekend, and it's clear he has the skills to pay many of his bills.

Both are running mono-black control decks, but have slightly different maindecks. Conventional wisdom holds that the first game will be all about Mind Sludge, and the second will be a race to Rancid Earth and Braids.

Ruel won the coveted right to play first. His opening hand featured Mind Sludge and most of the mana to cast it, so he was all smiles. However, Dougherty put a stop to that with a turn-three Rancid Earth, setting Ruel's clock back.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a679.jpg)Ruel spent his fifth turn playing Diabolic Tutor. He chose to fetch Morbid Hunger. Dougherty struck with Rancid Earth again, putting Ruel to just two swamps. Ruel pulled a third like the pro he is. He chose to wait on his Sludge, though, as it would only catch three of Dougherty's six cards.

Dougherty continued to play the waiting game. A Tutor found him a Sludge of his own. Ruel played Skeletal Scrying for three, finding desperately needed swamps, and a Rancid Earth of his own, minimizing Dougherty's Sludge.

Dougherty Sludged for five. Ruel chose to keep Laquatus's Champion and Sludge. Again, Ruel mised himself a Swamp to Sludge away all but one of Dougherty's cards. Watching Dougherty pitch Edict, Blood and Shambling Swarm gave him hopes that his Champ could survive. Dougherty just dropped Cabal Coffers and Edicted from the graveyard. Dougherty made no further play, and Ruel's face brightened when he saw the Shade he just drew. That joy was short-lived. Dougherty had a second Edict in hand.

Ruel practically fell out his chair when his deck turned up Skeletal Scrying. At the end of Dougherty's turn he drew up eight cards. Among them were Haunting Echoes, a second Mind Sludge, and a Nantuko Shade.

The Sludge cleaned Dougherty out, though he did put on a sly smile, trying to trick Ruel into targetting himself. Ruel, however, chose correctly. He placed the Echoes on the table and verified that Dougherty had Mutilate, Innocent Blood and Chainer's Edict in his graveyard. Then he showed him Nantuko Shade. Dougherty packed it in.

Ruel 1 - Dougherty 0

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a680.jpg)Ruel's opening hand for Game 2 was reasonable, holding Mind Sludge and Mesmeric Fiend. The fiend stole Dougherty's Rancid Earth, and saw that he held only a third land to go with his double-Sludge, Braids and Haunting Echoes.

The fiend got Edicted two turns later, but still did its job, keeping Dougherty from dropping Rancid Earth then Braids, the most powerful draw the deck has. Ruel dropped a Braids of his own. Dougherty floated a mana before sacrificing a land to play Scrying for two, desperate to keep from missing a land drop.

He did manage to Rancid Earth and keep some parity, but Ruel's deck was kind, serving up Mesmeric Fiend to increase the beats. Soon Dougherty was down to one swamp, and scooping it up.

Ruel 2 - Dougherty 0

Game 3 saw Ruel pull up another monster draw. Mesmeric Fiend again showed Dougherty's lack of a third mana-producing land, just a Coffers. Ruel was in the same situation. He took Nantuko Shade, seeing Chainer's Edict, two Braids and a Skeletal Scrying. Dougherty naturally chose to Edict.

Doughtery got some business of his own going with back-to-back Shades while Ruel floundered. Gesturing wildly at his deck, Ruel summoned forth the swamp he needed. He Mutilated away Dougherty's board, forcing him to topdeck a swamp or sit there helplessly. Regrettably, Dougherty chose the latter.

Down came Braids. Ruel even had a second waiting in the wings if Dougherty managed to find removal. He did not.

**Final Result:** Olivier Ruel defeats Rob Dougherty 3-0
