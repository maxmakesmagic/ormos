Welcome to the first week of *Magic Origins* previews here on DailyMTG.com! Some of the most powerful *Magic* cards ever printed give us a lot of options. [Commands](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=394613), [Charms](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=386467), and [split cards](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=369080) from various sets have taught us that spells with options tend to be very powerful. Burn spells are the original cards with options; we can use our [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt) to kill our opponent's creature, but we can also use it to kill our opponent. This versatility has allowed Red players to effectively control the board in creature matchups while simultaneously avoiding dead cards in matchups without opposing blockers. Burn spells are efficient and put pressure on the opponent. Today, we'll be taking a look at a powerful new burn spell that's sure to set Standard ablaze.

Burn spells vary wildly in power level, but 4 damage for three mana has generally been quite strong whenever it's been available in Standard. [Flame Javelin](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flame+Javelin) and [Char](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Char) were both excellent cards in their time. Let's take a look at Exquisite Firecraft:

![](http://media.wizards.com/2015/origins_askdf9aj2399v/en_iT7HXQz7eM.png)

It's a sorcery, which means it won't be strong in the clutch against an opposing [Abzan Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Abzan+Charm) for +1/+1 counters or [Become Immense](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Become+Immense), but 4 damage for three mana that can go to the dome is an excellent deal right now. In fact, [Bathe in Dragonfire](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bathe+in+Dragonfire) has seen significant play recently and this is a huge upgrade over that card. Spending three mana to deal with cards like [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix); [Anafenza, the Foremost](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Anafenza%2C+the+Foremost); [Thunderbreak Regent](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thunderbreak+Regent); [Stormbreath Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stormbreath+Dragon); and even [Dragonlord Ojutai](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragonlord+Ojutai) (if tapped) is a fine deal.

The thing that separates Exquisite Firecraft from its predecessors is its ability to seal the deal and win the game outright.

Sometimes, when playing burn against a control opponent, we'll find ourselves with an opponent that stabilized at a low life total with a fistful of countermagic. Exquisite Firecraft was designed with these specific situations in mind. By that stage of the game, we'll easily fulfill the spell mastery requirement and be able to fearlessly deal the final blow(s) with one or more copies of Exquisite Firecraft. The card punishes opponents that don't respect tester burn spells too. For example, we can have an opponent at 11 and cast [Lightning Strike](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Strike) targeting their face on their end step. In this situation, a lot of opponents wouldn't want to spend a [Dissolve](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dissolve) and valuable mana to fight over those 3 points of damage, but a pair of Exquisite Firecraft can easily end the game without any recourse for the [Dissolve](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dissolve)/[Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate) carrying opponent.

The mere presence of Exquisite Firecraft should be enough to discourage players from allowing themselves to fall below 4 life against red opponents.

Let's take a look at what a Standard deck might look like with Exquisite Firecraft.

# Post-*Magic Origins* Red

Martin Dang's Red deck has always played well for me in testing. [Wild Slash](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Slash) became more and more awkward as even the Abzan decks cut cards like [Rakshasa Deathdealer](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakshasa+Deathdealer). Against the more controlling and midrange decks of the format, [Wild Slash](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Slash) was often underperforming. I think it's safe to push the mana requirements of the deck a bit because it was rare we would cast [Wild Slash](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Slash) on the first turn in matchups that weren't the mirror or didn't involve [Elvish Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Elvish+Mystic). Exquisite Firecraft does a lot of damage for its cost and it's better at sealing the game with a finishing blow than any other burn spell in the current Standard. One thing that's especially nice about Dang's deck is that the copies of [Dragon Fodder](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dragon+Fodder) and [Hordeling Outburst](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hordeling+Outburst) we play are going to contribute to our Spell Mastery.

# Post-*Magic Origins* Burn

When building with a powerful burn spell, it's important to recognize the power of burn spells in multiples, especially when the card is very good at closing the game. This made me think about [Den Protector](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Den+Protector), which has already proven itself to be a great tool in the current Standard environment. By playing a lot more burn we can afford to include this form of card advantage, and we'll often be punishing opponents by following up [Eidolon of the Great Revel](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eidolon+of+the+Great+Revel) with a morph, thus giving them a very difficult decision when it comes to aiming their removal spell. One thing that's particularly exciting about putting [Den Protector](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Den+Protector) into the burn deck is the ability to double-cast [Become Immense](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Become+Immense) to dish out absurd amounts of unblockable damage against opponents that tap out.

Here's the [Den Protector](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Den+Protector) version of the deck.

This version of the deck seems weak to [Siege Rhino](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siege+Rhino), especially in Game 1 where it doesn't have access to [Roast](https://web.archive.org/web/20150625000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Roast), but the deck has a lot of punch in most matchups and seems like it should be able to close games fast enough to warrant some serious consideration.

Finishing off an opponent with the final burn spell is always a relief. With Exquisite Firecraft in the mix we'll likely be seeing a lot more wanton burn spells being aimed at the face. Control players will rue the hours they spent learning how to beat the red decks in post-boarded games as they find themselves helpless to the last 8 points of burn.
