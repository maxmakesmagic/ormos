Before I begin going over the red commons in *Seventh Edition*, I would like to clarify a statement in the previous article. When I said that Dark Banishing is the best pick in *Seventh*, I obviously meant "best common". Apparently I was not clear enough, so I reiterate. As I wrote in part 1 of this series, the greatest problem with *Seventh Edition* as a draft set is the occasional broken rare such as Worship or Opposition that will totally ruin your day, and Banishing just cannot compete with that. Having said that, I certainly maintain that it is the best common pick. To suggest (as a reader on the message boards has) that Lightning Blast is as good seems ludicrous to me, and I will explain why I feel that way in this article.

Among the five colors, red appears to get the shaft more often than any of the other four when it comes to its power in draft, and *Seventh Edition* is no exception. You've got some solid direct damage spells, but they are not quite as powerful in the format with so many large common creatures - and the red creatures themselves are nothing to write home about. This is not to say red is unplayable, but I find myself disinclined to draft it, going into the color only after having received some really strong signals from the player to my right (being passed a Blaze is something I'd consider a strong enough signal).

**Balduvian Barbarians**  
 Along with Serpent Warrior and Trained Armodon, it is one of the only three power creatures for three mana in *Seventh* - something to give you a jump - if even by a turn - on all those Hill Giants. Lower toughness makes Barbarians far less attractive, but even so they are a fine mid-pack draft pick that will help fill out your mana curve in almost any red/x archetype. Double red in the casting cost also makes the card slightly less attractive, especially if you chose to splash a third color for a spell or two - but in the end it still remains a solid creature.

**Goblin Chariot**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I would much rather have a Balduvian Barabrian than a Goblin Chariot, but in most *Seventh* drafts you will end up playing one or two creatures like this one. Telepathic Spies, Scathe Zombies - the ability is almost negligible but you will often need that extra creature, or an extra card that costs three mana instead of four. Nowhere near an early pick, it may nevertheless end up in your deck as 20th-23rd card.

**Goblin Digging Team**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This is not a card you ever want to main deck (see Raging Goblin for a lengthy explanation as to why), but there are enough quality walls in the set that you will occasionally sideboard it in. Only worth it against decks with two or more good walls which will usually be blue-white (say, Wall of Air and Wall of Swords). Since that is not going to happen often, Digging Team should not be chosen earlier than eleventh-twelfth pick.

**Goblin Elite Infantry**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card is horrendous. The only way you can possibly make it passable is by making sure there are no creatures at all available to block it. Even then, it just becomes another bear - a 2/2 creature for two mana that are already plentiful and already not very good in *Seventh Edition*. If you are forced to play with an Infantry in your deck, your draft is in trouble.

**Goblin Gardener**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A reasonable but not a very exciting creature, Goblin Gardener will forestall those huge monsters your opponent might be holding in hand by taking out a land when it dies in combat. Every once in a while you will successfully mana-screw your opponent and ride their misfortune to victory. More often than not though, you will only present them with a minor setback for your trouble of paying two mana too many for a 2/1 creature. Gardener or two might make the cut into your deck, but once again they will be late picks at best.

**Goblin Raider**  
 More so than any other color, red relies on some early beatdown, which makes Goblin Raider a reasonable pick - as reasonable as the bears get in this set. Red is the one color that requires you to have several two-drops between it and whatever color you combine it with, so make the time to draft a pair of these. Fortunately, you should be able to do so close to your tenth pick.

**Goblin Spelunkers**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 They are great if your opponent has mountains and just another 2/2 warm body if he does not. Thus, it's a better creature to play in your deck than Goblin Chariot or Scathe Zombies, but a card you will readily sideboard out if your opponent is playing the wrong colors.

**Granite Grip**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Bottom of the barrel material here. Holy and Unholy Strengths are cheap enough and powerful enough to be playable, and both of them enhance the creature in a way that makes it much tougher to remove. Granite Grip increases the power, but the toughness stays the same which means any creature you enchant with it will simply trade in combat with another guy losing you card economy. I would not play this card under any circumstances.

**Hill Giant**  
 The meat and potatoes of *Seventh Edition* draft is the one, the only, the original Hill Giant. In this creature-poor color, this is your best common creature and you better pick him pretty high - as high as fourth pick - and try your best to get multiples. Randy Buehler wrote a very interesting article on building *Eighth Edition* on MagicTheGathering.com recently where he explained that one of the huge design flaws of *Seventh Edition* was giving blue too good a creature base - citing Giant Octopus (a.k.a. blue Hill Giant) as an example. That is part of the reason red kind of sucks - other colors are getting their hands on the same toys as red, on top of the cool stuff they had from before (as is the case with blue).

**Lava Axe**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In a word, not so good. Any card that does not change the state of the game at all and costs five mana to boot is unspectacular. Taking off a quarter of your opponent's life total is really good if you dealt a lot of damage to them early in the game but they managed to stabilize the board with slower but larger creatures - however that situation will not come up 100% of the time and drawing a Lava Axe will often be a waste. Therefore, it is a card you should draft pretty late and only play in ultra-aggressive decks that are most likely to take full advantage of its ability.

**Lightning Blast**  
 Certainly the best red common in the set, Lightning Blast, Volcanic Hammer and Shock are the main reasons to play this color in the first place. Unfortunately, direct damage is not nearly as efficient as black removal. First of all, there are many common creatures with high toughness that will not die to all or some of the burn spells. Second, efficient damage prevention cards such as Healing Salve and Samite Healer will often ruin your day. On the up side, red direct damage is mana-efficient and can be cast as an instant, offering you an increased host of tricks. In Constructed play, the cheapest burn spell is best - Shock is very playable and even Volcanic Hammer has seen Extended tournament play. In Limited however, mana efficiency, however important, gives way to raw power - and Lightning Blast has got plenty of the latter. It is able to take out most (though surely not all) common creatures in the game and is easily first pick material in your draft.

**Lightning Elemental**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Similar in many ways to Giant Cockroach, this card's haste ability is a lot more relevant than that of Goblin Chariot. Four life points is a considerable life swing and you might catch an opponent with his creatures tapped, or force him to block with a superior creature - squeezing a few extra miles out of this card. One toughness is a considerable negative with cards like Nausea and Crossbow Infantry, but I still consider it a slightly better card and pick over a Cockroach when they come in the same pack.

**Pygmy Pyrosaur**  
 Surprisingly good, this little guy simply has to be blocked or removed in mid- to late-game - which means it may often take a creature or removal spell your opponent drafted much earlier than when you drafted the Pyrosaur to the graveyard with it. You can grab these around tenth pick, but they are useful enough to pick earlier if the pack is poor.

**Raging Goblin**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This card is bad. Really, really bad. Tireless Tribe is bad, too. Problem is, amateur drafters do not know this. Serious players are always overjoyed to see one of these little guys come out against them on turn 1. So much so that CMU came up with a name for inexperienced players as a group - "The Tireless Tribe." Unless a 1/1 creature for one mana has a truly awesome special ability, such as that of Llanowar Elves or Grim Lavamancer, it is not worth playing in draft. It simply does not perform well enough on the battlefield and, in almost every single game, it will act as an onlooker or a chump-blocker in every combat. If you are new to drafting or are an old hand at it but feel you need to improve your game, begin by cutting down the number or Raging Goblins and Goblin Digging Teams in your deck to zero.

**Reflexes**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Another utterly unplayable creature enchantment, first strike ability is hardly worth investing a card into.

**Sabretooth Tiger**  
 This creature is playable and slightly better than an average Gray Ogre (despite its lower toughness) because it can interact especially well with cards like Giant Growth or Shock to take out a larger creature. Sabretooth Tiger fits in fine into an average red deck and can be picked around seventh or eighth.

**Shatter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Never a main deck card, it will nevertheless be boarded in every tenth game or so, so it's a good idea to pick one or two up really late if you get a shot at it when there are absolutely no cards left that you might take for your main deck.

**Shock**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A very solid removal spell which you can never truly have enough of. Can be anywhere between a first and fifth pick, its pick quality is really around #3. Shock is not as good as Lightning Blast or Volcanic Hammer, but they don't normally come in the same pack.

**Spitting Earth**  
 Very similar to Corrupt, this card's power is affected by just how much red you are playing. A ton of Spitting Earths and Pygmy Pyrosaurs could make a pretty reasonable deck! As long as half or more of your mana are mountains, Spitting Earth is a fairly high pick and it will always make the cut if you play six or more mountains in your deck total.

**Stone Rain**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I've tried to draft land destruction decks time and again in a variety of formats. It works out every once in a while, but it is never a consistent strategy. In a normal draft, I will avoid picking up Stone Rain over anything else at all I might use, but will draft it really late over stuff like Reflexes and Regeneration and at least keep it in the back of my mind when sideboarding. 

**Tremor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This tiny Earthquake is the only red removal common in Seventh that isn't worth main-decking. It is not as effective as Nausea and even Nausea only gets main-decked half the time. Tremor is a reasonable sideboard card you pick up late in a pack and bring in against decks with a lot of one-toughness creatures.

**Volcanic Hammer**  
 Second-best burn spell in the set, I definitely take it over Shock, Hill Giant and Spitting Earth - which are the other top red commons following in the steps of Lightning Blast. Three power and three toughness are the marquee of the format, so any spell capable of doing that much damage is welcome in your deck.
