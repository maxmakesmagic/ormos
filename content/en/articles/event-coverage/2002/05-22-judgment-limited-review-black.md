This is the third installment in my series reviewing the cards in *Judgment* for the purposes of Limited play. You can find the other installments [here](/en/events/2002/ac/odyssey-block-limited-review).

### Black

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9f/ff/9fff46ae318e33a346999d1b6d518d22a0cce3836be09ecd88468b68e81c4ad7.jpg)Ladies and gentlemen, may I present to you the single worst color any one set has seen in the history of the game: *Judgment* black. The problems the black mage faces going into *Judgment* are many, here's a look at just a few:

*   There's one black kill spell, and it's best described as 'mediocre'.
*   There are a total of sixteen black cards.
*   An amazing SIX of them are common.
*   The best card in the color is going to get taken by non-black drafters looking for a splash.
*   Even that card has two sizeable drawbacks.
*   A disproportionate number of drafters will be in black because they'll want to get their hands on the best of Torment, so your sacrifice may be in vain

Black is a good match for any of the other four colors, but the selection in *Judgment* is so anaemic that playing three colors will almost be a necessity. If you pass a decent amount in black in *Odyssey*, don't bother trying to pick any of it up in *Torment*: The drafters behind you will cut off the cards you were hoping to get, and in *Judgment* you'll get... well... read on.

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d2/f8/d2f8884d200ef52b13bba9a0fcfe2cac250857e878b2be26694d9652756bb625.jpg)**Treacherous Vampire**  
 So, this is black's best card. 4/4 flyers for five mana don't exactly come cheap, especially if they come with a nice threshold bonus (and it is a bonus), but there are a couple of problems with this card. Of minor importance is the fact that the Vampire requires the sacrifice of a graveyard card every turn if you want to use it. Of more importance though is that, as befits its name, the Vampire's single-black casting cost means that non-black drafters will have to be tempted to take it to splash in their own decks. Woe to the black drafter.

**Sutured Ghoul**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I'm not a big fan of seven casting cost creatures, but this thing can end the game pretty fast. The Ghoul's potential is only limited by your ability to get through your deck and force your creatures to the graveyard, and that means you could be looking at a 10/10 trampler or even bigger. It wouldn't rank nearly this high in most sets, but for black, *Judgment* is the exception to the rule.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/27/a5/27a52042161b77e3a9ace8be56d4006fc1492340f8c46e27496f5b74a8bae9e7.jpg)**Guiltfeeder**  
 I'm not 100% convinced this card is that awesome now that Advocates are returning cards and graveyards are being emptied thanks to assorted *Judgment* cards, but the rest of black gives me no option but to rank it high here. The 'Feeder could well be a 0/4 for five or, conversely, could win the game in two turns. Let's hope it's on your side of the table for the latter.

**Masked Gorgon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If you can get yourself threshold pretty easily, this card is an absolute powerhouse. Protection from two of the five colors added to a 5/5 protection-from-banishes body is obviously very strong, but the Gorgon isn't always all that. Diligent Farmhand can block this 5/5 and survive the encounter before Threshold, and with most players likely to go green or white, you're going to find your 5/5 rare nullified by their most useless dork. Get threshold.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/54/19/5419c92f9195415946960a81a25b39f6d70ed18c3b76064f7c4fd7b3b8806977.jpg)**Toxic Stench**  
 How weak. The only black kill spell in the entire set is an Afflict without the cantrip aspect. It can't kill the annoying black critters that most one point kill spells are geared towards killing, either. Sure, it becomes a Banish after threshold, but I want cards that do something early on. This does, but it isn't enough. 

**Grave Consequences**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Cantrips may be the most valuable commodity black players can find in *Judgment*, because they get you to your *Torment* cards a little faster. The black player is generally accustomed to playing without many cards n the graveyard anyway, so going down to zero shouldn't prove too much of a problem, while other players may be relying pretty heavily on getting threshold or keeping their flashback and/or Incarnation cards around, so Grave Consequences could actually do more than cycle into a new card. Pick the stuff in your other color higher, but you can definitely find worse in this set.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d5/69/d5699b874ace21289f8baf486fb024d68aa5bca00cc24e2f02ec986740a0e4a2.jpg)**Balthor the Defiled**  
 Balthor is still a 2/2 and pumps other creatures, but a couple of things have changed: now he's black and even more overcosted. Fortunately, there's one great metamorphoses that occurred with Balthor's zombification, that being the ![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) ability, but to truly optimize that you'll have to play black-red, a very weak color combination now thanks to Judgment's weak selection in both colors. If you're black, he's definitely going to be playable, but how pickable he'll be completely depends on the contents of your deck.

**Treacherous Werewolf**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When this is arguably the best common creature your color offers you, you know you're not getting a lot more for your deck. Did you ever think you'd see the day where you actually hoped to open a Grey Ogre? Okay, that may be an overstatement as you'll always have another color to choose from, but the fact remains that the Werewolf is emblematic of black's woes in this set. Even after threshold, the 4/4 variety has a sizeable downside and doesn't evade in any way, but you've gotta do what you've gotta do. This is the price you pay for those Butchers, Fatigues and Torturers you'll get in *Torment*. Hopefully.

**Filth**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Great, the moment everyone stops drafting black, you can give all your guys swampwalk. I wouldn't run Filth main deck too often, but it's a great sideboard card that should win a lot of black on black games, and I like strong sideboard options enough to take them over passable main deck cards.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/magic/general/Earsplitting_Rats.jpg)**Earsplitting Rats**  
 2/1's for four aren't the greatest thing in the world, but regeneration is a rare and valuable commodity in this set and you can't ignore that fact. Throw in the fact that you can turn this guy into card advantage if you have no cards in hand and the help it can give you with regards to threshold and madness and you have a card that will make more decks than it won't.

**Stitch Together**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I wouldn't count on the threshold ability coming into play too often, and even if it did, by the time the black player has threshold, they shouldn't have much trouble casting the targeted creature in any event. That said, any black *Judgment* card that mixes well with black *Torment* cards is a plus, so if you don't already have too many Gravediggers and Morgue Thefts, this should be a keeper.

**Cabal Trainee**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I think people are going to be pleasantly surprised by this little guy. At 1/1 for one, it's capable of getting a point or two of damage in if it starts the game in your hand, and once the table has been gummed up by fully grown creatures, he's capable of trading with one of them thanks to the use of his ability in combat situations. Likely best in black-green, where combat tricks will be needed more often than in control decks, the Trainee will be good enough for most of your combat-oriented decks.

**Rats' Feast**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Feast isn't interactive enough to warrant a main deck slot, but graveyard depletion is more valuable now with the introduction of the Incarnations then it's been all year. The Feast is likely the best pure graveyard depletion spell available for sideboard purposes, so try to grab one around card forty or so.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/09/f5/09f55f09f099cc319f3e16e1f2a78b7b4a233585d59c6717b4823de973673d5b.jpg)**Morality Shift**  
 Picture this: you're holding the Shift, working your way to seven mana, finally get there, tap your seven lands and slam it in the table! Amazing right? Unfortunately, you had like three cards in the graveyard and you get decked a couple of turns later. At least you're assured of threshold though.

**Death Wish**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 In Limited, when you get good cards, you put them in your deck, you don't leave them out so you can pay three mana and half your life in addition to their casting cost. This concludes the lesson in simplicity for the day.

**Cabal Therapy**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 While there are cards in this format that will let you see your opponent's hand, without them this card is next to useless in Limited play. Now, if you could cast Duress first...
