### Ten Ways To Improve Your *Magic Online* Experience

* * *

In a lot of ways, logging on to ***Magic: The Gathering** Online* is like going on a vacation. There are a wide array of possibilities, most of which you control, some of which you don't. The advice in this article can increase your enjoyment of the game while giving you the ammunition to avoid some of the more common pitfalls that could spoil your "vacation." Familiarize yourself with these ten tools, and you'll find your online getaway far more pleasant.

### 1\. The Buddy List

The buddy list is a handy way to keep track of people you have things in common with. By typing **/addbuddy** *playername* (or **/addbuddy** *"player name"* if there's a space in the player's name), the game will save that player in a special list kept on the bottom of your game screen. This will work even if the player isn't logged on. Click **My Buddies** on the bottom of the screen and your full list of ***Magic** Online* buddies will pop up.

The nicest thing about putting together a buddy list is that you can avoid the randomness of the **Casual Room** and play games against folks with similar interests or decks. One of the pitfalls of casual play can be getting paired up with a player whose game habits aren't aligned with yours. The **Buddy List** can eliminate this if you are finding the casual rooms a bit chaotic for your liking.

### 2\. Clans

A well-organized clan is another way to socialize with a group of folks you're comfortable with. You have the option of either starting your own clan or joining one. Clans don't cost anything to join, so be aware of that if you're asked to front the captain a booster pack or some tickets.

Take a bit of time to investigate the clan to be sure the other members are people you want to associate yourself with. If you're a top-notch player looking for playtesting, don't join a clan of new players (unless you want to help newbies increase their skill levels). Conversely, if you want to trade a great deal, find a clan that specializes in it.

### 3\. Blocking Players

On your online vacation, you're sure to run into a few troublemakers whose idea of fun is to annoy other players. Luckily, you don't have to put up with this. Right click the player's name, select **Block user**, and you'll never have to worry about trade requests, game challenges, or private messages from that player ever again.

If a player goes out of the way to harass you, perhaps by following you to your games or such, then it's time to ask one of the Adepts to get involved.

### 4\. Moderators & Support Staff Room

![](http://wizards.com/global/images/magic_magiconline_articleak051403_pic2_en.jpg)

Stumped, stymied, or confused? Just head to the **Moderators & Support Staff** room with your question. You will always find an Adept in the room along with quite a few knowledgeable players who also might have the answer to your question.

This isn't a chat room, but it's a great place to go if you need help with game mechanics, card rulings, or general questions. Also, don't expect deck tips or trade advice, it's not the place for that type of information.

### 5\. The Adept Staff

At least one Adept can always be found in the **Moderators & Support Staff** room. Adepts move about the various ***Magic** Online* rooms as well.

Adepts are there to answer questions about the game and ensure folks follow the [Code of Conduct](https://web.archive.org/web/20041206233320/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=community/coc) of the game. Only a small number are DCI judges, so while any member of the Adept team might be able to help you with a card ruling or question, it's not one of their basic functions.

### 6\. The Help Files

![](http://wizards.com/global/images/magic_magiconline_articleak051403_pic3_en.jpg)

Click on the large question mark on the left side of the main screen to access the help files. It's the easiest way to glean the information you may need. You can use the index to simplify your search.

Try to check the help files before asking one of the Adepts. Most of the answers you need are answered in the help files. Of course, if you need clarification on something, by all means seek out an Adept to help you.

### 7\. The Wizards of the Coast Web Site

There are several parts of the Wizards of the Coast web site that can provide all kinds of handy information, including deck tips, strategy, or card opinions. The three you should check on a regular basis are [**MagicTheGathering.com**](https://web.archive.org/web/20050415044019/http://wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic), the [***Magic** Online* web site](https://web.archive.org/web/20050404082550/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic/magiconline), and [*Sideboard* Online](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/sideboard-online). All three of these pages are updated regularly with articles, advice, and tips.

### 8\. The Message Boards

There are message boards for both the [physical **Magic** game](https://web.archive.org/web/20030514000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://boards.wizards.com/tcg-bin/ultimatebb.cgi?category=3&origin=magic_magiconline_articleak051403) and [***Magic** Online*](https://web.archive.org/web/20030514000000/http://wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://boards.wizards.com/tcg-bin/ultimatebb.cgi?category=14&origin=magic_magiconline_articleak051403). Many of the players using the boards are quite knowledgeable about gameplay, **Magic** rules, and card mechanics. Check the boards for some friendly chatter or if you need advice about a new deck.

The boards have several sections, so be sure to use the right category if you have a question. By posting in the appropriate place, your chances of getting the information you seek will be greatly improved.

### 9\. Trading

The current **Trade** screen asks you to confirm a trade not once, but twice. If you click that confirmation button a second time, you are telling the system that you agree to that trade. Your **Magic** vacation isn't without pickpockets! Be sure you double check both the **You Give** and **You Get** windows to ensure the correct cards are in both.

You wouldn't hand your car keys to a total stranger, so don't accept promises from one either. A trade can only be 32 items at one time. If there is any reason that a fair trade can't be made in one 32-item block, you better know who you are trading with. If the player fails to live up to his or her promise, the Adept staff won't be able to help you.

Further, if you decide to go outside the friendly confines of ***Magic** Online* and seek to purchase or trade through Ebay or Paypal, you're on your own. If a deal falls through, or you are somehow the victim of a scam, Wizards of the Coast can't help you with your issue.

Also keep in mind that there is a specific area set up for trading, known as the **Trading Post**. Don't spam the game rooms asking for trades -- it's considered room disruption and inappropriate (not to mention rude) behavior.

### 10\. The Customer Service Web Page

From the [***Magic** Online* Customer Service web page](https://web.archive.org/web/20050403180103/http://wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic/magiconline/csmain) you can access Technical Support, the ***Magic** Online* Store, Community Support, Organized Play, and Rules Support. Most emails will be responded to within 24 to 72 hours, but make sure your questions are clearly stated and provide all pertinent information.

I hope this advice makes your online vacation a safer and more pleasant experience.

See you online!
