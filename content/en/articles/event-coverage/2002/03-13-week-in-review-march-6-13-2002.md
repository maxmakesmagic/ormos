### Coming Up: Pro Tour-Osaka

For the second year in a row, pro players will travel to Japan to play Block Constructed. This year the Pro Tour takes place in Osaka - one of Japan's largest cities located about a two hour flight south of Tokyo. Major teams have scrambled to adjust their rosters in order to prepare for this event. 

*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/801.jpg)Team Godzilla picked up PT San Diego Top 8 finisher Eric Froehlich to provide Zvi with a testing partner in his own time zone.
*   Team CMU recruited Ken Krouner and Brian Davis. Davis traveled to Pittsburgh a few days before the Pro Tour to get some live practice sessions in.
*   Your Move Games has an unprecedented number of players qualified for this event. The playtest group consists of 15+ players and more than ten are likely to show up in Osaka.
*   Ex-members of AlphaBetaUnlimited joined forces with a number of their friends from around west coast to come test at the "Magic Colony" widely publicized elsewhere on the *Sideboard*. They secured a partnership with The Jumble, which means they will be receiving tech from Brian Kibler and Ben Rubin. Will the Colony come up with a deck on their own, or play whatever the Jumble hands them?

There is any number of storylines to follow at the Pro Tour. Some of the ones to watch are:

*   Will Japanese players be able to repeat last year's performance and place one or more players in the Top 8, taking full advantage of competing on their home turf?
*   What kinds of an effect, if any, will my series of OBC Open Testing articles have on the metagame?
*   Can the French players, who have been doing extremely well this season in Limited events, perform well enough in Osaka to hang on to their high places at the Player of the Year race?

And, of course, the individuals to watch:

*   ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPAMS01/975.jpg)**Dirk Baberowski** - If you take the tournament results divided by the amount of **Magic** played, Baberowski may well be the second best player in the world. Will he post another strong finish?
*   **Kai Budde** - Will the Juggernaut beat any more of his records?
*   **Patrick Chapin** - Always an enigma, will this very original deck builder show up, and what insane deck might he bring with him?
*   **Jon Finkel** - He is moving further and further away from **Magic**. Will Finkel's legend continue, or will his performance suffer?
*   **Tsuyoshi Fujita** - Can last year's finalist top his performance?
*   **Donald Gallitz** - The man loudly proclaimed that he quit **Magic**. Any chance he might have a change of heart and show up? I'd give it a few more Pro Tours before he might.
*   **Mike Long** - Another legendary figure that might make a comeback - if he cares half as much as he used to.
*   **Katsuhiro Mori** - Perhaps the best Japanese player on the Pro Tour, can the current Rookie of the Year take Osaka?
*   **Zvi Mowshowitz** - Last year's winner has an amazing streak of top finishes in Block Pro Tours. Will he keep the streak alive?

### Coming Up: Osaka Masters Series

Eight teams will fight it out in the second Team Masters. Last year's winner AlphaBetaUnlimited will not be attending because one of its members, Ryan Fuller, has been suspended by the DCI. In their place, last year's finalist Panzer Hunter will play. The following teams will be competing:

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNY01/935.jpg)**Phoenix Foundation: Kai Budde, Marco Blume, and Dirk Baberowski**  
 A definite favorite to win. Six months ago, Budde took a chance, choosing to play with two friends both of whom have not played competitively in a while. The gamble paid off, with all three of the players continuing to do very well on the tournament scene after they won PT-New York.

**Les Plus Class: Nicolas Oliveri, Gabriel Nassif, Amiel Tenenbaum**  
 Unlike the star-studded Phoenix Foundation, this French team has much to prove. Can they prove their PT-New York finish to be more than a fluke by performing well at the Masters?

**Panzer Hunter: Itaru Ishida, Kazuyuki Momose, Reiji Andou**  
 A long-standing top Japanese team makes its second appearance at the Masters. A definite underdog last year, they took down some of the better-known teams to get to the finals. Now they get another shot.

**www.star-maker.nl/lap: Jelger Wiegersma, Victor van den Broek, Frank Karsten**  
 The Dutch won the mad ratings dash in the weeks preceding the invitation deadline to earn the top ratings invitation spot. Can they do well against other serious teams? It won't be as easy as beating up on the locals.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTNY01/984.jpg)**Your Move Games: Darwin Kastle, Robert Dougherty, David Humphreys**  
 Another definite favorite in an otherwise relatively somewhat star-shy field, Your Move Games has been together as a team since the inception of the team Pro Tour. These guys are a well-oiled machine and have been very good at working together in the past. If anyone has a good chance of taking down Phoenix Foundation, it is them.

**Team Outland: Nicolai Herzog, Bjørn Jocumsen, Eivind Nitter**  
 Although they have not received as much publicity as many of the other teams on this list, Norway's Outland has quietly put up the results good enough to get them into their second Masters tournament in a row. Like the Dutch, they were able to take advantage of the ratings invite system to get in. They too are under the scope to see if they can improve on last year's performance.

**Slay-Pillage-Massacre: Scott McCord, Jonathan Sonne, Eric Ziegler**  
 Another ratings team, these guys aren't well known but have the advantage of playtesting at Neutral Ground - a club that placed a huge number of teams in Day 2 at PT-New York. They too will be looking to make a name for themselves by attempting to take out the better-known players.

The eighth spot will be awarded to the team that wins the Gateway tournament. I will be playing with my Illuminati teammates Justin Gary and Zvi Mowshowitz. Like other teams, we earned DCI points to automatically qualify for the Masters, but the tournaments weren't recorded on time because of a very minor error in the submission. Our appeal was not granted, so we will be out to right all wrongs and win the Gateway tournament - but there are likely to be many other teams there to try and stop us!

### Magic Trivia

Last week's question:

It's your main phase in game three of an *Odyssey-Odyssey-Torment* draft. You have Cephalid Broker and 4 tapped islands in play, and one island in hand. Your opponent is at two life with loads of creatures to attack and block with. You activate the Cephalid Broker and are able to kill your opponent during your main phase. What two cards did you draw?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/dc/b4/dcb41a2de60b08bbca5b28d17b7bb034595c4ce2f07b224f3a8602fdb8fb9a75.jpg)Answer:

Fiery Temper and a Mountain. Discard Island and Fiery Temper. Temper will be removed from the game and you will have an opportunity to cast it until you pass priority. Since playing a land does not pass priority, you can play the Mountain, tap it for red mana and use that mana to cast Fiery Temper and win the game.

### Bad Play of the Week

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ad/bc/adbce6d393dc52149c42dbc6f2325e181eb8a6f7241dd4760d591ee21195c996.jpg)Tony Tsai was playing in an early round of a PTQ last weekend. His opponent had Chainer, Dementia Master in play, so Tony was in a bit of trouble. He got very excited when he drew Faceless Butcher - so excited in fact, that he immediately cast the Butcher, choosing to remove Chainer from the game. Tony forgot that Butcher is a Nightmare! When Chainer left play, its effect triggered and removed all other Nightmares (aka Butcher) from the game! Chainer came right back, and Tsai was down four mana and one of his best creatures. 

Despite this, Tsai won the match and went on to win the qualifier. Congratulations!
