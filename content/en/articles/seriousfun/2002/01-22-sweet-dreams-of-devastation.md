### Discarding for fun and profit

As the *Torment* prerelease draws near, the thoughts of this site turn to one of the key mechanics of the set: madness. Madness gives you the chance to play a spell “in mid-air” between your hand and the graveyard, as you discard it. Whether the discard is forced by an opponent, or your idea, the madness can happen.

Since this column tends toward those formats with more than two players, we’ll spend today looking at a card that gives you the opportunity to use plenty of madness spells, and with enough power to change the board on its own. As my MagicTheGathering.com colleagues’ dreams turn to madness, my dreams turn… to devastation.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ec/63/ec633edac0b43cec90dd9638843f6bc164ccb69e7a375532399ee97e9f5f1a9d.jpg)

Adjustable Wildfires are nothing to sneeze at. Especially when they only cost two mana. The discard is costly; but red mages are used to throwing cards away to get a short-term benefit (see: Gamble, Firestorm, Sneak Attack, Balduvian Horde, Sonic Burst). I see Devastating Dreams as one of the most “red” cards possible: it damages creatures, waxes lands, blows up your hand, is cheap on mana, and gives you a tremendous rush. It doesn’t damage opponents – at least, not until you play Repercussion – but you can’t have everything.

### YOUR DREAMS COME TRUE

The "dream" cards are a thread throughout the colors in *Torment*. Not all have been revealed yet; but with a little brain power, you can probably guess at the shape of some other ones. None of them are as immediately impressive as red’s (though green’s comes close). Devastating Dreams is likely to get play not just in multiplayer formats, but tournament formats as well. That’s good news for casual players who like to think about quality cards in creative ways.

Many players will start with some well-worn strategies, borrowed from past decks that used Earthquake and/or Wildfire for the same purpose. Rukh Egg is the classic example; but cards that penalize loss of permanents (Dingus Staff, Dingus Egg) are also familiar to many old-school players. The simple use of artifact mana will be another common ploy.

Let’s turn instead to the more unique aspects that this new card brings to the game. The random discard element and cheap casting cost mean at least four things for red mages in casual formats:

*Red mages may play more madness cards than other players.* Fiery Temper, which Mike Elliott [previewed](/en/articles/feature/2002/01-21-initial-stages-of-madness) yesterday, is absolutely ideal for finishing off a large creature facing the wake of Devastating Dreams… or for poking another player in the eye, if you’re that kind of person. There are other madness cards I’d love to point out to you; but of course, I can’t. (Who was the idiot who suggested my column come early in the week? Oh, yeah: that would be me.)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e3/7a/e37a43f851ebcec8af884878ff585c8e1b0a556d8167b6756995b06ebddaf721.jpg)

*Red mages can become more reckless.* This phenomenon is sort of like lemmings becoming more suicidal, but it’s true. If you draw two mountains, a Wildfire, and a bunch of white cards, you'll probably mulligan. But if you draw two mountains, a Devastating Dreams, and a bunch of white cards…well, at least you know you can clear the board if the early game goes badly for you.

*Red mages care about drawing cards more than usual.* That additional recklessness, and the very nature of Devastating Dreams, will require players to find ways to refuel. Wheel of Fortune is a clear choice; but if your group follows Type 1 restriction rules, you’ll only have one in your deck. Howling Mine is the next best option.

But let’s really open this baby up. Winter Sky, a trash coin-flip rare from *Homelands*, suddenly becomes amazing. Will the Sky deal one damage to the board, which fits the theme of your Devastating Dreams deck…or will it replace itself, keeping a card in your hand for the next Dreams? Oh, the humanity of it all! Scylla on one side, Charybdis on the other… *where can your opponents hide?!?!*

(Okay, not amazing. But I bet I still just made you click on the card link above, just to be sure. *“*Homelands *rare, amazing?”* you thought to yourself. *“I gotta check this out!”* I promise never to abuse your trust again; but this was necessary just once, since I’m fairly certain I just made someone at Wizards root out a computer image of the card, which they never would have done otherwise, thereby wasting their time, just like we all did when we opened this rare in a booster pack. What goes around, comes around, my friends.)

If you care to splash a color, your best bet is blue, which gives you a host of options. Start with a Quicksilver Dagger on a high-toughness creature, like Wall of Diffusion. I would also suggest Standstill and Rhystic Study, both rather cheap cards that can come down after you storm the board in your favor; Ophidian, which can survive a modest Devastating Dreams and then replenish your hand by attacking across an empty board; and the Kingfisher-style creatures that let you draw cards as they die.

For a real strategic challenge, consider Trade Routes. If you have the Routes out with six or so lands, and six cards (including Dreams) in your hand, you might play the Dreams with X=4, and tap the remaining four lands to return themselves to your hand. While everyone else loses four lands, you will lose only two, and you'll have lands ready to go in your hand. Later, if you have surplus lands but no Dreams in your hand, you can use the Routes to go looking for them.

Let’s look at what else Devastating Dreams does to red mages, beyond turning them blue, shall we?

### RECURRING DREAMS

*Red mages care more about the graveyard than they’re used to.* (Hey, look, they turn blue *and* black!) There have always been one or two particularly nasty recursive cards in red’s arsenal; but nothing like what black or green could generate. What few resources red has, however, are ideal for a Devastating Dreams deck. Hammer of Bogardan and Shard Phoenix are expensive for a deck that sacks lands; but they’re still nearly automatic. Squee, Goblin Nabob is, of course, an excellent choice.

Another graveyard strategy to consider would be using the large graveyard, rather than recurring it. Kindle and Flame Burst, Magnivore if you use enough sorceries, and even Sarcatog in a deck with artifact creatures are all worth trying. Also in this category: the considerable power of threshold for red, which gives you access to very efficient multiplayer cards such as Chainflinger and Shower of Coals.

*Red mages will still love green.* I haven’t talked about green much in my early articles here on the site, and it may not be clear yet just how much I enjoy that color. Red and green are allied colors for a reason: red can dish it out, and green can take it. I’m not always tricky with my decks, and sometimes having fun in **Magic** means just building a rather obvious deck that burns, smashes, and kills. I’ve built such a deck this week that uses most of the elements I’ve discussed above – Wall of Blossoms for card drawing, Spike Feeders (which, with the fruit in the artwork, give the deck its name) to offset some recklessness, and Fiery Temper for the madness. The recursion comes from a green uncommon from *Legends*, Reincarnation. Reincarnation gives you a shot at replacing a small, weak creature with a monster that you’ve discarded to Devastating Dreams earlier. (If you don’t have access to this card, the slower Pattern of Rebirth will work in similar fashion, pulling from your library instead of your graveyard.)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5c/b0/5cb0b7e94d8054ec93086d597c626005cf2c0077106f38482f8c2bacab4b7bce.jpg)

The fatties I’ve chosen are wurms, which, as we all know, love fruit. All play off of the noted absence of lands or creatures your opponents will have. It’s not very subtle; but you don’t win **Magic** by being more subtle than your opponents; you win by knocking them down to zero.

### DREAM ON

The random discard cost of Devastating Dreams is still very scary. But with enough hard work in deck construction, you can make that work to your advantage. Imagine how terrifying it will be for your opponents to watch you blow through your hand in maniacal fashion…and then reuse what you’ve discarded, or build off the size of your graveyard, or rebuild your hand faster than they imagined. Devastating Dreams is a very flexible board clearer, and should be high on your list any time you build a global sweeper deck.

One last thought here: Devastating Dreams, together with last week’s preview, Last Laugh. Enough said.
