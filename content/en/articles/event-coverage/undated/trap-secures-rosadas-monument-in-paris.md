## Event Coverage

![](https://i.ormos.uk/85/8f/858f3039dfa940939d414e3a12c6e29595fdf176095cc61bb918d749ef6d6502.jpg)

Grand Prix Paris has been a monumentous success, and for none more than Adrian Rosada. At 1,961 players, it was the biggest **Magic** tournament ever held, and after a total 6,767 matches over 19 rounds, he was left to hoist the trophy. The top 8 draft featured many new names and faces, but there was plenty of drama with powerful decks all around meaning that every match was hard fought, leading up to a truly thrilling final, where Rosada battled for his tournament life in one of the most exciting games of **Magic** that this coverage team has ever seen.

Sixteen have won a slot at Pro Tour-San Diego, but before that we now have only Grand Prix Minneapolis before the World Championships. While the Pro Player of the Year race didn’t change much this weekend, Lino Burgold’s strong finish has put him in third place in Rookie of the Year, just two points behind the leader. As a member of the German national team, a strong finish at Worlds for him could mean a great shot at the title.

Such a big event as this one has taken a huge effort from a massive number of people, and it has run incredibly smoothly even with such a throng of players. As the crowds around the arena disperse, we must bid you adieu, and once again congratulate Adrian Rosada, our Grand Prix-Paris 2009 Champion!

## Quarter Final – Lino Burgold vs. Gonzague Allouchery

*by Tobias Henke*

Lino Burgold from Germany is arguably the biggest name in this top 8. He is looking to add another Grand Prix trophy to the one he had received earlier this year at GP Hanover. His opponent, hometown hero Gonzague Allouchery, is bent on stopping him though, and his aggressive white and red deck might be able to trump Lino’s somewhat clunky red/green. On the other hand, Lino has proven time and again today to be able to win with the most absurd green decks. (Absurd in a good way as well as in bad way, that is.) We’ll see if his winning streak is coming to an end already.

Burgold thought long and hard about his opening hand. And a little bit longer. And then some. Finally he went for the mulligan, whereas Allouchery was fine with his seven. While Burgold’s six contained one [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) and a [Khalni Heart Expedition](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Khalni+Heart+Expedition) , one [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest) as the only land did not satisfy Burgold’s expectations. He instantly shuffled back his five, too.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7e/a7/7ea7fc46a3c1d27527c479e30f1d2c602d633e0451825d6d6efe591a64158ce9.jpg)

With four on the play and a deck that really needs all the resources it can get to power out some high-end casting-cost monsters, this game looked to be almost over before it had even started. The german player’s four, however, were excellent, with [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) on turn three being a nice match for Allouchery’s second-turn [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) . That is, until Allouchery laid [Scalding Tarn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scalding+Tarn) . The Frenchman also cast a less than impressive [Ondu Cleric](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ondu+Cleric) . On the other side of the battlefield, Burgold’s curve continued flawlessly with [Shatterskull Giant](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shatterskull+Giant) , but he did only have one card left in hand at that point.

Allouchery cast [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) and attached it to his Cleric, played another land, and when Lino declared no blocks, even sacrificed his [Scalding Tarn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scalding+Tarn) to come over for the full package of nine damage. Post-combat he also made a [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) , returning a land. Burgold went through his options. He decided [Shatterskull Giant](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shatterskull+Giant) might as well attack, putting Allouchery at 15, and passed the turn right back.

On the French players next attack [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) blocked a 2/3 [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) ; nevertheless Burgold took five damage. After combat Allouchery summoned [Kor Hookmaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Hookmaster) to keep [Shatter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shatter) skul Giant from untapping. Burgold passed without a play again, and when Allouchery’s next turn included a regular land drop as well as [Kor Cartographer](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Cartographer) , Burgold just nodded and sadly shuffled up his cards.

Lino Burgold 0 – 1 Gonzague Allouchery

For Game 2 Burgold’s hand was everything he would have liked it to be in Game 1. Four lands, nicely split between [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest) s and [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) s, [Oran-Rief Survivalist](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Survivalist) , [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) , and [Inferno Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Trap) .

Allouchery’s draw was not bad either, however, with [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) on turn one, followed by [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) on the second, [Ondu Cleric](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ondu+Cleric) on the third. Meanwhile, Burgold had cast his Ally, and a [Grazing Gladehart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grazing+Gladehart) . The latter attacked, gained life, and Burgold summoned [Greenweaver Druid](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Greenweaver+Druid) . Allouchery revealed to not have a third land, and [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) took out his 2/3 flier. Allouchery’s offense was put to a stop. A full stop. And his [Kor Sanctifiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Sanctifiers) off a third land were not going to change that and neither was [Kor Outfitter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Outfitter) .

Meanwhile, Burgold mustered an offense of his own, enchanting [Grazing Gladehart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grazing+Gladehart) with [Savage Silhouette](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Silhouette) . Life totals went to 16 to 15 in Burgold’s favor, and more life was to gained for Burgold, when he summoned [Frontier Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontier+Guide) , a deadly (or rather, lively) combination with [Grazing Gladehart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grazing+Gladehart) .

All of a sudden Allouchery attacked with his [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) , [Kor Outfitter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Outfitter) , and [Kor Sanctifiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Sanctifiers) . Outfitter and [Oran-Rief Survivalist](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Survivalist) traded, and Allouchery restocked with [Pillarfield Ox](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pillarfield+Ox) . The 4/4 [Grazing Gladehart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grazing+Gladehart) was not to be deterred though. [Baloth Woodcrasher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baloth+Woodcrasher) came down for Burgold and again Allouchery was forced to play an aggressive creature but stay on the defense, tapping down the Baloth with [Kor Hookmaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Hookmaster) , in fact simply delaying the inevitable.

[Murasa Pyromancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Murasa+Pyromancer) advanced the allied strategies on Allouchery’s side, shot [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) dead, and allowed [Tuktuk Grunts](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tuktuk+Grunts) to smash in as a 4/4 creature. Burgold just took the damage, used his [Frontier Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontier+Guide) at end of turn to get back to 14 life.

On his turn he attacked with [Baloth Woodcrasher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baloth+Woodcrasher) (with the help of yet another land drop being 8/8 trample already) as well as the [Savage Silhouette](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savage+Silhouette) ‘d [Grazing Gladehart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grazing+Gladehart) . The latter was blocked by [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) , the former by [Ondu Cleric](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ondu+Cleric) and [Pillarfield Ox](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pillarfield+Ox) . Being at eight life, even another land (via [Frontier Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frontier+Guide) ) would not have been able to kill Allouchery. The [Inferno Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Trap) Burgold had held in his hand from the very start, however, changed the math, so that one life turned into minus three and the score turned into...

## Quarter Final – Niels Viaene vs. Jeremy Dezani

*by Tim Willoughby*

Niels Viaene has an awkward relationship with mulligans. In the last round of the swiss, a game down, he found himself in a situation of taking a mulligan to 4. He somehow got himself out of that one, but found that in his very next match, here in the top 8, he was down to 5 cards on the play.

With a mono red deck, he was up against a more polychromatic concoction from his opponent, Frenchman Jeremy Dezani. Starting with an [Ondu Cleric](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ondu+Cleric) , Dezani had a [Disfigure](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disfigure) for the [Highland Berserker](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Highland+Berserker) that Viaene led with, making a [Teetering Peaks](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Teetering+Peaks) rather less special.

A [Marsh Flats](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Marsh+Flats) brought Dezani his second [Plains](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plains) , and soon thereafter a second [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) allowed a [Nimana Sell-Sword](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nimana+Sell-Sword) . In spite of this, Viaene was all about the aggro. He had been building a [Zektar Shrine Expedition](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Zektar+Shrine+Expedition) up, and on the turn that he fired it off, he also had a [Goblin Bushwhacker](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Bushwhacker) , who quickly got suited up with [Goblin War Paint](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+War+Paint) . The resultant attack put Dezani to 10, but when a [Pillarfield Ox](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pillarfield+Ox) came down for the Frenchman, it looked like he had weathered the storm, sitting as he was with bigger creatures on the board. A [Goblin Ruinblaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Ruinblaster) from Viaene looked a little outclassed, and a [Journey to Nowhere](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Journey+to+Nowhere) from Dezani soon meant that it was the only creature in play for Niels.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/04/1b/041b9c59a405e5effe8c2527bc11a5ab8efe94aebb195fdb26e194dbab54b171.jpg)*Niels the Mulli-gunner prepares to shoot down his opponent with his mono red deck*

A few swift attacks from Dezani ended things. In spite of his mulligans due to not having enough lands, he had ultimately been a little flooded in the first game.

Niels Viaene 0 – 1 Jeremy Dezani

Niels led off in Game 2 with [Quest for Pure Flame](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Quest+for+Pure+Flame) , which Dezani had to have a careful read of. (Burgold had indeed removed all [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) s from his deck at this point.)

[Kazandu Refuge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kazandu+Refuge) opened for Burgold, netting him an early life point bonus. [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) turn one threatened early retaliation for that affront, and Landfall duly sent Burgold to nineteen. With [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) up next for Ecker, a 4/5 threat loomed in the guise of a humble 0/1. A bit like [Tarmogoyf](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tarmogoyf) , really.

[Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest) and [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island) joined the Refuge for Burgold without him adding anything to the board, but end of turn [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) thinned his library and set him ahead on mana. [Merfolk Seastalkers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Seastalkers) and [Expedition Map](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Expedition+Map) was his turn, but [Kor Hookmaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Hookmaster) – a card that had served Ecker well throughout the Top 8 to date – meant that the [Steppe Lynx](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steppe+Lynx) could reduce Burgold to eleven. The [Merfolk Seastalkers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Seastalkers) stemmed the bleeding the following turn, while Ecker threatened to take to the skies with [Shepherd of the Lost](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shepherd+of+the+Lost) .

Burgold offered up a [Timbermaw Larva](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Timbermaw+Larva) , which, like so much in Zendikar, prefers to be attacking rather than blocking. [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) found a new home on Ecker’s [Shepherd of the Lost](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shepherd+of+the+Lost) , and Landfall meant Burgold took another five, and stood in dire peril at six life. Ecker cast another flyer in [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) , and put Burgold in even more trouble with mountainwalker [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) . Time was running out for the Grand Prix Hanover Champion, and in a lengthy discussion with himself, Burgold tried to construct a way out of his predicament.

Part One of whatever plan that was involved Grazing Gladeheart and a two point life bump via Landfall. Part Two involved, rather worryingly, passing the turn. Part Three? Take seven damage, fall to one, and see another [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) hit play.

There was no Part Four.

Danny Ecker 2 – 0 Lino Burgold.

## Final Match - Danny Ecker vs. Adrian Rosada

*by Tobias Henke*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/59/e159bcbfd4a12ceb5428ee2a5a88a945c2d71428995fa7e903ed24db7f78372f.jpg)*GP Paris 2009 finals match: Ecker (Left) vs. Rosada (Right)*

1,961 players entered the tournament early on Saturday, two made it this far. And within the hour we are going to have a champion, whether it’s Danny Ecker with his hyper-aggressive white and red deck or Adrian Rosada with the green and black cards.

Ecker started off quick with [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) , [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) , and [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) , whereas Rosada made his first play on turn three, in the form of [Vampire Hexmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vampire+Hexmage) . That fended off [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) for now, but couldn’t stop [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) which took on [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) to put Rosada at 14. [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) was passed from Skyfisher to Threader this time, and made Rosada double-block the [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) . Ecker did have [Burst Lightning](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burst+Lightning) , though, to take out the [Hex](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hex) mage before damage, killing the 1/4 free of charge. He also had a second [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) . [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) gave Rosada a solid blocker, provided Ecker did not have another land or, for instance, a [Windborne Charge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windborne+Charge) ! The white sorcery turned both [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) s into 4/3 monsters. Rosada simply blocked [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) , and took the eight damage, falling to four.

Meanwhile, [Oracle of Mul Daya](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oracle+of+Mul+Daya) was severely malfunctioning on Rosada’s side, never revealing land. Rosada, without play but with some amount of frustration, passed the turn back to Rosada who cast [Goblin Shortcutter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Shortcutter) , came over for six damage and played [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) .

Once again no land for Rosada and no play either. He did have [Hideous End](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+End) waiting, as previously broadcast on Radio Mul Daya. Ecker shot [Oran-Rief Recluse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oran-Rief+Recluse) with [Magma Rift](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Magma+Rift) and went in with his team. Oracle and [Goblin Shortcutter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Shortcutter) traded, [Kor Skyfisher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Skyfisher) received [Hideous End](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+End) , and [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) and [Kor Aeronaut](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Aeronaut) came in for four, leaving Rosada at eight.

Rosada rebounded with [Joraga Bard](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Joraga+Bard) , while Ecker once again had the trump card in [Kor Hookmaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Hookmaster) . Another four damage for Rosada. He was not letting go, though, and summoned [Tajuru Archer](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tajuru+Archer) , to shoot down [Kor Aeronaut](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Aeronaut) , and have a blocker at the ready for [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) . Ecker, however, simply attacked with [Kor Hookmaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Hookmaster) , which Rosada chose not to chump-block. (Two life left.)

Ecker made [Kor Sanctifiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Sanctifiers) , Rosada [Vampire Hexmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vampire+Hexmage) . Next Ecker played and used [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) to turn his [Kor Sanctifiers](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kor+Sanctifiers) into a 4/5 monster, but Rosada’s [Vines of Vastwood](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vines+of+Vastwood) kicked in to turn his blocker into something even larger. (Still, two life left.)

Ecker had another [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) , Rosada [Timbermaw Larva](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Timbermaw+Larva) . The next couple of turns saw Ecker euipping, and subsequently boosting, creature after creature with [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) . The only problem was, Ecker was losing creatures and kept drawing lands. Meanwhile [Timbermaw Larva](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Timbermaw+Larva) started attacking for some non-trivial amount of damage. And when [Baloth Cage Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Baloth+Cage+Trap) surprsisingly traded with an [Adventuring Gear](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Adventuring+Gear) -powered [Cliff Threader](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cliff+Threader) , Rosada was even able to finish things off rather quickly with [Eldrazi Monument](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eldrazi+Monument) .

Danny Ecker 1 – 1 Adrian Rosada

![](https://i.ormos.uk/12/d6/12d673e086e2a22c691a1867092823fdf8d6538d4fa11ccc176b5699c7285ca1.jpg)*Adrian Rosada has one man left to beat*

Ecker went first for the final game. And went to five. Cards, that is. He had [Blazing Torch](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blazing+Torch) on turn one, but no two-drop, or three-drop. Meanwhile, Rosada had a nice curve of [Blood Seeker](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Seeker) , [Vampire Hexmage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vampire+Hexmage) , [Hideous End](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hideous+End) for Ecker’s first creature, and [Territorial Baloth](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Territorial+Baloth) .

He followed up on that with [Oracle of Mul Daya](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808204514/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oracle+of+Mul+Daya) and this time around the Oracle hit land. The 8/8 Baloth charged in and simply won the game and match and tournament right there and then, on turn six.

Congratulations to Adrian Rosada, champion of Grand Prix Paris 2009, the biggest tournament in the history of the Game!
