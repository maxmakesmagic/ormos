Regionals are coming up soon and I assume that you want to qualify for your National Championship. Since *Onslaught* became legal, the only big tournament was the Masters Series in Chicago. Last week, I talked about the Wake deck that I ran there and how the event went. I play quite a bit of Standard on ***Magic** Online* and I don’t think that the Wake deck is a good choice for the current environment any more. After the Masters, Jeff Cunningham and Peter Szigeti wrote articles on green-blue madness and suggested running a Ray of Revelation maindeck along with Upheavals in the sideboard. On ***Magic** Online*, people listened and the matchup got worse; Upheaval is something you really don’t want to see. Right now the metagame on ***Magic** Online* looks like this:

**Tier 1:**

1.  Psychatog
2.  Green-blue madness
3.  Green-red beatdown
4.  Astral Slide/Lightning Rift

**Tier 2:**

1.  Black control
2.  Sligh
3.  Black-red Reanimator
4.  Squirrel Opposition
5.  Beasts

I heard there were a few guys crazy enough to play what is called Tight Sight in the Masters Gateway, a blue-green deck based around Future Sight and a win condition with Early Harvest, Predict and Krosan Reclamation. If you have some rating points to donate or just want to have fun with a wacky deck, you might want to give it a try. But as this article is meant to help you to prepare for Regionals, so I’ll skip that truly awful deck.

Online, I generally only play against the mentioned tier 1 decks anyway. I’ve seen all of the tier 2 stuff here and there very infrequently. There is a small difference between online Standard and the Regionals format obviously – *Legions* won’t be avaible on ***Magic** Online* for another two weeks. The good news is that *Legions* will hardly impact the format, in my opinion. I have heard from several people that there is a black beatdown deck that features Graveborn Muse and Withered Wretch. I haven’t played that myself, but I was told that it is good but not great. The problem matchups for this deck are green-red and cycling decks. Not being able to deal with Astral Slide and Lightning Rift is a serious problem and Firebolt, Fiery Temper and especially Violent Eruption aren’t great news for a creature-based strategy, especially when there is a Compost in play and, suddenly, all those removal spells are cantrips.

If I had to play in Regionals tomorrow, I would go with either Psychatog or green-red beatdown. Both decks are very solid. Psychatog doesn’t have a really awful matchup while green-red has trouble with both the cycling deck and its nightmare matchup: Beasts. It is good against Psychatog and green-blue madness. It also has a great matchup against Sligh and a pretty good one vs. Squirrel Opposition.

Going into the Masters Series, Dirk Baberowski and Marco Blume both knew they were going to play green-red pretty early on in testing and concentrated on that. The result is this deck they ended up running:

The maindeck is pretty straightforward. You can’t go with less then 22 lands with cards like Violent Eruption and Call of the Herd in the deck. We were playing Reckless Charge for most of the time in testing but eventually decided that it wasn’t good enough. All decks either have sufficient blockers or enough instant removal to counter it. Elephant Guide, on the other hand, is an excellent card. There are only a few good answers to turn 2 Wild Mongrel and turn 3 Elephant Guide. The card is needed, especially against blue-green in game 1, when you don’t have access to Ensnaring Bridge and have to fight past Arrogant Wurms and Roar of the Wurm tokens.

Seton’s Scout is the only card that catches immediate attention, as every other non-land card is included four times. Admittedly, the card is not very good, but you can only play four Mongrels and you need to do something on turn 2. Some players chose to run Blistering Firecat over a two-drop, as all existent creatures for two mana are just not very good. *Legions* solves that problem, as it gives the deck access to Skirk Marauder. 2/1 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) is okay on its own, obviously not great, but being able to cast it as a morph and deal two extra damage in mid or late game makes it far superior to anything else avaible. I wouldn’t change anything in the maindeck other then replacing the Scouts with Marauders.

The one thing that has to be changed quite a bit is the sideboard. We knew that there were a ton of Psychatog decks around and Dirk and Marco built their sideboard accordingly. I don’t think that you need that much hate. I am not even sure if it was such a good idea for them to run that much. When you want to board in nine cards, you obviously have to remove the same number and the deck itself is set up quite well against ‘Tog. Also adding five cards that cost four mana is not exactly a good thing, as it pushes the mana curve way over the top. I don’t think that those Boils are really needed. You won’t get more then three islands anyway and you probably have to wait until turn 5 or later to achieve that. If they have two or three other lands in play, you won’t harm them all that much. If you resolve something for four mana, it could as well be a Phantom Centaur, which is a lot more powerful. If that guy hits play, all you need to do is to keep some guys for Edicts and Bloods around while the Centaur does its work.

Another excellent sideboard card against ‘Tog is Compost. Most ‘Tog versions run at least four Smothers and four Chainer’s Edicts and most of them also have either Innocent Blood or Ghastly Demise. One thing you have to know is that if you resolve a Compost or two, you need to change the way you play this matchup. If there is no Compost in play, it is a good idea to point your burn at your opponent’s head and try to build up enough creatures to attack past a blocking Psychatog. If you resolve a Compost, it is a superior plan to fight against the Psychatog, as long as your opponent doesn’t have too many cards in hand and graveyard. That way, you force him to pitch cards from his hand. Eventually he either has to let the ‘Tog go, which is great news as you also get to draw a card, or he has to pitch those black cards he kept back to minimize the damage from Compost. That turns your cards into cantrips and you should win the exhaustion war. If you play it the other way, chances are that he can use his life total as a resource and build up on lands to go for an Upheaval while your Compost doesn’t really accomplish anything. If you just draw one or two cards with it, it was pretty useless.

The most important card in green-red’s sideboard is Ensnaring Bridge though. This little gem turns the matchup against blue-green from even to pretty favorable. Most people don’t realize that they need to board in Naturalize and, even if they do, you are not guaranteed to draw a Bridge, which leaves the Naturalize in their hand useless as you should board out the Elephant Guides here. Now, if you happen to draw it, they need the answer pretty fast as the green-red deck has enough burn to win the game rather quickly.

We had the most trouble against the cycling deck when sideboarding. We tried to board Pillages and Stone Rains to prevent them from casting Wrath, Teroh’s Faithful and Exalted Angel, but against a lot of cheap cycling, that plan just doesn’t work out often enough. The next thing we tried was boarding in Lightning Rift, but that turned out to be crap as well. They would just cast their Renewed Faiths, Teroh’s Faithful and Wraths of God and sit back until they dropped the Angel for ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif) and there is not much green-red can do about that. A ton of enchantment removal didn’t work for the same reason. They don’t really need their cycling stuff. Wrath, life gain and the Angel just beat you on their own. The solution might be in *Legions*: Caller of the Claw. If this card is any good, then it has to be great against a deck packing both Wrath of God and Slice and Dice, along with more removal in Lightning Rift. I am not sure if it is that good against ‘Tog, as you shouldn’t get more then one token on average, but it has to be great against any version of Wake and the cycling deck. I think three Callers of the Claw is a good number. The last two cards should be Naturalizes. Being able to get rid of Astral Slide here and there is nice and you might run up against a Wake deck or a red deck designed around Ensnaring Bridge. Playing green and not having access to two or more Naturalizes in the board just seems wrong to me.

Oh, and just one more comment: green-red with Anger is just absolutely awful. If you get turn 2 Wild Mongrel, you are in a great shape anyway with this deck. But if the Anger deck doesn’t draw the Mongrel, it loses to everything. Turn 4 Anger and turn 5 Arrogant Wurm is NOT impressive.

And now to my favorite deck these days: Psychatog. The version I brought with me to Chicago isn’t very good right now. It had both Standstill and Chain of Vapor and only one Compulsion in the maindeck. That design is excellent against green-red, but I tested too much against that because Dirk and Marco were playing it all the time. There were three versions played in the Masters Series that looked good to me. Ben Stark and Neil Reeves ran the deck that Ben used to win the Gateway. While their design does have all the tools, I don’t think that 24 lands are enough; there should be at least 25. Also, having only three Deep Analyses that provide you with real card advantage don’t seem to be enough. I think the correct way to go is a mix of the decks played by Dave Humphreys and Antoine and Olivier Ruel.

The major problem I have with Antoine’s decklist is the use of too many weird lands, which is clearly a tribute to his Frenchieness. Cycling lands are good, but there are clearly too many. I am also not a huge fan of the Coliseum, although they might be playing that as they expected a lot of Psychatog and probably figured that the pain should not be important at all. But with Underground River, Polluted Delta and Deep Analysis in the deck, I think you have to draw a line at some point. Dave’s deck, on the other hand, looks very solid except for the fact that he chose to run the Cunning Wish targets in the maindeck, instead of running the Wish itself. He does run Compulsions, which seems pretty important to me right now. If you resolve one of those in the Psychatog mirror, your opponent doesn’t have one and you don’t miss land drops early on, it is really tough to lose.

The only time you actually want to cast this guy on your third turn is against green-red and sligh. Those two decks are not reason enough to maindeck the fourth.

The two Edicts could also be one Edict and a Blood, but in the matchups where you board it in, other than against blue-green, you also want to board in the fourth ‘Tog and I don’t really want to cast a Psychatog on turn 3 and then draw into Innocent Bloods. Another good choice would be more Ghastly Demises, but as I mentioned, there might be a black beatdown deck and against that, I’d rather have the Edicts. The Duresses should be boarded in against anything that doesn’t cast creatures on turns 1 and 2. The Persecute is pretty much another Upheaval, just cheaper. If you resolve that in the mirror, it should be game over. Deep Analysis also costs four mana and when they cast that, they give you an opening for Persecute quite often. It is also nice against black control, as they play the waiting game most of the time and try to overload your countermagic by casting multiple spells in one turn. That plan is not such a great idea if you draw that Persecute…
