You decide</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Frank Karsten is an interesting candidate for Resident Genius on this week Invitational ballot. He is one of the few candidates there because of his reputation as a Limited thinker as well as his skills as a deck designer and player. Frank's Limited skills first came to light on the Pro level when he reached the Top 8 of Nagoya with his fabled List for <a>Champions of Kamigawa</a>. He had rated every single card in the block in order on the flight. He continued to tinker with the document until it looked more like the route Billy from Family Circus took to go straight to school than an orderly list. </p>&#10;      <p>Yesterday when I spoke to Frank about his Sealed Deck strategy he promised to explain his method for card sorting that helped him to analyze his card pool. When I walked in this morning Frank was hunched over a scrap of paper scribbling what looked like perhaps a chemical formula. It turns out that it was a graph representing the twelve piles he sets out when breaking out his deck. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I thought people might want to use this method at the PTQs,&quot; Frank explained, &quot;This way they can see all the color combinations next to each other.&quot;</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="13"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Feb. 5: 11:02 a.m. - Cats and Dogs Living Together…Mass Hysteria!</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gprich06/b_walls.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Kenji Tsumura, left, and Gabe Walls prepare to Draft in Pod 1</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>&quot;I object to this draft pod!&quot; exclaimed Ken Krouner with a laugh. &quot;Can someone tell people that the last draft pod is not supposed to be harder than the first one?&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>That was a variation on a theme that everyone was talking about this morning as we got ready for the first draft. While I would not exactly deem the first pod - which featured reigning Player of the Year Kenji Tsumura and Grand Prix Philadelphia winner Jon Sonne - a slam dunk for anyone 'fortunate' enough to be seated there, it was not as chock full of names as table eight. </p>&#10;      <p>Draft Table Eight</p>&#10;      <p>Eugene Harvey<br> Katsuhiro Mori<br> Kyle Smith<br> Zach Parker<br> Frank Karsten<br> Nicholas Neary<br> Patrick Sullivan<br> Ken Krouner</p>&#10;      <p>Katsuhiro Mori and Frank Karsten were destined for a first round rematch of the finals of the 2005 World Championships. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="14"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Feb. 5: 11:40 a.m. - Draft one: Gabe Walls and Kenji Tsumura</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Trophy Hunter" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Trophy%20Hunter&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Before the draft Kenji and Gabe were talking about their draft preferences. Kenji expressed an affection for the five-color archetype in this new format and when Gabe found himself sitting to Kenji's left he knew he was going to go blue. His decision was made even easier by a decidedly mediocre first pack - he took <a>Clutch of the Undercity</a> passing almost nothing. </p>&#10;      <p>Kenji had a tougher time with his first pick. He isolated <a>Trophy Hunter</a>, <a>Brainspoil</a>, <a>Shambling Shell</a>, and <a>Vinelasher Kudzu</a> as the cards on his plate and finally settled on the Hunter. Gabe happily took the <a>Brainspoil</a> and the subsequent <a>Stinkweed Imp</a> that Kenji passed him after the Japanese player took <a>Bramble Elemental</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Things got a little tricky with Kenji's third pick when he veered into black for <a>Brainspoil</a> over <a>Overwhelm</a> and <a>Transluminant</a> creating some overlap between his and Gabe's colors. Kenji did consider white briefly when his next pack offered up Boros Guldmage and <a>Conclave Equenaut</a> - instead he took <a>Nullmage Shepherd</a>. He veered again with a fifth pick <a>Vedalken Dismisser</a>. <a>Hunted Dragon</a> was in the next pack but Kenji was prepared to let someone else take that bait and picked up <a>Sewerdreg</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Kenji continued to be Green-black-X in the second pack when he nabbed a second <a>Brainspoil</a> with his first pick. <a>Bramble Elemental</a> ended up in front of him despite being tempted by Circu, <a>Peel from Reality</a>, and <a>Remand</a>. He succumbed to the lure of blue when he was passed <a>Glimpse the Unthinkable</a> one card later. </p>&#10;      <p>Gabe was raking in blue ahead of him though with <a>Snapping Drake</a>, double <a>Compulsive Research</a>, and an impossibly late <a>Halcyon Glaze</a>. He could scarcely believe it as every blue card he passed - except for one Peel - came back around the table including two <a>Remand</a>s. </p>&#10;      <p>Guildpact seemed to give Kenji a splitting headache as he couldn't really decide what direction he wanted his deck to go in. He finally decided that Orhzov was going to be the road he followed and took a pair of <a>Blind Hunter</a>s sandwiching an <a>Ostiary Thrull</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Gabe was in perfect position for Izzet and first picked that color combination's Guildmage and just about any card he wanted in the common slot over the rest of the pack. He was of mixed emotion when he long-ranged <a>Izzet Chronarch</a> around the table. He was excited to get it back but sad that it was the only one at the table because he would have had them all.</p>&#10;      <p>Kenji was miserable after the draft and did not feel like he could even win a match with his draft deck. </p>&#10; He has once again put himself in contention for another spot on the Tour, needing only a Top 16 finish to earn a trip to Prague in May. Feeding John for this draft is Gerry Thompson, an outstanding Grand Prix player whose skills never quite seem to carry him to Pro Tour glory.</p>&#10;      <p>Back at Worlds, the thing players dreaded the most was drafting a Boros deck next to one of their neighbors. This was thought to guarantee both players a 1-2 record or worse, submarining one's chances at big money, no whammies. Since that time, however, Sam Gomersall managed to win a thousand-person Grand Prix by being the only Boros drafter at his table, making that most derided of archetypes considerably more inviting. However, the question still remains: Can you post a good record by sharing Boros cards with your neighbor?</p>&#10;      <p>Fiorillo got the classic <a>Mark of Eviction</a> vs. <a>Selesnya Evangel</a> pick for pack 1, taking the Mark. Fiorillo then took the Thundersong out of that pack.</p>&#10;      <p>The next pack saw Fiorillo pause over a <a>Watchwolf</a> that Thompson hadn't given a second thought, while Thompson busied himself going heavy Boros, snagging a <a>Screeching Griffin</a>, with Fiorillo taking a Vedalker Dismisser.</p>&#10;      <p>By pack 6, Fiorillo had fallen to the full hook, snagging whatever leftover Boros cards Thompson didn't take, in this case a <a>Skyknight Legionnaire</a>. As for Thompson, he had the makings of a very quick Boros deck that looked to have some solid late game as well.</p>&#10;      <p>Fiorillo's first pick in pack 2 was the same as pack 1, as was Thompson's, giving Fiorillo a pair of Marks now while Thompson had two Helixes (taking his second over <a>Faith's Fetters</a>).
