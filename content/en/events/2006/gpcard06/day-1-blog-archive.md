My nefarious plan to kidnap all the other reporters so they send me off to exotic climes like Manila has backfired. As there's no one else available I also have to cover slightly less exotic locations like … urm Cardiff. Oh well maybe I should have left some food for Hanno and Jörn before I flew off to the Philippines last week.</p>&#10;      <p>On the subject of food, one of the things that's different for this tournament is that the judging crew and staff have all been given a rather nice snack box to keep them going for the weekend. Hold a tournament in London and the guys from the North don't travel down. Hold a tournament outside London and the London guys don't travel to it. Given the exorbitant cost of our public transport, this isn't exactly surprising and the British Grand Prix are traditionally the next smallest after the Scandinavian tournaments.</p>&#10;      <p>370 is actually very low by European standards. Does this mark a malaise in British magic? I went rooting through the past country breakdowns to check the numbers.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcard06/b2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>This is what it looked like at deck registration</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>GP Cardiff 2006<br> ENG 208<br> WAL 36<br> SCO 24<br> IRL 7</p>&#10;      <p>GP Nottingham 2005<br> ENG 197<br> WAL 9<br> SCO 21<br> IRL 16</p>&#10;      <p>GP Birmingham 2004<br> ENG 387<br> WAL 10<br> SCO 33<br> IRL 18</p>&#10;      <p>GP London 2003<br> ENG 177<br> WAL 10<br> SCO 8<br> IRL 12</p>&#10;      <p>With the exception of the bumper turnout at Birmingham the British attendance has been fairly constant.</p>&#10;      <p>However…<br> GP London 2003<br> FRA 107</p>&#10;      <p>GP Birmingham 2004<br> FRA 25</p>&#10;      <p>GP Nottingham 2005<br> FRA 49</p>&#10;      <p>GP Cardiff 2006<br> FRA 13</p>&#10;      <p>Could it be that the French have been driven off by the English limited prowess (Oh dear it appears that my patriotic circuits have just been blown by my reality processor)</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, March 25: 2:09 pm - Hard to Get Byes</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcard06/b3.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Ben Ronaldson</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Grand Prix Byes aren't as easy to get as they used to be. A quick scan of the round one pairings saw a few names I wouldn't have expected to see in action this early. Richard Edbury is still probably the most famous Welsh player, despite making London his home now. Back in the day Edbury was part of the Palace crew that caused such a stir in constructed a few years back. Also in action from the Palace (and the guy who gave it the name as he lives in one) is Ben Ronaldson. Ronaldson was once named the best player never to make a PT top 8 by Scott Johns. But the Palace guys have been out of circulation for a while now and will find themselves scrapping it out right from the start off. Ben told me that they tend not to play much constructed nowadays, but he has drafted a lot recently. Maybe this weekend we'll see them make a comeback.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, March 25: 2:47 pm - Local players to watch</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcard06/b4.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Welsh National Team Captain - Madog Wiliams</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Welcome, welcome. It's that time of the day when I turn the spotlight on the local names to watch. As it's a British Grand Prix I would be talking about the usual names such as David Grant, Stuart Wright, Quentin Martin and fellows (But not Sam Gomersall - I'm guessing he's off to the team GP in Madison).</p>&#10;      <p>But as we're in Cardiff I think it's only fair I actually focus on the Welsh section of the British player base.</p>&#10;      <p>First off I have to mention Richard Edbury. Edbury is probably the most successful Welsh player there's ever been. While he doesn't play so much nowadays (the reason he doesn't have any byes for this tournament) Edbury managed to rack up 45 pro points from the 12 Pro Tours he played in.</p>&#10;      <p>Also a worth a mention is last year's Welsh National team captain Madog Williams (if he doesn't get nobbled by Jules Jardine on another mammoth drinking session). Simon Cox was also a member of the same team that traveled to Japan.</p>&#10;      <p>I'll round off my picks with a couple of names that have been familiar faces on the PTQ circuit for the past few years: Martin Lawrence and Roy Williams. If there's any new up-and-coming Welshman I've passed over, I apologize. Go prove me wrong and win this tournament.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="5"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, March 25: 3:34 pm - Vote For Fan Favourite</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Since there's been some voting irregularities (translation: Swap busted the system) the vote for the Invitational Fan Favorite is back online.</p>&#10;      <p>Anyone wishing to place their vote for their favorite player should head off over <a href="/en/events/2006/mi06/dave-williams-and-pierre-canali-are-the-fan-favorites">here</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>I am of course supposed to remain impartial in all of this and couldn't possibly mention my choice. (I'm sorry Geoffrey, please put the gun away).</p>&#10;      <p>Actually, if you're unsure, personally I'd probably recommend Pierre Canali, Shu Komuro, Gadiel Szleifer or Geoffrey Siron as all of these guys won a Pro Tour last year and aren't currently going. But then again this is the fan favorite, so just vote for the guy you think will contribute the most to the Invitational.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="6"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, March 25: 5:47 pm - Sealed Deck Construction with Frank Karsten</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcard06/b5.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Frank Karsten builds his deck</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Okay so technically Frank built this about four hours ago. That's one of the problems with local Grand Prix, so many people to chat to, so many <a>Lightning Helix</a> to sign (actually I'm lying about the latter part, but I can dream okay. *sob*).</p>&#10;      <p>When it comes to deck construction I always go to my own personal Resident Genius, Mr Frank Karsten, to shine a light on the intricacies of sealed deck construction. Karsten is one of the top Dutch pro's and known for having very methodical methods for tackling Limited.</p>&#10;      <p>I walked over and as you can see from the photo Frank had already arranged his cards into the grid system that allows him to easily spot the interactions between his guild cards.</p>&#10;      <p>I've remarked before that Ravnica-Guildpact sealed deck is fantastically difficult to build. On this occasion Karsten's deck looked remarkably straightforward. Red (with a <a>Flame Fusillade</a>), green and blue looked the strongest colors. Black had a couple of nice removal in <a>Last Gasp</a> and <a>Douse in Gloom</a> and in the end Karsten splashed for it. White was easy to discard as the only good mono-white card was <a>Oathsworn Giant</a> and the other good white cards were in white-black, a color that wasn't much stronger than a splash.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcard06/b6.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>The Grid!</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The only card that raised an eyebrow for me was the <a>Barbarian Riftcutter</a>.
