### You'd be amazed what seven mana can buy

You've been there: you're playing blue, you have a Confiscate in hand and ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) available…but you just can't choose.

Should you take the Thorn Elemental from player A? The Maze of Ith from player B? The Subversion from player D? Or the Erratic Portal from player E? (You're player C.)

Well, why not pay ![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) more and take all of them?

Blatant Thievery easily ranks among the top five multiplayer cards from *Onslaught* – no small feat for a 350-card set that has some ferocious group bombs in it. However many opponents you have, that's how many permanents you get – for good, without any fragile enchantment clinging onto it. Does the overlooked *Urza's Saga* card Brand actually get good again? Not necessarily; but you're going to make a few of your friends consider it for day or so after you soak them with this.

Like [Spelljack](/en/articles/seriousfun/2002/05-07-you-dont-know-jack) in *Judgment*, Blatant Thievery is limited only by your opponents' imagination. The flip side of that is, it's not easy to build a deck "around" a theft card, since you don't know what your opponents are going to do.

The Vineyards, Benefactors, and Explorers give all players the chance to find and cast the spells they want – we're hoping for permanents here. (Nasty instants and sorceries will require Counterspell, and since you only have four, expect to lose some games this way. Where you don't have the rares I list, consider countermagic ranging from Dismiss to Hesitation to Misdirection.) Hunted Wumpus and Charmed Griffin tempt your opponents to lay down their best shots. Vodalian Illusionists protect your new acquisitions, if they're creatures, from bounce or other removal. And the cephalids are there to whip through excess mana – this deck uses a lot of it.

It's easy to imagine a turn four or five Blatant Thievery, with the accelerants in the deck. But you have enough creatures to suppose you'll have much more time than that.

This deck will have trouble with untargetables such as Multani, Maro-Sorcerer and Zephid; but when you're stealing five or six other cards, perhaps you can find something helpful, like a Fog Bank or Soldevi Sentry, to keep those animals at bay.

A deck like this may be particularly brutal in team play, where your teammates benefit from your wumpi and griffins, but do not get hurt by your thievery.

Beyond this path and a few variants, though, Blatant Thievery is just fine as a single-copy "wacky" card that you throw into a deck or two to wreak havoc. Nothing wrong with that. But no matter how you play it, you'll face a couple of questions. Will you have the right answers?

### HOW DO I CHOOSE WHAT TO STEAL?

There are different ways to approach mass stealing. You can align all of your pickpocketing choices with one of the criteria below, or mix 'n match – after all, you'll have plenty of targets.

*   <table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="Blatant Thievery" src="https://i.ormos.uk/3e/38/3e3844cf4dc1f5f3fd12ca070f407e3f1a638ffa25e42c782d5247ebc3d54288.jpg"><br><i>Gimme, gimme, and gimme.</i></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

    **The largest threats (offensive approach)** – take the fattest creatures, nastiest enchantments, and biggest body-smashing artifacts that can take you to victory. (Think Beast of Burden, Pyromancy, or Sliver Queen.) This will get plenty of negative attention, but exactly what will your opponents do about it?
*   **The largest threats (defensive approach)** – if you already have a decent path to victory, you can choose those permanents that look the most like they'll get in your way. (Think Grave Pact; Tangle Wire; or Aboshan, Cephalid Emperor.) Leaving a Delraich under another player's control sends a statement – your Delraich doesn't worry me. (Don't think it says, "please don't attack me, buddy" 'cause that's not going to work. If you don't have an answer for it, take the Delraich while you can!)
*   **The subtle things.** Does Player A have a single mountain? (It may be a better idea to color-screw him, than take a minor creature like a Patrol Hound.) Does Player B have an Elephant Guide on an otherwise killable creature? (If you control the enchantment, *you* get the token.) Did player C just play a Cradle Guard with only three lands in play? (Take a land, watch the creature die on her next upkeep.) Blatant Thievery may seem flashy…but remember, it's still *blue*. It's okay to be clever.
*   **The sackables.** While it may seem counterintuitive to attempt to steal, say, a Pernicious Deed or Seal of Doom or Bloodfire Colossus, these choices make sense more times than you may think. Blatant Thievery doesn't have to be about *getting* the permanent…it can be about *getting rid of* the permanent. To illustrate: if a *Legacy's Allure* is on the table with nine counters on it and the same player has Draco, it doesn't make sense to take anything but the Allure, and force the controller to blow it on something else. (If two different players own these permanents, you're probably in a fix. According to the soon-to-be-published FAQ, "If one of the permanents you target changes controller before Blatant Thievery resolves, you won't gain control of that permanent." That means gaining control of Draco is next to impossible.)\*
*   **The stuff you can't use.** A bit like sackables, this includes stuff like Parallax Wave (which can be rendered harmless), Child of Gaea (which you may not be able to upkeep), Legacy Weapon (which you probably can't activate), and a hoard of other stuff that you just want to keep out of someone else's hands. Don't be embarrassed to take this stuff if it's cramping your style – but don't just take it because it "seems powerful". Be strategic.
*   **The funniest stuff.** Or *don’t* be strategic…see if I care. Target a Howling Mine, just because you think it works better when it's sitting closer to you. Target a Gaea's Cradle, even though you're playing a creatureless deck. Target a Sleeper Agent, in a brash attempt to deal three damage per round to the previous controller instead of two.

### DO I TAKE WHAT'S BEHIND DOOR #2?

One of the burning questions you ought to be asking in this age of *Onslaught* is, "What about that [morph](/en/articles/seriousfun/2002/09-10-metamorpho-whoa) over there?" For purposes of this question, I assume you haven't seen the card face-up yet, and that the controller has shown evidence of multiple types of morphs that it could reasonably be.

It's certainly reasonable to take a morph that seems likely to be blue, since you'll be able to morph it yourself. Of course, opponents are unlikely to morph their creatures in response to Thievery for your convenience, so you'll probably be stuck with that 2/2 if you pick it. But consider the lovely concept of opportunity cost: would you rather have a "dumb" 2/2 creature, or face down an Exalted Angel?

While it's interesting to compare such a morph to a known quantity that same player may control (say, Serra Angel), the comparison becomes even more intriguing when you've got two unknown morphs. If you've got a choice of morphs, and nothing else interesting to take, how do you choose?

Break it down as much as possible. Which did the player play first? (This must remain public knowledge, remember.) The one played first may be less desirable, since the controller's been willing to risk it as a 2/2 for longer. Also, has the player attacked or blocked with either one recently? How much mana has she left herself for the past few turns? And so on.

If Blatant Thievery and morphs together have taught us anything, it's that *Onslaught* is going to test our memories and our knowledge – will you take the time to learn all the morphs, and their morph costs? Will you cross-reference that knowledge with your memory of what lands each morph-controlling opponents has left open for, say, the past two rounds?

I'm not saying you *have* to do that stuff. But I *am* saying that the better your memory and knowledge are, the less chance you'll have of pulling a Headhunter, and the more chance you'll have of pulling a Grinning Demon.

### ARE MY SLEEVES THE SAME COLOR?

Sounds like a dumb question, but please check as Blatant Thievery resolves, and at the end of the game.

I was just playing a deck last night that used a "takeaway" mechanic, and to my alarm drew an absolutely unplayable Phantom Nantuko that a friend of mine had in the same, nice, shiny, white sleeves I used. This is mildly funny; but it's also a little embarrassing (and, of course, illegal in tournaments whether you meant to do it or not). Do be careful, at the end of any game where you have gained control of, removed from game, or otherwise displaced other people's cards. After all, they're not *your* cards.

Not once the game ends, anyway. In the meantime…enjoy them!
