Noticeably near the top are a bunch of rare cards—the planeswalkers, of course, as well as cards like <a>Profane Command</a> and <a>Mirror Entity</a>. Uncommons even get the nod, since <a>Shriekmaw</a> is so good. Cards like these change the game immediately, and players respect their power. Also swimming amidst the school of powerful bomb rares is an unlikely candidate. It's not as flashy, it clearly isn't as hard to get, and yet it's every bit as powerful as some of these rares can be. <p></p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Mulldrifter</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Yeah, I said it. The flying fish isn't anything spectacular, and yet it's strong enough that most players consider it among the top ten cards in the set. I even saw it go over a <a>Liliana Vess</a><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20130702130146/http://gatherer.wizards.com/magic/draftools/draftviewer.asp?draftid=2_15_2008_1&amp;player=6&amp;pack=1&amp;pick=1&amp;showpick=true"> during a draft</a> yesterday (a pick I don't really agree with, but the fact that it's even a consideration is a pretty strong statement). Quentin Martin perhaps said it best yesterday when I asked him what card he really wanted to see to send him down the path for a solid deck, and he said that all he wanted was <a>Mulldrifter</a>s, seas and seas of <a>Mulldrifter</a>s. Commons with that kind of respect and power level are fairly rare, to say the least.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, February 16: 11:49 a.m. - Embracing Local Customs</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Scott Johns</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <p>I'm pretty sure that, at first, the only thing I could think was, &quot;It must be the jet lag. This can't <i>actually</i> be happening.&quot; </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        </p><table>&#10;          <tbody><tr>&#10;            <td>&#10;              <div>&#10;                <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/86/fe/86fe0b09d0acea93a8b491393071db2e62c3fbe4ef9c02696eb30ff8ad92f50b.jpg">&#10;              </div>&#10;              <div>&#10;                <i>The fountain in front of the Petronas Towers is a lovely place for a Valentine's Day stroll.</i>&#10;              </div>&#10;            </td>&#10;          </tr>&#10;        </tbody></table> It was about this time, on a morning stroll below the breathtaking Petronas Towers, that a woman completely unknown to me was administering what can only be described as a &quot;very enthusiastic&quot; hug. Now, I was <i>completely</i> at a loss at this point why this woman was hugging me and I had no idea what to do. In fact, this was so unexpected that I basically just froze there. I mean, I'm pretty sure I've never found myself asking someone &quot;Why are you hugging me?&quot; In fact, as this went on a bit more, on some absurd level I began to worry that now, not only do I not know why I'm getting hugged, but I'm also now doing a pretty bad job of it myself, I'm so caught off guard. <p></p>&#10;      <p>After what probably wasn't anywhere near as long as it felt, the happy woman disengaged and shot me a big smile. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;You don't know why I did that!&quot; </p>&#10;      <p>I'm a nice guy, and on some level I'm now worried that I'm letting her down for whatever it is I'm missing, so I go with straight honesty. &quot;Yes, you are absolutely right!&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>As the nice lady goes on to explain, in Malaysia (or, perhaps, just Kuala Lumpur, I admit I wasn't clear on this part) it turns out that Valentine's Day is a very popular holiday, and many locals celebrate it as a way of showing love not just for family and those close to them, but for all people, even complete strangers. As she explained it, what's more in the spirit of Valentine's Day than just giving a big hug to somebody you've never met, just to be nice?</p>&#10;      <p>Of course, not everybody feels quite so open about this kind of thing. So, as this mercifully helpful woman explained, those who really love this kind of Valentine's Day make sure to wear red very prominently while out in public. That way you know who to hug, and those that don't want such treatment get to opt out (which, I can only imagine, is a very important part for this kind of thing to work). </p>&#10;      <p>As you can surely guess by this point, I was wearing a very red shirt on my morning stroll. I got another big hug from another woman a couple blocks down the path on the way back to the hotel, so at this point I'm going to assume there must be something to the story. If anyone knows more, please make sure to enlighten us on the message boards because I admit I was mystified the rest of the day trying to figure out the best way to proceed. Afraid of messing something up, I switched to a blue shirt for the second half of the day, but that will definitely be a morning walk I won't forget any time soon!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, February 16: 2:06 p.m./1:06 a.m. Draft Viewing from a Distance</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Brian David-Marshall</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <p>I am going crazy here. I am going stir crazy and my wife is hiding all my <a>Lave Axe</a>s. </p>&#10;      <p>There is a Pro Tour going on, I am not there, Jon Finkel is tearing it up, and there is some Earth Two version of me and Randy in the Tournament Center. Did anyone else spit their morning coffee all over the computer screen when they <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20130702130146/http://gatherer.wizards.com/magic/draftools/draftviewer.asp?draftid=2_15_2008_1&amp;player=7&amp;pack=1&amp;pick=1&amp;showpick=true">saw his first pick</a>? Admittedly it was not the strongest pack in the world, but <a>Thorntooth Witch</a>? I have to admit I did not see that coming. I probably had it third or fourth out of that pack but there is a reason I am watching the draft and that Kenji is being watched. <p></p>&#10;      <p>I was talking with Jon Becker as we both flipped through people's picks in draft one, and Yoshitaka Nakano's first pick in the second pack is Becker's nightmare. Jon is legendary for his no-pass list; cards he will simply never pass because he would A) gladly pervert his mana base to play them, and B) is certain that the card will come back to haunt him. Nakano opened <a>Garruk Wildspeaker</a> in his <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20130702130146/http://gatherer.wizards.com/magic/draftools/draftviewer.asp?draftid=2_15_2008_1&amp;player=2&amp;pack=2&amp;pick=1&amp;showpick=true&amp;alwaysshowpick=false">second pack and boldly passed it to Raphael Gennari</a>—despite having highly drafted multiple green cards in the first pack!!!</p>&#10;      <p>It was obvious that green had dried up for Nakano and he had shifted his draft toward being blue-black. While I normally disagree with Jon about his no-pass list, I don't know that I would have the stones that Nakano displayed while taking <a>Silvergill Douser</a> over the game-breaking green card.</p>&#10;      <p>It is a fascinating table to watch and if you have not already checked it out you should do so immediately. After all, how many chances do you have to sit down and face the same exact decisions Kenji Tsumura had to face? And once you are done with that, you can skip ahead <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20130702130146/http://gatherer.wizards.com/magic/draftools/draftviewer.asp?draftid=2_15_2008_2">to the next draft viewer</a> and put yourself in the place of Jon Finkel or Nicolai Herzog. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, February 16: 2:17p.m. – It's a Head Scratcher</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Scott Johns</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <p>What do Hall of Fame legends do when they clash against each other in the 7-1 bracket? But judging by what I just saw in the Feature Match area, the Islands love Quentin Martin right back, so who can really blame him?</p>&#10;      <p>So, for your sadistic reading pleasure, here's one good way to totally demoralize an opponent. First, get a <a>Sage of Fables</a> into play. Then, play <a>Silvergill Adept</a> revealing <a>Merrow Harbinger</a>, putting a +1/+1 counter on the adept and drawing a card in the process. Round 11, I wandered around looking for fun stuff to write about when I happened upon Paul Cheon playing Mattias Kettil. It was Game 2 and Cheon was on the play. He played a turn-three <a>Preeminent Captain</a> and then went off. On turn four, the Captain brought his friend the <a>Game-Trail Changeling</a> across to say hello to Mattias. Cheon also played a <a>Kithkin Zephyrnaut</a>. The Kinship from the Zephyrnaut revealed a <a>Kithkin Greatheart</a>, effectively making the Zephyrnaut into a Serra Angel. On top of that, the Greatheart, once drawn, got to come into play tapped and attacking via the Captain and, thanks to the <a>Game-Trail Changeling</a>, was a bit bigger than average. Mattias just looked on in stunned disbelief—Cheon had just blown by him and killed him on turn five. Cheon laughed about his good luck after the game.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;That's the dream. [<a>Preeminent Captain</a>] turn three and then on turn four—MOOSE YA!&quot;</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="7"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, February 16: 4:10p.m. – Trendy</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Nate Price</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <p>One of the most important things players can study when preparing for any tournament is the current trend in the environment. For Constructed events, trends help you define the metagame, thereby making your deck selection and prospective test gauntlet a bit more focused. Here in Limited, it can help a player figure out what to expect during the course of a draft as well as helping figure out which archetypes are producing the statistically strongest results. If seventy percent of the undefeated decks in a Draft format are of a certain archetype, it stands to reason that the archetype must be fairly strong by comparison to the others available.</p>&#10;      <p>I wandered around to check out some of the trending happening here at Pro Tour–Kuala Lumpur, and wasn't at all surprised by my results. </p>&#10;      <p>Blue is one of the strongest colors in the format. Its commons pack an enormous punch both tribally and not. Cards like <a>Silvergill Douser</a>, <a>Mulldrifter</a>, Æthersnipe, and <a>Pestermite</a> are all very powerful cards that any player is usually happy to open. Perhaps more important than that, though, is how strong the synergy between the blue tribes is. Faeries and Merfolk combo together better than any other two tribes in the format. </p>&#10;      <p>When taking a look at the decks drafted by some of the best performers this weekend, trends definitely start to appear. Six out of the eight decks that Guillaume Wafo-Tapo and Jon Finkel drafted up to this point this weekend were base blue, including three decks that were almost monoblue. That's a pretty strong endorsement of the color. Admittedly, the blue decks tend to be more controlling, which does tend to fit Wafo-Tapo's and Finkel's play styles, which might also lend to their proclivity for the deck.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        </p><div>&#10;          <div>&#10;            <a>&#10;              <img alt="Orchard Warden" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Orchard%20Warden&amp;options=">&#10;            </a>&#10;          </div>&#10;        </div>The other major deck I've been seeing perform strongly was a big surprise to me. Mattias Kettil and Rogier Maaten have managed to get near the top of the standings on the backs of Swamps and Forests. Between the two of them, they managed six decks featuring Treefolk and Elves, though mostly Treefolk. That really surprised me, but Rogier put it very well when he explained that &quot;it's a very underdrafted deck, and it's got some very powerful cards. I never looked to specifically draft it, but it was always just there for me.&quot;<p></p>&#10;      <p>It had always seemed to me that Treefolk had the potential to be quite good. The creatures are all solid, and no one really seemed to want them since so many people were focusing on Elves in green. But every time I've gotten a Treefolk deck to come together, it looked really good, but failed to really do anything when it came time to play. But then again, I'm kind of bad.</p>&#10;      <p>Treefolk have the advantage of having very solid bodies , albeit a little more expensive than the other tribes. They also have access to a bunch of multiplicative abilities as the game goes on. You get an <a>Orchard Warden</a>, a <a>Thorntooth Witch</a>, and a <a>Battlewand Oak</a> in play, and each Treefolk you play gains you life, kills a creature, and pumps one of your men. It can get degenerate really fast, and once you've put it together, it's hard to stop because they're so hard to kill.</p>&#10;      <p>With as much emphasis has been placed on creature type in this set, though, it appears that there's a dichotomy in how important being tribal is. <i>Lorwyn </i>and <i>Morningtide </i>have given players reason after reason to pay attention to the creature type listed on the cards, but some pro players have spent all weekend making it an afterthought to the quality of the cards in question. After all, if a deck is blue for <a>Pestermite</a>, <a>Mulldrifter</a>, <a>Fallowsage</a>, and a few other assorted creatures, what do you call that deck? Is it Merfolk? Is it Faeries? If you have one of these cards, you usually tend to take a card of the appropriate creature type over an off-type card of slightly better card quality simply because it can become so powerful in conjunction with these cards.
