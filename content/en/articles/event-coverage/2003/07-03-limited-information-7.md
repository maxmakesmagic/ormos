To no one's surprise, Zombie Cutthroat was the public's clear winner, receiving more than half of the votes. Three other clerics, Zealous Inquisitor, Edgewalker, and Karona's Zealot, also received a significant (more than 5% of the total) number of votes. The public seems to feel that this deck needs a three casting-cost creature, with about 66% preferring sheer power over tribal synergy. While the professionals give Zombie Cutthroat the credit it is due, they explore some issues the public may have missed.

**Dan Bridy**: Karona's Zealot. Zombie Cutthroat is a great card in any deck, but the tribal nature of clerics, more so than most other tribes, is really important. Zealot is also a straight-up better card. A huge toughness plus the Kor Chant ability is really insane. Also of note are Dragon Scales and the Zealous Inquisitor, but being common, you will most likely be able to scoop those up in later picks.

A startlingly similar percentage of the pros polled concluded that Zombie Cutthroat is the pick, with seven of the twelve (58.3%) taking the monster tempo card. Karona's Zealot, Carrion Feeder, and Dragon Scales also have their supporters. Each option adds something different to the deck. Zombie Cutthroat is the most powerful card in the abstract, and with five three-drops, is a reasonable addition to the mana curve. Carrion Feeder accompanies Deftblade Elite as a second powerful one-drop. Dragon Scales, given the wealth of effective targets and shortage of spells, adds a great deal to the deck's synergy. Taking Karona's Zealot, or any other cleric, makes the deck more tribal and enhances every other tribal effect. Note that none of the pros even considered taking Putrid Raptor, as this deck has only one zombie, making its morph cost prohibitive at best. 

The first step in making this decision is to determine which is best among the clerics. One would think that a gold cleric that affects all other clerics' casting costs would be a power card, but Edgewalker falls far short of that, and can quickly be eliminated. In a format based on variable creatures, playing a card face up on turn three has inherent disadvantages, as your opponent can make all the correct decisions while you are left guessing. The return for such a disadvantage needs to be much greater, as Edgewalker will be very situational even in a dedicated cleric deck such as this one. This deck only has four clerics that are more expensive than Edgewalker, so even casting it on turn three is unlikely to be worthwhile. Exiled Doomsayer suffers from similar problems. A one-two for two needs to be useful all the time or devastating some of the time, and this cleric is neither. While it does slow down morph-triggered abilities and large morphed creatures, that won't add up to a playable card unless your opponent has a specific type of deck and draws a specific portion of cards. What's even worse is that the effect is symmetrical, and while this deck has an abnormally low number of morphs, slowing down a Skinthinner, Daru Mender, or Daru Sanctifier could cost yourself the game.

This brings us to the real decision among the clerics, namely Karona's Zealot or Zealous Inquisitor. Karona's Zealot's biggest edge here is that it has morph, due to the aforementioned disadvantage of playing a turn three face up creature. Despite this advantage and Karona's Zealot's absurdly powerful morph-triggered ability, I prefer the Inquisitor. In a deck without the prevalent cleric theme, the Zealot might be better, but the Inquisitor's cleric type serves to offset the disadvantage of not playing a morph on turn three. Additionally, Karona's Zealot has the same weakness as cards like Second Thoughts and to a lesser degree Willbender. I would take Echo Tracer over Willbender and a card such as Repel over Second Thoughts, because a good player will reduce the effectiveness of reactive cards such as these. If you pass the turn on turn five without a play, be it with Second Thoughts in hand in O-T-J draft or with face-down Karona's Zealot in O-L-S draft, a good player will immediately put you on the appropriate card and play around it. It's just too unlikely that you passed the turn because of a lack of options. While Zealous Inquisitor is just as slow and even easier to outplay when applied defensively, it is actually a very efficient attacker. The reason for this is that the threat is greater than the execution, and you will rarely have to actually tap your land unless you've attacked inappropriately.

We are now left with Zombie Cutthroat, Dragon Scales, Carrion Feeder, and Zealous Inquisitor as options. While I agree with the pros that Carrion Feeder is quite underrated right now, I must respectfully disagree with Tomi Walamies that the card has a particularly valuable role in the cleric deck. Playing a cleric deck is like filling in the pieces of a puzzle, upon the completion of which your opponent can not attack or block effectively anymore. It is not typically to your advantage to trade cards or quickly beat down, strategies which benefit Carrion Feeder tremendously. In black-green, the card has tremendous value. Your creature count will be high, your objective will often be to trade cards and eventually trump their latest play, and some creatures will even offer value when sacrificed (such as Symbiotic creatures). However, I would be hard-pressed to take Carrion Feeder over Zombie Cutthroat even in a deck that gets maximum value out of the Feeder.

Zombie Cutthroat, Dragon Scales, and Zealous Inquisitor still remain as valid choices. The next logical step is to compare the value of the two creatures, since this deck is loaded with men (most of which are clerics) and is very short on spells. It is likely that the decision will come down to a superior creature versus an inferior spell, a choice made easier by narrowing down the creature options to a single card. While many of the pros made great arguments about the value of going tribal as strongly as possible, and although Zealous Inquisitor is truly a fantastic cleric, I can't justify taking any of the creatures over Zombie Cutthroat. With ten clerics and a virtual guarantee that one will return on the wheel if you do not first pick one, you can afford to trade tribe for power. I completely agree with Bob Maher that despite its cost of five life, the Cutthroat is actually valuable to the deck because of its defensive application. Players will typically want to avoid blocking morphs when you have plains and swamps, with cards such as Daru Lancer, Gravel Slinger, and Aven Liberator punishing them for such a move. There are also essentially no cards that punish them for letting morphs through in this archetype, as flipping over Haunted Cadaver is not even a good play in the early turns. With Dirge of Dread, Cabal Archon, Whipgrass Entangler, Doubtless One, Grassland Crusader, and Swooping Talon all providing situational methods of killing your opponent, you really just want to survive. Your opponent will have to “be the beatdown” against you, and a 3/4 on turn three will shut down non-fliers on the spot.

So Zombie Cutthroat is the best creature for the deck, and over half of the public and professionals prefer it. Despite all of this, I would pick Dragon Scales out of this pack. Only receiving an anemic 2.5% of the public vote, this card will truly shine in this deck. Deftblade Elite, Aven Redeemer, Doubtless One, Daunting Defender, Grassland Crusader, and Swooping Talon are all exceptional targets while a Scaled Battlefield Medic or Whipgrass Entangler is nothing to sneeze at either. While Frozen Solid or Pacifism would be a rather abysmal outcome following Scaling something up, if they actually kill your creature, you are likely to recover the card disadvantage. You already have two six drops, both of which are exceptional targets, and you will likely pick up more in such a fat-laden set (such as Noble Templar as mentioned by Maher). The spell count is a huge factor in my preference for Dragon Scales as well. A superior creature-spell ratio is worth the sacrifice of card quality, and there are only three spells in this deck so far. Despite the Cutthroat's ability to keep you alive through the early game, I assert that Dragon Scales will often do the job even better. Were you to draw one or two of your three drops and either Dragon Scales or Zombie Cutthroat, they should often do an equivalent job of keeping you alive. Note that that none of these potential creatures are even the optimal creatures to enchant with the Scales. While I would be more inclined to take the Cutthroat at a table where I know the Scales are being undervalued, this exercise is in the abstract, so one can not count on this. While disagreeing with the great one Bob Maher and over half of the world may come back to haunt me, I have to stick to my guns: Dragon Scales.
