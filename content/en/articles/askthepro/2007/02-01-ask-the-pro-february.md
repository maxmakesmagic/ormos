Wizards of the Coast welcomes Hall of Famer Raphaël Lévy as the featured player here at "Ask the Pro." A fixture on the Pro Tour for the last eight years, Raphaël is uniquely positioned to answer your questions about the life of a professional **Magic** player, give a historical perspective on the game and high-level tournament scene, share stories about travelling the world, and talk about the role **Magic** plays in his life.

Seventh on the lifetime Pro Points list, Raphaël began his Pro Tour career back at Pro Tour-Paris in 1997. He became a regular on the tour starting at the 1998 World Championships. Since then he hasn't missed a Pro Tour, an astounding streak of 49 consecutive events. He was recently honored as a member of the [**Magic** Pro Tour Hall of Fame](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/tournaments-events-trading-card-game-magic-the-gathering-2)'s 2006 class and was inducted at the 2006 World Championships in Paris. Send your question, along with your name and location, via this email form. Answers will be posted every Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday.

This way you can play your bigger threats before your opponent, and put him on the defensive. If you're playing a defensive deck, having a land on the board a turn before your opponent allows you to keep up the tempo and not fall behind against a fast draw.</p>&#10;<p>The reasons why you would want to draw first:</p>&#10;<p><b>In Limited:</b><br></p><li>You're playing a very defensive deck, and you know you can catch up the tempo you lost by drawing, thanks to card advantage your deck could provide. </li>&#10;<li>If your opponent's deck has a poor mana base, and you think they will have trouble playing all his spells. </li>&#10;<p></p>&#10;<p><b>In Constructed:</b><br></p><li>You have a deck which strategy aims at have eight cards on turn one. In this example, I'm talking about reanimator decks that would keep their first hands on the draw so they could discard on turn one and have something to reanimate on turn two. </li>&#10;<li>In some mirror matchups, when it's not about damage, or speed but about card advantage, it's sometimes better to draw first. For example, in the first &quot;Red Deck Wins&quot; mirror matches where it was a war of attrition, or the first draw-go decks where the goal was to have as many lands in play as possible. </li>&#10;<p></p>&#10;<p>Nowadays, it's very rare to draw in Constructed. In Limited, some players draw first in most of their matches. For example, the Ruel brothers' old teammate Florent Jeudon often lets his opponent play first, so does soon-to-be French superstar Guillaume Wafo-Tapa.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

With more players playing the game, and the rest of the world just getting starting, the United States was THE <b>Magic</b> nation.</p>&#10;<p>The United States's supremacy started to decline around 1998-99. I remember around that time, a grudge match took place between Canada and the U.S. Canada swept the match and declared: &quot;Next time, we'll just play against the Czechs!&quot;, announcing the rise of the European powerhouses. </p>&#10;<p>Soon enough, Kai Budde led the way for Germany. <table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>February 15, 2007</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Q</b>: Would you ever play more than 40 cards in a Limited deck?<br> -Derek<br></p>&#10;<p><b>A:</b> I got to talk about this issue last week when I was in Avoriaz with my fellow draft partners. No one really seemed to agree with me on this point.</p>&#10;<p>Everyone agrees on the fact that a deck has better chances to perform well if its size is optimized. With a thinner deck, you have better chances to draw into your best cards. That's logical.</p>&#10;<p>I have played 41-card sealed decks in the past, but I had good reasons for that, or at least, reasons that I considered good enough to think that my deck would be better with more than 40 cards. </p>&#10;<p>The only reason I see myself, and saw myself in the past, playing with 41 cards, is to have a better manabase:</p>&#10;<p></p><li>In a deck with a shaky curve, many early and late drops, 17 lands in a 40-card deck may be too few, and 18 lands too many. In order to balance things right, 18 lands in a 41-card deck sound better than the two options above.</li>&#10;<li>In a three-color deck with no mana fixers (often a Sealed Deck problem), you sometimes want to be able to have access to more colored lands, without having to cut one of your 23 other cards, as 18 lands in 40 cards might be too many if your deck is cheap. 7-7-3 or 7-6-4 (number of lands of each color), may not be satisfying to support all your colored cards. 7-7-4, 7-6-5 might be what you want to have.</li>&#10;<p></p>&#10;<p>I am not advising playing 41-card decks, I'm just saying that in some very rare cases, playing 41 cards is better. The odds of drawing your good cards are very, very similar (the one-card difference is mostly negligible).</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
