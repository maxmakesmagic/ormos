### Why all the other colors want to be green

Every couple of expansions, I come up with a Multiplayer Card [Hall of Fame](https://web.archive.org/web/20021022000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.starcitygames.com/php/news/archive.php?Article=Anthony%20Alongi&origin=mtgcom_daily_aa43) that lists the best cards for group play in every color. As I prepare the next Hall of Fame, I'm finding that green comes out looking the rosiest.

Why is this? Personal bias? No. I like black and blue a bit more than I like green, and I like red just as much. Recent power cards? Perhaps. Genesis and the new Kamahl are both rabid beatings in multiplayer. Peer pressure? Quite possibly. Based on emails I've received, I'd say I have maybe a total of three readers who don't like green.

![Green: The Martha Stewart of the Magic world.](https://i.ormos.uk/1e/93/1e93aab9c2844695ea2822c659df7c77163961803f2d4e1b723b68c91f2fb032.jpg)

But of all the potential reasons for green making an excellent showing for multiplayer time and time again, I'd settle on this: No one throws a party like the green mage.

The Martha Stewart of the **Magic** world, Ms. Green keeps all the other colors tuned in and focused on figuring out how to make a good dressing for Hunted Wumpus meat or which wine to serve from Eladamri's Vineyard. But they just can't figure out how to make their "concoctions" come out the same way.

### PARTY WITH A TWIST

In most cases, you'll play one of green's "party" cards with every intention of getting more out of it than any other player. For example, your Awakening deck might start something like this:

*   4 Awakening
*   4 Dwarven Driller
*   4 Seeker of Skybreak
*   4 Tahngarth, Talruum Hero

And your Aluren deck may start something like this:

*   4 Aluren
*   4 Equilibrium
*   4 Ghitu Slinger
*   4 Man-o'-War

This method is called *breaking the symmetry of a card --* everyone gets a benefit of some kind, but you're going one step beyond and making sure that no one can use the benefit as well as you can. It's a very "Johnny" thing to do, if I may borrow that Wizards R&D term. For tournament players like "Spike," it's typically too risky, as cards spent winning are spent breaking symmetry -- and if you do manage a deck that's successful on the scene, a mirror match becomes a coin flip. "Timmy" will play with a symmetrical card but doesn't care if the symmetry is broken or not. I'll talk more about that later.

Breaking the symmetry is nothing new to seasoned players, but newer players may need some practice with this. I'd like to take a moment to suggest some advice to those players as they try this skill for the first time. Consider this an episode of *Martha Stewart Living,* without the Wall Street connections. We'll use Dual Nature, a near-forgotten rare from the rant-inspiring *Prophecy* set, as our test subject.

**Step One: Read every word on the card.**

Dual Nature states:

"Whenever a creature card comes into play, its controller puts a creature token into play that's a copy of that creature.  
 Whenever a creature card leaves play, remove all tokens with the same name as that creature from the game.  
 When Dual Nature leaves play, remove all tokens created with it from the game."

There's a lot going on there, isn't there? Let's move on to Step Two.

**Step Two: No, seriously. Read every word on the card.**

The key phrases I'd focus on are "creature card comes into play" (which will limit the kinds of cards that work), "its controller" (which opens up roads to affect opponents), "a copy of that creature" (so something can be replicated), "leaves play" (which is more open than "is put into a graveyard from play"), "all tokens with the same name as that creature" (which might be more than just the one token it created, right?), and "leaves play, remove all tokens created with it" (again, a very open field of circumstances for a potentially large effect). There's also the "removed from game" aspect, which is interesting in that it doesn't just kill the darn things.

This step is the Johnny skill boiled down to its essence. Johnny reads the whole card. Be like Johnny. Read the card.

And then read some more . . .

**Step Three: Find as many rulings as you can on the card.**

Even if the card has never been changed (and the vast majority have not), a good rulings database will help you clarify questions about the card -- and might bring up points that you'd otherwise overlook. My favorite source for rulings is [**crystalkeep.com**](https://web.archive.org/web/20021022000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.crystalkeep.com&origin=mtgcom_daily_aa43).

Here's a short list of Dual Nature rulings:

*   The ability that creates the creature token only triggers when a creature card (or artifact creature card) comes into play. It doesn't trigger when a card of another card type comes into play as a creature.
*   If a Legend card comes into play, the creature token is put into play, but the "Legend rule" immediately sends it to the graveyard before you can do anything with the token.
*   If Dual Nature leaves play after the ability that creates a creature token triggers but before the token is put into play, the token remains in play indefinitely because the leave-play ability resolves before the token is in play.
*   If the creature card has any "comes into play" abilities, the creature token will have those, too.
*   The creature token doesn't copy any additional costs paid for the creature spell. Even a kicker cost was paid for the original creature, no kicker cost was paid for the copy.
*   The creature tokens created by Dual Nature follow the same rules as all other creature tokens.

All right, what has potential here? The card type thing is interesting, but last time I checked, there aren't any cards that influence what card types you or your opponents play. (You might consider Blood Oath or Ostracize to counteract other creature-heavy decks, I suppose, but that's probably too specialized.) Back burner.

The Legend reminder is useful -- after all, if you could find a card that turned creatures into Legends (yes, yes, I know you know it already, but just hold tight for those readers who aren't as amazing or as clever as you!), well then, you could deny opponents their creature tokens! Front burner.

The timing bit with Dual Nature leaving play before the token reaches play sounds really cute. We might consider cards that bounce Dual Nature to our hand. Front burner.

The ruling on "comes into play" abilities sounds pretty sweet, doesn't it? Many players will already have thought of this, but that's the beauty of a good ruling database -- it assumes nothing and lets those of us who occasionally forget cool interactions have that "a-ha!" moment.

The kicker bit is less interesting -- it denies you an option (kicker creatures aren't worth it in your deck) and does nothing to help you screw opponents (as you can't force them to have kicker cards!).

I would normally insist we go check out general rulings about creature tokens, too (and I suggest you do so when you have time), but let's stay on task. We have a couple of good possibilities for breaking the symmetry of a party card, so let's get to it.

**Step Four: Seek the cards that play into your front burner strategies.**

The Legend bit is a good place to start. Get yourself to a database and type "creature type" in the card text field. Search. (You may have to eye an *Onslaught* spoiler, as most databases haven't updated yet.) You shouldn't pay attention to a lot of the stuff, such as Storage Matrix or Tribal Flames. You'll also get a list that looks something like the following:

*   Belbe's Portal
*   Caller of the Hunt
*   Chainer, Dementia Master
*   Coat of Arms
*   Dralnu's Crusade
*   Extinction
*   Legerdemain
*   Tsabo's Decree
*   Unnatural Selection
*   Urza's Incubator
*   Vesuvan Doppelganger
*   Volrath's Laboratory
*   and so on

Well, I don't think we have to look any further, do you? Unnatural Selection is already at the front of many casual players' minds, given the *Onslaught* bent. And the cheap "Legend trick" is already well known. So we know the center of the deck: You'll use Unnatural Selection's ability to turn both your opponent's creature card and the token into Legends. Presto! For 2 mana, you nullify the trick for an entering creature and it collapses like a cheap soufflé. (I'm still on the Martha metaphor, people. Try to keep up.)

There are many other avenues you can take, of course.
