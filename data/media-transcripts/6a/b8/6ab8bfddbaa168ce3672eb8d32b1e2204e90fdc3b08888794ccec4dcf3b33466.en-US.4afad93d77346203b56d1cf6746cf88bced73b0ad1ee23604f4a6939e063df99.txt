Worlds is being held in San Francisco this year at the Fort Mason Center, which is just across the Bay from Alcatraz. That was the setting as 304 Magic players from 56 countries showed up to compete for the title of 2004 World Champion. 5 Europeans lead the way with perfect six and O records after day one. Croatian player Sasha Zork is in first on tiebreakers. A mathematics student from Zagreb, he used an Urza Tron fueled tooth and nail deck with maindeck pyroclasm to post his record, trying to improve on last year's 12th place finish. Bruner Cravalho from Switzerland also went six and O. He only lost one game today in route to his record with a green white slide deck. Finished player Andre Hiraman was the only Affinity player to go 6 and O. The day started with 31% of players choosing to run Affinity, but by the end of day one everyone was talking about the remarkable diversity of this field. All 5 undefeated players played different decks and there were 12 different decks used to post 51 records or better. The other two six Anos were both French players. Antoine Ruel ran the table with a mono red land destruction deck and Manuel Bevan turned the trick with a combo deck based around Krark. Clan Ironworks was a great day for the French. In addition to those 26 and O records put up by Ruel and Bevan, the French national team also ended the day tied for first. Gabriel Nassif put up A5 in one with blue white control and Olivia Ruel also went five and one with a green white control deck that didn't even bother with Astral Slide. Alexandra passes three and three. Mark was good enough to leave France and 1st overnight ahead of Finland, South Korea and the Czech Republic on tiebreakers. Speaking of the national team competition, another big story was the mediocre performance of the hometown team. U.S. National Champion Craig Crumples did his part, posting five wins, but Bill Stead went to very disappointing 04 and two, and Team USA couldn't even crack the top 20 necessary to show up on the nifty new leaderboard introduced at Worlds this year. All in all, was quite a good day. The diverse field led to some great magic and the storylines for this world are starting to come together. Perhaps my favorite storyline is the Player of the Year race. Gabriel on the seat and Nikolai Herzog were checking in with each other every round to find out how the other guy was doing. Herzog put up a foreign too, but in the sieve didn't one better at 5:00 and 1:00. And don't forget Richard Osterberg. His 41 in one has him right in the thick of it, and this race may come down to Sunday. Day 2 will be booster draft and a lot of players think this is the format where they can make their run back toward the top of the standings. Stay tuned to magicthegathering.com.
