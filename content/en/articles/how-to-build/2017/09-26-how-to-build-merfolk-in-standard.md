It all began in *Limited Edition (Alpha)*.

Merfolk begin in *Magic*'s very first set, and my favorite tribe started off with a splash. The quintessential Merfolk has proven so popular it's been reprinted time and time again, working its way into many a *Magic* player's life and heart at the same time.

Who can forget such an iconic creature?

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&multiverseid=114)](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=114)

Ah yes, the seminal creature of the tribe: [Merfolk of the Pearl Trident](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+of+the+Pearl+Trident) . . . Wait a minute, something's not quite right there. Let's try again.

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&multiverseid=110)](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=110)

That's a little better. Merfolk entered all of our lives nearly 25 years ago, and I've never looked at sushi the same way since. Beginning with the very first printing of *Magic* and up to the release of *Ixalan* this week, Merfolk have been an integral part of the Multiverse (except for a [dark period we don't like to mention](/en/articles/feature/2008/06-25-merfolk-resurfaced)). In that time, the tribe has put together quite the storied history, making their scaly presence known from the kitchen table to the Top 8 of the Pro Tour.

So you can be sure that when I found out *Ixalan* contained Merfolk, I was hyped beyond measure for the opportunity to sleeve them up in Standard again. But first we have to answer a more basic question: what makes Merfolk good?

# How the School Swims

First and foremost, it's key to know that fish swim in schools for a reason. It offers community protection, and of course there's strength in numbers.

Merfolk operate the same way. Thanks to a critical number of "[lords](/en/articles/makingmagic/2017/08-07-cats#lord)"—namely, [Lord of Atlantis](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lord+of+Atlantis), [Master of the Pearl Trident](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Master+of+the+Pearl+Trident), [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey), and sometimes even [Coralhelm Commander](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Coralhelm+Commander) or [Merfolk Sovereign](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Sovereign)—Merfolk can create a very large board very quickly, despite that all the creatures in question are simply 2/2s by themselves. With a few lords on the field, suddenly a couple [Cursecatcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cursecatcher)s and [Silvergill Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvergill+Adept)s represent a devastating army.

And often that army can't be stopped—literally. Thanks to the eight two-drop lords, islandwalk is a very real threat, especially in older formats where players are almost always playing some form of blue cards. And when they aren't, no problem! [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) takes care of that issue while denying opponents a color and drawing you a card at the same time.

It's a simple recipe, but one that has proven stubbornly resilient through the years, from Vintage to Legacy to Extended to Modern and even Standard.

As you can see, the strategy has stayed fairly consistent over the years, and the same core pieces have led to sustained success. The climate around them changes, but the Merfolk swim on.

That brings us to *Ixalan,* a brand-new exciting world where Dinosaurs roam the land alongside bloodsucking Vampires while Pirates raid along the coast. But while the swashbucklers may sail around the sea, these waters still belong to the Merfolk.

# The Merfolk of *Ixalan*

There are two major mechanics associated with the Merfolk on Ixalan, and both are something a little different. But first we have to take note of something major: Merfolk are green again! We've seen a touch of this before, all the way back in *Apocalypse* with [Gaea's Skyfolk](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gaea%27s+Skyfolk), but in *Ixalan* this has been pushed even further to pretty great effect. Being in green makes perfect sense for the primary mechanic Merfolk take advantage of: the distribution of +1/+1 counters and the tricks that enables.

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Jade+Guardian)](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jade+Guardian)

Being focused across blue and green gives the aspiring Merfolk player a ton of options, including the ability to make [Herald of Secret Streams](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Herald+of+Secret+Streams) truly devastating. There are a ton of Merfolk in *Ixalan* that can pump the team with +1/+1 counters, and the exciting [Kopala, Warden of Waves](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kopala%2C+Warden+of+Waves) will follow things up to protect your team—or at least your [River Sneak](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=River+Sneak), which will end games all on its own with a little tribal support.

The second mechanic somewhat associated with Merfolk is explore, which is one of my favorite mechanics Wizards has released in years. After all, it is a pretty cool all-upside mechanic where both outcomes are desirable but also very different in their application. [Merfolk Branchwalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Branchwalker) is very similar to [Silvergill Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvergill+Adept), my favorite card ever printed and a staple of Merfolk decks through the years. Branchwalker brings that same power to Standard and will form the backbone of any Merfolk deck that pokes its head over the surface.

The last time a competitive Merfolk deck was in Standard was a decade ago, when Jan Ruess took the Merfolk-Wizard hybrid to the Top 8 of [Pro Tour Hollywood 2008](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/gindy-puts-name-in-lights-2) following the release of *Shadowmoor* and the addition of [Cursecatcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cursecatcher) to the deck*.*



As you can see, the deck then was very reliant on being tempo-oriented. [Cursecatcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cursecatcher) was the ideal one-drop, but [Tideshaper Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tideshaper+Mystic) would do in a pinch. From there, a host of good two-drop Merfolk filled the curve, while [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret) could also accelerate a turn-three [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation) or [Venser, Shaper Savant](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Venser%2C+Shaper+Savant). [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) would then sit at the ready to clean things up while [Sygg, River Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sygg%2C+River+Guide) made combat a sinking proposition for opponents.

We can apply some of those same tactics to Merfolk in *Ixalan* Standard. [Kumena's Speaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kumena%27s+Speaker) is an aggressive one-drop, and [River Sneak](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=River+Sneak) will end games quickly while we keep opponents busy with disruption like [Kopala, Warden of Waves](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kopala%2C+Warden+of+Waves) or the newly Merfolk-themed [Spell Pierce](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spell+Pierce). [Merfolk Branchwalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Branchwalker) provides a lot of advantage no matter what you're exploring, and [Shapers' Sanctuary](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shapers%27+Sanctuary) guarantees you won't be beaten by spot removal. We don't have any of the "traditional" Merfolk lords, but [Metallic Mimic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Metallic+Mimic) does a mighty fine impression, and [Vineshaper Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vineshaper+Mystic) is an outright bomb a lot of the time.

# Putting it All Together

*Ixalan* Standard is going to have a lot going for it. A lot of fun decks survive rotation—as does Ramunap Red—but the tribes coming in the newest set are certainly going to make an impact. That includes Merfolk, which are tricky to build with but will certainly be able to take people by surprise. Here's where I'll be starting with at my first *Ixalan* Friday Night *Magic.*

There are a ton of ways you can go with Merfolk in *Ixalan.* One fun card I passed up is [Deeproot Waters](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deeproot+Waters), which provides an unending source of Merfolk tokens in the right deck. And it's possible some sort of Sultai (black-green-blue) deck could take advantage of [Vineshaper Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vineshaper+Mystic) and [Herald of Secret Streams](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Herald+of+Secret+Streams) to go nuts with [Winding Constrictor](https://web.archive.org/web/20170926000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Winding+Constrictor). I'm sure we've only made a ripple of the surface of what Merfolk will be able to accomplish in *Ixalan*, but I'm sure looking forward to playing my favorite tribe in Standard once again.
