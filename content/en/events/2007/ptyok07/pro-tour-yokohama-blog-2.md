The fact that it was unsanctioned meant that we got to draft with various Wizards of the Coast staff, including Mr. Devin Low, who, somewhat unfairly for this draft, had already seen the cards for our pre-Prerelease playtime, thanks to having been a designer on the set. </p>&#10;      <p>Never have <i>Time Spiral</i> and <i>Planar Chaos</i> been drafted so quickly, or with as little idea as to archetypes. For the likes of BDM, Richard Hagon, Pro Tour photographer Craig Gibson and I, these were packs we were all pretty familiar with, but there was no real way to know how they would fit in with what was to come. The other half of the draft had Wizards staffers Greg Collins (Grand High Poobah of Event Coverage), Kelly Digges (Sub-Grand High Peon of Event Coverage, with the power to edit his job title as necessary), Noah Weil (Development Intern) and the aforementioned D-Low (designer on <i>Future Sight</i>). I'm guessing that each of them had <i>some </i>idea of what was coming.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Virulent Sliver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Virulent%20Sliver&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>There were a few speculative picks running around. As is his wont, BDM was all about forcing Slivers, in the hope that he'd get there with some fun in Pack 3. As it happens, he lived the dream and won with both Sliver Legion and Virulent Sliver in separate games (and once with both on the board). Meanwhile, I found myself picking <a>Timecrafting</a> pretty high, in the hope that there would be some suspend juice in the final pack that made it worthwhile. I already had a <a>Stormbind</a> and a <a>Disintegrate</a> from Pack 1 (BDM took <a>Vesuvan Shapeshifter</a> over the burn spell), so I was pretty happily red, and I got some sort of a hookup with an Arc Blade that turned out to work like <a>Stormbind</a> number two when helped out by <a>Jhoira's Timebug</a>, an insect whose power has increased greatly over time. Of course, when my pair of 'Binds faced off against Devin's Lumithread Field, I was a sad monkey, but still, taking a game off the set's designer still feels pretty good. </p>&#10;      <p>Despite (or perhaps because of) Devin's valiant leadership, his team succumbed to the force that was Team Noah/Craig/Kelly/Tim. The main story from our first draft? It was a great deal of fun. With so many new keywords, playful spins on old mechanics, and the wacky new borders (I literally almost fell of my chair when I saw Blind Phantasm… and cannot begin to imagine how cool it must look in foil), <i>Future Sight</i> has a lot going on.</p>&#10;      <p>Tonight's draft is going to be fun… All of a sudden we have half an idea of what we want to try to do.            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <i>I caught up with Head Judge Sheldon Menery during a rare lull in the Pro Tour action. He was nice enough to share his experiences as Head Judge of Pro Tour-Yokohama, common rules questions, his favorite Future Sight cards, and more.</i>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: We've just started Round 14 of this 19-round event. How would you say the tournament has gone so far?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: The tournament's gone really well. Yesterday we were an hour plus ahead of schedule, which is <i>extremely</i> rare for pro tours. We're still thirty minutes ahead today, which is even more impressive considering a 30-minute ruling along with some slow play infractions at the end of Round 9. I would say this is the single smoothest Pro Tour I've ever been to.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: What are the responsibilities of the floor judges of the event? </b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: The judges' main responsibility is to have a speedy round turnover. They've done a great job with this. Their big skill this weekend has been making the quick rulings when needed. I attribute it to a really strong judge mentorship program in place now. It really helps the judges know what to do before showing up. That kind of experience really shows. The players should also get credit for knowing what they're doing, and not being argumentative when they get a ruling they disagree with. </p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: In the past some players were known for picking on an indecisive judge and getting a ruling that favored them. That's no longer true?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: That has been a problem before. These days, the Pro Tour judges get more training which gives them more confidence when making a ruling. Those players don't have the toehold of a wavering judge to debate with. Right now most players listen to and agree with a judge's ruling. There are still appeals of course, but there's a <i>lot</i> less getting the floor judge to change their stance on a ruling.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: Are there any special challenges to running a Pro Tour in a foreign country?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: Not so much for us. The side events area can have some problems with the language barrier, but the fact that most pros speak some English really helps. The judges are on top of language issues that could pop up.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: Speaking of, I heard one player was ejected for trying to cheat using the language barrier.</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: Yes there was one player who tried to obfuscate and take back moves using the language barrier. We came over to investigate, and he attempted to deceive the investigating team. He was discovered changing his story, so he was disqualified without prize.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: Moving on, have there been any common rules questions this weekend?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: Not so much now, but in the beginning of the tournament there were two big ones, both involving Teferi. One of them was &quot;If Teferi's out, can I suspend a creature at instant speed?&quot; and the answer to that is yes. The other was &quot;If Teferi's out, can I suspend a creature at instant speed while a split second card is on the stack?&quot; and the answer to that one is of course no. We haven't had questions like that recently; most people who make Day 2 are up on stuff like that. One player was heard to remark, &quot;If I had known I could suspend on my opponent's turn with Teferi out, I would have played a different deck.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: Any rules issues the PTQ players should watch out for this season?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: Yes! Make sure the guy you're playing face down actually has morph!</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: How many pros made that mistake this weekend?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: So far four separate players have played the wrong card as morphs.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: What's the penalty for that?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: It's a game play-game rule error, which is usually a warning. If a player immediately notices they played the wrong card and calls a judge over they'll probably get a warning. However, considering this involves hidden information and the potential for abuse, if the situation is caught later it can be a game loss.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: Finally, what's your favorite <i>Future Sight</i> card you've seen so far this weekend?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>A: Angel of Salvation. I love the flash with convoke. She's going straight into my Elder Dragon Highlander deck. I also really like Venser, Shaper Savant for his ability to stop uncounterable spells. Must stop the Obliterate!</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Q: Thanks for your time, Sheldon!
