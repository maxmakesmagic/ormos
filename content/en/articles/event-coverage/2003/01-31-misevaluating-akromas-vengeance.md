It is easy to misevaluate Akroma's Vengeance. Morphling knows I've done it for months. This card looks unusually similar to every other inferior Wrath of God reprint we see every time a new block appears. The problem is that Akroma's Vengeance isn't a Rout without the instant-speed option or a Kirtar's Wrath that doesn't give you 1/1 flyers. It's not any kind of Wrath of God reprint, despite its color dedication; Akroma's Vengeance is a sneaky update to Nevinyrral's Disk.

If you are a longtime serious player and by "longtime", I mean you have played tournament **Magic** for 5-6 years at least, you will probably recall at some time wishing your Nevinyrral's Disk had come into play untapped. How much additional mana would you have spent in order to make this happen? Two? Would a six-mana Nevinyrral's Disk have saved you from the inopportune Crumble of the oncoming Erhnam n’ Burn'em player? Did you have the game locked up, with five or six islands worth of counter mana open, only to lose to a Scavenger Folk in play? How much mana would you have paid over the maddeningly cheap ![4 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/36/29/3629288966daa042decf01d3752789ab6676fddd67c3679fa580cd3baecae158.gif) + ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif) of Nevinyrral's Disk to prevent your opponent from drawing two more cards from Howling Mine during that interim turn, or to avoid having to look at that beautiful combo-breaker, sitting there tapped forever, as the Stasis player refused to ever let you untap this nemesis of lock decks?

Akroma's Vengeance does not ask for very much at all. It asks for just one more mana! Akroma even tries to entice you into playing with her Vengeance by adding cycling, so that it will never be a truly wasted draw. I'll hook you up with the threshold, big boy! I'll give you Wrath of God redundancy over the four copies you are legally allowed to play. Come on, slip this six mana sweeper into your deck; you won't be sorry. No takers? Anyone up for a 6/6 purple-haired chick with a broadsword then? What if I throw in every relevant creature ability? Anyone? Anyone?

Sure, Akroma's Vengeance lacks some of Nevinyrral's Disk's original versatility. You can't put a four mana down payment on it and let it sit there the entire game while the opponent tries to figure out how much he can afford to commit to the board in order to entice you into popping said Disk, while still reserving enough resources to be able to still win the game. There are no cool combinations between this card and Capsize, or with Argivan Restoration, two old-time friends of the original Team CMU's Nevinyrral's Disk optimization squad. Akroma's Vengeance probably wouldn't fit very well into the Necropotence deck, playing simultaneous defense against hard-hitting opponents and your own life-stealing enchantment...

But it is awfully versatile nevertheless.

In the early game, Akroma's Vengeance plays as a bad Impulse, letting you move through your deck to more relevant spells, or at least ensuring you make your crucial developmental land drops. In the late game, it not only gives you Wrath of God redundancy, but it lets white control decks "cheat" on answer cards. Standard today is a world where control decks have to contend not only with the creature rush of u-g decks, but enchantment-based board control. Because running main deck Disenchants can mean depressingly dead draws against the wrong opponents, Akroma's Vengeance may be just the kind of card a traditional u-w should look for, especially since some of its main opponents will be counter-poor or counter-deficient decks designed to dedicate multiple permanents to the board.

Here is the first draft of a deck designed to take advantage of Akroma's Vengeance. Actually, it was originally designed to take advantage of Krosan Tusker, but Akroma's Vengeance seemed a good fit. In an effort to negate obviously bad card combinations, I avoided cards like Compulsion and Mobilization in this build and even have a Wrath-proof win condition. Please bear with me for a moment because, as I have been playing in tournaments since 1994, it never makes sense to me when Weissman/Fortress u-w decks are not present in the metagame.

Having three copies of two different cards with similar abilities is generally a sign of indecision as to which is the superior. As you might have guessed, this deck started off with four copies of Wrath of God and two copies of the card we are discussing today. As I drew out the cards more and more, I wanted to be able to make big sweeps with the Vengeance, or cycle it away in the wrong matchups. That being said, against the blistering offense of u-g, not having four copies of Wrath of God may prove fatal, whereas against the slower decks, especially with as much card drawing as this deck has, you may have enough time to recoup a no-Wrath draw.

At some point, I tried to model this deck after the original Draw-Go that Andrew Cuneo posted in late 1997... Steel Golems backed up with tons of permission. The problem, of course, is that while the creature decks of today are actually slower than they were in the heyday of Jackal Pup and Fireblast, Standard, following the rotation of Absorb, Dromar's Charm, and Undermine, has a distinct lack of tournament quality hard counters. Circular Logic, the current "other Counterspell" of choice, is most effective when played with the help of a Merfolk Looter, Psychatog, or Wild Mongrel and is generally less effective than Complicate in such a permanent-poor deck as this one. On the upside, this deck gets to run Krosan Tusker, a self-contained two-for-one and one of the most absurd control cards to have come out of the current block.

Yes, I think this deck has some issues. The last incarnation of Enforcer-Go (designed by Kibler and played by Hegstad at Ryan Fuller's Masters) bowed out of Standard following the release of Chainer's Edict. This deck has much the same vulnerability to that staple card. In fact, like many u-w control decks through the years, it seems allergic to the color black in general. That being said, black control is currently taking a back seat to the various u-g decks, polychromatic white board control decks and Psychatog decks. Withered Wretch and company notwithstanding, there is no Standard black beatdown deck to speak of so, for now at least, this deck doesn't have to weather that particular storm. In any case, the most relevant threats a black deck can post against this kind of deck cost five or more mana, and this deck can fight them with Krosan Tusker, Deep Analysis and permission.

Even given its shortcomings, a deck like this one does have some interesting natural advantages. It gets a powerful card drawing tool in permission matchups that can't be either matched or countered by a Psychatog player. Against the board control decks, it can use its permission to stop key cards like Mirari and Exalted Angel, then wipe up the rest of the board with a single Akroma's Vengeance. U-g decks may be diverse in their focus on madness, threshold, or Opposition strategies, but (Squirrel Nest notwithstanding) all tend to be vulnerable to Wrath of God.

The card I was most interested in drawing was Nantuko Monastery. I sort of feel like this deck wants more land anyway, so maybe upping the Monastery count and cutting a couple of Enforcers is the way to go. The only problem with that, of course, is that when shifting the kill from one efficient g-w threat to the other, we change the deck from Edict-vulnerable to Smother-vulnerable. Even if we cut all the Mystic Enforcers, though, the last Krosan Tusker or two can always be used as a post-Wrath threat, especially late game, when the library becomes low on basic lands.

As I suggested earlier, as much as this article is about how interesting a card Akroma's Vengeance is, the deck almost certainly wants four traditional Wraths of God before it goes for the expensive new card... going to seven copies (four of one and three of the other) might actually be right.

Sadly, even if everything seems to be working properly, the deck is drawing a mix of mana and spells, it has enough permission to defend itself early, and at least one Tusker to set up the right lands in the first five or so turns, the u-w archetype has a big hole in the fast creature kill department. Recently, here on the *Sideboard*, a number of players talked about how the color white could be improved for constructed play. In my opinion, more than bringing back a broken card like Armageddon or establishing the next rebel mechanic, white just wants a fast answer to other decks’ efficient creatures. Because of that, as good as a deck like the one above can be made against opponents like Slide and u-g, I don't know that it is even possible to answer Psychatog and Shadowmage Infiltrator, except via permission.

All of that being said, I think u-w could be an interesting deck to fool around with, and could be competitive with the right tuning, in the right field. Until next, enjoy sweeping up your opponent's permanents.
