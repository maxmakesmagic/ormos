Another Pro Tour, another spectacular weekend of drafting and Constructed hype. For this edition of Running the Gauntlet, I've picked out a few decks you might find yourself piloting during this Pro Tour's *Magic Online* Gauntlet. Remember, the Pro Tour Gauntlet is not just the selection of decks that made the Top 8, it is a selection of decks that did very well at the Pro Tour. While you might certainly get a deck that was piloted by Shota Yasooka or Carlos Romão, you could also get a deck piloted by anyone else who had a strong showing in the Constructed portion of [Pro Tour *Kaladesh*](https://web.archive.org/web/20210503002314/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/ptkld/).

Speaking of Shota Yasooka, let's start things off by taking his Grixis Control list for a spin. An insane removal package backed up by powerful creatures such as [Thing in the Ice](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thing+in+the+Ice) and [Torrential Gearhulk](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Torrential+Gearhulk) left Yasooka with a ridiculous 8-1-1 record in the Constructed portion of the Swiss rounds.

Next up is Matt Nass's Termur Aetherworks deck. This deck looks to cast what can be a turn-four Eldrazi off the back of [Aetherworks Marvel](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Aetherworks+Marvel). Four copies of both [Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ulamog%2C+the+Ceaseless+Hunger) and [Emrakul, the Promised End](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Emrakul%2C+the+Promised+End) can end the game in spectacular fashion. Let's take a look.

A bit on the opposite side of things from Shota's Grixis Control and Nass's Aetherworks combo was Makis Matsoukas's Red-White Tokens deck. Going 8-0-2, Makis's deck looked to put the pressure on early and, dare I say, not step off the gas.

Our last deck for this look into the Pro Tour Gauntlet is Pierre Dagen's Blue-Red Spells. As you'll see—and similar to Shota Yasooka's deck—this does not run many win conditions. Four copies of [Dynavolt Tower](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dynavolt+Tower) headline this list, but even Pierre stated it was the four [Niblis of Frost](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Niblis+of+Frost)s from his sideboard that generally did the most work. Let's take a look.

This was supposed to be the Pro Tour of [Smuggler's Copter](https://web.archive.org/web/20161018000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smuggler%27s+Copter), and while that card made a definitive mark in many of the Constructed (and Limited) decks, the format was much more diverse than people were giving it credit for. Remember, this was simply a look at some of the awesome decks you might find yourself randomly getting while playing in the *Magic Online* Pro Tour Gauntlet, but there are many more you might find yourself piloting.

For more information about the Pro Tour *Kaladesh* Gauntlet, including entry options, decklists, and more, be sure to read the [accompanying article](/en/articles/magiconline/2016/10-18-pro-tour-gauntlet-kaladesh).

Until next time, may all your draws be gas and all your Vehicles crewed.
