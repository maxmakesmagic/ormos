We all know the story of the [Top 8 decks of Pro Tour *Guilds of Ravnica*](https://web.archive.org/web/20201024153056/https://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/ptgrn/top-8-decklists-2018-11-10) by now: a sea of aggressive Red-White Aggro decks with lonesome Jeskai Control and Izzet Drakes representing "anything" else.

While it's true Red-White Aggro emerged on top and will be an impressive deck until *Ravnica Allegiance* rolls around in January, it's also true there's a little more to the story. The Top 16—often known as "those who narrowly missed Top 8 by just one match somewhere during the tournament"—showed a little more representation from the format:

*   Izzet Drakes
*   Three copies of Jeskai Control
*   Two copies of Golgari Midrange
*   Two copies of Red-White Aggro

In the world where the Top 16 and Top 8 finished in flipped order, it'd be control decks that looked a little more dominant where four different archetypes made an appearance. And digging deeper into [Pro Tour *Guilds of Ravnica* standings by format](/en/events/2018/ptgrn/standings-by-format) reveals even more interesting decks.

For example, Guillaume Gauthier picked up a perfect 30 points in Constructed, missing a better finish by going just 2-4 in Draft. His deck of choice?

Just like the powerful Red-White Aggro decks, Mono-Blue Tempo is low-to-the-ground aggression built on one- and two-drops. [Mist-Cloaked Herald](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mist-Cloaked+Herald) and [Siren Stormtamer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Siren+Stormtamer) are both evasive, with the latter building in a counterspell for removal. [Merfolk Trickster](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merfolk+Trickster) keeps the path open when countermagic isn't needed, and [Warkite Marauder](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warkite+Marauder) keeps the offense afloat by neutralizing the best blockers.

But none of that is successful without the toolkit blue offers here. [Curious Obsession](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Curious+Obsession) not only pushes through more damage, it allows an aggressive deck that's playing out its cards to draw more of them. It's also just one mana, and on a creature like [Mist-Cloaked Herald](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mist-Cloaked+Herald), you'll always have an attack to make.

[Tempest Djinn](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tempest+Djinn) is as big as the creatures get, but when your mana base is just 20 copies of [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island), it's fair to say that if it lands on curve, it's a 3/4 *with flying* and a critical 4 toughness. [Shock](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shock), [Lightning Strike](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Strike), and even [Deafening Clarion](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deafening+Clarion) can't deal with it.

Unlike those [*other* aggro decks that might fold to an unexpected](http://twitter.com/magicprotour/status/1061717625456283655) [Settle the Wreckage](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Settle+the+Wreckage), Mono-Blue Tempo won't let you down.

But Pro Tour *Guilds of Ravnica* wasn't just about aggressive decks. Both [Esper Control](/en/articles/event-coverage/2018/11-10-deck-tech-esper-control) and [Grixis Control](/en/events/2018/ptgrn/deck-tech-grixis-control) had solid showing in the hands of excellent players, as did Dimir Control piloted by Mark Jacobson.

Jacobson notched 21 points—a 7-3 Constructed record—with his take on control. More importantly, since Wilson Mok played his Round 16 match out against Makihito Mihara (which both knocked Mihara out of Top 8 contention and ensured Mok would have top seeding in the playoff), Jacobson was playing for a potential win-and-in to Top 8 against eventual Pro Tour Champion Andrew Elenbogen.

The narrow difference between sneaking into Top 8 and missing Top 16 really can come down to one match!

Jacobson's deck is precisely the kind of control deck you'd imagine described as Dimir. Eschewing *both* white and red like the popular Jeskai Control builds, Dimir Control leans totally on black removal and signature blue-black cards in Standard to take over games and close them out.

[Thief of Sanity](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thief+of+Sanity), [Hostage Taker](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hostage+Taker), and [Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer) are a three-four-five curve of threats and answers that no other deck offers. [Thief of Sanity](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thief+of+Sanity) effectively draws you extra cards against most decks by feeding you a steady stream of extra spells you can cast. Against aggressive decks, you can take powerful things like [History of Benalia](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=History+of+Benalia) or [Conclave Tribunal](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Conclave+Tribunal) away from them. Against other control decks, the extra [Negate](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Negate)s and countermagic will add up quickly.

Of course, [Hostage Taker](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hostage+Taker) removes a creature that's already hit the battlefield while [Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer) is a gigantic 6/6 that can block, attack to end a game quickly, *and* convert life into card selection.

Big Red is the kind of archetype that pops up over time, when red has a range of answers and a reason to lean into some of the larger midrange things it can do as a color. Against a sea of aggressive decks, cards like [Demanding Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Demanding+Dragon) look great: if your opponent sacrifices a creature, they're down one more way to close the game, but if they don't they're taking 5 and *still* facing down a large 5/5 blocker in the way. [Electrostatic Field](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Electrostatic+Field) similarly blocks well and ensures the series of red removal spells to follow inch a life total down along the way.

[Goblin Chainwhirler](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goblin+Chainwhirler) still kills multiple 1-toughness creatures while [Rekindling Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rekindling+Phoenix) is still the powerful, hard-to-kill blocker and flying threat we know it for. It's a simple-looking deck with a ton of answers, threats, and consistency all baked in with more options and ways to navigate matchups in the sideboard:

*   [Lightning Mare](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Mare) laughs at [Essence Scatter](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Essence+Scatter) and any Izzet Drakes decks hoping to block.
*   [Fight with Fire](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fight+with+Fire) can kill everything short of a [Doom Whisperer](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Whisperer), which is another way of saying *almost anything your opponents are playing*.
*   [Risk Factor](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Risk+Factor) and [Legion Warboss](https://web.archive.org/web/20181115000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Legion+Warboss) can apply pressure to even the most powerful control decks.
