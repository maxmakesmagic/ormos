Every time I think of the *Arabian Nights* expansion, I wonder whatever happened to the other international offerings that Wizards of the Coast never explored. Who wouldn't love to see a "Pacific Islander Mornings," or "Central American Siestas," or "British Afternoon Tea" expansion? ("I'll play Stiff Upper Lip on my Galapagos Tortoise and prevent 3 of the damage from your Equatorial Heat Wave. Cheerialoha, señor!")

But it seems likely, given the investment in creative staff that Wizards has made, that **Magic** will continue to explore completely new and imaginary worlds for the foreseeable future. Nothing wrong with that, I suppose. So *Arabian Nights* will have a great deal of nostalgic value--it's the only expansion that draws extensively on an existing mythology.

Even if you ignore the mythology angle, casual players owe *Arabian Nights* a great deal. This expansion holds Juzam Djinn and Erhnam Djinn, the holy grails of black and green fat (even though both have arguably been outshined by more recent beef). Ring of Ma'ruf served as the inspiration for the recent set of Wish cards, which include Cunning Wish and Living Wish. Cyclone and Ifh-Biff Efreet solidified Hurricane's flavor in green, allowing the color to continue dealing at least some measure of direct damage in later variants such as Squallmonger. Dancing Scimitar can still stop just about any conventional threat you'll find at the table. And, of course, for 5-Color fans, the format would not be complete without an ante card like Jeweled Bird.

Jay will likely do up a bunch of Arabian theme decks on Thursday, so I'm going to confine my remarks to a few specific cards that have stood the test of time in multiplayer play. I can't possibly cover every good card, and many *Arabian Nights* cards exist that would work very well with recent cards but won't get specific mention here. For example, Bazaar of Baghdad is a real winner in an age of threshold, flashback, and Incarnations like Genesis. And Kird Ape is a simple, all-around good creature in any format that allows Taiga. The cards that follow are just my personal favorites--nothing more and nothing less. (Don't expect Shahrazad.) The list is in alphabetical order, not necessarily arranged by power level or corresponding to my affections.

### 1\. Diamond Valley

Being that this is still one of the most amazing multiplayer lands out there, I'm embarrassed to say how long it took me to discover it. I was using High Market for the longest time (and still do at times, because the Valley is fairly expensive and hard to obtain).

Why does Anthony recommend this card, when he professed during life-gain week to an ambivalence (at best) about the mechanic? I like the signal it sends. It reeks of "don't even bother" to your opponents when they're trying to figure out who or what to target. This is especially true when the Valley's sacrifice is used to your advantage--by sacking a Penumbra Wurm, perhaps, or a Serra Avatar. Or why not enchantment-based tricks like Foster or Remembrance?

I know many readers like hearing about new cards blended with old ones. So if I were to find a recent card to join Diamond Valley, it would be Genesis, with Dawn of the Dead as runner-up.

### 2\. Drop of Honey

I did a bit on this in a "Casual Fridays" column and got a short and sweet email from a reader who advanced the simple but elegant theory that "Drop of Honey blows." He was not entirely incorrect. Drop of Honey is an inconsistent card, especially in multiplayer games. It's quite out of flavor (though it does favor the player with fat, all else being equal), and it's fairly slow even in the best of situations.

But it's still green creature destruction, and it's still worth exploring. You can go one of at least four ways with this card. First, you can go creatureless, which is a little difficult for green but not impossible. (You'll use a few artifacts, too, plus cards like Treetop Village and Still Life.) Second, you can use lots of small creatures and force them into the graveyard with Drop of Honey, triggering some awful event like Grave Pact. Third, you can go fat and just use Drop of Honey as fairly random utility against small "tap" creatures like Royal Assassin or Hallowed Healer, which green normally can't touch. Fourth, you can use untargetable creatures, which green has an abundance of (Jolrael's Centaur, Nimble Mongoose, Blastoderm, etc.). According to the Oracle, the Drop is now a targetted effect--much like its *Legends* counterpart The Abyss--and will handily overlook your team.

Current combo? Try Last Laugh to accelerate Drop of Honey's effect.

### 3\. Elephant Graveyard

Elephant decks have never been incredibly powerful, but they're a common recreation among veteran casual players. Just a few **Magic** Elephants include War Elephant (two versions, right here in *Arabian Nights!),* Wild Elephant, Noble Elephant, Iron Tusk Elephant, Bull Elephant, Rogue Elephant, Rampant Elephant, Vintara Elephant, Assault/Battery, and Elephant Resurgence.

A bit heavy on the four and five slots, no? You essentially build with four Moss Diamonds and four Marble Diamonds--and every other green and/or white accelerator you can find.

In *Odyssey,* Elephant decks got a boost. Elephant Ambush, Rabid Elephant, and, of course, Call of the Herd combine to "spread out" your mana curve (the novelty of a three-drop Elephant is no doubt what has Call of the Herd trading in the secondary market for $20) and give your Elephants a bit more panache. Of course, only Rabid Elephant works with Elephant Resurgence; but I'm inclined to blame the problem on the Resurgence which, after all, is in *Prophecy* and therefore, by definition, cannot do anything right.

In *Portal Three Kingdoms* - legal environments, you can also play Southern Elephant, which is a pretty good deal (3/4 for four mana) in case you don't have enough to do on turn four.

### 4\. Khabál Ghoul

Just about every old-school player out there was no doubt waiting for me to mention this card. Before the Cabal, there was the Khabál! And they didn't mess around with tournaments or pretty spheres. While many three-drop black creatures have since outstripped the Ghoul in terms of base power level (from Phyrexian Negator to Nether Spirit), none have the capacity of the Ghoul for growth when combined with other multiplayer staple cards.

There's Oracle text for the Ghoul, but it's clarifying, not modifying: *At end of turn, put a +1/+1 counter on Khabal Ghoul for each creature put into a graveyard from play this turn.* Newer players need to realize this means the Ghoul does *not* have to be in play when the other creatures die. (There's no rules trick here; the text speaks for itself. We're just used to the counters and such being put on creatures "whenever" something happens, not several phases afterward. Compare Khabal Ghoul with Dauthi Ghoul, and you'll see what I mean.)

I think the most punch for your Ghoul comes if you play a March of Souls – Spreading Plague combo (the first set of creatures dies to the March, then a new wave of white creatures comes in and everything dies again to the Plague); but you can forget being unique and just work with Wrath of God or Pernicious Deed or whatever. Once you get the Ghoul large enough, you can use more recent cards like Last Laugh or Glory to end your opponent's pain.

### 5\. Rukh Egg

Perhaps the best known of the five cards I'm showcasing today, Rukh Egg screams for a successor in some future set. (The Egg itself may be too powerful, but Rukhs in general could make for an interesting red creature type.) This card most often combos with Earthquake to give the red mage board supremacy with a lone 4/4 flyer; but you can use cards in any color to get what you need. For one mana or less, you can play Metamorphosis in green (hey, another *Arabian Nights* card! We oughta do a theme or something), Scapegoat in white, Mask of the Mimic in blue, Reckless Abandon in red, and Cabal Therapy in black.

I think my favorite recent (if rather involved) Rukh Egg combo involves using Balthor the Defiled to put my Eggs back together again. Oh, and I can't forget the obvious affinity the Eggs have to Diamond Valley.

### A Word of Caution to the Tourist in Arabia

As a very early set, most of the uncommons and rares (and even commons) in the set are very hard to find and are fairly expensive. Newer players can get burned easily. Ask around your group to borrow or trade, and have your concept nailed down and more accessible cards in hand before you spend any money at all. The last thing you want to do is waste resources on four copies of a somewhat narrow card that end up in your box because you realized a combo didn't work, or you couldn't get your hands on a single common Rabid Elephant to go with your Elephant Graveyard, or whatever.

Of all the cards in the set, I think most casual players will find Rukh Egg to be the best value--it's common, and because it's versatile you'll use it over and over. The bottom line is that you just can't get to love the *Arabian Nights* set without breaking a few Eggs.
