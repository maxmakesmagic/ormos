<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, May 7: 10:22 am - Round 7 Cuts</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Josh Bennett</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Fifteen minutes into the round, Murray &quot;The Mauler&quot; Evans was regretting his decision to wake up. In an elimination match, Benjamin Caumes handed him a 2-0 defeat before you could say Jack Robinson.</p>&#10;      <p>It took a little longer for Masahiko Morita, but he soon found out that it's tough to beat someone who has access to 58 cards. Thanks to two <a>Myojin of Seeing Winds</a>, Tsuyoshi Ikeda only had two cards left in his deck by the time he dealt the killing blow in Game 1. Game 2 followed a similar (but less ridiculous) pattern, and Ikeda swept the match.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/ad/70/ad70ca5a158be4857610622e07db024baa8ece367f64b31ea21fc28d3c8db0a6.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>A crowded rail watches Gadiel dispatch Kamiel.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The remaining Feature Match was Kamiel &quot;The Ideal&quot; Cornelissen against Gadiel Szleifer, whose hat was turned at a jaunty angle, showing that he meant business. The Dutchman was with White Weenie, while the up-and-coming American had a spicy Hana-Control. The back-and-forth of three games brought them to time. After the five turns, it was clear that Szleifer had control of the game, so rather than give them both a loss, Cornelissen scooped to him.</p>&#10;      <p>The bloodbath continues as our field of 105 players was cut down to just 69. Those going back to bed include:</p>&#10;      <p>Brian Kibler<br> Ryuchi Arita<br> Antoine Ruel<br> Bram Snepvangers<br> Bernardo Da Costa Cabral<br> Craig Krempels<br> Akira Asahara<br> Adam Horvath<br> Diego Ostrovich<br> David Brucker</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, May 7: 11:41 pm - Around the Hall</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Josh Bennett</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Just some quick counts for those of you who are interested. Looking at the decks of the undefeateds and 6-1's, we come up with the following counts for the format's key cards:</p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/25/e0/25e05365013f52a8fcae51504b0c3c0121109447be058b25453883ef1163f1b4.jpg">&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Umezawa's Jitte</a>s: 43</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Sensei's Divining Top</a>s: 43</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Sakura-Tribe Elder</a>s: 48</p>&#10;      <p>Those are of a possible 72. Every beatdown deck ran the full number of Jittes. Every green deck ran the full number of Elders. Opinions on non-beatdown Jittes and how many Top's to run varied.</p>&#10;      <p>Also, those 18 decks include a number of White Weenie decks (as well as Morgan Douglass and his army of Ninjas), surprising those who assumed the later rounds would be a sea of control-on-control matches. Could it be that the control players weren't as ready for the Great White Hype as they thought they were?</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, May 7: 12:30 pm - The Field Narrows</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Josh Bennett</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Morgan Douglass finally earned his second loss with ninja at the hands of Jelger Wiegersma's green-white control deck in Round 8. It was a bit of 2-0 carnage, witnessed by Invitationalists Sam Gomersall and Julien Nuijten.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/00/24/00242d73b274e985ebb5ff7b6b2682528fb259273c006218916c05f2552c0133.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>TOGIT to go: Either Fabiano, left, or Harvey would be out after Round 8.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>&quot;Well, I guess I can't have turn-two <a>Ninja of the Deep Hours</a> every game,&quot; bemoaned Douglass.</p>&#10;      <p>Kamiel Cornelissen's match finished soon after, never a good sign for the glacial Dutchman. Osamu Fujita dispatched him 2-0, sending Kamiel to the sidelines.</p>&#10;      <p>Last to finish was the match between teammates Gerard Fabiano and Eugene Harvey, an elimination match. Harvey won the first game handily, but his draws were uncooperative in games two and three. Despite failing to maximize his position (&quot;He should have massacred me,&quot; said Eugene) Fabiano emerged victorious.</p>&#10;      <p>Benched This Round:</p>&#10;      <p>Masahiko Morita<br> Osyp Lebedowicz<br> Richard Hoaen<br> Itaru Ishida<br> Tomohide Sasagawa<br> Ryoma Shiozu<br> Andrew Pacifico</p>&#10;      <p>The field has narrowed to just 44 players, an odd sight at a Pro Tour Day Two. So far, the players have been awarded a total of $118,650, with much more cash to come.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="4"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, May 7: 12:59 pm - The Rotting Giant Guy</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Josh Bennett</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>During the Extended Qualifier Season, there was a little deck that turned a lot of heads: Jim Roy's Metamorphose-Scepter deck. It was an interesting idea, locking someone under Metamorphose and winning at your leisure (Jeff Cunningham ran a Metamorphose in his board of Scepter-Chant against Control), but the one thing people kept coming back to was the single Rotting Giant in his sideboard. The most curious of them all was old-timer John Shuler.</p>&#10;      <p>Now Shuler's not the sort of guy who lets things go easy. He frantically scoured the Internet message boards for a hint at the reasoning for the Giant. He tried again and again to track down Roy's email address, but he failed. Then the idea came to him: Roy would be at the Pro Tour he'd qualified for! It was so simple -- 180 miles was nothing compared to quieting his demons. Of course, by the time Shuler showed up at the tournament on Friday, wild-eyed and expectant, Jim Roy was nowhere to be found.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/93/08/930840d6cbca11931d16787aa3a93cd3a0e8bd854b6e860a1f5f03cbbb37980c.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Jim Roy tried to reveal his secret to John Shuler.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Roy heard about this crazy man who had traveled 200 miles to ask him a question, and so this morning he took an opportunity to give Shuler a call. He got the answering machine, and so left a detailed account of his playtesting and the rationale behind his mighty Giant. </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I'm pretty sure Shuler's going to have a heart attack when he gets this,&quot; said Brian David-Marshall </p>&#10;      <p>Roy is making good use of his qualification. After eight rounds, he was sitting pretty at 7-1 with multicolor control. He unhesitatingly calls <a>Sakura-Tribe Elder</a> the most important card in the format.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Don't forget to tell him your philosophy of 1-of's,&quot; reminded Cunningham.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;It's not something I think a lot of people give thought to,&quot; answered Roy.</p>&#10;      <p>Basically, Roy believes that there are a lot of times when diverse threats are better than redundant threats, something that's made very clear with this block's focus on legends. For him, it's a logical extension of the idea of maximizing the value of the cards you draw.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Ok, maybe Yosei's a bad example,&quot; Roy said.</p>&#10;      <p>And this isn't a philosophy he follows lightly.
