### Friday, May 28th

* * *

*Featuring Justin Ziran, Ramon Arjona, Chris Galvin, Randy Buehler, and Joshua Bentley*

**wizo\_celebration**: Welcome one and all! Thanks for joining us today! The panel will be answering the top-rated questions put to a special forum of **Magic** Online message board over the past two weeks. After each question, chat attendees will have the opportunity to submit follow up questions for clarification. Justin, would you like to go first?

**wotc\_justin**: Hey everyone, I'm Justin, the Brand Manager for **Magic** Online.

**wotc\_ramon**: I'm Ramon, Development Manager.

**wotc\_randy**: Hi, I'm Randy Buehler and I'm the Director of **Magic** R&D. Among my duties and helping WotC run **MagicOnline**

**wotc\_chris**: I'm Chris Galvin, Vice President of Organized Play. Let's get to the questions.

**from\_the\_boards**: What are the plans, if any, for implementing a "3-2-1" Swiss format for 8-person draft and constructed queues? To be precise, the format is 8-person, with a "1 pack per win" prize structure, but using Swiss pairing instead of single elimination. At the end of three rounds, there will be one person who has never lost (3 packs), 3 people who lost once (2 packs) and 3 people who lost twice (1 pack). Complete payout is 3-2-2-2-1-1-1 for a total of 12 prize packs. This is one of the most highly sought formats on the forums (and therefore I expect this should be rated highly), and yet has NEVER been addressed, to my knowledge, by Wizards. If it is not going to happen, we would at least like to know why.

**wotc\_chris**: There are currently no plans for Swiss format draft queues. There are two reasons for this. First, we think it's appropriate to actually award prizes to people who win at least much as they lose under normal tournament conditions. Second, the desire for a tournament forma where you pay an entry fee and just keep playing as much as you want to is valid...but that's why we developed Premier events. Hopefully Premier events will be back online soon, if the recently completed Beta is any indication.

**roamer3025**: Will there be Premier Events that take less time then the standard 6+ hours?

**wotc\_chris**: Once we have Premier Event functionality is available on production again, we will review the total list of offerings for Premier events. So, in short, possibly yes.

**jimwolf**: Will there be Premier Drafts? I only remember tournaments.

**wotc\_chris**: By tournaments, I assume you mean sealed deck. Large draft tournaments turn out to not be as popular as might imagine. We've tried running them on **MTGO** to limited success. Having said that, once the functionality is back, we'll review all requests. We've tried Premier Event drafts in the past. We'll try them again.

**wotc\_randy**: We have definitely heard the calls for Swiss-paired events that are shorter than traditional Premier events and we do plan to try to find a way to do them. We just think the 8-person queues are the wrong place for them

**from\_the\_boards**: Are there plans for Wizards to release an official list of known issues (Card bugs and game functionality problems)? Right now the only method for users to get any insights into what problems exist is searching through Bugzilla. However, it is not customer friendly at all. There needs to be a better plan to get this information into the hands of players. (If there are no plans for implementing such a list, how does Wizards plan to make this information more accessible to the customer base? 

**wotc\_ramon**: Yes, we intend to provide a known issues list. We are currently working on our process for generating and updating that list regularly for players to reference. We expect the list to reside somewhere on the M:TG website.

**Trystero**: What is the timeframe for this list to be available?

**wotc\_ramon**: Next couple of weeks, most likely.

**jimwolf** : Will this list appear on the updated **MagicOnline** website? It's getting old over there.

**wotc\_justin**: Yes, the website is old. We intend on updating the **MTGO** site soon.

**from\_the\_boards**: Are there any plans to implement optional text comments in buddy lists and block lists? It would really handy to have this in game, to help us remember why certain individuals landed on either list in the first place. Examples: "Buddy List: Goodguy1-very helpful trader, Goodguy2-Had lots of fun with this person in a match. Block List: Nastyguy1-Scammer. Nastyguy2-Bad experience in-game."

**wotc\_justin**: Short answer is not yet. The longer answer is that we agree that this feature would be very handy but our priorities for v2.x are to: One: return functionality. Two: ensure stability. Three: work our way through the bug list. That is not to say we will not add a feature here and there but that will be the exception, not the rule. Buddy list comments are on the v3.x wish list.

**Legorol**: Do you have any plans to make the block list longer than it currently is, at least?

**wotc\_justin**: Most of the follow question are feature request that will be handled in 3.x.

**from\_the\_boards**: A long time ago an uproar went through the community as the prize structure of Premier events were drastically changed (and decreased). The payout used to be depending on the amount of players participating in that event. The same as it is in real life which **Magic** Online wants to mirror as closely as possible. Then, because of the various payouts which "completely confused" the people it was drastically decreased to a constant payout per event. Why was the prize structure really changed? I don't think people who are actually able to learn and play **Magic** will get confused by a various amount of prizes. Will the payout ever be fixed again? Or do you think the lesser payout is the right one (making more profit and such alike)? 

**wotc\_chris**: Your question is predicated on the belief that we set our tournament prizes based on an expectation of the impact they will have on our sales. This predicated assumption is false. Factors that influence our decisions about prizes include appropriateness, fairness, feasibility of implementation, and an understanding of the kind of behavior that a given prize structure will provide incentive. For instance, if we gave out one prize pack to everyone who played in a tournament except the guy who comes in First Place, that produces one kind of behavior. If we give 1000 prize packs to the guy in first place and zero to everyone else, that produces another kind of behavior. Our belief, based on what we've learned over the last 10 years with **Magic**, is that most people are best served by a prize structure like we have now. At those tournaments that Wizards runs itself we use versions of the same prize payout from **MTGO**. Or, to be more specific, the **MTGO** prize payouts were based largely on how we give out prizes at side events at the *Pro Tour*. What happens at events that are nod directly run by Wizards is up to the people running those events.

**Lrgetrout**: So why not have several different payouts available, and let the people choose which one they want?

**wotc\_randy**: We can definitely consider that The one thing we're committed to is always advertising the exact prizes for a tournament ahead of time and then giving away exactly what we said we were going to give away We have in the past had tournaments with double prizes, etc. And we will continue to do that in the future.

**from\_the\_boards**: Most online games/programs when updated allow you to "view recent changes" after the update is completed so you can look at what the heck you just downloaded. It seems like Justin will post updates in the Announcement area, which is great, but still, wouldn't something that we can view on the client be better for everyone? Not everyone reads the boards and knows what is going on. I don't think it would hurt anyone/thing, only help. 

**wotc\_ramon**: We certainly want to devise a more effective means of communicating information on client and system updates. In the near term, we are planning on posting a link to the information about recent updates in the Main Room announcements on the client. The information will also be available at a dedicated location on our website. We will be posting the URL sometime next week.

**from\_the\_boards**: As you all know, the Editor 4 copy limit was recently lifted. Open format was used as a sort of Online Vintage format, where all online cards could be played (as opposed to Online Extended where 4 can't). However, now open games are predominantly used to play decks that use the option of adding more than 4 copies of a card to a deck. Are there any plans to introduce such an Online Vintage format now that Open will no longer be able to serve that purpose? Or do you think Open should be sufficient until IPA rotates out of Extended?

**wotc\_randy**: When we made the change to the Deck Editor, we knew that this would fundamentally change the meaning of the Open format. Specifically, Open would now truly mean "open" as it was always intended) and we expected most people who used to play Open to switch to Extended. Of course, you are right to point out that there actually a 4-card difference between "old open" and Extended: the 3 pre-Invasion promo cards that we distributed plus the one card currently banned in Extended. We didn't think it was worth having a whole separate format just to accommodate those 4 cards, but I have heard more complaints about this subject over the last month than I was expecting (plus this question received a lot of votes), so I am going to ask the appropriate team to re-open this issue and make sure we're happy with our decision. You are right to point out that we will definitely need some sort of "Online Vintage" format eventually, but our plan has been to introduce that format when IPA rotates out of Extended (in the year 2008). In general we believe it is a bad thing to have too many different formats floating around (because it splits the audience and you wind up with some people who want to play format A and some people who want to play format B and they all wind up sitting around waiting for opponents instead of actually having fun playing **Magic** against each other) so I think we're probably going to stick to our initial decision. After all, people can still play with their Sliver Queens and Entombs in Open. But we do hear you and I'll make sure the right people revisit this issue.

**jimwolf** : There are far more people available to play against **Magic Online** as compared to your average card store. Can't this population support more types of formats?

**wotc\_randy**: Well yes, but there's still a limit If we get into the business of making formats that are only four cards different, we'll wind up with too many.

**legorol**: There are several popular casual formats such as "peasant," which are not supported officially. Do you plan to add more fun formats like that?

**wotc\_randy**: We do plan to add more formats to the game in the future, yes. There's nothing imminent, but the number of available formats (including casual formats) will indeed go up over the years.

**from\_the\_boards**: Who is the target consumer for **MTGO**? What is the place of **MTGO** relatively to the paper version? Is **MTGO** a competitor or an add-on for the paper version? What is the place of **MTGO** under the whole Wizards umbrella? In your budget, priority, desirability, is **MTGO** equal, lower or higher than the paper version?

**wotc\_justin**: We have two primary audiences. First, there's the electronic hobby gamers and non-**Magic** TCG players. Second, there is the lapsed **Magic** TCG player. This doesn't mean that a consumer that does not fit these audiences is not important to us. There is nothing farther from the truth. These target audiences provides a framework for the business team to make decisions, such as where do we advertise and what do we say in those ads? Regarding **MTGO** place under the Wizards umbrella. **Magic** is considered one of Hasbro's core brands and thus is treated just like any other core brand with respect to budget and priority (it's very important). When I sit down with Kyle (Director of **Magic** Marketing) and Joe (Vice President of **Magic: The Gathering**) and talk about **MTGO**, it is always within the context of the **Magic** brand. The only time the brands are separated is when we talk about executing tactics where are **Magic** ads placed? Where are **MTGO** ads placed?). Our philosophy is brand first, then platforms.

**twilightaria**: Can you explain what "core brand" means for those that don't know?

**wotc\_justin**: It's a short list of the most important brands to the company.

**jimwolf**: Do you foresee a time when **MTGO** could "grow" in stature? Like a time when we would rate our own *Unglued* and *Unhinged* style set?

**wotc\_randy**: We definitely imagine **MTGO** growing both in stature and revenue and features. As far as *Unhinged* goes, the decision not to release it on **MTGO** had nothing to do with stature The simple truth is that "Un-sets" (like *Unglued* and *Unhinged*) rely too much on physical interacts, such as dexterity cards that don't make sense on **MTGO**, like running to buy your opponent a soda. Too many of the mechanics can't be implementing within a computer game, so we're releasing it for paper only.

**from\_the\_boards**: For a long time, we have known that **Magic Online** has had a significant memory leak, making it difficult to do everything from trade to play games after you've been on for a little while. What, if anything, is being done to address this problem? Can we expect a fix in 3.0? Can we expect a fix beforehand? 

**wotc\_ramon**: We very much would like to fix the memory issue in the client, but other fixes have taken priority. We do not know if it will be fixed the v2.0 environment, it should not be an issue for v3.0

**from\_the\_boards**: I think it would be highly educating and helpful to have a function that let you replay a draft from your point of view, with the standard play, pause, fast forward and rewind functions. 

**wotc\_justin**: We all agree! This feature is on the v3.x wish list. As I mentioned before, our priorities are to restore functionality and stability, then tackle some of the pesky bugs.

**from\_the\_boards**: Can we get some kind of discount for buying multiple boosters at once, like the box discount at many retail shops? Has this ever been considered? Are there "on-line" reasons as to why this can't be done?

**wotc\_justin**: Our price is based on business decisions that were made at launch. We don't have any control of what price retailer sells paper **Magic** for. We want to provide a level playing field (in terms of price) for consumers no matter how many booster packs they buy.

**from\_the\_boards**: Can we get an actual "support staff" in the MaSS room? The adepts are woefully inadequate for the job, since they have no more info than the customers do and can only provide canned links to websites, help files, or email addresses. 

**wotc\_josh**: Good evening, folks! I should be able to answer this. This is Joshua, the Software Support Manager for Customer Service. It is the job of the Adepts to help make the community a better place. Specifically, "to moderate the game rooms, keeping them clean for our community to enjoy." It is the job of Software Support to support the technical aspects of the game and store. This includes troubleshooting, help with ordering, etc. Due to overwhelming workload, we pulled the Software Support folks out of the MaSS room so they could provide better turn around times via phone and email. Will we ever staff the room with Software Support folks again? Currently there are not plans to do so. Their job is to answer our phones and emails, while the "support staff" we do provide are the Adepts. The Adepts are available on a constant basis, but due to the number of cries for help they receive, they need to be able to use quick responses and direct people to the answers they need without having to spend a great deal of time manually answering each individual question.

**from\_the\_boards**: What steps are being taken to prevent another disaster during the *Fifth Dawn* release like we saw during the *Fifth Dawn* and *Darksteel* releases? What is the back-up plan in case something does go wrong? Will there be any special events for *Fifth Dawn* at all? 

**wotc\_ramon**: We are taking several steps to ensure that the *Fifth Dawn* release goes smoothly. First, as with other sets, we will be beta testing the set to ensure there are no known card rules bugs when we launch. Additionally, we have worked very hard in recent months to improve system stability and ensure our system will support the *Fifth Dawn* release. If we do encounter issues with the release, our back up plan will depend on what those problems are, but our overriding priority is to stabilize the system anytime we encounter issues. It is probably safe to assume that there will be special events for *Fifth Dawn,* we are await the results of the Premier Events beta to finalize those plans.

**gnarly\_gnome:** Have the *Darksteel* release events been completely abandoned?

**wotc\_justin**: No, they have not been abandoned, there is a question later in the queue about this.

**from\_the\_boards**: Is there a time frame for fixing some of the more minor bugs such as the "My Games" bug inability to offline message buddies. Have these been pushed to the back burner to focus on more pressing issues like Premier Events, Leagues, and the Invitational?

**wotc\_justin**: Generally speaking, our plan is to provide a stable game platform, return functionality (Leagues, Premier Events, and the Free Trial Server). Once those major pieces of work have been completed we will shift out focus to fixing the issues that have been reports. Those issues will be prioritized by consumer impact & resource needs

**from\_the\_boards**: In future closed betas, will high level/pro players still be allowed to use the server as free *Pro Tour* practice, despite their rudeness towards actual testers/willingness to admit that they have no intention of actually beta testing at all? 

**wotc\_justin**: Moving forward, we will move to a 'sign up for beta' model for testing. There may be a need to run open betas but as a general rule testers will be selected from the sign up list. Initially, we will use our current tester list (which includes high level players, Level 3+ judges and select **MTGO** users) and a number of users from the 'sign up' list. After the next beta, we will evaluate the performance of the testers and invite those that performed up to our expectations to subsequent betas.

**sir\_bojangles**: Will the high level players be evaluated also?

**wotc\_justin**: Yes.

**circular\_logic**: Given the potentially enormous number of people who "sign up," how will testers be chosen?

**wotc\_randy**: A bunch of people asked this question. No one in the room right now actually knows what the selection process is though. We will look into this and try to make sure that team provides you guys with the information you want.

**from\_the\_boards**: Are there any plans to make the 1700 room actually appeal to 1700 players? On average, a 4-3-2-2 draft now consists of four to six 1700 level players, which means their argument that they "can't find enough drafters to draft in there" argument simply doesn't fly. Given the current state of the room, if there are no plans to actually support the room, then it should simply be eliminated. 

**wotc\_randy**: I definitely think it is time for us to analyze the results of our 1700-room experiment and figure out what's working and what is not working. Lately, we've been spending our time and energy thinking about more important issues, but with the Premier Events beta going well it looks like we may be able to get to this issue soon. You are right to point out that some aspects of the room have clearly failed: it has not become the cool place for 1700+ players to hang out and chat, it has not become a place where good players can playtest casual constructed and know they're opponent will be another highly rated player, and there are also not enough people there who want to play constructed tournaments either. On the other hand, the 9-5 draft queues are at least somewhat popular. Of course, they don't fire as often as the queues in the regular draft room and many 1700+ players prefer to draft right now rather than wait for a 9-5 to start (still others prefer to draft against weaker competition), so they haven't been a total success either. Like I said, it's time for us to analyze all the data and figure out what's best for the game and the community. I'm honestly not sure at this time what the right answer is, but we're definitely planning to look into this and figure out how to make things better.

**sir\_bojangles**: Would you ever consider doing a sub-1500 queue for players who just need drafting experience that isn't against really good people?

**wotc\_randy**: The problem with that is that it's easy to abuse anyone who wants to get into the room can just by losing on purpose. We've heard lots of calls for tournaments and things that have a ratings "cap," so it's not that we haven't heard you, it's just that those ideas have a fatal flaw. Anything we do to make those rooms attractive will just lead people to lose on purpose.

**from\_the\_boards**: How large is the programming/development team of **MTGO**? Is somebody on site 24/7? What exactly happens when the server crashes in the middle of the night and can't reboot itself?

**wotc\_ramon**: The size of our staff is confidential, but we are on call 24/7. When the server crashes, adepts make calls to Customer Service and the Hasbro Command Center. The Hasbro Command Center notifies the on call staff at Wizards and the troubleshooting process begins.

**from\_the\_boards**: What will the new **MTGO** Tuesday articles be like? Will we be seeing more articles discussing current issues and future plans (not necessarily problems, but things like v3.0 development, new features, etc.)? Will it be a strategy-based article discussing things like League and Online Extended play, which are exclusively **MTGO**-based? Or will it become just another weekly article that just happens to mention **MTGO** from time to time? 

**wotc\_justin**: The simple answer is that it will be a combination of everything you mentioned. I've been very careful so as to not define what Chad writes but rather provide guidelines. Every article will have a definite **MTGO** angle but other than that I have given free reign to create a column that is relevant for you the consumer. We might even give Chad access to sneak peaks of upcoming sets and v3.x development plans. What you won't see in the column is a list of know issues, updates on Beta tests

**from\_the\_boards**: This has undoubtedly been posted before, but since its a vital part of the **Magic Online** game and since its in my opinion has been obsolete before version 2.0 I want to point out my frustration over this old trade system. The limit of 32 objects per trade is an absolute horror. I have bought auctions on Ebay of 12,000+ cards and it literally takes hours to trade. I currently have more than 70,000 cards on an account that are extra and it's really frustrating to think about organizing them. With this current structure of trade system everything is reduced to manual work. The strength of the computer is not used and that is unacceptable. Here are four suggestions for an improved trade system: 1.) Use the idea of virtual repacks. Making it possible to mark a full set or all of a certain card into a virtual object that can count as part of the trade limit. 2.) Simply raise the trade limit. But raise it high. 3.) Allow easier way of marking cards for trade, like have an option of making one of each card in *Invasion* tradable, or one of each common in *Invasion* tradable. Do this in a few clicks, even if I have unequal numbers of the various *Invasion* commons. 4) Allow the possibility to transfer everything marked for trade over to the trade window with one click, instead of having to click on every single cards (this will only gets worse in the future when peoples account are getting bigger). I hope there will be some serious work on the trade system in the near future as being a seller of **Magic** online cards is extremely time consuming.

**wotc\_justin**: We are aware of the limitations of our current trade system. There are many strategic issues that need to be discussed before we decide to change the trading system. We plan on reviewing the trade system in version 3.x. In the mean time, the raising the 32-card limit is being discussed but as of today we can't give you a definitive answer as to how that limit will change.

**paradisio**: Are there any plans to revise the marketplace? We know that there are several different kinds of customers who want trades to work in several different kinds of ways our goal for v2.0 was to make three options available, which we thought would cover everyone adequately. Of course, casual trading turned out to be bugged and we had to remove it from he game So trading will get better eventually, but it won't get better until after v3.0.

**from\_the\_boards**: Will you be soliciting player based suggestions as to features for v3.0? The developers have said that they read the suggestions on the boards. Is it possible we could get confirmation of what ideas they like and dislike?

**wotc\_justin**: Absolutely, but I want to move the suggestion box from the message boards (which tend to get crazy) to another official location on our website. I'll shoot you an announcement with the link next week. 

**from\_the\_boards**: What are the current plans for general compensation of the *Darksteel* crash? 

**wotc\_justin**: We have not forgotten about *Darksteel* crash. We have been thinking of creative ways to reward you for your patience. We'd like to incorporate it into Premier Events. There will be more news once Premier Events are up and running.

**rivalry**: What about the events and the avatars themselves? Doesn't seem fair that only a few got them.

**wotc\_randy**: We are considering options so we can work those into the general compensation.

**gnarly\_gnome**: any chance of *Darksteel* events? Many of us would still love to try DDD drafts

**wotc\_randy**: We're imaging some sort of combination *Darksteel* avatar and Welcome Back Premier Events event. As far as DDD drafts go, we're not planning to have those.

**jimwolf**: Are there any plans to make this a more regular event?

**wotc\_justin**: Funny you should ask. The answer is yes. We'll let you know when the next chat session will take place. We want to get through more of the questions. I'll post the date and time next week.

**wizo\_celebration**: Thanks for joining us! I'd like to thank the Wizards folks for joining us and answering so many questions!

**wotc\_randy**: Thanks for coming to listen to us guys.
