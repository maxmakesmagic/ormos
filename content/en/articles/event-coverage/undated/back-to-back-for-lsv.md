## Grand Prix Atlanta 2008

![](https://i.ormos.uk/80/76/80767dc38f56c6e1e270dadab012b5cd7783e16ca49fb706b91122a2178966ce.jpg)

It's kind of hard to believe that just a couple of weeks ago, we were watching Luis Scott-Vargas hoist his first Pro Tour trophy and here he is again hoisting his second Grand Prix champion trophy. Back-to-back victories are almost inconceivable, and yet LSV has managed to do just that, and even across completely different formats! LSV has proven his skill time and again, and his victory here just cements that he is one of the best all-around players in the world. Luis Scott-Vargas has definitely earned the right to be named the Grand Prix-Atlanta champion!

## Top 8 Draft - Murderer's Row

*by Nate Price*

I found quite the little alley of talent along one side of the table. Luis Scott-Vargas sandwiched between Gerry Thompson and Steve Wolansky is quite the little murderer's row of players to contend with in this Top 8. When you get players of this caliber so close to one another, you can expect signals to be crisp and decks to be strong.

From the get go, the three of them set themselves in their colors pretty well. Thompson started off with an [Oblivion Ring](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oblivion+Ring), which was the obvious choice in his pack. [Feral Hydra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Feral+Hydra) LSV's first pick, but he quickly abandoned it when he noticed the Esper cards coming through Wolansky, who had been selecting Bant cards to go with the [Battlegrace Angel](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Battlegrace+Angel) he opened. Thompson marked himself as the Naya drafter with his third-pick Wooly Thoctar (his favorite card from the weekend). As the packs continued, it became clear that LSV was in the perfect seat for Esper. Or rather that Wolansky was, but chose to stick to his decision for Bant.

At the end of the pack, Thompson's deck was a little sub-par to start with, but he hadn't really locked himself in as the Naya drafter. He had very few red cards, and would be able to switch to Bant if things started to lean that way. LSV was solidly Esper, but he had picked up a couple of Tri-Lands and might be able to pick up some Grixis cards if he felt the urge. He managed to pick up Big Poppa [Sphinx Sovereign](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sphinx+Sovereign) early in the pack, as well as some very late [Tidehollow Strix](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tidehollow+Strix)es and a [Windwright Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windwright+Mage). That's about as clear a signal as you can get that people didn't like [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island)s. Wolansky had a similar situation to Thompson. He was solidly in white and green, but he wasn't necessarily Bant. The next packs would determine for certain the shards they represented.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/80/ce/80ce44cc996fc0e7eb9175a2f21713b1211b18ad50e5a7a4ecd42bde9bdae8c2.jpg)*These guys might as well form a gang.*

Thompson stayed true to the green white cards he had, and when he picked a [Ridge Rannet](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ridge+Rannet) about sixth, it became clear that he meant to stay Naya. He didn't really see any red cards, but he clearly meant to grab them when he could. LSV kept strong with his Esper cards he picked up a bunch of Capsules of both the Courier and Executioner variety, as well as a couple of [Sanctum Gargoyle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sanctum+Gargoyle)s to recur them. Add them to his Spire Gargoyle, and you have a powerful air force. Thanks to his multiple Tri-Lands, he was able to dip into red for a [Kederekt Creeper](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kederekt+Creeper). That showed how powerful the versatility of the lands can be in this format. Wolansky kept his plan alive, just snagging all the best Bant creatures, but especially focusing on the exalted creatures.

Thompson came out of this pack with a few new tricks, such as a [Qasali Ambusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Qasali+Ambusher), but he was also forced to take a bunch of average cards in positions where LSV was picking up monsters. As you can guess from that, LSV just continued picking up the most unreal cards. His deck got more consistent with the addition of the Capsules and more resilient with the [Sanctum Gargoyle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sanctum+Gargoyle)s. Right now, I'd be hard pressed to find a better two-pack deck at the table. Hell, he may be able to beat what some people make with three with his first two packs. Wolansky's deck got pretty nuts when he was passed an [Empyrial Archangel](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Empyrial+Archangel) for his second pick from LSV. It made a nice top end for his army of Stewards of Valeron and [Sigiled Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sigiled+Paladin)s.

 

![Mayael the Anima](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Mayael+the+Anima)

The third pack is what really drove it home. Thompson got a [Mayael the Anima](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mayael+the+Anima) and another [Qasali Ambusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Qasali+Ambusher), as well as some tricks like [Naya Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Naya+Charm). His deck really found the pieces to make it a solid competitor in the third pack. LSV just continued to upgrade his average cards to good ones. He started by dipping further into red when he opened [Caldera Hellion](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Caldera+Hellion). He also snagged a [Vithian Stinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vithian+Stinger) in the third pack. These cards served the dual purpose of strengthening his deck while weakening Thompson's since he would most certainly windmill them if they reached him. Perhaps the most surprising card he got in the last pack was the fifth pick Shardling Sphinx. At this point, though, it was pretty clear that no one else really had islands, and surely no one else had artifacts. Combine that with the [Master of Etherium](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Master+of+Etherium) he got earlier, and his deck had a bazillion rares that everyone wants for their Esper deck. Wolansky finished off strong with a few more exalted creatures and some more combat tricks. His deck has enough speed to win early, and enough exalted to win late if goes that far.  

## Quarterfinal Roundup

*by Nate Price*

Gerry Thompson vs. Brett Piazza

The first couple games of this match were fairly one sided one way or the other. For the first game, Gerry got an early 3/3 [Wild Nacatl](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wild+Nacatl) and some exalted creatures to pump it. He had enough removal to make sure that Brett never caught up, and eventually he fell. Brett got his revenge in the second game, though. Brett had all the men in the world while Gerry sat watching Brett beat his face in.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/80/55/80555f458bfc7d9db596c7444613776050462cea898e8cacd7decb7f37addfe6.jpg)

The third game wasn't too much better. A second-turn [Sigiled Paladin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sigiled+Paladin) was eaten by a [Fleshbag Marauder](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fleshbag+Marauder) that took down a [Dregscape Zombie](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dregscape+Zombie) in the process. Gerry used a [Naya Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Naya+Charm) to get it back, and he quickly replayed it. Gerry added a [Rakeclaw Gargantuan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakeclaw+Gargantuan) to his side to start the push. Brett was stuck without any [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain)s, which stranded a bunch of removal in his hand. When Gerry played [Titanic Ultimatum](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Titanic+Ultimatum) to take the game, Brett revealed his unfortunately all-red hand.

Gerry Thompson defeats Brett Piazza 2-1

Luis Scott-Vargas vs. Ken Adams

Luis rolled over Ken in the first game. He managed to get a ridiculous [Etherium Sculptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Etherium+Sculptor)-fueled [Master of Etherium](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Master+of+Etherium) in play, and the giant artephile smashed Ken into oblivion. Ken got right back in the next game, though, with a nice series of plays. He had gotten a lot of early aggression against LSV's slower draw, and managed to drop him to six. However, LSV had finally started to get back into it with his Shardling Sphinx, and was now a mere two turns away from killing Ken. Ken drew a [Blightning](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blightning) to drop LSV to three. LSV sent his flying team in and dropped Ken to two. Ken drew his card, played a land, and used his [Naya Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Naya+Charm) to return the [Blightning](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blightning) from the bin to finish LSV off. Such a dramatic comeback really awed the crowd, who seemed to rally behind Ken's improbable escape.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/a7/ac/a7accc6cf86586c6c5e31b5a734c5e2011370b3463049140c8bd72c038fd1ea3.jpg)

One game does not a match make, though, and LSV got a similar steamroll hand to the first game and just ran right over Ken. Ken wasn't completely helpless, though, and used [Blightning](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blightning) to end the game, aimed at himself instead of LSV.

Luis Scott-Vargas defeats Ken Adams 2-1

Chris Fennell vs. Chris Pait

This match featured two incredibly close games, and if Fennell hadn't teamed up with another planeswalker, he might not have pulled out the win. Fennell trusted [Ajani Vengeant](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Vengeant) to take control of the board early, but Pait got to use [Naya Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Naya+Charm) to tap Fennell's men out, allowing an alpha strike to finish the planeswalker off. One down, one to go. Fennell had built up a nice army with [Necrogenesis](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrogenesis), though, and was able to slowly start to make some pores in Pait's defense. Eventually, the levy broke and Fennell flooded in for the win.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b4/8b/b48b5b47a78a9954e2ef05b230dbd3e4b943a5f30285ac32439b434dd27eea87.jpg)

The second game featured an army of [Necrogenesis](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Necrogenesis) tokens from Fennell that were facing off with a [Cavern Thoctar](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cavern+Thoctar). Fennell never had enough of them in play at once to take it down, but he was able to keep his life total safe. Over a few turns, through a flurry of attacks and removal spells, the board ended up featuring only a [Naya Battlemage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Naya+Battlemage) and a [Guardians of Akrasa](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Guardians+of+Akrasa). Eventually, though, the [Ajani Vengeant](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Vengeant) came back and helped Fennell's team find the promised land of the Semifinals.

Chris Fennell defeats Chris Pait 2-0

Steven Wolansky vs. Tomoharu Saitou

This match was epic. I kind of figured it would be when I saw that these two had the honor (read: unfortunate pairing) of playing each other in the quarters. This was an exalted battle, which is incredibly boring for a good portion of the game since no one really starts to block until there are a few creatures on the board. Then it goes from boring to confusing. Saitou had the early advantage, as his creatures were better and larger natural and consequently even better with exalted. Even still, Wolansky managed to stall the game long enough to get his [Empyrial Archangel](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Empyrial+Archangel) out.

It didn't look like it would be enough, especially since Saitou had a [Rockcaster Platoon](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rockcaster+Platoon) in play. However, Saitou couldn't play recklessly. In the early exalted exchange, he had gotten whittled down nearly as bad as Wolansky. Wolansky eventually got a [Rockcaster Platoon](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rockcaster+Platoon) of his own into play, and the giant rhinotaurs played an important role in deciding the game. The Archangel had knocked Saitou down to five, which was lower than Wolansky. That meant that he couldn't really afford to wait. Saito swung with his team in an attempt to win it, but Wolansky merely had to activate his Platoon twice to clear the skies of the [Waveskimmer Aven](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Waveskimmer+Aven) and [Cloudheath Drake](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudheath+Drake). That also dropped Saitou to one, and Saitou's attack only dropped Wolansky to one. Steve just untapped and swung back to take the first game.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/11/85/1185617600133081b9452811b5ad3555b4a3130d5b053e037ac50f1c61d7c48a.jpg)

The next game had a third-turn Wooly Thoctar for Saitou which threatened to end things fast. Wolansky had his own monster early in the game. Well, kind of. His second turn [Manaplasm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Manaplasm) got some serious action thanks to a [Waveskimmer Aven](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Waveskimmer+Aven). After the attack, Wolansky led 15-13. Saitou just kept playing monsters, though, and his [Rockcaster Platoon](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rockcaster+Platoon) came down for the second game. Wolansky kept up with the exalted guys, and his new [Akrasan Squire](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Akrasan+Squire) went right alongside his next conditional monster: [Knight of the Skyward Eye](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+the+Skyward+Eye). Over the next couple turns, Wolansky failed to get anything really effective on the board, and the Thoctar went the distance.

Gerry had to keep it alive, though, and he used a [Sigil Blessing](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sigil+Blessing) to make sure it survived. The Goyles knocked him to five after the Capsule finished the Guide off. Gerry had nothing other than a [Druid of the Anima](https://web.archive.org/web/20220629234223/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Druid+of+the+Anima) next turn, and LSV rode the Gargoyles to the title.
