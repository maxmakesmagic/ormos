### 

Okay, so maybe I should get smacked around a couple of times for the horrible pun in the title, but the deck discussed below does deserve a careful, umm, examination. Day 1 of World Championships served to create the current Standard metagame. With no major Standard tournaments forthcoming, the metagame would get to stay this way until *Onslaught* becomes legal in Standard, so one should pay attention to what was successful and what wasn't.

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>Psychatog, Still Public Enemy #1?</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

One might assume that Psychatog dominated the tournament - and perhaps one wouldn't be all that wrong. After all, two of the three people to go undefeated in Standard utilized 'Tog - and the overall finals of the tournament saw Psychatogs battle against each other. However, statistics show that 'Tog was not the most successful Standard deck. Randy Buehler recently posted a breakdown of decks that achieved a 5-1 or better record at Worlds and the results were as follows:

*   u-g madness - 6
*   u-b 'Tog - 4
*   r-u-b 'Tog - 3
*   Opposition - 1
*   b-r-g Braids - 1
*   u-g threshold - 1
*   Hunting Grounds - 1
*   r-u madness - 1
*   r-g Beats - 1
*   Red Zone 2k2 (aka r-g-w beatdown) - 1

One might argue that despite the design differences, u-b and r-u-b 'Tog should fall within the same category, which would make that the most successful archetype. Whether you buy that argument or not, u-g madness decks cannot be ignored regardless.

One of the six madness decks at 5-1 was navigated by yours truly. Although its design was a team effort, Zvi Mowshowitz deserves most of the credit for the main deck, and Rob Dougherty for the sideboard. Here is the list of what I played:

9 Forest  
 9 Island  
 4 Yavimaya Coast

1 Aquamoeba  
 4 Arrogant Wurm  
 4 Basking Rootwalla  
 4 Merfolk Looter  
 4 Wild Mongrel  
 3 Wonder

4 Careful Study  
 4 Circular Logic  
 4 Counterspell  
 2 Deep Analysis  
 4 Roar of the Wurm

Sideboard

1 Bearscape  
 4 Phantom Centaur  
 4 Spellbane Centaur  
 4 Sylvan Safekeeper  
 2 Unnatural Selection

Much has been written about the archetype over the last six months, so I am going to Concentrate on the latest changes and additions to the deck which, I believe, helped me achieve a better result in Standard than a more traditional version would have.

### The Mana

![](https://i.ormos.uk/54/45/54452d901ceae610146a6145387e21d31c890ab0d6e8b67a2927611f578bf754.jpg)

Although the mana seems like it should be fairly straightforward in a two-color deck, it is not. U-g madness is a very aggressive deck that relies greatly on having a smooth mana draw - you need both blue and green mana right away. It was a really close call between playing the configuration I eventually went with or running an extra island and one less forest. It was also a dilemma as to whether to play Centaur Gardens. In a number of matchups, Garden becomes an excellent weapon, especially since the deck gains threshold easily. However, whereas Garden helps in your good matchups, it can be a serious liability in the matchups that are already difficult. I chose not to play any, but if you disagree, try replacing two forests with a pair of Gardens. I do not really like Cephalid Coliseum in the deck at all basically because you can't really afford to play TOO many painful lands, and Garden is simply better for what this deck does.

### The Creatures

The most important card in this deck is Merfolk Looter. For example, the matchup against Psychatog is generally won or lost based on whether you can keep a Merfolk Looter in play. In addition to providing card advantage, Looters are also one of the many ways to drop a turn 3 Arrogant Wurm, or a turn 4 Roar of the Wurm. The creatures in this deck truly are self-explanatory, so I will only touch a couple of the more interesting points briefly:

*   What's the deal with Aquamoeba?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/83/36/8336d5b10c98a9c7d8de1152b00700bfbc16fcd49fc21cf96b8212d0765eb7c9.jpg)

Essentially, this is the fifth Wild Mongrel. There are so many cards you want in your graveyard, four Looters, four Mongrels, and four Careful Studies still isn't enough. Aquamoeba is actually pretty good in its own right - you can activate it almost every turn without actually losing card economy to attack for three. So why is there only one of them? There simply isn't enough room. There are plenty of cards you want to be playing, or could be playing in this deck, but only enough room for sixty. 

*   3 Wonders or 4?

This is a tough call. On one hand, with so much library manipulation you will rarely get stuck with too many Wonders, and you definitely want to have one in your graveyard in most matchups. On another hand, drawing more than one Wonder does nothing for you and playing a fourth one uses an ever-so-limited spot in your deck. After much testing, it was decided that three is a right number - but if you REALLY like your Wonder, playing a fourth is not a mistake, just a choice.

### The Spells

![](https://i.ormos.uk/be/e2/bee221747817537867b2c0273a776d682d82a6f07225ffe9b67fb36bcd98107b.jpg)

You've got your Roars, Careful Studies and Circular Logics... but where is Aether Burst, Repulse or even Unsummon? That is what makes this version of the deck superior - or so we believe. There is no bounce! It has been replaced with more countermagic. This is actually quite logical. Against what deck do you really want to return their creatures to hand at the moment as opposed to casting more creatures of your own, or countering more of theirs? Four additional Counterspells are a huge boon to the deck. You are now capable of often out-countering a Psychatog deck - especially if they are forced to spend some of their counters early to slow down your creature onslaught. Although eight is not a huge number of counterspells, keep in mind that you are going through your cards very fast with help from Looters and Careful Studies, and so would be drawing your countermagic with reasonable consistency. Depending on what the situation is on the table, you will either use Counterspells very frugally, or be willing to stop pretty much anything your opponent might try to cast - this is dependant entirely on the situation, but you will generally know the right answer when it comes up. 

### The Sideboard

Unnatural Selection and Spellbane Centaur are your weapons in the mirror match, as well as against Opposition decks. Selection will allow you to change the first creature's type to Legend and kill off any more copies of that creature by changing them to Legend as well. There cannot be two of the same Legend in play at the same time, and so the second one immediately bites the dust. This works pretty well against Squirrel tokens, too. Obviously, Selection will do nothing with Spellbane Centaur in play, so although you might board them in at the same time, you must also be careful in how you play them.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b6/39/b63907223958da33a6e43b5afedd1da90f3ba89a47169e611d79112d05b05046.jpg)

Phantom Centaur is possibly the best creature in *Judgment*. Remember Blastoderm? He is essentially back in this reincarnated form. Centaur gets boarded in against Psychatog, red-green, monoblack, and just about every rogue deck out there. So you might wonder, why is this card not in the main deck? Fact is, there isn't enough room - and while you may bring it in against very different archetypes, you will be boarding out different cards to do it. Against Psychatog, you are going to take out Roar of the Wurm which is very susceptible to their Repulse and possibly Slay, whereas against an aggressive deck like red-green you might let go of some of your Counterspells, and you will get rid of Wonders against creatureless monoblack. 

Bearscape is pretty good against Burning 'Tog. Low on counterspells, they expect their Flametongue Kavu and other creature-control spells to dominate the board and will find it difficult to deal with a stream of 2/2 bears. Your job against 'Tog is to either kill them before they can get off the Upheaval/Psychatog combo, or force them to Upheaval prematurely, without the mana to play Familiar or 'Tog. If you can do that, you are at a great advantage, as you are now replaying the early game, in which your aggressive deck has a definitive advantage over controllish Psychatog. Bearscape is a spell that can help force them into an early Upheaval. A number of u-g players prefer an Upheaval of their own - but while Bearscape is a less powerful spell overall, I find it to be better in this particular matchup.

Finally, Sylvan Safekeeper can annoy any number of decks that rely on targeted creature removal such as Flametongue Kavu, Slay, or Repulse to deal with your threats. I believe I jumped the gun a bit playing four of them though. The only change I would make to the deck right now would be replacing a pair of Sylvan Safekeepers in the sideboard with a third Unnatural Selection and one Upheaval.

Like it or not, u-g madness is one of the most important archetypes in Standard at the moment and will remain so until October. If you do not play it, definitely make sure that whatever deck you choose to utilize in Standard is capable of beating it.
