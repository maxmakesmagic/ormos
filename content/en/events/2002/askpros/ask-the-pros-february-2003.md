Do you own Type I cards?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>John Larkin</b>: I used to own the power blue cards and a <a>Library of Alexandria</a>. They, slowly but surely, got sold off while I was in college. I once &quot;drank my Library&quot;... I sold it at 8 pm to a friend of mine and then went to the pub. Woke up the next morning with no money, no Library and a very large hangover.<br><b>Victor van den Broek</b>: I play some Type I. I own some regular cards and have a Collectors Edition I use to proxy the power cards with. I don't feel the need to go to Type I tourneys yet, even though there's a pretty active scene here in Holland.<br><b>Gary Wise</b>: I played Type I once a year, three rounds at a time until the Invitational moved to <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i>. The only true Type I card I own is a <a>Black Lotus</a> autographed by 35 people that I traded for at the Three Rivers Game Con in 1996.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

The perception of how strange a card is changes after it wins a few games. At some point, people would have said<a> Volrath's Shapeshifter</a>, <a>Firecat Blitz</a>, <a>Phantatog</a> or <a>Sutured Ghoul</a>. Actually, I guess <a>Phantatog</a> still counts.<br><b>Matt Vienneau</b>: <a>Sleeper Agent</a>! I was playing a <a>Stasis</a> deck at the local store and someone brought an entertaining, but not really competitive, <a>Sleeper Agent</a> deck! I could never lock him - he could always just drop swamp, <a>Sleeper Agent</a> and, all of a sudden, I'm taking 3 a turn and can't attack him back! It was a humiliating loss that my opponent delights in to this day. Hi Steve!<br><b>John Larkin</b>: I died to a <a>Bog Imp</a> once back in 1997 or so, when I had a <a>Waterspout Djinn</a> in play. I forgot to pay the upkeep. I don't think I have ever forgotten an upkeep effect since!<br><b>Kai Budde</b>: <a>Rorix Bladewing</a>. Whenever I lose to it, I look at it and try to figure out what R&amp;D tried to accomplish by designing it. Well, after some time, I just start to think about something easier to grasp - like the meaning of the universe… <br><b>Tomi Walamies</b>: I have most likely lost to everything, except <a>Great Wall</a>, but I was honestly surprised to get annihilated by <a>Benevolent Bodyguard</a> in the Standard portion of Worlds in Sydney.<br><b>Gerard Fabiano</b>: Probably <a>Demonic Consultation</a>. You might ask how I could lose to this card. Well, I topdecked it and consulted for &quot;Humpus Wumpus&quot; instead of <a>Hunted Wumpus</a>. I blame <a>Demonic Consultation</a>. I hate that card!<br><b>Zvi Mowshowtiz</b>: <a>Lapis Lazuli Talisman</a>. Once upon a time, going infinite required real effort. </p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

It took about two months in early 1996. Since then, I've missed only one non-Worlds Pro Tour in six years!<br><b>Raphael Levy</b>: It took me a few weeks, after I joined Team Legion in 1997 and knew I had to prove to my new teammates who I was! <br><b>Gary Wise</b>: Two years, twenty PTQs and thousands of miles logged. I'm a product of desire.<br><b>Kai Budde</b>: Three or four years.<br><b>Tomi Walamies</b>: I have no idea how you classify pro. From the moment that I started playing to when I made a Pro Tour Top 8, seven years passed.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table><tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td> <b>February 21, 2003</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/24/ed/24ed5be5578a04d4bd33e38ab1834ea7e497fbcee8f490f096ac18a1ccfd9f54.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><p><b>Question</b>: Where is the hardest place to qualify for the Pro Tour in the United States?</p>&#10;<p><b>Answers:</b></p>&#10;<p><b>Matt Vienneau</b>: Not Rochester, NY! I qualify there all the time!<br><b>Antonino de Rosa</b>: I am from Florida, so I am sure the PTQs are not nearly as difficult here as in other places, but if I had to pick a place, I would probably say the Northeast. Every time I read a report from that area, I always hear a couple of names that have had success on the Pro Tour and, from what I understand, they have some of the biggest turnouts in America.<br><b>Ken Krouner</b>: There was a time when New England was, by far, the most difficult. Every Boston PTQ was like a GP, name recognition-wise. Now I would have to say the Ohio Valley. They have strong players like Max McGuffin, Kenny Hsuing, Tim Aten and Derek McKlasky and they have been known to draw nearly 300 people.<br><b>Eric Taylor</b>: Hawaii, because you can't ever play. Heck, even Alaska has more tournaments. Players, in general, are good all over - anyone who thinks the East Coast is tough but the Midwest is easy is just a grumpy loser, in my opinion. I was in Kentucky the other day for a PTQ and I was surprised at how good the players were and how deep the talent was.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
