In **Magic's** humble beginnings, there was a cardinal rule; always, ALWAYS shack the pap (or: Shock the Pup, for the lingo-less). This maxim, loosely translated, meant that you should use your removal to clear the way for your creatures. For a long time, this was the beatdown player's mantra. Countless Llanowar Elves, River Boas, and Jackal Pups went down in flames at its command.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/aa/00/aa0013cdd84ea72275608e13442a4d760c8a4c738d0dbe52a6e43d19ce6876c9.jpg)

Like nearly everything in **Magic**, however, this too came to change. For a long time the only creatures who had anything but targets on their backs were all pretty much too weak for tournament play - they were things like *Legends*' Blazing Effigy and *Weatherlight*'s Abyssal Gatekeeper. Urza's Block started to make killing creatures a painful business, with a host of creatures with leaves-play effects that made shacking every pap no longer the best policy, but few had the raw power to see the light of Constructed tournament play. *Destiny*, however, brought with it the king of leaves-play effects, and **Magic's** first true Constructed-quality porcupine - Academy Rector.

Rector's primary strength, of course, was its ability to bring together combination decks, like Fruity Pebbles or Bargain, with its ability to tutor for a specific enchantment and put it directly into play. This strength, however, was amplified by the sort of effect Academy Rector had even before it made its trip to the graveyard and out of the game. The threat of a Rector fetching a game-winning enchantment was so great that most beatdown decks simply could not attack while it was in play for fear of its controller blocking with it. Academy Rectors have held off more Wild Dogs and Pouncing Jaguars than probably any other creature in the game, and certainly more than any other 1/2 creature - it is well deserving of its nickname: Moat.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/18/5e/185e7e318427f84c96bea9f51b1f21e9072b32c5ab59b80ef3c558e66c2e38e9.jpg)

The days of Rector, however, are come and gone, and in many ways the maxim of old holds true once again. Creatures today are killed with abandon, falling like dominos to Fire, Urza's Rage and, most importantly, Flametongue Kavu. This defining card of the Standard environment makes players hope their opponent plays a creature - any creature - so they can slam down a 4/2 on top of it and seize control of the game's tempo. Elves, Birds, Elephants, Beasts - even Jon Finkel himself - they all go down before the fiery breath of everyone's favorite Kavu. No format before has ever screamed so loudly for a quality creature with some quills to defend itself.

Enter Shambling Swarm, the anti-FTK. Like matter and anti-matter, the two are polar opposites, exploding into nothingness if they ever collide - but the Swarm comes out ahead, taking an Elf or Bird or other hapless one toughness creature with it to oblivion. It's no Academy Rector, but in the much slower-paced, more creature-oriented world of the modern Standard environment, it has a similar "Don't Kill Me" sign stamped directly on whatever semblance of a forehead it can muster. All those Flametongues licking their chops for something to kill are just going to have to wipe up their drool while this guy is around.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/28/4b/284b76dceeb20aeba721601cd2d9347ee1d26e97f641b0333e95602aa1410370.jpg)

The impact of the Swarm goes beyond just his rivalry with the ubiquitous Flametongue Kavu. Sick of being trampled by herds of Elephants? Shambling Swarm can actually maintain card parity against opposing Calls, taking out a pair of elephants by blocking one, or devastate your opponent's mana by shooting down mana creatures. Even Beasts fall before the Swarm, dragging another two toughness worth of creatures with them.

At ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) for a 3/3, Shambling Swarm isn't the most efficient of creatures, but he's large enough to do his job well. He's certainly not so small that your opponent can simply ignore him and never kill him, as on offense he's a reasonably fast clock. On the other side of the ball, as a black creature, and a three power one at that, he's perfectly suited for mixing it up with Jonny Magic, capable of blocking and killing Shadowmage Infiltrators. If your opponent tries to kill him to clear a path, Jon Finkel is sure to be in a world of hurt populated by -1/-1 counters.

The Swarm will be a powerful addition to base black decks in Standard, which are currently fighting a losing battle in the card advantage race against flashback spells and Fact or Fiction. The Swarm can make up for that disadvantage quite well, particularly coupled with red damage to finish off creatures shrunken by its graveyard effect. Base black decks suffered a crippling blow with the loss of the speed of Dark Ritual, but cards like Shambling Swarm and Braids, Cabal Minion provide card advantage and utility to make up for that loss of blazing speed.

Those two creatures, in fact, are likely to become quite the pairing. Braids decks have particular problems with aggressive green decks capable of producing a large number of permanents quickly, particularly when those permanents include Birds and Elves to accelerate out of mana lock range. What better permanent to sacrifice to Braids in that situation than one that wipes out your opponent's entire board position? Insert a smattering of removal to mop up the survivors, and few decks are capable of recovering from that kind of disadvantage.

While Shambling Swarm is not the moat Academy Rector once was, it stands to make quite an impact on the Flametongue-happy Standard environment. With Birds and Elves and Elephants and more all waiting to fall to the lashing tendrils of this big black beastie, one thing is clear; this is one pap you don't want to shack.
