It was all or nothing in this round - a win would allow these players to continue playing tomorrow while a loss would knock them out of the competition and end their dream of winning the Grand Prix this weekend.

For Denmark's Thomas Enevoldsen, that dream had become a reality once before; he won Grand Prix Strasbourg last year. His opponent Andrea Giotta from Italy on the other hand was still trying to bolster his resume with a similar accomplishment.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gputr14/andrea-giotta.jpg)

*It was Andrea Giotta's first feature match and he had to overcome a former Grand Prix Champion!* 

 

Enevoldsen was running the aggressive Red-White Deck and he had an early [Young Pyromancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Young+Pyromancer), but Giotta found [Dreadbore](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreadbore) to deal with it. [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix) was a different matter, though, and it started to reduce Giotta's life totals turn after turn.

 

The Italian tried to fight back with [Nissa, Worldwaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nissa%2C+Worldwaker) and [Chandra, Pyromaster](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%2C+Pyromaster), but Enevoldsen simply ignored the Planeswalkers and sent both [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix) and [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) directly into his opponent.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gputr14/finish-game-1.jpg)

*Enevoldsen stepped on the gas pedal and set the world on fire!* 

 

Just when it appeared like Giotta could stabilize, thanks to a [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix), Enevoldsen decided that it was time to wrap things up and after a [Shock](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shock) at the end of Giotta's turn and an attack with [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix) and [Stoke the Flames](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoke+the+Flames) on his following turn, he had seemingly set the whole table on fire!

 

Thomas Enevoldsen takes the lead!

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gputr14/thomas-enevoldsen.jpg)

*Thomas Enevoldsen now had to win only one more game to remain in competition!* 

 

The second game started seemingly slow, but it turned into a race momentarily; Giotta was the first to make a play with [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix) and Enevoldsen packed the one-two punch that were [Boros Charm](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Boros+Charm) and [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix).

 

[Blood Crypt](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blood+Crypt) made it 11 on Giotta, but it got him [Xenagos, the Reveler](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Xenagos%2C+the+Reveler) in return. He added a Satyr and [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix) and he then decided to race his opponent, sending both of them in.

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gputr14/race-game-2.jpg)

*The race was on and Andrea Giotta turned his creatures sideways!* 

 

Enevoldsen simply passed after attacking with [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix).

 

9 more to go.

[Xenagos, the Reveler](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Xenagos%2C+the+Reveler) ticked up and Enevoldsen responded with [Skullcrack](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skullcrack).

6 more to go.

A second [Skullcrack](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Skullcrack) followed when Giotta cast [Rakdos's Return](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos%27s+Return). The Italian attacked and that made it 8 on Enevoldsen; he then passed play, leaning back in a pose that indicated "bring it if you have it."

Enevoldsen drew...

... and he ripped [Stoke the Flames](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoke+the+Flames)!

![](http://media.wizards.com/2014/events/gputr14/finish-game-2.jpg)

*Enevoldsen's deck didn't let him down!* 

 

Thomas Enevoldsen advances to Day 2!

 

Before he left, Enevoldsen asked us to give a quick shout-out to his friend Soren Rosenfjeld who might be cheering for him at home this weekend.

Andrea Giotta on the other hand was very self-critical after the match. Asked what cards he would have wanted to draw, he immediately replied: "Magma Spike to deal with [Chandra's Phoenix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chandra%27s+Phoenix)."

He then went on and explained how he misplayed: "In the first game, I didn't play very well. I have to play in a different way; I should have kept [Dreadbore](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreadbore) and play [Courser of Kruphix](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Courser+of+Kruphix) instead. I can then add my Planeswalker and use [Dreadbore](https://web.archive.org/web/20140809000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dreadbore) later to deal with the Phoenix. That would have been much better!"

This is the kind of approach you need to take if you want to improve as a player. While this was Andrea Giotta's first feature match, he was looking forward to many more to come.
