### Grand Prix-Barcelona

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPBAR02/917.jpg"><br><i>Noah Boeken has another day in the sun.</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

Held so soon after Pro Tour-Osaka, Grand Prix-Barcelona was skipped by many of the Grand Prix regulars. I was preparing for another week-long trip to Asia and Kai stayed at home to feed a cat. Nevertheless, just over 700 other players did attend the tournament. Majority of these players were from Spain and Portugal, with a number of French and some of the other Europeans making the trip. United States were represented only by Ed Fear and Andy Stokinger, but neither advanced to Day 2.

After two days of competition, France came to dominate the tournament for the second week in a row. In addition to placing three of their own in the Top 8, Florent Jeudon, Antoine Ruel and Osaka's Top 8 finisher Christophe Haim all managed Top 16 finishes. The Netherlands were the second most successful country. In addition to winning the tournament, they got another Top 8 finish and a Top 16 finish, despite far fewer people making the trip. The highest-finishing local player was also one of Spain's best-known. Carlos Barrado, with a pair of Pro Tour Top 8 finishes to his name, added a fourth place at GP-Barcelona to his resume.

The French in the Top 8 are all very well known on the Tour. Levy and Canu have long been staples at European GPs. Olivier Ruel's second Top 8 finish in as many weeks allowed him to overcome me for third place in the Player of the Year race by a point - a lead that lasted only a week as I fought back with a Top 8 in Malaysia. This was the first career Top 8 for Stan van der Velden, who has had a hand in many a Dutch winning deck. Another Top 8 rookie is Germany's Reinhard Blech. 

Finalist Cristoph Lippert, also of Germany, has enjoyed a number of tournament successes as far back as Grand Prix: Cologne where he placed in the Top 8. Barcelona is his third Top 8 and second in a row after Antwerp. Which brings us to the winner. Noah "the Broken" Boeken earned his second Grand Prix win (first being GP-Helsinki) and seventh Top 8 overall that weekend. Boeken has had a dry spell, lacking top finishes in recent months. Winning this Grand Prix may be exactly what he needed to put him back on track. 

### Grand Prix-Kuala Lumpur

Grand Prix circuit returns to Malaysia for the second time. I suspect that despite relatively low turnouts, organizers simply love the gorgeous Sunway Lagoon Resort - where the event was hosted - as much as I do. In addition to a pair of Grand Prix, this location hosted a **Magic** Invitational and APAC Championships. 

This year, only 240 players showed up for the tournament. It was a disappointing number, since the previous GP-KL gathered about 300. A low international turnout was partly to blame, notably lacking many of the Asian GP circuit regulars from Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Japan. Singapore was very well represented thanks to being only a driving distance away. It dominated the top of the standings, earning four of the Top 8 slots. 

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPKL02/923.jpg"><br><i>Ding Yuen Leong</i></td></tr></tbody></table>

Champion Ding Yuen Leong has made Singapore national team twice, and earned a third-place finish in Grand Prix-Singapore a year ago. In that Top 8, he was also joined by the finalist Chang Chua. Singapore's Yujian Zhou, Thailand's Vee Sirilertvorakul and Philippines' Edsel Alvarez all earned their first Top 8's over the weekend. Best-known players in the Top 8 were me, Nick Wong, and Tobey Tamber.

An American living in Taiwan, Tamber has done extremely well for himself in Asian GPs, winning Kaohsiung. This was his fifth GP Top 8. Nick Wong has made Top 8 in Kaohsiung also, and won Grand Prix-Nagoya with Trevor Blackwell and I as teammates. I earned my 17th Grand Prix Top 8 in an effort to keep up with Ruel and Jensen in the Pro standings. 

A number of people have asked me to elaborate on the difficulties of getting to this GP, reported by Josh Bennett in the coverage. So here goes.

A funny thing happened to me on the way to Malaysia. I arrived in Singapore several days earlier, to hang out with my friends and then make the five-hour bus trip over to the Grand Prix. Steve Tan, Nick Wong and I crossed the Singapore border on Thursday afternoon, hoping to make it to KL early enough for a nice dinner. It was not to be. At the Malay border, authorities refused me entry! The reason? My passport was full. There was no place to put the entry stamp.

To be fair, it's my own fault. My passport has been full for a few months now, and various officials have been just stamping over the older stamps - with me being too lazy to spend a day obtaining a new passport. Apparently though, this was not good enough for the Malay. I called the American embassy from Nic's cell phone. "Sure, we can help you," they said. "Come see us on Monday."

"Monday! But I need to get to Malaysia by tomorrow," I gasped. 

"Sorry. We are closed on Good Friday and over the Easter weekend. See you on Monday."

Ouch. As much as I enjoyed Singapore, I was not keen on spending the weekend there. Despite what they said over the phone, I have watched enough bad TV shows and movies to know that there are some emergency personnel always available at the embassy. So we took a taxi back (luckily it is only a 15 minute ride from tiny Singapore's border with Malaysia) and showed up at the doorstep of the embassy. 

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I was far more fortunate than Peter Szigeti...</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I got the same answer at first. Apparently, at the end of the business day the extra pages which an embassy official can add to one's passport are locked away in a safe, and there was no one there with an authority to open it. No one at the U.S. Embassy with an access to a bunch of blank pages? Give me a break!

I persisted and finally got a sympathetic ear from one of the vice-consuls. Like others she left for the day and we talked over the phone. She said that, if I were willing to reimburse the Embassy for the overtime of an assistant with access to the safe, she would ask that person to come back to work and take care of it for me. Total charge - two hundred and eighty five U.S. dollars! Since the whole thing took less than two hours, I am really curious just how much assistant vice-consuls make per hour and whether I am in a wrong profession. 

Despite this highway robbery, I am glad things worked out in the end. $285 is not so much to pay once you have splurged on the plane ticket all the way to Singapore, and I would have ended up spending that on hotel bills and food just to wait for my non-refundable, non-exchangeable ticket to go home. Besides, I was far more fortunate than Peter Szigeti. 

Peter's friend Greg Rosebeary booked a ticket for him directly from an airline on a credit card. When Szigeti showed up at the airport, he was asked to see the actual card. When he could not provide it, he was not allowed to board the plane. Rosebeary himself was already on his way to Kuala Lumpur and could not be contacted in time. 

Rosebeary himself arrived safely in Kuala Lumpur and we had dinner Friday night. However, that was the last anyone at that Grand Prix has seen of Greg! He failed to show up at the tournament on Saturday morning and was not seen on his favorite IRC channels since either. I hope he is safe and nothing bad happened to him - but as of the time I am writing this, I simply do not know for sure. Greg, if you are out there reading this, let us know you are all right!

### Noir and Correction #2

I received well over 100 emails regarding the Noir article posted last week. There are several very common questions/suggestions so I would like to address them here.

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I am not as insane as I appear to be.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

First of all, the article should have said that I since replaced a third Gerrard's Verdict with a fourth Braids. I have no idea why that sentence got edited out, but assume it was a mistake. So when I talk about sideboarding out 4 Braids, I am not as insane as I appear to be.

Second, those of you who wondered about the lack of Shambling Swarm are right. Since the writing of the article, I took out the remaining two Verdicts and replaced them with Shambling Swarms to improve mirror and r-g matchups.

Finally, many of you wondered about the lack of Ichorid. The problem with Ichorid is that it's good in the matchups you already win (control) while useless in the tougher matches (aggro). Thus, it did not make the cut. If your metagame features a lot of control and a lot of Braids variants, Ichorid main deck or sideboard is a viable option. Similarly, Rat or Mesmeric Fiend remains a metagame call - which at the moment is swinging toward Mesmeric Fiend because of so many Braids decks being played.

### Play of the Week

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d8/87/d887fd1e83bdc0da0fb55c38d7d2bfeee6ffa8ff89e3cdcb613fe76f78198978.jpg)Alex Saltzberg has been hounding me to use his Play of the Week and I have a feeling that unless I post it, he won't stop - so here goes. (and don't get any ideas, unless you talk to me as often as Alex does, I won't post your play just because you ask often enough!)

Alex was playing a Standard game on **Magic** Online. His opponent drew a good spell and was ready to make his move. He cast Duress first to make sure Saltzberg could not counter - taking out the only counterspell Alex had, a Circular Logic. He then cast his spell. Saltzberg smiled, and paid the madness cost of Circular Logic, countering it.

Since his opponent never passed priority after casting Duress, Circular Logic remained in its madness "mode", ready and waiting to target the next spell that came along.

### Bad Play of the Week

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTSD02/a551.jpg)Pro Tour-San Diego champion Farid Meraghni did not go to PT-Osaka. "I have enough points to qualify for the Nice Masters," he reasoned, "and with no points falling off, will pick up more at PT-Nice, qualifying for the next individual Masters as well. In the meantime, I am not very good at Constructed and the ticket to Osaka is quite expensive, so I will sit this one out."

Invitations to Nice Masters were posted last week, with threshold being 40 points. Meraghni has 39 pro points to his name. Had he showed up in Osaka, he would earn at least 2 pro points and a minimum of $2000 in Nice. Sometimes, the worst play you can make is not to play at all.
