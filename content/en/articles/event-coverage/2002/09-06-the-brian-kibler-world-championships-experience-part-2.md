So when we last left our intrepid hero - that is, me - he was disheartened and frustrated by a decidedly mediocre 3-3 performance on the Standard day of Worlds. Quite frankly, I'm not entirely sure how it happened. The matchups that I lost - with the exception of the one against John Ormerod - were all favorable in testing - and when I say favorable, I don't mean the sort of "we tested last night and won 5-3" statistically insignificant advantage that many players toss around when they complain about losing good matchups. I mean 140-60 sort over favorable, the kind of results that you can only be really sure of when you've played hundreds and hundreds of games. Despite that, round after round, one of my teammates came back from the tables shaking his head, having lost again to Psychatog.

To ease our sorrows, we went out to dinner that night to a nice - albeit slow - restaurant near the site. Some of my companions chose to not just ease, but drown their sorrows, with cocktails and shot glasses aplenty making their appearance on our table over the course of the meal. I recalled my experience last year in Toronto, when after a dismal Day 1 I went out to a local karaoke bar and woke up the next morning with a pounding headache, and decided I'd be better off not repeating that feat. I kept my alcohol consumption to a single drink, and after good eats and good chats, headed back to the hotel to sleep.

My illness, however, decided that was not the correct plan, and kept me up most of the night with coughing fits. What little sleep I managed to get ended at around 6 AM, when I found myself entirely unable to stay in bed anymore, so I got up and got on Ben's laptop. I spent much of the morning chatting with friends from home, who encouraged me and wished me good luck despite my uninspiring start. It's always nice to have people who believe in you, especially when you're having trouble believing in yourself. Thanks, everyone. You know who you are.

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds02/a588.jpg)

I go out to a nearby café for breakfast with Ken Krouner, Peter Szigeti, Alex Borteh, Brian Hegstad, the Maryland crew of Huey, Brock, and Linde along with the ever-present Tammy Ehrhart, and the ever-late Dan Clegg, who walks in to jokes about his "bum jacket". This jacket looks like something one might find at the local homeless shelter, and combined with Dan's usual disheveled appearance created quite the amusing sight. While we were eating, I made a comment to Ken Krouner - who usually practices veganism - about how his toast was slathered in butter, to which he replied "Yeah, and this is an egg sandwich". Ken explained that keeping vegan while traveling is quite difficult, and that he had given up after the first day of Worlds. Apparently animal products grant Ken superpowers, because after he started scarfing them down on Day 2, his performance sure seemed to improve.

We get to the site relatively early, which gives us time to wander around and watch as groups of players lay out booster packs and discuss the possible first picks. I joke around about my well-known penchant for drafting aggressive red-white decks, saying patently ridiculous things like I'd take Ember Beast over Cabal Patriarch, but in large part, I'm just passing the time until pod seating goes up. I already know who's in my draft pod, because you can determine it easily the night before from the final standings on the day, so I'm pretty much just itching to sit down and draft. Soon enough...

I only recognize about half of the names and faces at this table, with Rob the obvious other big name, and Craig Wescoe and Louis Boileau both fairly well known for their success in Grand Prix play. Yuen I'd played the day before, but the other three players were completely unknown to me. Normally my plan in *Odyssey* booster draft is to concede to the color preferences of my neighbors and just take a completely reactive approach, but I have no knowledge of the players sitting around me, so I don't have any sort of base of information going in. Time to wing it.

I open a pack with Roar of the Wurm, Puppeteer, Cephalid Looter, and Afflict as the most notable cards. Since Roar is clearly the most powerful card, and despite a preference for blue over green - as well as a preference for blue-green as a deck for Roar - I take it, figuring the Puppeteer and Looter will put my neighbors both into blue. Since *Torment* blue is very powerful and I'll clearly be getting cut off from that direction, I decide to pass all the blue that comes to me, barring something absurd like getting a 2nd pick bomb in the color. I second pick a Muscle Burst, thinking that r-g is a fine option, especially with Barbarian Bully in *Judgment* to discard my Roar, and third pick Patriarch's Desire, recognizing that the only black card of not that I've passed so far has been Afflict. Fourth pick brings me an Ember Beast, which I quickly scoop up, and the next few picks give me a Werebear and a few other relatively insignificant green and red cards, more or less evenly split. Then my pack comes back, and I'm shocked to see the Afflict still in it. I quickly scoop it up, and despite having only an Afflict and Patriarch's Desire in the color so far, mentally consider myself a heavy black mage for *Torment* since black is clearly so underdrafted. While sorting my cards after the first pack, I try to figure out whether I'm better off playing red-black or green-black, and decide to simply see what comes.

In *Torment*, I open Faceless Butcher and grin, get passed a Soul Scourge and smile, and then get passed another Butcher and can scarcely suppress a giggle. Underdrafted indeed. The rest of my *Torment* picks are almost all black, full of Organ Grinders, Waste Away, a second Soul Scourge, and other such goodies. I pick up a couple late red cards as well, with Barbarian Outcast and Petravark both looking quite promising for a b-r deck. *Judgment*, however, gives me other ideas, as I open and pick Phantom Tiger, get passed another Phantom Tiger, and look at my fourth pack to see Grizzly Fate staring back at me. Black-green it is. My deck fills out rather nicely, with an aggressive curve, powerful late game cards, and - while not much true removal to speak of - a pair of Faceless Butchers. I'm quite happy as I register the deck, and expect to go at least 2-1, if not 3-0 with this monstrosity.

### Round 7 - Craig Wescoe
