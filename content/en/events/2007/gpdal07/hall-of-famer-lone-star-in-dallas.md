Nine years after winning his first Grand Prix and less than three months after being inducted into the Pro Tour Hall of Fame, Raphael Levy has won a second Grand Prix title, and boy did he have to work to do it. When the weekend started, there were 747 other players in Texas standing between Levy and his crown, but by the time Sunday afternoon rolled around, there were only 7, and they included U.S. National Champion Paul Cheon, Pro Tour-Hawaii winner Mark Herberholz, Pro Tour-Charleston Top 8 member John Pelcak, and 2005 Player of the Year Kenji Tsumura along with Jim Davis, Paul Nguyen, and Alex Ledbetter.

Levy's quarterfinal went smoothly, as he dispatched Heezy in two quick games, but the semis were an epic battle against Kenji Tsumura that had both players and the crowd gasping for air. Levy and his Jotun Grunts eventually overcame the Japanese master's Loam deck 2-1, putting Raph through to the finals. On the other side of the bracket, swiss leader John Pelcak squeaked past Alex Ledbetter's innovative Beat Stick deck 2-1, while the Dual of the Pauls finished quickly with Cheon defeating Nguyen 2-0. Cheon and Pelcak's match was nearly as good as the other semi, and ended with a huge roar from the crowd as Cheon carefully played around double Shrapnel Blast from Pelcak to win 2-1 as well. This set up a masterful final where both Cheon and Levy could have lost multiple times, but Levy and his angry animals managed to pull through and win it 2-1.

**Congratulations to Raphael Levy, the Grand Prix-Dallas 2007 champion!**

Raph has been making Top 8 at a lot of Grand Prix lately, and was the only player still active to get inducted into the Hall of Fame at last year's World Championships. His Grand Prix quest has taken him all over the world and will take him to Singapore and Amsterdam in the next few weeks. Mark isn't as active on the GP Circuit as Raph, but he recently won Pro Tour--Honolulu to make up for it and has made it to the Top 8 in two other Pro Tours.</p>&#10;      <p>The matchup is the Domain Zoo mirror, but the decks are somewhat different. In this match, Mark will probably play a more controlling game style because of the card advantage he can gain with <a>Dark Confidant</a> and <a>Sensei's Divining Top</a>. He would also like to note that Pat Chapin designed his particular version of the archetype. Raph's deck has got more aggressive creatures and pump spells to back them up. I think Mark has a slight advantage because he's got the Top to outdraw Raph, and he's got the 4 maindeck <a>Lightning Helix</a> whereas Raph has only two in the sideboard. Raph does have a few cards that can be very dangerous when they get active though; both <a>Armadillo Cloak</a> and <a>Umezawa's Jitte</a> can turn board positions around completely.</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 1</h3>&#10;      <p>Mark won the roll and both players kept their hands. Mark lead out with <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> and <a>Tribal Flames</a> on Raph's 2/3 <a>Kird Ape</a>, but Raph just replaced it while Mark played and used <a>Sensei's Divining Top</a>. <a>Sudden Shock</a> took out the Lavamancer and Isamaru came down for Raph, while Mark searched for spells with the Top and a fetch land. He found <a>Vindicate</a> for Raph's <a>Kird Ape</a> but Raph played <a>Armadillo Cloak</a> on the lone Isamaru and time was running out for Mark. Another fetch land gave him three new cards on top of his deck, but none of them were useful, and the combination of <a>Gaea's Might</a> and <a>Tribal Flames</a> sealed the game for Raph.</p>&#10;      <p>Levy 1 - Herberholz 0</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 2</h3>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/03/a0034a7f083f03c70665ae338f93823a7e2fa2f541bc729d1625307b0f2ed7a8.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Raphael Levy</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Mark started but Raph took the initiative with <a>Grim Lavamancer</a> and <a>Wild Mongrel</a>. Mark tried to take the <a>Mongrel</a> out at end of turn with a <a>Lightning Helix</a>, but Raph shrugged and discarded two <a>Wooded Foothills</a> to save it. Mark made sure of it this time with <a>Tribal Flames</a>, and Raph added <a>Kird Ape</a> to his board. Another <a>Tribal Flames</a> for Mark took out the <a>Grim Lavamancer</a>, and after all the lands and Lavamancer shots the life totals were 14 for Mark and 13 for Raph. Raph took another three but regained four life with <a>Loxodon Hierarch</a>, and the board was now 4 lands each, Hierarch and <a>Kird Ape</a> for Raph against Mark's <a>Grim Lavamancer</a>, but he quickly added Isamaru and a <a>Kird Ape</a> of his own to the board. Raph broke the stall with Umezawa's Jiite, as he put it on <a>Kird Ape</a> which was blocked by Isamaru and finished off by <a>Grim Lavamancer</a>. A Jitte counter traded for the Lavamancer and it was looking very bad for Mark, facing a Jitte and Hierarch with a lone <a>Kird Ape</a>. Mark's life total was so low at this point that he had to <a>Vindicate</a> the Hierarch, leaving himself with no cards in hand. The top of his deck was not another <a>Vindicate</a> and Mark packed it up.</p>&#10;      <p>Levy 2 - Herberholz 0</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Feb 25: 6:46 p.m. - Quarterfinals: Jim Davis vs. Kenji Tsumura</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Nate Price</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/0e/5e/0e5ee87b63e6764813e5716aad6d23f2c8e3938b0e51330d37045d9c63871afd.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Kenji Tsumura</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Both of these players are experienced <b>Magic</b> professionals, so this Top 8 stage should be nothing new to them. Jim Davis is a real grinder from Long <a>Island</a>, New York. He went to most of the Pro Tours in the last couple of years, and even paid his way to some of the more expensive Pro Tours that he qualified for on rating. However, he was only able to accumulate 16 pro points last year, and has recently fallen off of the Pro Tour train. He's playing a Goblin deck that he calls Charlie's Angels, testing it primarily online. He decided on Goblins because he really wasn't comfortable with any of the other decks in the format. So he found a deck that he felt had good matchups against the major decks and ran with it. His opponent this round is Kenji Tsumura. </p>&#10;      <p>Kenji Tsumura-where to begin? He's recognized as one of the best players on the planet by every <b>Magic</b> player, and is called by his &quot;other head&quot; Richie Hoaen &quot;better than Kai.&quot; That's quite a statement, but you'd get a decent number of people backing the claim. Kenji is a rare talent, someone who is capable of applying himself and raising his game to near unheard of levels. When his Limited game wasn't strong, he took some time and learned to draft. The results? He was a Top 8 machine during the Limited Pro Tours and Grand Prix of 2006. Kenji was already a powerhouse in Constructed before he devoted his time to Limited. Apparently, his shift in focus hasn't dulled his edge.</p>&#10;      <p>Jim won the die roll with a little pump of the fist. &quot;That's step one, right,&quot; he joked as he went to continue shuffling his deck. Jim's aggressive Goblin deck was looking forward to starting things off as quickly as possible, but he wasn't too happy with the first seven cards his deck provided him.
