As you know, **Magic** artists sometimes take more than one crack at a piece before they hit the nail on the head. Although that makes it sound like they're painting with hammers (not true, as far as we know), it's really because the art director frequently has specific goals. Take, for example, Giant's Ire:

Now let's look at one that's a little more subtle: Hillcomber Giant!

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<div id="div4"><div><img src="http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/arcana1000/1546_Hillcomber_Giant_Sketch1.jpg"></div>&#10;<p>In the original art by Ralph Horsley, the giant's a little bigger than a 3/3. See that cow on her belt? She must be <i>huge</i>! The creative team called this “giantflation”—the tendency of giants to look <i>too</i> giant.</p>&#10;</div>&#10;<div id="div5"><div><img src="http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/arcana1000/1546_Hillcomber_Giant_Sketch2.jpg"></div>&#10;<p>The cow has been replaced by a goat, shrinking her down to an acceptable size. Her face has also been tweaked, making her less &quot;ogre-y&quot;.</p>&#10;</div>&#10;<div id="div6"><div><img src="http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/arcana1000/1546_Hillcomber_Giant_640.jpg"></div>&#10;<p><i><a>Hillcomber Giant</a> final art by Ralph Horsley</i><br></p>&#10;</div>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
