[Regionals](https://web.archive.org/web/20060314114551/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=events/magic/regionals) and the [***Magic** Online* Worlds Qualifier](/en/articles/feature/2003/04-21-worlds-qualifier-faq) are approaching and two major tournaments took place last weekend. National Championships in the Netherlands and England are over and they provided us with some more information about the environment. Unfortunately, there is no real coverage from England, but the Dutch site [http://www.kvdeckmasters.nl](https://web.archive.org/web/20030424000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.kvdeckmasters.nl&origin=sideboard_strategy_20030424a) did a good job for their Nationals. I can’t really discuss all the decks played there, as that would be a bit too much, but I think that most people are interested in seeing lots of decklists right now. I am going to discuss the decks that finished first and second at Dutch nationals a bit and otherwise put up decklists for various decks that are solid choices in the Standard environment right now.

First of all, I’ll give you the limited information I got from England. Arrturi Bjoerk, hailing from Finland and studying in London right now, took the tournament with Cunning Wake. Mike Groves and his blue-green madness deck finished 2nd while Chris Clapton played a strange blue-black control deck that I will talk about more later on. Will Turner is the alternate for the English team and played madness as well. Mike Major with Canu Oppositoin, Ollie Schneider with Psychatog, Tom Harle with black-white Astral Slide control and Simon Marshall-Unitt with the green-black-red reanimator Rob Dougherty posted a few weeks ago on StarCity all lost in the quarterfinals. Ollie Schneider won the Swiss on tiebreaks after going 5-0-1 with his Psychatog deck.

While there were a lot of different decks in England, the Dutch kept things simple. Most of them, in fact, are followers of Jeff Cunningham and Antonino de Rosa. The common deckbuilding strategy for that tournament was clearly to look for the 56 cards to supplement the four Wild Mongrels. 59 people played green creature decks and with a total of 105 players that means that more then 50% chose to play with green creatures. This high amount of creature decks explains why a lot of the Dutch professionals chose to run Cunning Wake. Tom van de Logt, Victor van den Broek, Jelger Wiegersma and Kamiel Cornelissen all chose this archetype and did quite well with it, posting a combined record of 16 wins, 4 losses and 2 draws. A total of 11 players ran Wake, while only 10 went with Psychatog. The high percentage of Wake decks also meant that Slide wasn’t a great choice for the tournament, even though there were so many creature-based strategies.

The Top 8 after 12 rounds looked like this:

1.  Van Wageningen, Matthijs - Blue-Green Madness
2.  van Ravesteijn, Tom - Blue-Green Madness
3.  Karsten, Frank - Black Control
4.  Wiegersma, Jelger - Cunning Wake
5.  Witt, Alexander - Blue-Green Madness-Threshold
6.  Maaten, Rogier - Blue-Green Madness
7.  Ferguson, Maarten - Black-Green
8.  Van de Hanenberg, Frank - Green-Red Beatdown

Only one of those decks didn’t run basic forests while five of them had four copies of Wild Mongrel. In the finals, Rogier Maaten’s blue-green deck defeated Jelger Wiegersma’s Cunning Wake. Rogier’s decklist looks quite interesting, as I haven’t seen a blue-green deck like his yet. What makes it even more intersting is that Rogier didn’t lose a single match all weekend – He went 6-0 on Day 1 and then won the Top 8.

No matter how good the deck is in general, his victory against Jelger doesn’t really surprise me at all. If you want a madness build that beats Wake and does so over and over again, you have found your list. Quiet Speculation is annoying on its own, but maindeck Upheaval is brutal. After sideboarding, there are even more Counterspells, which are great as the Wake player has to resolve a Wrath at some point and a second Upheaval to make sure you draw one in case the Cunning Wake deck can stabilize the game. But the maindeck Upheaval and Counterspells in the sideboard are the minor changes here. The only permanent madness outlet this deck has is Wild Mongrel. Merfolk Looter and Aquamoeba got the boot. That obviously means that Rogier couldn’t afford to run Arrogant Wurm. He went with Phantom Centaur instead. That change seems like a good idea in general. The Wurm is only superior against Psychatog and Wake as being able to cast it as an instant is very good in those matchups. Against all kinds of green decks and against Astral Slide, Phantom Centaur is superior. Not having Merfolk Looter or Aquamoeba obviously means that you are really dependant on Wild Mongrel. The deck still has Roar of the Wurm, Wonder and Circular Logic which all need madness outlets to be really effective. Careful Study certainly helps, but you are going to draw into some of those madness cards eventually when you don’t have a Study ready. It seems that Mr. Maaten took that calculated risk and went quite well with it. Cutting the madness outlets allows you to run other cards. A full set of Unsummons improves the mirror and any matchup against other green decks, especially agianst green-red. Elephant Guide is not very frightening any more and Roar of the Wurm tokens disappear for just ![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif). I totally agree with the choice of Unsummon over Aether Burst as well. The one mana difference is actually quite huge and if you run Aether Burst and run into some sort of blue control deck that packs Bursts itself, you are in serious trouble, as his Bursts are suddenly a lot more powerful while he usually won’t have more then one creature in play anyway.

The things I don’t like about this deck are the Nimble Mongooses and the white splash. I don’t think fetch lands are a good idea with City of Brass already in the deck and a basic plains maindeck asks for trouble. I’d cut all of that and replace the fetch lands with the appropiate basics and the plains with a forest. The Mongooses should be two Aquamoebas or Merfolk Looters and a fourth Phantom Centaur. The Phantom Centaur seems very good in every single matchup right now and I think that six permanent madness outlets are necessary for such a deck, even without Arrogant Wurm. And the deck doesn’t really reach threshold all that fast without Mental Note and Breakthrough.

I have to admit that I thought that this deck was pretty bad when I first saw it, but Chris proved me wrong here. He took a similar design to a respectable finish on Day 1 and then made the National Team going 2-1 in the Top 8. I haven’t seen his decklist unfortunately and I am pretty sure that he changed a few things. The idea is obviously to make an early Standstill and discard Riftstone Portal either to Compulsion or the old fashioned way. As soon as you have access to green and white mana, your opponent is forced to break the Standstill, as your Monasteries will kill him otherwise. The deck is filled with removal to make sure that you can make a Standstill without a creature in play. Innocent Blood is a lot better if you don’t have Psychatog as well. But not having ‘Tog and Upheaval makes some matchups worse as well. Wake isn’t really easy any more as without Upheaval, it is quite difficult to win at all. The deck also doesn’t have that much card drawing with just Compulsion and Deep Analysis. Maybe one maindeck Haunting Echoes would be a good idea, as it provides you with a one-card win condition against other control decks. I can’t really say how much potential this deck has as I didn’t really consider it a serious contender until this weekend, but if you are playing in Regionals, you should be prepared for this deck. At least it is good to know that if your opponent is playing Monastrey and Riftstone Portal, you don’t have to expect Upheaval.

### The Summary of this Weekend

If you want to play with Wild Mongrels, you better have a good plan for the mirror match. Blue-green and green-red are most likely all over the place. Psychatog doesn’t seem to be that hot and certainly didn’t post impressive results. There is probably going to be some Cunning Wake and you might want to be prepared for it. Flaring Pain is the card of choice for green-red and Upheaval for any blue deck. If I had to play this weekend, I would go with either Wake, the cycling deck or monoblack control. You need to be brave with black as Compost can wreck your day, but it is a pretty good deck and running four Composts doesn’t seem to be a great idea right now, as black decks aren’t doing that well overall. Wake and cycling are both solid anti-creature strategies. Both have problems against Psychatog but again, I wouldn’t expect too many ‘Tog decks.
