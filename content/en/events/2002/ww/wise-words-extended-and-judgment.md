![](https://i.ormos.uk/74/b0/74b04c551d93ace2acc47fccf6d47151fd094429877af0d378931c4b57dbcb18.jpg)Some weeks I have to wait for inspiration to hit me before I know what to address in Wise Words. Other weeks, the topics that need to be addressed sort of fall into my lap With Friday's big Extended announcement barely preceding the *Judgment* Prereleases Saturday, this has been a busy week for Magicians worldwide, but for me, this is gravy. I have topics set up for the next month at least.

There are others writing for the *Sideboard* whose analytical superiority with regards to all thing Constructed make them far better qualified to write about Extended's redefinition, so I thought it best to get my two cents - and only two cents - in and then move on to other things: namely *Judgment* and The People's Heel, Peter Szigeti. 

### The New Extended

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6a/7b/6a7b1a0a7e40ee409e92ea0787c2857fdb8fdc3ccf7bbcd9ef20e2e371f71ca9.jpg)Right, first off, R.I.P dual lands. When I saw the announcement, I was as stunned as the rest of you, but like the **Magic**-submerged guy I am, I immediately started thinking about the changes and what they'd mean. The dealers would be despondent, the old players up in arms, while the new kids who'd already spent their allowance on babes, beers and Britney would finally have their chance at the brass ring. 

Now, here's what I've got to say to each of you: Dealers, don't worry about it too much. Yes, your duals, Forces, Thaws and other old time powerhouses are now worth less, but you'll be compensated. One by one, players who thought they had every card they'd ever need are going to come sulking to you asking for good deals on Cursed Scroll, Vampiric Tutor and other cards rendered obsolete by what is now an obsolete format, and every three years they'll come back to you for more, because in 2005, the Scroll is gone too.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/57/10/5710012e484b20d71118124380ecd6a25521518d1f9f281db690046b5661bc35.jpg)Old timers: I'm one of you, and yes, it's annoying that I own forty white bordered cards that will never again get played outside of future Invitationals (hopefully), four FoWs, four Thaws, three Gaea's Blessings and so on, but you know what? In the long run, this change is going to make for a better format, and as was said Monday on Star City (To the author: I apologize but I couldn't find the quote while writing this. It was a good one though), you've owned those little ten dollar investments for years. Did you expect to use them for a couple of decades and then sell them at twice the value? You've played with them enough to cover your costs, and frankly, if you haven't, you made a poor investment anyway. 

Yes, Extended has been great of late, with as many as thirty archetypes with multiple variations proving to be competitive, but it's become a study in metagame maneuvering. Look how little *Torment* affected the format: Deep Analysis was cool with Intuition, and Overmaster and Grim Lavamancer both warrant some testing, but other than that, there really wasn't much else. Yes, *Odyssey's* effect was a profound one, but I think you're going to find that more often than not, *Torment's* effect would be the example followed and that's boring.

A few of the folks in this group have complained then complained more about how much work they've put in and how now that work's been put to waste, but to me it's obvious that those people have lost touch with what **Magic** is first and foremost: a hobby. Hobbies are supposed to be time-consuming, remain active and never stagnate. Extended was stagnating. Now it never will.

New Kids: If you're sick of going to Extended PTQs only to get beaten by the old-timers and their superior card stock, this is the single best thing that could ever have happened to you, but now you have to grab for the prize. There's a brand new environment out there just waiting to be exploited, and as with every other new environment in the history of the game, those who work hardest will see the dividends. Start working. Build your old Standard decks from the last five years TODAY. Look at the other set lists and figure out what'll be good with them NOW. Brush the dust off of those old boxes you put in the basement right after you finish reading this and start learning your new format. You do that and stay diligent and I guarantee that come that first set of Extended qualifiers, you'll have such an advantage on the field that your chances of winning your first match, making your first Top 8 or even winning your first PTQ will be better than they've ever been.

Wizards of the Coast has been fighting the good fight in the attempt to get the public at large to take this game more seriously on the point of social acceptance, and that means that every supported facet must be accessible to the public, and sad to say, Extended wasn't. Imagine being a new player and coming into the present Extended environment and trying to be competitive... impossible. Now, that old card-barricade has vanished with one stroke of Organized Play's keyboard. Good job WotC. Anyone want to buy some Tundras?

### My five Favorite *Judgment* cards

Now that the Prerelease is past, a few of the cards have stuck themselves in that part of my brain that makes me warm and fuzzy whenever accessed. This list isn't necessarily pertaining only to Limited play... it's just the stuff I really want to cast a lot in the next year or two:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/82/4f/824fb630fb74476edc9a47a7248e5151a9b041574489a32e00206b0ae418e981.jpg)5) **Spelljack** - In a post to the UK newsgroup, Craig 'Prof' Jones wrote about a game where he Spelljacked Rabid Elephant, untapped, played Upheaval and then cast the Elephant. While not all of my uses for the card have been quite as obscene, every time I've resolved Spelljack, I knew I was going to win. While the gaining control of your opponent's spell is nice, it's the fact you can cast it whenever you want regardless of color, cost and the like that makes it so unbelievably powerful. Yes, ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) is going to be tough to do and yes, having to leave that kind of mana open is difficult, but in the end, **Magic's** a game, games are supposed to be fun, and let me tell you, when you see your opponent's reaction, if you're the competitive sort, there's nothing more fun than resolving Spelljack. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/16/fb/16fbbee0edb4c7f5c678c1f14e5e58f5b9fc13bec7bb8f7cc6f979a7fd420bba.jpg)4) **Mental Note** - This card is so subtly good. For one mana, you get three cards in the 'yard, almost getting you half way to threshold. You offer yourself the chance to get a free flashback card, cycle yourself to more land and it draw a card regardless of how else it's helping you. Right now you may be looking at this as a Limited card, but give it a few weeks: when people star piecing together their Quiet Speculation decks, there may be a place for Mental Notes in them.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/31/51/31514a7cd8076aac09ab7912704039df1ff82e5d1a0daa49209b1b2d8371cca0.jpg)3) **Arcane Teachings** - One word: Splashable. I had two of these in the sideboard of my blue-green sealed deck Saturday and more and more found myself wishing I'd played a couple of mountains for them. Arcane Teachings is going to stay in the Limited ranks, but while there, it's going to be the bright spot in red's otherwise awful three months. "Target creature becomes an absolute monstrosity that will strike down phantoms and one toughness creatures before attacking for fifty points a turn". Sounds like a plan.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/63/7b/637ba52e2fdb42b61bf8f60e585df1be6af4716e95be42e9970bbf3a6ca223f3.jpg)2) **Grizzly Fate** - Before the first round of the Prerelease, a couple of people looked at the Grizzly Fate in my deck and asked if it was really all that. I wasn't 100% sure myself, but I had Overrun, and it seemed like making multiple guys would be a good thing for me to do. What I didn't know is that it wasn't a couple of guys: It was eight. Every time. Grizzly Fate won't be seeing much use in Constructed play, but the ability to make eight 2/2 creatures (I even Muscle Bursted an opposing creature to get to threshold once) is rare and powerful, making the Fate a true smash to the face.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/03/fe/03fe2e9af53874997237fe6bd5f8099fd3d19d9854c38eec0d8a9e40e0acbcb9.jpg)1) **Seedtime** - I think this is what Randy Buehler was talking about when he wrote about green's strength. For a while, the term EOTFOFYL (End of Turn Fact or Fiction You Lose) was in vogue, but now EOTFOFNFFTTACMTCWPAG? (End of Turn, Fact or Fiction. Now Feel Free to Take a Couple More Turns. Can We Play Another Game?) seems more like it. Go ahead, tap out to Fact or Fiction, Counterspell or what have you. Now I'll tap my four land and take a couple of turns after this one. Seedtime is the most devastating color hoser I can remember since Boil and Perish, and neither of them was a two-mana instant. TIME WALK WASN'T EVEN AN INSTANT! I haven't really been paying too much attention to the way Standard will look with *Judgment* as English Nationals is this weekend, but I know this: casting and resolving this spell is going to be incredible. It'll be like 1995 all over again. 

With a little testing to do before the weekend and a couple more installments to write in the *Judgment* series, we're going to have to call this a wrap. Start drafting *Judgment* and building those new Extended decks and to those of you not convinced about the new 1.X, I say only this: The basic structure of the competitive game is based on fluctuation: every three months there's a new set, every year a new block. Stop moaning for the past: the future's going to be a lot of fun. Have a good one.
