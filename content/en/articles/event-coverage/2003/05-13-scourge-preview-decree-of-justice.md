Casual vs. competitive. It's a rift that has separated the **Magic** community from day one. Competitive players want efficiency, casual players size. Competitive players want to win ten games out of ten, casuals one out of ten, as long as that win is ridiculous and explosive. I can remember building a Shaharazad/Fork deck long time ago, whose primary path to victory was forcing my opponent to say “sorry, I have to go now”. Now I try to beat the other guy as fast as humanly possible.

Casual and competitive players agree on very little. On ***Magic** Online*, the most coveted cards are Call of the Herd and Reya Dawnbringer. Call is an efficient powerhouse, but not much fun. Reya is massive and impractical, but when she gets going its just… too… cool! The problem is the two cards are light years apart: as a result, Constructed players won't bother to try out Reya, while the casuals will have to resort to other sources for their elephant decks.

Every once in a while, R&D strikes gold with that rare combination of power and fun that forms a card that lies in the happy medium between our two halves. Fact or Fiction was that kind of card, combining card drawing insanity with the fun of the split. Now, with *Scourge*, a new attempt to please both Spike and Timmy is available to deck builders of the 60 and 260-card deck varieties. Ladies and gentlemen, I present you with Decree of Justice.

Imagine it: You're locked in a creature standoff, ten lands in play, no one going anywhere and then you draw this sorcery. Suddenly, you have three Serra Angels in play, dancing side by side with a flurry of steps that culminate with a swift kick to your opponent's life total. Sorry pal, not much you can do: the angelic chorus got your number.

Ask a casual player what's better than an angel and the obvious—and perhaps only—answer, is a horde of them. For six mana, Decree of Justice gives you a token equivalent to lady Serra, ignoring the fact that bounce spells kill it, but if that were all the card did, competitive types wouldn't even sniff. Sure, it would be good in Limited play, but the original herself isn't seeing play in Standard, so a less efficient version wouldn't be worth the time of day. Unlike the Angel, which peaks in power on turn five, Decree of Justice gets better and better: eight mana gives you two Angels, ten gives you three, five million gives you 2,499,998, but really, at least when it comes to Constructed play, I don't see the Decree being cast that often, at least in the competitive arena: there, its going to get cycled.

Think about the applications with Mirari's Wake. Sure, early in the game, it's only going to get you a dork or two as you look to get closer to your key cards like Wrath or Wake, but once the enchantment is down, insanity prevails. Here we have an uncounterable effect that, when used with lots of mana, produces lots of token creatures at instant speed. Get three Wakes in play, end of turn make forty 1/1s, each of whom gets +3+3, untap, attack, extend the hand.

Is it better than Elephant Ambush? It doesn't remove itself from the game a la Ambush, but with multiple Wakes out, the cycling effect makes an army of 3/3 or bigger creatures at the end of turn. That may even allow the Wake deck to cut a lot of the cards it needs to combo opponents out. Once *Scourge* is released into the Standard environment, my guess is you're going to see these two cards played together a lot.

Decree of Justice won't see play in Extended, where it's just going to be too slow, and I'm not certain that you'll see it being used in Block either, where the constant presence of Astral Slide and Starstorm seem to limit its effectiveness, not to mention the loads of mana needed to optimize it. In Limited play though, you're going to see a lot of happy white-mage faces when the sorcery gets cracked. Large evasion creatures are an all-too-valuable commodity in Limited play, even in the Timmy-esque *Onslaught* Block environment. This is where the Decree is going to get hard cast. Of course, your opponent won't be around long enough to talk about it; when a few angels hit them square in the chest, it'll all be over except for the complaints about how their deck should have beaten yours and how they drew 11 lands in 21 cards. 

The simple fact is that none of their excuses will be the reason they've lost. They've lost because you're the lucky scrub (in their parlance) who opened the ridiculous bomb. One angel they might have been able to handle. Two is definitely a stretch. But when you make four of the blessed things, there really aren't too many solutions. Well, aside from extending the hand of course. They can always hope you don't draw it in game two.
