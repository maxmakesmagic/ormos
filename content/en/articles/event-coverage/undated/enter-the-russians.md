Nicolay Potovin wins Grand Prix-Stockholm!

It was only a few years ago that **Magic** was first printed in Russian, with a fanfare launch tournament in Moscow featuring a selection of top pros. Now the country has its second Grand Prix champion in Potovin. When Pro Tour-Philadelphia Top 8 member Andre Müller beat Kenji Tsumura in the semifinals, he proudly declared "For me the tournament is over" -- but he had not reckoned with the might of Nicolay Potovin and his powerhouse red/blue deck, which romped to victory in two games straight. 

The Russian contingent was out in force at Grand Prix-Stockholm, and while there were many Germans, a local hero and a tiny hero from the far east all vying for the title, none could stop Potovin from lifting the trophy with his countrymen at the end of the day.

Congratulations to Nicolay Potovin, Grand Prix-Stockholm champion!

When the Deathspore got active, its sickening effect was thrown straight back at it with <a>Willbender</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Mueller threw a sacrificial <a>Deepcavern Imp</a> in the way to slow down the damage. Flying attacks took him down to 11 and then 7, but now his heavy ground pounders were starting to rumble back in as the Phantasmagorian and <a>Sporoloth Ancient</a> smashed back across the red zone.</p>&#10;      <p>The tide was turning against the Japanese player. The Spider was back on defence and then a <a>Faceless Butcher</a> plucked the Malach out of the skies. Now there were no good attacks and Kenji had to hold his forces back.</p>&#10;      <p>At the end of turn Mueller pulled off some brutal madness tricks. <a>Undertaker</a> discarded a <a>Grave Scrabbler</a>, which was madnessed into play. This allowed Mueller to fetch back two monsters and was enough to force Tsumura to scoop as he was completely overwhelmed on the board.</p>&#10;      <p>Mueller 1-0 Tsumura</p>&#10;      <p>Tsumura's start in the second game was much better. He suspended <a>Knight of Sursi</a> and followed with a <a>Blade of the Sixth Pride</a>. He skipped a third turn play but had an Aven Augur on turn four.</p>&#10;      <p>Mueller wasn't out of the game by any stretch. <a>Stronghold Rats</a> showed up on turn three and started taking chunks out of Tsumura's hand, forcing him to discard first Walk the Aeons and then <a>Castle Raptors</a> to leave Tsumura with no cards. A <a>Faceless Butcher</a> took out the Aven Augur and then a <a>Penumbra Spider</a> appeared to slow down the incoming damage.</p>&#10;      <p>It was just a little too late though as Tsumura's air force of <a>Knight of Sursi</a> and <a>Ghost Ship</a> were able to carry him over the finish line just as Mueller's ground forces were starting to look dangerous.</p>&#10;      <p>Mueller 1-1 Tsumura</p>&#10;      <p>It was Mueller's turn to get off to a flyer. A suspended Dustwasp was followed by a hasty <a>Deepcavern Imp</a>. Mueller even got to turn the echo to his advantage as he madnessed a <a>Grave Scrabbler</a> into play for the bargain price.</p>&#10;      <p>Tsumura wasn't hanging around either. For the third game in a row he suspended <a>Knight of Sursi</a> on turn one (well he does have three of them!) and followed up with <a>D'Avenant Healer</a> and a couple of morphs.</p>&#10;      <p>This game wasn't looking too hot for <b>Magic</b>'s hottest player. Mueller had managed a really aggressive start and Tsumura was stuck on three land.</p>&#10;      <p>Mueller was on top and piling on the pressure. A <a>Penumbra Spider</a> appeared and was sent into the red zone with the freshly unsuspended Dustwasp. Tsumura had the active Healer and blockers to try and stem the assault, but Mueller was utterly relentless as a <a>Sporoloth Ancient</a> entered the play area.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Knight of Sursi" src="https://i.ormos.uk/21/72/21723fc5c830dd776c512a0ca40aef5e0adaca0f9fcf473b07a8988b2fce691c.jpg">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>On the following turn Mueller hammered home the last nail. Tsumura, still stuck on three land, tried to double block the Ancient and then <a>Pongify</a> his own <a>Knight of Sursi</a> after damage to leave himself with a fresh Hill Giant. He was undone by a lowly <a>Muck Drubb</a>, as it flashed from Mueller's hand to take the bullet. Tsumura was now even further behind on monsters and it got worse as even more jumped from Mueller's hand.</p>&#10;      <p>Kenji might be the best player on the planet right now, but he hasn't got the Top 8 invulnerability field that Kai used to possess. Mueller simply blew him out of the water with that third draw.</p>&#10;      <p>I think Mueller might have undersold his deck a little. There's a fair bit of meat in there and he has a whole toolbox full of madness tricks.</p>&#10;      <p>Andre Mueller beats Kenji Tsumura 2-1 and advances to the final.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="5">&#10;        &#10;      </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, May 6: 11:30 p.m. - Finals: Andre Müller vs. Nikolay Potovin</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Craig Jones</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/c2/46/c2465f6013624af1b281e434ca927d1279d058429298a04464b7d5168afd5cd2.jpg">&#10;            <i>Nikolay Potovin</i>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>After 17 rounds of full <i>Time Spiral</i> block Limited, Grand Prix-Stockholm came down to a single match. On one side, Andre Müller, German pro with a Pro Tour Top 8 to his name and plenty of Pro Tour experience besides. On the other, Nikolay Potovin of Russia, a master of <b>Magic Online</b>, who periodically ventures into real world <b>Magic</b> when the rewards are great enough. Kengur is his name online. Watch out for Russian Sharks next time you draft. </p>&#10;      <h3>Game 1</h3>&#10;      <p>Andre won the roll, and had the first play of the game in the form of <a>Deathspore Thallid</a>. He followed it up with <a>Deepcavern Imp</a>, to allow for three points of attacking. Only two got through, as Nicolay had a Dead to kill the 1/1, who would be particularly powerful in Andre's green/black deck. The following turn <a>Aether Membrane</a> looked good to keep the Imp on the defensive, but it still brought out a <a>Gorgon Recluse</a> via madness when its echo was paid. Andre tried for <a>Undertaker</a>, but before it could come online Nicolay used a <a>Ghostfire</a> to take it down. </p>&#10;      <p>Andre played <a>Stronghold Rats</a> and passed. Nicolay checked that there were no creatures with shadow on his opponent's side of the board, and played a <a>Jodah's Avenger</a>, drawing an ooh from the assembled crowd. </p>&#10;      <p>Andre played <a>Penumbra Spider</a> and passed. Nicolay had Serendib Sorcerer and a morph. He took a hit from <a>Deepcavern Imp</a>, and smashed back the following turn with an Avenger that had both shadow and vigilance. </p>&#10;      <p>Andre was on the defensive, but couldn't do much about the Avenger, or indeed the <a>Primal Plasma</a> or <a>Triskelavus</a> that joined it. A <a>Midnight Charm</a> killed off Serendib Sorceror, but that was the least of the German's worries. Both Nicolay's 4/4 creatures ran into the red zone, forcing a double block on <a>Triskelavus</a> with both <a>Penumbra Spider</a> and <a>Deepcavern Imp</a> from Müller. <a>Triskelavus</a> died, but made 3 tokens on the way out, and killed the Imp. One token killed <a>Stronghold Rats</a>, and then Nicolay had a <a>Bonded Fetch</a>.</p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/0d/a2/0da2dacc5d4c305aadffabc7fec34321594cc8960c1590f6328f52343365e38e.jpg">&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Jodah's Avenger</a> attacked for four thanks to shadow and double strike, and Nicolay made another morph before passing. Andre just had a <a>Muck Drubb</a> for his turn, and got hit for four again after passing. Nicolay then refilled with <a>Think Twice</a> - just in case. </p>&#10;      <p>Müller was stuck without answers to <a>Jodah's Avenger</a>, and quickly scooped up his cards when he saw that he had no outs this game.</p>&#10;      <p>Andre Müller 0 -1 Nicolay Potovin</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 2</h3>&#10;      <p>Andre had an <a>Undertaker</a> but no dead bodies for it at the start of game 2. He simply bashed in with it for a turn, before seeing it humbled in its status as biggest creature on the board by a <a>Spiketail Drakeling</a>. Nicolay had a morph to follow up, and commenced his air assault. Andre played <a>Gorgon Recluse</a> as a test spell, but it was not good enough to kill off the Drakeling. Nicolay had a <a>Coal Stoker</a> and <a>Aether Membrane</a> to build his board with, and Andre a <a>Faceless Butcher</a> to cut it down. He took <a>Spiketail Drakeling</a> with it, but this was soon replaced in the flying beatdown role by <a>Whip-Spine Drake</a>. </p>&#10;      <img src="https://i.ormos.uk/35/93/3593ca436e8f1571ae3d1e0ab7e90d9bd8a7dad6c244cd24e4b08111815ab801.jpg">&#10;      <p>While the air was undoubtably Russia's, Andre tried to claim the ground with a <a>Sporoloth Ancient</a>. He was humbled in this too, by a Serendib Sorcerer. The only constant source of damage that Andre had was <a>Gorgon Recluse</a>, which Nicolay was hesistant to block. The air force grew with <a>Triskelavus</a>, which seemed to outclass the <a>Muck Drubb</a> for Müller - known as 'TrashT' (his <b>Magic Online</b> nickname) to many of his friends. </p>&#10;      <p>Trash had a <a>Deathspore Thallid</a>, which immediately used <a>Sporoloth Ancient</a>'s fresh Saproling to kill Serendib Sorcerer. It was all for naught though. The air force would inevitably take it. </p>&#10;      <p>Just a turn later, a Dead/Gone from Nicolay cleared the way enough for a lethal attack -- one that made him Grand Prix-Stockholm champion!</p>&#10;
