The raptor fences are down! Roughly 30% of all players in Day 2 of Grand Prix Paris were running a [Deathmist Raptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20150510000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathmist+Raptor) deck of sorts. Although there's always the lysine contingency if it really gets out of hand, it is fair to say that we're living in a Jurassic Standard right now.

[![](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Deathmist+Raptor)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150510000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathmist+Raptor)

The Raptor does it all: It attacks for a reasonable amount, trades with almost any opposing creature, and keeps on coming back from the dead. Yesterday, I described [various approaches to beating it](/en/events/2015/gppar15/seven-ways-to-beat-deathmist-raptor-and-den-protector). Today, I want to highlight the diversity of [Deathmist Raptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20150510000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Deathmist+Raptor) decks. 

I counted as many as nine (!) different megamorph archetypes on Sunday. Some, such as Abzan or Bant, are more established; others are more, well, out there. For all of them, I selected an exemplary list and briefly analyzed their key selling points. The archetypes are sorted in descending order of popularity.
