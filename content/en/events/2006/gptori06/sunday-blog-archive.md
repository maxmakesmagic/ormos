<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, June 4: 8:01 a.m. - Back on the bus</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gptori06/2b3.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>These guys mean business… it's not the fun trip it looks like.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>I saw the sun rise over the Alps that surround Torino. I was woken by the first rays of the sun tickling my face. I felt the fresh breeze of the cool mountain air washing through the billowing curtain of the hotel room window. I had a hard time waking up. As Dutch judge Frank Waremen noted at breakfast this morning: &quot;It may be beautiful, but it's just too early.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>The staff hotel is a 30-minute bus ride away from the tournament site, so all the judges and staff had to get on said bus at 6:45 a.m. Yesterday evening, after taking the scenic route, the bus arrived at the staff hotel at 11:30 p.m., so there was not much time between yesterday and today to sleep. Nonetheless, working ourselves awake, we will bring you everything from GP Torino today: Two drafts, each followed by three rounds of play, and the Top 8 draft and matches.</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, June 4: 11:50 a.m. - Drafting with Bram Snepvangers</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gptori06/2b1.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Bram Snepvangers recording his Windreaver-powered deck.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Bram made day two with a clean 9-0 record. He drafted on table one in the first draft, sitting in seat four with Enrico Torazza (Italy) on his right and Georgios Kapalas (Greece) on his left. From Bram's point of view, the draft presented a rather weak array of card quality, even though the Dutchman first-picked <a>Last Gasp</a>, passing <a>Strands of Undeath</a>, <a>Compulsive Research</a>, <a>Drift of Phantasms</a>, <a>Viashino Fangtail</a> and <a>Boros Guildmage</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>The second pick was from an even weaker selection, when Bram took <a>Conclave Equenaut</a> over another <a>Strands of Undeath</a> and <a>Elves of Deep Shadow</a>. Another Equenaut joined his pile over <a>Helldozer</a>, <a>Elvish Skysweeper</a> and <a>Skyknight Legionnaire</a>. As an aside, the aggresssive Boros start is definitely underdrafted, and this table was no exception with <a>Boros Swiftblade</a> and Sellsword Brute being shoved like hot potatoes through the later picks. (Tune in to moxradio later in the day, as they have a very interesting feature coming up on this topic.)</p>&#10;      <p>Pick four brought Bram a <a>Centaur Safeguard</a> over <a>Boros Swiftblade</a> (see?), <a>Viashino Fangtail</a> and <a>Votary of the Conclave</a>. Pick five brought <a>Drift of Phantasms</a> over <a>Caregiver</a> and <a>Strands of Undeath</a>. Two Signets (Boros and Dimir) followed, and the rest of Ravnica went uncontroversial with <a>Courier Hawk</a>, <a>Courier Hawk</a>, <a>Convolute</a>, <a>Conclave Phalanx</a>, <a>Caregiver</a> (as a 12th pick over <a>Boros Swiftblade</a>), and another <a>Courier Hawk</a>. The overall card quality seemed weak at this side of the table, and there were no indications that any other color would have been better than the white Bram took.</p>&#10;      <p>That made Orzhov in Guildpact and Azorius in <i>Dissension</i> the obvious choices. Guildpact gave Bram a solid first-pick in <a>Pillory of the Sleepless</a> (over <a>Orzhov Euthanist</a>, <a>Ghost Warden</a>, <a>Silhana Starfletcher</a> and <a>Sword of the Paruns</a>), also a very good target for the Drift's transmute. He stayed on track in pack two, with <a>Ghost Warden</a> over another Euthanist, <a>Scab-Clan Mauler</a>, <a>Wildsize</a> and <a>Torch Drake</a>. Because he shunned green in Ravnica, a switch to green at this point would have meant going four colors, and the power of the available cards didn't justify that at all.</p>&#10;      <p>Guildpact's pick three was <a>Orzhov Signet</a> over <a>Castigate</a>. and <a>Ostiary Thrull</a> (over <a>Mourning Thrull</a> and <a>To Arms!</a>) and <a>Shrieking Grotesque</a> followed. Then, pick six had <a>Absolver Thrull</a>, <a>Train of Thought</a>, <a>Thunderheads</a> and <a>Torch Drake</a>. It took Bram a little while to finally decide on the <a>Torch Drake</a>, probably for mana curve reasons.</p>&#10;      <p>He shunned another <a>Absolver Thrull</a> for <a>Droning Bureaucrats</a>, and then <a>Castigate</a>, <a>Petrahydrox</a>, <a>Runeboggle</a>, another <a>Castigate</a> and Infiltrator's Magemark (over <a>To Arms!</a>) rounded out Guildpact. Clearly, at that point, any of the Azorius goodies, <a>Azorius Signet</a> and <a>Azorius Chancery</a>, would be very welcome to Bram's as yet unexciting deck. It had an ok curve, but lacked the smashing power that can be crucial in this format.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Windreaver" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Windreaver&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>In <i>Dissension</i>, Bram opened <a>Seal of Fire</a>, <a>Rakdos Ickspitter</a>, Hide//Seek and <a>Beacon Hawk</a>, deciding against the removal spell and going for <a>Beacon Hawk</a>, shipping solid Rakdos to Georgios Kapalas on his left. The second pack had <a>Azorius Chancery</a>, Freewind Falcon, <a>Condemn</a> and <a>Guardian of the Guildpact</a>. Bram chose the creature over the removal and went with Guardian. Another <a>Seal of Fire</a> passed through Bram's hands on pick three in favor of <a>Carom</a>, followed by a Chancery and an <a>Aurora Eidolon</a> (passing <a>Anthem of Rakdos</a>).</p>&#10;      <p>Then, pick six had <a>Soulsworn Jury</a>, <a>Ocular Halo</a> and <a>Windreaver</a>. The 1/3 was the obvious pick, but surprisingly late, indicating that the lack of higher quality Azorius came rather from the packs than from the picks. Passing a <a>Sporeback Troll</a> at seventh pick, Bram finished the draft with Chancery number two, <a>Beacon Hawk</a>, Hide//Seek (coming around), <a>Vision Skeins</a>, <a>Beacon Hawk</a> and another white Eidolon.</p>&#10;      <p>The draft did shape up well, with white providing the meat of the deck and a very solid mana curve, but Bram was not satisfied with the <i>Dissension</i> picks. &quot;The cards I wanted didn't really come&quot;, he said: no <a>Azorius First-Wing</a>s, no <a>Minister of Impediments</a>, no <a>Azorius Guildmage</a>. (That one was snatched away to seats before Bram, when Wesimo Al-Bacha picked the U/W powerhouse to get some shape into his four-color grab bag.) Still, Bram has a very well-shaped mana curve, a sackful of fliers, <a>Windreaver</a> and <a>Last Gasp</a>, Pillory with a Transmuter as removal. The deck is capable of at least a 2-1 record. We'll see how it goes!</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="2"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, June 4: 1:34 p.m. - Feature Match Round 11: Raphael Levy - Bram Snepvangers</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Bram was the last player with maximum points, and he was trying to keep it that way. Raphael, while being his friend, obviously wanted to change it. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gptori06/2b2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Bram Snepvangers, happy about his perfect score</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Raphael got a slightly faster start, with <a>Elvish Skysweeper</a> and <a>Aquastrand Spider</a>, but was stuck on three mana. Meanwhile, Bram made <a>Centaur Safeguard</a>, <a>Ostiary Thrull</a>, and <a>Conclave Equenaut</a>. The Thrull traded with the Spider, and the Equenaut kept hitting in the air, while Raphael was desperately trying to get to five mana so that he could use his Skysweeper. He didn't make it, and had to concede in the end. </p>&#10;      <p>Game 2 started bad for Raphael as well, as he had to mulligan twice. He recovered pretty nicely though, with <a>Civic Wayfinder</a> and <a>Gruul Turf</a> making up for the card disadvantage. The two started a race, with Bram summoning flyer after flyer, while Raphael got to deal with most of them with his <a>Elvish Skysweeper</a>. While Raphael had a <a>Gruul Guildmage</a>, two copies of <a>Aurora Eidolon</a> on Bram's side made it difficult to use it effectively. Bram got in some damage with his flyers when Raphael couldn't deal with them right away, while Raphael could make his creatures virtually unblockable by keeping mana open for his Guildmage. Raphael then improved the board position significantly by playing <a>Twinstrike</a> while he was hellbent, leaving Bram with only three creatures. Bram beat him to four life. Raphael attacked with an innocent-looking Guildmage, but Bram didn't feel save at eight life and blocked with <a>Ostiary Thrull</a>. A wise move, as Raphael revealed <a>Psychotic Fury</a>, which would have been lethal. Still, Bram looked in bad shape, as he would die next turn, and he only had two 1/x creatures. However, he attacked, and smiled when he revealed the good old <a>Wojek Siren</a>, dealing just enough damage. </p>&#10;      <p>Bram Snepvangers beat Rapahel Levy, 2-0</p>&#10;      <br>&#10; He had 30 of a possible 36 points, and the best tiebreakers, so 2-0-1 or possibly even 2-1 would be enough for him. </p>&#10;      <p>His first booster was quite tough: Apart from a <a>Dimir Cutpurse</a>, it included <a>Selesnya Evangel</a>, <a>Selesnya Guildmage</a>, and <a>Faith's Fetters</a>. In the end, Antoine picked the Guildmage. While he thought the Fetters would be better, the Guildmage gave him most options, with white, green, and even the under appreciated Selesnya, if the cards came the right way. The next pack had <a>Veteran Armorer</a> and <a>Belltower Sphinx</a>, and Antoine stayed on course with the Armorer. Then came <a>Trophy Hunter</a> over <a>Drift of Phantasms</a> and <a>Brainspoil</a>, <a>Transluminant</a> over <a>Screeching Griffin</a> and <a>Golgari Rot Farm</a>, and <a>Fists of Ironwood</a> over <a>Boros Guildmage</a>. The rest of the first round were some Selesnya fillers. </p>&#10;      <p>Drafting Selesnya isn't really easy in Ravnica-Guildpact-<i>Dissension</i>. The guild distribution does not allow for a three-color combination that includes a guild from each set. Antoine was fully aware of that, and was prepared to sacrifice his Guildpact booster for Simic- and Azorius-goodies in <i>Dissension</i>. So he started with <a>Ghor-Clan Savage</a> over <a>Mortify</a> and <a>Ogre Savant</a>, two cards that are generally considered to be better. Next up was a <a>Shrieking Grotesque</a>, which he valued higher than <a>Silhana Starfletcher</a> and <a>Orzhov Basilica</a>, even though he probably wouldn't be able to pay the black mana. The quality of cards he picked for the rest of the pack was - as expected - pretty low. <a>Gristleback</a>, Infiltrator's Magemark, and <a>Torch Drake</a> were the most useful. </p>&#10;      <p>When he opened his <i>Dissension</i> booster, he was hoping for some gold cards to make up for the lost booster, but after checking his pack multiple times, he had to settle for <a>Stoic Ephemera</a>. The <a>Helium Squirter</a> he took next looked better, but the rest of <i>Dissension</i> didn't work out as planned. He got solid cards like two <a>Azorius Chancery</a>, <a>Mistral Charger</a>, and two <a>Guardian of the Guildpact</a>, but as he said after the draft &quot;I basically traded my Guildpact booster for a <a>Helium Squirter</a>.&quot; His deck still has enough of the stables to get him the 2-1 that may be enough, but it wasn't the monster he was hoping it would turn out to be. </p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="6"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, June 4: 3:27 p.m. - A taste for mayhem</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gptori06/2b5.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Low mana curves make a happy Bracht.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Maximilian Bracht is not only a constructed deckbuilder, he also has interesting ideas on RGD draft. Because the format is very slow, Bracht wants to be attacking for six when his opponents put down their first Signet. Boros cards like <a>Sell-Sword Brute</a> and <a>Frenzied Goblin</a> (&quot;a solid first pick&quot;) go around late, and Bracht has almost no competition at a draft table when going for his strategy.</p>&#10;      <p>The first draft of today did not go as well as he wanted, finishing his pod 1-2, but watching the second draft showed what Bracht wants the deck to be like. Bracht says drafting the curve is crucial: &quot;I really would have liked another one-drop creature, but four should be ok, too.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>His second draft started off with <a>Stinkweed Imp</a> over <a>Golgari Rotwurm</a>, <a>Bramble Elemental</a>, <a>Selesnya Evangel</a> and Oordrun Commando. He hate-picked <a>Golgari Brownscale</a> second pick, with <a>Watchwolf</a>, <a>Roofstalker Wight</a> and <a>Goblin Spelunkers</a> in the pack. Bracht had no intention to play the two Brownscales he picked at all, but took them &quot;because they just kill me&quot;. And his key cards will come around later in the draft anyway.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Viashino Fangtail</a> as the third pick (other cards of note: <a>Compulsive Research</a>, <a>Mausoleum Turnkey</a>) made for a great start in red. And there were some very unusual picks to watch fro Bracht: <a>Fiery Conclusion</a> made it over <a>Indentured Oaf</a> and <a>Oathsworn Giant</a>. <a>Goblin Spelunkers</a> bested another Oaf, a <a>Dowsing Shaman</a> and <a>Rally the Righteous</a>.
