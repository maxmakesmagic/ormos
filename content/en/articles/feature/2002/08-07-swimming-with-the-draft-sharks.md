### *Magic Online* Drafting Tips & Tricks

* * *

So, you're new to ***Magic** Online,* eh? Come closer, young padawan, as you have many secrets to uncover. You may already be an exceptionally good **Magic** player, but there are some things you'll want to learn before you dive into the shark pool.

Here's why this whole draft thing is interesting. One, it's a small tournament, so it's great for honing your competitive skills. Two, drafting is more fun than most other formats. And three, there are rewards. Whoever wins an 8-player draft gets eight booster packs and the runner-up gets four packs. The extra packs are easily traded back for event tickets.

What does that mean? Win drafts and you'll get to draft again for free! Win one draft out of two and you can draft free for life. I'm not saying that everyone can do it. For the system to work, there have to be small fishes and sharks to eat them. It's time to pick your side!

I am not the greatest **Magic** player on Earth (far from it), neither am I the best drafter. However, I know how to use ***Magic** Online* to its fullest. Sometimes that little edge is what separates victory from defeat. Since launch, I've paid for two drafts and played in seventy. I've even heard stories of former beta testers who've only used their free coupon and are still drafting off their winnings. There's something **Magic** about this game....

Let's start with a piece of advice that will save you many headaches while drafting.

### Stay On

Though ***Magic** Online* requires a rather low amount of bandwidth, you shouldn't have several downloads running during draft or play. That will slow you down or even disconnect you and make you miss picks. You don't want that to happen, so before the draft starts, pause that Napster clone. Now that you've taken care of that, let's move on to the draft itself.

Booster Draft is easily the most popular **Magic** format. And on ***Magic** Online,* you'll find lots of players in the **Sanctioned Tournament** area, mostly in the **8-player Draft** room.

Get in and click on the little eye in the top right corner to display a friendlier tournament list. Then it's time to pick your format. I'm a big fan of *OdysseyTM-Odyssey-TormentTM* tournaments. You might prefer *Seventh EditionTM* or *InvasionTM-PlaneshiftTM-ApocalypseTM* formats, depending on which cards you're more familiar with. Join your favorite and you end up in the tournament queue.

### Spot the Opposition

Check the information about your seven opponents. Note which have the two or three highest Limited ratings on the table and where they sit at the table. If you are passing to one of them, write down the player's potential colors, possible spoilers (Shower of Coals, Overrun, etc.), and any surprise effect cards (such as Shelter or Sylvan Might). Get this information on a piece of paper or into your Notepad window, ready for use if you ever face that person.

### Stop Staring at Everyone

Now that you've spotted your main competitors for the draft, you don't need to look at the table with all players any more. Click **Picks** to see the cards you've drafted.

### Filter Your Picks

As you draft your seventh or eighth card, you probably have an idea of what colors you'll end up playing. At that point, start filtering your picks. Click **Color sort** to get a better idea of your color balance, then right-click each card you draft that you know you won't play (off-color cards, weak late picks) and select **Hide**. That will allow you to start deck construction before everyone else.

### Watch for Card Quality

Filtering has another advantage: it lets you evaluate how your draft is going. To give you a general idea, you should be able to draft between 7 and 9 playable cards in your first booster, 8 to 10 in your second booster, and 10 to 12 in your last booster. If everything goes reasonably well, you should have 26 to 28 good cards to choose from for a deck with 22 to 24 spells in it.

If you're stuck with 9 or 10 cards on your screen as you open the third booster, there's a problem. It might be a good idea to right-click and select **Show all cards**, then think about adding a third color. You can spot this problem quite early, usually at the beginning of the second pack.

### Draft Your Deck

Any great drafter knows it's not enough to just pick every good card that comes along and pray that they will fall together as a deck. You have to make logical choices and look for combos. For example, do you have lots of discard so you can abuse madness in the *Torment* pack? Do you really need another four mana-cost creature? Wouldn't that late pick Demoralize go well with your Rites of Initiation?

***Magic** Online* allows you to make better choices as you draft, if you use it correctly. Click **Type sort** and see how many creatures you have. Yes, having only six playable creatures near the end of the third booster could be a problem. Another tip: click **Cost sort** to optimize your mana curve. That will allow you to spot problems that most people only see during deck construction, like having six four-mana cost creatures and just one that costs three. The **Rarity sort** button is, alas, rarely useful, except for you rare drafters out there.

### Be Greedy, But Only To A Point

Okay, I'll say something a little controversial. It's actually okay to rare draft in some situations. Although your primary goal is to win drafts, your overall goal is still to play as many drafts as possible, right?

Say you've been drafting blue-black and open a Call of the Herd and a Cephalid Looter in your second booster. Though the Looter would fit perfectly in your deck, it might be better to just take the Call of the Herd. First, it's an excellent card that you can splash if you want to. Second, when the draft is over you'll be able to trade Call of the Herd for a draft set easily. That's different from making the finals, but the result is the same: you'll get to play one more draft.

Try not to do this very often. Don't rare draft cards you know won't pay for your next draft. It's a waste of your time and your money. In seventy drafts, I have rare-drafted only a couple times.

### Save Your Deck

Once you're done building, save the deck to your computer. Click **Save** as and give the deck a name you will recognize, such as "red-green-july-17.dec". Then click the **Deck Editor** tab and load the deck you just built. Minimize the **Pool** window so only the **Deck** and **Sideboard** windows are displayed.

Now you have your complete decklist and sideboard available whenever you want to peek at them. This is useful when you must calculate probabilities of drawing a card, if you want to make sure how much creature removal you have left, etc. The **Statistics** window really helps when you're looking for this information.

### Time to Play!

What happens now is up to you and your **Magic** skills, but a few optimizations will help you be as efficient as possible.

The stops are set so I don't have to constantly click **OK** every time I have priority. They also prevent stupid mistakes, like forgetting to remove madness cards from the game. These are my presets for draft games, but in Constructed I might change them slightly to reflect my deck. For example, if I'm playing a creatureless deck, I don't need a stop for my declare attackers step.

### Use F2 and F4

These two very useful keys are shortcuts. F2 is exactly like clicking **OK**, except it's faster and less cumbersome than moving your mouse over the button.

F4 should be used more carefully, as it yields priority for the remainder of the turn (unless an event interrupts the turn, such as damage, attack, or the opponent casting a spell). It's still useful because it helps you earn clock time.

### Use Ctrl

I'm mentioning this because many people don't know how to respond to their own spells. Play your spell as usual, but press down the Ctrl key while doing it. This gives you priority to react before your opponent can do anything. This is very handy in situations where, for example, you want to achieve threshold in response to casting a spell that exploits it. My favorite situation is: I'm holding Shower of Coals, Sonic Burst, and another card. I have 5 cards in my graveyard, 7 available mana and my opponent is at 7 life. Without using Ctrl, I'd have to risk randomly discarding my Shower of Coals before I can cast it with threshold.

### Use Your Notes

I had you take notes earlier for a reason. Remember to use them if you play against the person you passed to twice.

If you ever get to see your opponent's hand, take time to note all the cards on a piece of paper and scratch them off as he or she plays them. You may also take a screenshot and paste it into your favorite image editor.

### What to Do with Your Winnings

*Torment* packs currently go for three tickets, usually two for five tickets at the **Trading Post**. *Seventh Edition* and *Odyssey* packs are worth slightly more. Remember to hop back in the draft area once you have your ammunition! The more drafts you play, the better you'll get and the more drafts you'll win.

It's also a good idea to sell or trade the Constructed-worthy material you drafted. Even if you don't win your draft, this can often get you the product you need for another one.

### Conclusion

Now you've learned the basics. Go practice! Your ability to play **Magic** is what wins games and I just gave you tips to complement that ability. However, they're just techniques. The real catch is to be good at **Magic**, and playing a few games is worth twenty articles.

I hope this article helps. Don't hesitate to write me at poulidor@wanadoo.fr with questions or comments or talk to me on ***Magic** Online* (I'm "manuelbevand" there). In the meantime, happy drafting!
