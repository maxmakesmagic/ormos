Welcome to Non-Theme Week! Before I begin exploring the non-existent theme for this week, I thought I'd take some time to address a very important issue. Everybody wants to be like me! It may seem hard to believe, but with my lack of hair, poor vision, dimples so deep you could sheath a sword in them, and a physique that makes Hank Hill look like Hank Aaron, I'm the standard against which all others are judged. It's cold and lonely up here on this pedestal. Worse still, everybody wants to build decks like me, too! That's because when it comes to deckbuilding, not only do I sing it, but I bring it as well. Each and every week, thousands of readers assemble card-for-card copies of my decks and take them to tournaments unaltered. Like I always say, “It's my way or the highway.” (Sometimes I say, “It's their ways or the airways,” but I rarely say, “It's your way or the doorway.”) 

Okay, none of that is actually true (except the dimples part). I'm concerned because I've been getting some mail from readers who are frustrated that they can't build the decks I write about because they don't have card X or card Y. While it's flattering that people would want to make exact copies of my decks, the truth is, I'm not that smart, my decks aren't perfect, and even though I'm a rich and famous **Magic** writer, *you don't have to do exactly what I do*. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_cm18_picMain_en.jpg)

I know, I know. You're as Shocked as Psychatog. Yes, it's true, you are free to think for yourselves! Isn't that awesome? My point is this column isn't meant to be an art exhibition, and the decks aren't untouchable works of art. You can sit back and appreciate them if you want, but you are also free to take them apart and rebuild them the way you want to, based on your play-style and your card collection. In fact, I encourage you to do this. The mail I most enjoy getting is written by people who've been inspired to build their own decks, or people who've taken a deck I built and made it better. Think of House of Cards as a brainstorming session, the beginning and not the end of the process.

So if I build a deck with four Steam Vents, and you don't have any Steam Vents, that doesn't mean you can't go ahead and build the deck as best you can anyway. The *Ravnica* dual lands are great cards, there's no doubt about it. I've been using them in a lot of decks (more than I realized), because they're good and because I have sets of them in my \_Houseofcards account. So, yeah, they're good, they're pricey, and you'll probably want some if you intend to play in tournaments, but they're definitely *not* required if you want to have fun building decks and playing **Magic**. The same goes for any card.

### Egg-cellent Reader Ideas

Like I said, I love hearing from readers who've been inspired to build their own decks after reading one of my articles. Conversely, I love to hear from readers who just want to share their ideas and, as a result, inspire *me*. A few weeks ago, I built a couple of decks based around *Guildpact's* Hatching Plans. The next day, I got an email from a gentleman named Kurt Dresner. It seems that Kurt has been hatching some diabolical plans of his own. He writes:

> “I've built a few decks online that use Hatching Plans (I'm so totally a Johnny). One of them Reweaves (another crappy rare!) them into Debtors' Knells. The other uses Crack the Earth/Reweave and Hatching Plans to make a nice splicing/card-drawing engine. Neither is really tournament worthy, but I've had some awesome games.”

I believe it. Kurt didn't provide me with decklists, but that's okay. I built some of my own. After playing with them, I have to wholeheartedly second the “awesome games” part. I started off with a U/W deck, hoping to Reweave Hatching Plans into Debtors' Knells. The problem with relying on this plan is that you could very well hit another Hatching Plans with Reweave. One way to improve the odds of hitting Debtors' Knell is to play the full four copies, but I can't imagine a deck wanting that many copies of a such a late-game card. I decided to go with two copies. Debtors' Knell won't do much if your opponent has an empty graveyard, so I included a pair of Dampen Thoughts as well. You can also use Reweave to put creatures into your opponent's graveyard. With Ethereal Haze, Candles' Glow, and a seemingly endless string of chump-blockers courtesy of Spiritual Visit, this deck stalls more than my '91 Ford Tempo. At the same time, Spiritual Visit's tokens are great Reweave targets. With no other creatures in the deck, you are guaranteed to turn that 1/1 speedbump into a 5/6 roadblock (Blazing Archon). Rounding out the deck is Terashi's Grasp, which is a fine utility card. In a pinch, or if there are no other targets, you can aim it at a Hatching Plans.

### Hatching some Dragon Eggs

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_cm18_pic1_en.jpg)

I've used Hatching Plans, Reweave, and Debtors' Knell, but that still leaves Crack the Earth. Many people suggested Crack the Earth for the two Hatching Plans decks I wrote about a few weeks ago. Those decks were U/B, however, and there really was no need to add another colour to get an effect the decks already had access to. However, with Reweave and a deck focused on Splicing, Crack the Earth makes much more sense.

What Blue or Red Enchantments could I 'weave Hatching Plans into? I quickly narrowed it down to two: Meishin, the Mind Cage and Form of the Dragon. They're both big and flashy. They both cost a million mana, which makes cheating them into play with Reweave very appealing. Since I'm not too big on mind cages in general, I opted for the latter. Like Debtors' Knell, Form of the Dragon costs seven mana. Some would say this is a whopping amount, but I'm not convinced it is. Whopping or not, the fact that Form of the Dragon requires Red mana is an advantage, considering that we're going to be putting it in a Splice-heavy deck. Why is it an advantage? The answer, of course, is Desperate Ritual. With four mana available, two of it Red, and two Desperate Rituals in hand, you can generate seven mana. Play the first Ritual and Splice the second one onto it. This will give you six Red mana. Use two of that mana to play the second Ritual, and, voila, seven Red mana. 

Muddle the Mixture is there for its versatility. It can Transmute for Hatching Plans, or an answer card like Consuming Vortex or Glacial Ray. Additionally, it will protect your Forms from most removal, and protect *you* from direct damage spells once the Form is on the board and working its Dragon magic. I was convinced of Muddle's usefulness after I lost a game to an unexpected Lava Axe. Ouch!

### I Dream of Djinni

What if you took the token-to-fatty part from the U/W deck and combined it with the U/R Splice engine from the second deck? From what I've heard, you end up with peanut-butter and chocolate all over the place. Or in cup form, I'm not sure. Either way, you end up with something cool.

Without the White necessary for Spiritual Visit, I turned to Baku Altar to provide the Reweaveable tokens. For the “fatty,” I turned to Replication-machine, Djinn Illuminatus. It's been said that if you can untap with the Djinn in play, you should win the game. I agree. In this deck, Reweave is a lot like *Visions* favourite Natural Order, except you can do it at Instant speed at the end of your opponent's turn. This makes it much easier to get to that untap step with your Djinn still intact. If you end up drawing both copies of the Djinn, you'll be unable to Reweave it into play, obviously. You'll have to hardcast it, but as with Form of the Dragon, Djinn Illuminatus' seven-mana price-tag means that a pair of Desperate Rituals can make this possible as early as turn 4 (turn 3 if you have all four Rituals). 

Why Djinn Illuminatus? As Saturday School columnist [John Carter explained](/en/articles/feature/2006/01-14-guildpact-goodies):

> “Djinn Illuminatus loves splice. Replicate copies the spell, including choices such as additional splices. The Djinn lets you copy the fully-spliced spell for just the cost of the base spell. For example, if the play Kodama's Reach and splice on Glacial Ray and Horobi's Whisper, the Djinn will allow you spend ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif) (as many times as you can) to make a another Reach-Ray-Whisper.”

Just switch Kodama's Reach with Lava Spike or Eye of Nowhere, and Horobi's Whisper with Reweave, and Reach-Ray-Whisper with Spike-Ray-Reweave. Most of the time you won't actually have to make spells that complicated. Lava Spike, Replicated a few times, will probably be enough seal the deal, kill the bad guys, and get the girl.
