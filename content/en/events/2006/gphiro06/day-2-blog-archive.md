<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <b>BLOG</b>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;  <tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="1"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Sunday, Aug. 20: 11:41 a.m. - Draft 1 with Itaru Ishida</b>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>Itaru Ishida is one of Japan's most respected and venerable pros. The Tokyo resident has one Pro Tour and <i>seventeen</i> Grand Prix top 8s to his name. He's also up for the Hall of Fame this year. A strong showing could garner Itaru a few more votes for that honor. He entered today at 11th place, a good place to start the quest for another top 8.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Pack 1</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Itaru's opening pack had a number of choices. <a>Magmatic Core</a> and <a>Gelid Shackles</a> looked decent, but the real decision to Itaru was between <a>Garza's Assassin</a> and <a>Ronom Hulk</a>. Itaru spend all of his allotted time going back and forth between the two cards, finally deciding on the 5/6 beast. His next pack rewarded that Green decision with an <a>Ohran Viper</a> over <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, and then <i>another</i><a>Ohran Viper</a> over a <a>Lightning Storm</a>. </p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/ishida.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Itaru Ishida</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>His fourth pack put Itaru at a quandary. Was Itaru Ishida ready to pick a second color? Non-Green options included <a>Greater Stone Spirit</a>, <a>Boreal Griffin</a>, <a>Chilling Shade</a>, and <a>Ronom Serpent</a>. The only relevant on-color card in the pack was <a>Into the North</a>. Itaru decided to play it safe at this point and took the snow-land fetching sorcery. </p>&#10;      <p>His fifth pack forced Itaru to pick a second color, as literally there was only one Green card, a <a>Frostweb Spider</a>. Luckily for Itaru, his decision was an easy one, with a <a>Chill to the Bone</a> as clearly the best card in the pack. His 6th pick gave Itaru another <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, making it look like Ishida-san was definitely in the right place. The remainder of the packs gave Itaru a snow land, some <a>Gutless Ghoul</a>, and a couple potential sideboard cards. Itaru had a very reasonable start to a strong G/B deck. My quick peek to the drafter on the left showed that the next pack might not go as well; that gentlemen was straddling W/G and W/B. One of Itaru's colors was definitely going to be cut in the next revolution.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Pack 2</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Itaru Ishida's opening booster for pack two was remarkably weak, with only <a>Phyrexian Etchings</a>, <a>Phobian Phantasm</a>, <a>Frostweb Spider</a>, <a>Surging Might</a>, or <a>Snow-Covered Island</a> as possibilities. Itaru seemed annoyed but took the <a>Surging Might</a>, his first. The next pack was a lot better as Itaru grabbed <a>Coldsteel Heart</a> almost instantly, with only a cursory look at the rest of the booster. Itaru's next two picks were the 3/3 <a>Simian Brawler</a>s, the second one at the expense of another <a>Chill to the Bone</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Itaru's fifth pick was a real gift, a <a>Zombie Musher</a> in an otherwise dry pack. The 6th pick <a>Chill to the Bone</a> seemed to indicate that the drafter on his left had not gone with Black; good news for Itaru. The remainder of the boosters again contained mostly filler, with another <a>Gutless Ghoul</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Forest</a> and a 14th pick <a>Phobian Phantasm</a>. Itaru's deck could only be described as decent at this point. With two <a>Ohran Viper</a>s and three <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, Itaru was certainly doing alright, but overall, it wasn't spectacular. A peek to Itaru's right side showed me the drafter on his right was also going G/W. While his Black was wide open, the Green cards were getting cut rapidly.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Pack 3</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Itaru's first pick in the third pack was one of Itaru's most difficult decisions in the draft: <a>Boreal Centaur</a> or <a>Aurochs Herd</a>. The Herd is one of <i>Coldsnap</i>'s best commons, but it looks a little worse as the first aurochs card, in pack 3. Itaru was having similar thoughts and went with <a>Boreal Centaur</a>, a card that fit his curve very well. The next pack was an easy <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a> over a <a>Snow-Covered Forest</a>. The third pack wasn't quite so simple. Itaru had to choose between <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, <a>Surging Might</a>, <a>Aurochs Herd</a>, <a>Snow-Covered Forest</a>, and <a>Deathmark</a>. Itaru narrowed his choices down to the Herd and the <a>Deathmark</a>, ultimately deciding to go with the 4/4 trampler. That decision looked like genius when his next pack gave him another <a>Aurochs Herd</a>. 6th pick gave Itaru his fourth <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, but more interesting, it was about to give his neighbor a 7th pick <a>Juniper Order Ranger</a>. I have no idea what the guy on Itaru's right took over it. The Green/White drafter to Itaru's left looked surprised, but happily added it to his pile. Itaru rounded out the rest of his packs with little guys and a <a>Hibernation's End</a></p>&#10;      <p>Itaru Ishida's deck remained consistently average throughout the draft. While a pair of Vipers and a pair of <a>Aurochs Herd</a> provide card advantage, a lack of Recover cards made his necessary <a>Gutless Ghoul</a>s quite a bit worse. If Itaru can find some early offense and clear the way with his triple Chill(plus one in the board), he has a good chance of winning the match.      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>The buzz is in. Tokyo's Shouta Yasooka has been a long established leading constructed deckbuilder, with a reputation arguably surpassed only by Go Anan and Tsuyoshi Fujita. He led an unorthodox squad with rogue savant Ryuichi Arita to a fourth place finish at GP Hamamatsu, and followed up with a Pro Tour win with Kajiharu80 at Charleston. A 7-1 finish placed him at Table One, with undefeated Kentarou Nonaka to his right and former World Champion Julien Nuitjen to his left.</p>&#10;      <p>Yasooka's first pack couldn't be harder. It confronted him with <a>Blizzard Specter</a>, <a>Krovikan Mist</a>, <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a>, Surging Aether, <a>Squall Drifter</a>, and <a>Sek'Kuar, Deathkeeper</a>. He shrugged and took the legendary Orcish Shaman. He followed up with a <a>Zombie Musher</a>, passing Nuitjen a <a>Mouth of Ronom</a>, another <a>Squall Drifter</a>, and <a>Karplusan Strider</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>With the next pack, Yasooka laid down the cement on the road he would travel. He took <a>Skred</a> over <a>Aurochs Herd</a>, <a>Greater Stone Spirit</a>, and <a>Gelid Shackles</a>. By choosing the one mana instant, he was obliged to take snow creatures and lands later on. Unfortunately for him, Nonaka had settled in Black/Green, ready to poach all the good black removal. Later picks in pack one gave Yasooka expensive but potent troops and removal, with two <a>Phobian Phantasm</a>s, a <a>Krovikan Rot</a>, <a>Thermopod</a>, <a>Greater Stone Spirit</a>, and <a>Orcish Bloodpainter</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>The second pack saw Yasooka drafting a <a>Disciple of Tevesh Szat</a>, followed by <a>Tresserhorn Sinks</a>. Yasooka was hunting for removal, and found it lacking. After a <a>Magmatic Core</a> and <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, the goods stopped coming. He took another <a>Tresserhorn Sinks</a> and snow-covered <a></a><a>Swamp</a>, and got a few late creatures of dubious quality. </p>&#10;      <p>The third pack showed some promise, as Yasooka snagged his first <a>Surging Flame</a>, a second <a>Skred</a> and <a>Chill to the Bone</a>, and another <a>Zombie Musher</a>. He made a questionable pick by taking his fourth snow land, a <a></a><a>Mountain</a>, over a <a>Ohran Yeti</a>. His deck was in dire need of Hill Giants. Late picks were a <a>Lightning Serpent</a> and a third <a>Tresserhorn Sinks</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Yasooka groused as he registered his deck. &quot;It's weak, weak, weak!&quot; With Japanese Nationals on the following weekend, he had devoted little time to <i>Coldsnap</i> draft and as such decided to take a passive strategy instead of coming into the format with a specific plan. The two swapped war stories from the day after sitting down. If you weren't aware of the circumstances, the match would seem like any casual throwdown at Friday Night Magic. But at stake was a Grand Prix Top Eight berth, provided a draw in the final round. At Pro Tour Charleston, Oiso finished in 18th place, Nakamura 20th. A win today would catapult either player into a strong showing in the Player of the Year race.</p>&#10;      <p>Nakamura won the die roll and went first. Both players kept their hands. Shuuhei's <a>Kjeldoran Outrider</a> and <a>Phyrexian Ironfoot</a> failed to get the early licks in, thanks to a pair of <a>Squall Drifter</a>s. Shuuhei made a <a>Boreal Centaur</a>, but it got smacked down by <a>Stalking Yeti</a>. Oiso was ahead with two active attackers to Nakamura's zero. </p>&#10;      <p>The tension spiked as Oiso played the first of his many <a>Surging Sentinels</a>. Nakamura cheered at Oiso's good fortune as he failed to find any friends. But another Sentinel on the following turn brought out five more. Nakamura's good cheer vanished. A <a>Kjeldoran War Cry</a> on the following turn sealed the match.</p>&#10;      <p>Oiso 1-0 Nakamura</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gphiro06/nakamura.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Shuuhei Nakamura</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Nakamura went first and took a mulligan. Oiso sent the four braves towards Nakamura, and the Centaur took down a soldier thanks to a <a>Swift Maneuver</a>. </p>&#10;      <p>Both players took a few turns to fortify their positions and summon reinforcements.
