But yesterday, he managed to extract just six points out of four draft rounds. A 3-0 record in Standard preserved his chances at Top 8 this year, but he had to play to his strengths to win.</p>&#10;      <p>Unfried kicked off his <i>Time Spiral</i> booster with a choice between <a>Might Sliver</a>, Jolrael, Empress of Beasts, <a>Castle Raptors</a>, and <a>Stonebrow, Krosan Hero</a>. Not wanting to fight for green, Unfried picked <a>Castle Raptors</a>. Second pick saw him choosing <a>Griffin Guide</a> over <a>Benalish Cavalry</a>, <a>Ivory Giant</a> and <a>Keldon Halberdier</a>s. Pick 4 gave him the direction to go in: <a>Tendrils of Corruption</a>, over <a>Empty the Warrens</a> (the only one he would see) and <a>Icatian Crier</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Couldchaser Kestrel was next in pick 5, but pick 6 elicited a swearword from Unfried. <a>Yavimaya Dryad</a> and <a>Goblin Skycutter</a>? Another <a>Rathi Trapper</a> came his way for the second pick, along with a <a>Whitemane Lion</a> (which he shipped). Another Lion got the nod over <a>Saltfield Recluse</a> as pick 2 of <i>Planar Chaos</i>, and in pick 3, yet another <a>Whitemane Lion</a> showed up. Jörg chose <a>Blightspeaker</a> instead.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/joerg_unfried.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Deep in thought, but happy deep inside: Jörg Unfried.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <i>Planar Chaos</i> continued without any controverse picks, from <a>Cradle to Grave</a> (5th) over 6th pick <a>Voidstone Gargoyle</a>, and then <a>Rathi Trapper</a>, another <a>Cradle to Grave</a>, and <a>Midnight Charm</a>.        <div>&#10;          <i>by Hanno Terbuyken</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>While the species of the black-white striped, two-legged zebra is a common one at <b>Magic</b> tournaments, the black-red striped variant is harder to find. They are only common among large herds, and typically hide behind desks or in crowds of their white-striped brethren. Their proper name is &quot;iudicis ruber&quot;, generally known as &quot;head judge&quot;.</p>&#10;      <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/falko_goerres.jpg">&#10;      <p>Here in Aschaffenburg, Falko Görres is wearing the red stripes. I had the chance to sit down with him for an interview.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>How often have you been wearing the red-striped shirt?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Falko Görres: &quot;In physical form, I'm wearing the red stripes for the first time. But three weeks ago, I gained experience at Irish Nationals, which I also head-judged.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>How did that happen?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;I got the call on Thursday, asking if I could fly to Ireland the next day. Their headjudge was incapacitated on short notice, so Wizards UK asked me. There were just four judges, including me, for 98 players.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>How was that different from here?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;The Irish nationals were difficult insofar as they are run on a very different level compared to here. We had a lot of deck trouble because the players just weren't used to using decklists. We had to remind the players of things that are almost second nature to the players here. It was a very helpful preparation for me before coming to Aschaffenburg.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>How do you collect enough judging experience to be a good head judge?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;In all tournaments where you are the head judge, and are not alone, you gain a lot of experience, for example at PTQs. Also, I've been to enough Nationals to prepare mentally for the challenges I have to face here. In the past three years, I judged at nine Nationals all over Europe!&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>What's the biggest challenge when being the head judge of German nationals?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Coordinating the 22 judges. I have to do less actual judging and more guiding, taking care that my team leades do their job just as they advise the floor judges.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>You have been to other countries' Nationals, as well as judging on the Grand Prix and Pro Tour circuit. How important is the integration into the international judge community?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;It is very important. It allows you to expand your horizon, get insight into how other judges in other regions and areas work. The IRC judge chat channel on Efnet is especially good for that, apart from personal meetings at tournaments. The exchange of ideas and perceptions happening there is invaluable for the judging community.&quot;</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Is &quot;Nationals Hopping&quot; a common sport among judges?</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>&quot;There are a good handful of level 3 judges who travel around Europe to judge or head-judge various Nationals. There are many smaller countries who have enough players for Nationals qualifier tournaments, but have no level 3 judge. I have a second Nationals coming up: In two weeks, I'll be head judge at the Danish Nationals in Denmark.&quot;</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="3"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, Sept 1: 12:22 p.m. - Round 9: Daniel Rodi vs. Andre &quot;TrashT&quot; Müller</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Hanno Terbuyken</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/daniel_rodi.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Is this the face of a potential winner, dear Daniel Rodi?</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>On 18 points, both of these players are under pressure to win. Already, the race to top 8 has begun in the player's minds. Daniel Rodi is a recent PTQ winner, hoping to prove himself on the big show. Andre Müller has had an abysmal Constructed performance with his &quot;fog&quot; deck. He was under a different pressure altogether: Not being qualified for Worlds, he feared for his Limited rating. He already announced that if he got even one Limited loss today, he might just drop from the tournament just to preserve his Limited Rating, by preventing the chance for a second loss.</p>&#10;      <h3>Game 1</h3>&#10;      <p>Daniel won the die roll, mulliganed, and came out of his green-red gates with <a>Mire Boa</a>, <a>Thallid Germinator</a> and <a>Bloodshot Trainee</a>. While Müller was able to kill the <a>Mire Boa</a> with <a>Deathspore Thallid</a> action, <a>Sporoloth Ancient</a> joined Daniel's board and made Andre cringe, who had just the <a>Thallid</a> and a morph. <a>Sunlance</a> from Müller dispatched <a>Thallid Germinator</a>, but Daniel refueled with kickered <a>Citanul Woodreaders</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>The morph was <a>Zoetic Cavern</a>, blocking one attack from Daniel.            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>The day was drawing to a close, and these two were fighting for their spot in the Top 8. Florian came into the match with 27 points. He needed the win. Tobias had 30 points and was in the &quot;win-and-in&quot; spot. A draw would potentially keep him alive, but barely, because then he'd have to win out in the last round. Tobias piloted a Glittering-Wish-Based control deck of his own design, Florian Reiter was with U/B Teachings.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 1</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Tobias played first and came out hard with Loxodon Hierarch. The next major play was Castigate from Tobias, seeing Compulsive Research, Angel of Despair, Court Hussar, <a>Damnation</a>, Signet, <a>Sudden Death</a>. He took a while to decide on the Compulsive Research, but when the Castigate resolved, he moved to draw a card. Florian made a move to stop him and Tobias realized it immediately. The players called a judge, and Tobias received a warning for looking at extra cards, as he hadn't drawn the card yet.</p>&#10;      <p>Moving on, Tobias kept up the pressure, casting Troll Ascetic and activating his Treetop Village to take Florian down to 14. Florian had nothing but mana on his side of the board, but two Court Hussars had presented him with a good selection. A choice piece in his hand was the <a>Damnation</a> he used to destroy Loxodon Hierarch and Troll Ascetic, while Tobias was tapped out. Treetop Villge was also not alive for long, as Florian had Angel of Despair to kill it. That Angel in turn played catch with Tobias' <a>Wrath of God</a>, and the board was reset to mana only on both sides.</p>&#10;      <p>Florian was first to break the quiet, with an <a>Aeon Chronicler</a> of his sauntering into the removed-from-game-zone with one counter. Florian was tapped out, but Tobias could not capitalize, and after he had untapped again, Florian revealed Castigate. Wrath, <a>Glittering Wish</a>, and Angel of Despair showed up in Tobias' hand. Florian decided that the Wrath was too dangerous. Chronicler came in for five, and Beacon of Unrest returned Angel of Despair to destroy one of Tobias' lands.</p>&#10;      <p>A smile he could not hide stole itself on Tobias' face when he drew his next card: <a>Wrath of God</a>, from the top. &quot;It's still looking better for you&quot;, he said, and he was right: Florian was drawing a ton of cards. Alas, Tobias had entered topdeck mode, showing <a>Harmonize</a> off the top to refill, making both players smile - one in happiness, the other in disgust.</p>&#10;      <p>Troll Ascetic from Tobias proved that <a>Harmonize</a> had brought business. Florian had his third Court Hussar and his fourth Compulsive Research, but no smile found his face. Castigate again. and he saw: Putrefy, <a>Glittering Wish</a>, Angel of Despair and Troll Ascetic. Discarding Tobias' Angel of Despair was no easy decision for Florian, who clearly didn't like what he saw.</p>&#10;      <div>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <a>&#10;            <img alt="Damnation" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Damnation&amp;options=">&#10;          </a>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </div>&#10;      <p>Castigate from Tobias, from the top, revealed Angel of Despair, <a>Damnation</a>, <a>Sudden Death</a>, and three Signets. The axe hit Angel of Despair, the obvious choice. Florian's <a>Damnation</a> got Troll Ascetic; the players were now trading on a one-to-one basis. As both players knew, Tobias had another troll Ascetic in his hand. It came down to the accompaniement of Glitttering Wish, getting Grave-Shell Scarab.</p>&#10;      <p>&quot;Look, I have cards in my deck as well!&quot;, demnaded Florian when he lifted his next draw off the top. It was <a>Aeon Chronicler</a>, quickly suspended with three counters. Troll Ascetic hit, taking Florian to 8. Grave-Shell Scarab followed. Florian responded with a second Chronicler, suspended for one, to stop the bleeding soon.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias attacked, Grave-Shell Scarab caught the inevitable <a>Sudden Death</a>. Another <a>Glittering Wish</a> returned the previously discarded Angel of Despair to his hand, but would the Agnel be able to kill Florian's frist 7/7 Chronicler? no, because Florian had Castigate. Tobias responded with Putrefy to actually kill the Chronicler, but the Angel on his hand was gone. Florian played <a>Extirpate</a> on Grave-Shell Scarab to see Tobias' deck.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias was now under pressure, because the second Chronicler came in and hit for 7. Both players were on 8, but Tobias had no pressure and no defense - yet. Florian wanted to make sure and played beacon of Unrest on Tobias' Hierarch. Tobias had one of his own, though. making Florian say, &quot;Does this ever end?&quot; (Which is just what you are thinking while reading this, probably.) It did, with Florian's Chronicler taking down Tobias.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias Rasir 0 - 1 Florian Seiter</p>&#10;      <p>The 60-minute round was half over at this point, even though both players had proceeded at a brisk pace.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 2</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>The players sped up even more, quickly laying their first succession of mana. Where Tobias had <a>Edge of Autumn</a>, Florian had Signets, but this time it was Tobias to surge ahead in card advantage, with <a>Harmonize</a>. Florian chained Signet into Signet, but the first aggression came from Tobias with Troll Ascetic. He cracked double <a>Flagstones of Trokair</a>. Florian had <a>Tidings</a>. The amount of card advantage gained in this entire match already seemed enough to fuel an entire top 8 of draft mathces.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias made a giant leap when he played <a>Glittering Wish</a> for Crime // Punishment, offing Florian's three Signets. He made the audience laugh when he calmly plunked down two new ones. Tobias cracked with Treetop Village (that caught Concdemn) and Troll Ascetic. Another Treetop Village came down, and a second <a>Glittering Wish</a>, getting Angel of Despair. &quot;Yes, there's one in the sideboard, too!&quot;</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/both.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>The table in the feature match area is just a normal table, but it carries all the drama.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Florian spent a <a>Damnation</a> on the Troll Ascetic. Tobias made <a>Teneb, the Harvester</a>, showing the massive beats his deck could deliver. Florian had a second <a>Damnation</a>, but Angel of Despair was still looming, possibly waiting for <a>Aeon Chronicler</a> as target. <a>Sudden Death</a> from Tobias got Treetop Village, though Florian catched a Warning for mis-tapping his mana.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias wanted more mana, again cracking a pair of Flagstones and playing Farseek. The first Castigate made an appearance from Florian. <a>Extirpate</a>, Angel of Despair, and Putrefy - what would he take away from Tobias? Angel of Despair, &quot;again&quot;. Florian then reanimated one of Tobias' Troll Ascetics with Beacon of Unrest, dealing the first damage to Tobias, who then had Wrath.</p>&#10;      <p>Like a power game of tit-for-tat, the players gave their respecitve opponent no leeway. This was a match well worthy of the finals themselves Beacon of Unrest for a Troll, again, put Florian in the attacker's seat, even though Tobias had Treetop Village for blocking. Tobias' Wrath dispatched Tobias' Troll.</p>&#10;      <p>A series of meaningless exchanges of <a>Harmonize</a>s, Castigates and <a>Extirpate</a>s later, Tobias had punched Florian to -2 life with Treetop Village.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias Rasir 1 - 1 Florian Seiter</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <b>Game 3</b>&#10;      </p>&#10;      <p>Ten minutes left on the clock. A draw would benefit Tobias exclusively, but both players needed the win. During their speed-shuffles, they were already mentally preparing for the extra turns. </p>&#10;      <p>Tobias mulliganed on the draw. As always, mana development was at a prime for both, with Farseek one one and Signets on the other side. <a>Harmonize</a> was matched by Compulsive Research. Castigate from Florian showed two lands, a Putrefy, <a>Glittering Wish</a> and three <a>Wrath of God</a>. Creatures would stand no chance here! Florian took <a>Glittering Wish</a> and suspended <a>Aeon Chronicler</a>. Thank god that thing draws cards, otherwise the Wraths would make it entirely unusable.</p>&#10;      <p>With an eye on the clock, Tobias plunked down <a>Teneb, the Harvester</a>. Florian made Angel of Despair, answered by Tobias' Castigate a turn later. &quot;That's mean&quot;, commented Florian, showing Teachings, Wrath, Mortify and Compulsive Research. Tobias took Teachings.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/florian_seiter.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Tit-for-tat on table one with Florian Seiter.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Angel of Despair and <a>Aeon Chronicler</a> hit Tobias for 11, taking him down to 9 life. The inevitable and expected first Wrath reset the board. Tobias tried <a>Teneb, the Harvester</a>, which Florian snagged right out of the air with Mortify. <a>Glittering Wish</a> from Tobias promised big beats again, with Grave-Shell Scarab going to his hand and then Troll Ascetic being played from his hand. Florian went to 15. </p>&#10;      <p>Angel of Despair from Florian killed the Scarab, which Tobias chose not to dredge back at the next opportunity. Instead, he Putrefied the Angel of Despair, not Knowing that Florian held <a>Damnation</a> in his hand. The extra turns came nearer. Tobias decided to dredge up and play the Scarab so he could have a beater for the extra turns. Florian followed suit, Beaconed up Angel of Despair and killed a land. In response, Tobias Putrefied the Angel. Had Florian targeted the Scarab, Tobias ould have had to decide between drawing a card and Putrefying.</p>&#10;      <p>This way, Grave-Shell Scarab took Florian to 8, then 4, But Florian had <a>Aeon Chronicler</a> coming into play, and Mortify for the Scarab to remove the pesky blocker. Tobias was on 9. Florian could not make his Chronicler big enough to kill Tobias. The result was going to be a draw, but after a short deliberation phase, closely watched by the head judge, Florian decided to concede to Tobias. Florian would have had to win both this and the next round. Since a win here was not in reach, he decided to give his opponent the three points he needed to make top 8.</p>&#10;      <p>Tobias Rasir 2 - 1 Florian Seiter</p>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <a name="8"> </a>&#10;      <li>&#10;        <b>Saturday, Sept 1: 5:57 p.m. - Decklists: The JSS Top 8 Decks</b>&#10;        <div>&#10;          <i>by Hanno Terbuyken</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/mini_magic.jpg">&#10;      <p>Not four, but five trips to Worlds New York could be won at this Nationals.        <div>&#10;          <i>by Hanno Terbuyken</i>&#10;        </div>&#10;      </li>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <br>&#10;      <p>The last round of the German Nationals was a round full of drama. Table 1 had Tobias Gräfensteiner (31 points, OmniChord) playing against Tobias Rasir (30 points, G/B/W Wish Control). Gräfensteiner refused to draw them both into the Top 8, a thing he is notorious for. Rasir was a virtual lock, but if he had lost that match, he was in danger of not making it as one of the 10-4's. On table 2, the same situation happened between Dennis Johannsen and Paul Borczyk.</p>&#10;      <p>While these matches kicked off under tension, Thomas Koch (Gruul) and Michael Müller (ldiot Life) decided on an intentional draw to get into the Top 8 without trouble. Bodo Rösner (Angelfire) beat Alexander Fanghänel (Dredge) to secure his slot, just like Sven Johnson (TarmoRack) defeated Daniel Steinsdörfer (G/W/B Rack). Paul Porojan, GP Strasbourg finalst, managed to beat Stephan Niebler in a Gruul mirror match, but ended up with the unlucky 9th place. Well, someone has to…</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gernat07/DSCF0312.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>The final table 1 match between Tobias Gräfensteiner (left) and Tobias Rasir.</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>On table 7, the match's beginning was intercepted by a deck check. The players had a coupe of minutes free. &quot;I just asked my buddy what my opponent was playing&quot;, related one player after the event. That question and the following answer was overheard by a judge, and after consultation with the head judge, the player - still in the running for Top 8! - was disqualified.</p>&#10;      <p>Meanwhile, on the top two tables, the players decided to draw anyway, in one case with legal damage on the stack.
