I tested quite a bit on ***Magic** Online* for the Standard portion. I tried all sort of things, but I didn’t want to run a black creature deck as it loses horribly against green-red. Blue-green madness looked like the most powerful deck by far, but the mana base is awful and I didn’t want to play quite random mirror matches all day. Black control looked interesting but, after all, the deck loses to itself way too often. It is quite strange that a 23 swamp and 3 Cabal Coffers mana base is unstable, but that is just the way works out. Whenever I played that deck, I was yelling at my computer to give me more lands. I tried 24 swamps and Tainted Pacts, but I still didn’t like the deck at all. Fortunately, there was a good backup plan. My old buddy, Psychatog. That guy had never let me down (so far) and it seemed just fair to give him another chance after he gave me a 7-1-1 record at Nationals last year. I tried a lot of different versions and at some point I had one deck I liked a lot that was running Shadowmage Infiltrators, Merfolk Looters and Unsummons.

Wait a second - did I just hear Zev yell “SEE? I KNEW IT!” all the way from New York? Well, sorry buddy, but I trashed the deck again. It definitely was very good, but just like zombies, it couldn’t beat green-red. Phantom Centaur is tough to deal with and as soon as Finkel gets burned with a Compost in play, you really wish the Infiltrator could phase out and back in at will, just like Jonny Magic himself does on the PT.

In the end, I came up with a version of Zevatog that was very good against blue-green and Wake and decent in the mirror. But, as I expected a fair amount of green-red, I had to discard that deck. The only thing I had left was a regular control version. Marco Blume, Patrick Mello and I ran the same version.

When the pairings went up Friday morning, I was quite unhappy that I had to face a pretty good player in the first round. The good news was that I was pretty sure that he was running Wake, which is a good matchup, especially after sideboarding.

The first game was rather unexciting. Pierre dropped his one Sparksmith on his second turn and had two goblins to go with it while I didn’t find my Pemmin’s Aura. He didn’t have the ‘Smith in the next game but he did have a Drinker of Sorrow on his third turn. I don’t consider that card all that playable and wasn’t really worried, until he enchanted it on his fourth turn with a Dragon Shadow. Suddenly, I was on a three turn clock. The turn before the Drinker was going to kill me, I drew Piety Charm. I killed the enchantment and that was game over, as I had otherwise superior creatures on the board. I was a bit afraid of the Dragon Shadow and boarded in a Daru Sanctifier to deal with that. My draw in the last game wasn’t really exciting and he dropped his Sparksmith again but he didn’t have the second goblin to go with it, so all was good. I eventually dropped my Skyreaver, but he killed everything on the board with Slice and Dice, Sparksmith and Death's-Head Buzzard. My hand was just the Sanctifier that I boarded in, but I drew into Noble Templar and he was already pretty low on life. A Reaping the Grave fetched him his Sparksmith and the Buzzard, but the combo isn’t exactly great. He eventually stalled the ground with a Crypt Sliver but I had a Mistform Shrieker and he was down to five when he had to block with his Buzzard. He put the –1/-1 effect on the stack and shot my morph. I used the Shrieker’s ability to make him a goblin so he would go down to just 2 life while the only creatures on the board were going to be my Templar and the Sanctifier. Then I unmorphed it, looked at my morph and it was… Whipgrass Entangler.

You’ll have to excuse me for a second now. I am going to smash my head against a wall. While I am doing that, you can look up Whipgrass Entangler and realize that it is not a morph creature.

Well, when I saw the card, I just scooped it up as I could hardly believe what just happened. I was 100% sure that I had drafted two Daru Sanctifiers, but when I checked my sideboard, there was another Whipgrass Entangler sitting there. I could go on and try to explain what happened by blaming the use of German cards names, which are not exactly the best translations I have ever seen and because of that I just read the card name and registered it in the decklist to make sure I would have no problems there. I could also say that the pictures are pretty similar and that one has an activation cost of ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif) and the other a morph cost of ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif). But after all is said and done, the reality is that I screwed up really, really badly. Pierre told me after the game that he was holding Dragon Tyrant and would draw the needed 10th land on his next turn and I was feeling a bit better as I would’ve lost anyway. But someone watching the game was friendly enough to clarify that my next draw would’ve been Decree of Justice and there was no way he could’ve survived six or seven soldiers on two life with just the Dragon in play. Thanks for shattering the illusions that my mistake didn’t matter, pal.

With that loss, I found myself in the tough bracket. The four guys on 7-2 all had pretty mediocre decks while the players on 6-3 had the good stuff. I beat Andre Mueller’s Timberwatch Elf/Bane of the Living deck with about six good two casting cost creatures, thanks to a Frontline Strategist that I reused with Weaver of Lies and mana flood on his side. Florian in the last round was the double Skill, double Tracer guy and showed me those two Skills in the first game. His draws in the other two games weren’t as good and he thought that Master of the Veil would morph for four instead of three mana and that cost him game 3.

Usually, I would be pretty happy with a 5-1 in Limited, but not this time. Anyway, that is certainly enough whining for now. If you are qualified for U.S. Nationals, it is a lot more interesting for you why I ran Psychatog in Standard anyway.

The answer is pretty simple: I didn’t have anything better.

If you look at my results, you will notice that I won a lot of matches 2-1. I rarely won the first game with this deck. But the win percentage after boarding is really high. When I lost to Sligh and green-red after board, it was due to the green-red player drawing four spells in a row when I had removed all creatures he had by turn 6 on 18 life with a Persuasion in hand. He just refused to draw a single creature I could steal and instead got a burn spell every turn while my deck gave me four lands in a row. Eventually, he drew Call of the Herd with zero cards in hand and made two tokens. I stole one but his next draw was Volcanic Hammer and he just removed my token and started attacking. I topdecked Concentrate and it got me two Counterspells, that would have won the game a turn earlier, but that way, I just died to the elephant. Against the Sligh deck, I kept a hand with two islands, two Counterspells and an Engineered Plague going first in game 2 and didn’t drew another land until it was way too late. Such stuff happens unfortunately, no matter how big your advantage is.

I already gave the decklist to a few people and most of them wondered what the one Cunning Wish is about. For most of the time in the last two months, I ran the ‘Tog version that I used at a big Standard tournament in Sweden where I had three Duress in the main deck. Since then, both zombies and elves appeared and Duress is pretty bad against those decks. But without Duress, it is almost impossible to resolve an Upheaval in the mirror or against Wake. And against black control, you sometimes lose because they just sit back with several Smothers and an Innocent Blood and it is just not possible to win and you get decked. The answer is Mana Short, but I hate playing multiple Cunning Wishes, as I usually draw them in my opening hand against beatdown and any hand with Upheaval, Cunning Wish and one other slow card is pretty much a loss. With just one Wish, that risk is as low as possible while you do have access to one Mana Short at some point in the game. With Compulsion in the deck, it is easily possible to find that when you really need it. The sideboard doesn’t really have Wish targets as all. The one Ghastly Demise and one Chain of Vapor are fine on their own and I board them in quite a few matchups.

The popularity of this deck has decreased a lot lately, especially as green-blue runs main deck Unsummons these days, which turns that matchup from favorable for green-red into a nightmare. It is still a very good deck and should be out there. The key here is to take as little damage from creatures as possible so that you can afford to not counter the first few burn spells. If the game goes like that, they will be out of gas pretty fast as the first few burn spells have no real effect on the game. Phantom Centaurs are very, very annoying. If you survive the first few turns, you should be able to pull it out. The deck doesn’t have as much power in the midgame as blue-green and runs out of gas pretty fast.

Game 1 favors them as this version has too many dead cards with nine creature removal spells and just two Compulsions. If you draw one of them and keep it around for a few turns, you usually win though. Psychatog is surprisingly powerful on its own. If you get one down, they have to be careful not to tap out for something, otherwise a Concentrate or a Deep Analysis might do the job.

-4 Smother, -2 Engineered Plague, -1 Cunning Wish, -1 Innocent Blood, -1 Psychatog, -2 Force Spike  
 +4 Duress, +1 Mana Short, +1 Chain of Vapor, +1 Compulsion, +1 Deep Analysis, +1 Zombie Infestation, +1 Circular Logic, +1 Ghastly Demise

After you board 11 cards, the matchup gets very favorable for you. The four Duresses are a pain for them and they have nothing that is even close to being that powerful. You won’t get stuck with useless cards any more, as there just aren’t that many. If you know that they don’t have Exalted Angels, you could board out all Innocent Bloods, but that is just too risky in my opinion. Demise is better then the third Blood, as they might have Nantuko Monastrey. Maybe it is better to just keep in the Cunning Wish instead of the Demise as it can fetch back countermagic if you remove it with Psychatog. I haven’t really tried that yet, so I am not sure. Anyway, after boarding, you are definitely winning this if you don’t lose to mana problems.

This matchup is the second reason for the one Cunning Wish. Winning without Mana Short is really tough if they sit on some Smothers and an Innocent Blood. You need to use your card drawing to get to a Compulsion and sit behind that until you can Mana Short, Upheaval-‘Tog and Force Spike or Circular Logic his Innocent Blood. Sounds difficult, but it really isn’t. They can beat you game 1, but it isn’t all that likely.

-4 Smother, -1 Cunning Wish, -2 Psychatog, -2 Force Spike  
 +1 Compulsion, +1 Deep Analysis, +4 Duress, +1 Zombie Infestation, +1 Circular Logic, +1 Engineered Plague

The third Plague comes in as they permanently deal with Nantuko Shade and Undead Gladiator, which are quite annoying otherwise. Duress helps a lot, but the best card you have is Zombie Infestation. Just put it on the table as soon as possible and then sit back behind removal and countermagic until you draw that game-winning Upheaval. If you lose, it is usually to early discard followed up by a Nantuko Shade.

All four Plagues come in only against elves, zombies and goblins. Zombies are a bit difficult, as you need to board in the Zombie Infestation as they have more then enough ways to deal with your Psychatogs.
