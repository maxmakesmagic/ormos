I'm sitting here in Madison, Wisconsin on Thursday morning, enjoying as much hospitality as Courtney Maher can afford a gamer slob like myself. If you'd asked me two months ago where I'd be right now, this wouldn't be the last place on earth I'd have suggested, but it's closer than the first by a long shot.

The road here was an unlikely one, with the convergence of many events required to even get me facing the right direction. The plan all along was to play in Boston with my Hampton Court Palace teammates Ben Ronaldson and John Ormerod, but sometimes the best laid plans are just that: plans. In other words, they aren't finalized realities, and that means that fate, karma, kismet or what have you can waylay them if those forces some believe to be greater than we conspire to make it so. 

<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <h3>&#10;        <i>You have to do what's best for you.</i>&#10;      </h3>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

First things first, despite prognostications from the rest of the English **Magic** community that our winning a PTQ would be a cinch, we never made it to the draft portion in our two attempts. That left us needing to qualify on pro points, and with both John and Ben missing multiple events throughout the year, John and I headed to Worlds needing eleven pro points. After Day 2, we were a combined 13-11. Things were looking bleak.

At the beginning of Day 3, I was talking with Bob, a friend from way back in our PTQ days (that's 1996) and he explained that he and Neil Reeves were in the same situation, trying in what looked like a futile attempt to qualify their team with the soon to return Dave Williams. I casually mentioned the possibility of a team up, should neither team qualify, and the suggestion was received warmly. 

Through the day, as John and I put up more wins than losses, the idea of teaming with the Americans grew on me for a lot of reasons. First, Ben hadn't been prioritizing **Magic** as a more regular pro would as of late, with our roommate Alice and his affections for the lass proving more important. Additionally, John, who ended up on a team with Wilfried Ranque and Antoine Menard, was only going to one more Pro Tour this year due to work issues, and it seemed to me that Houston was the one he should attend. Throw in the fact that Bob is one of the better players on the planet and that most of the people who knew them both agreed that in Limited, Neil was the better of the two (In fact, many told me he may well be the best Limited player in the States) and you had a lot of temptation. I talked to John, who as per usual offered me sage-like advice:

"Gary, I'm in computers. Ben's a professional tennis player and you're a professional **Magic** player. You have to do what's best for you." You've got to love the man's heart. We finished Day 3 with enough points to qualify Team HCP, but by that time I was conflicted. I sent off an email to Ben and agreed to play in the Sunday team tournament with Bob and Neil. Ben's email came back with his blessing, we went through the tournament without a loss, CRUSHING Team Punisher's finest in the final round of Swiss with elimination on the line (two draws) and the decision was made. I booked a ticket to Madison, giving me time to catch up on an *Odyssey-Torment-Judgment* format that I'd grown rusty on, and I'll be here 'til the day before the PT.

Now, why am I telling you all this? Simple: Its Team PT time, and that means for the last many months, there's been jockeying, politics, break ups and reformations going on that makes a gossipmonger like myself drool. This is the fun stuff, seeing who'll team with whom, who'll be left out in the cold by this very socio-political game. There's a lot that goes into the decision of who to play, from friendship to respect, causing bonding and angst, because when you decide who you're playing with, you're deciding who you feel you can put your trust in, or more importantly who you can't. There's a lot more at stake than a win and a check.

The story of our team's formation is a unique one, but so are all the others. Each three-man 'story' came together in its own way. It's not like pro sports, where players are bound together by contracts; there's a shared history and camaraderie in every name you'll see on the page in the Boston coverage. What follows is a look at the team ups of the best players in the world. Sorry if you've been left off; it's nothing personal, but these are the guys whose names are known in the hearts and minds of all gamers. These are the ones to watch.

**Kai Budde, Dirk Baberowski and Marco Blume - Phoenix Foundation** 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cb/00/cb001f6c012ef1e3834e175d8d45426d4135d12c563e0a7ec33b1b0506ba6c9d.jpg)

In case you've been living under a rock for the last twelve months, let me set you straight; this is the best team on the planet. What's that you say, rock man? How about Antarctica? What happened to Potato Nation? Sorry buddy, those teams are defunct, and these guys are better than those guys ever were. Simple fact is that only one team in history has won with a Masters and a Pro Tour, and these guys are the defending champs on both fronts. That's a lot of gusto to swagger into a tournament with. As good as they are at **Magic**, the Foundation's greatest asset is the confidence of supermen that got Kai to bet me a Sunday dinner on Foundation vs. the World in Boston. I never make bad bets, but I must admit that I've made better ones than this. Then again, I know some pretty dumb gamblers.

**Chris Benafel, Pat Chapin and a Mysterious Third** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MASTERSNOR01/680.jpg)

Linked in part by Chris's girlfriend Liz Lempicki and her Michigan home, Chris and Pat were originally slated in with Alex Borteh, but the Ohio native's Worlds 2001 finish has fallen off the pro point tabulation tables as far as Masters is concerned, and that plus University has forced a re-prioritization that has left him less interested in staying competitive in the pro game. One scenario that's out there could see the two teaming up with the now pro pointless Dave Williams in an attempt to grind their way in with three of them flying Borteh out for one last hurrah and playing with him if the Williams gambit fails.

  

**Jeff Cunningham, Gab Tsang and Brian Hacker** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/WORLDS2000/images/126.jpg)

Long before team drafting was a sanctioned format, it was a popular sport among PT regulars looking for a little more adrenaline while waiting for the end of PT rounds, and Tsang and Hacker have been teaming up since before time began. With both players attending fewer PTs today than in the past and old teammate Igor Frayman no longer in the competitive ranks, it was time to find some fresh blood, which they did in the form of Jeff Cunningham, the young Vancouver native who spent the year in Tsang's native Toronto for school. After Tsang's strong Cleveland showing, expect another one from the team whose core came 7th two years ago.

**Eric Froehlich, Kamiel Cornelissen and Noah Boeken** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/masterstok01/662.jpg)

One year ago, new wunderkind Kamiel teamed with Eric and Kyle Rose for a strong showing in New York, but with Team ABU splitting up in the wake of Ryan Fuller's suspension, Noah was forced to look for a new team, and with big Kyle not playing actively, this seemed like a natural fit. Noah and Kamiel are reunited for the first time since Worlds, where prognosticators suggested their team with Tom van de Logt would emerge triumphant. They hope to avenge a disappointing this time around.

**Nico Herzog, Eivind Nitter and Louvre Crnobori** 

And 

**Oyvind Odegaard, Sigurd Eskeland and Endre Skjetne** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/masterssd02/a945.jpg)

In one of the nicer stories going into this Pro Tour, newcomers Louvre and Endre didn't have the points to get to Boston themselves, so an agreement was reached by these six Norwegians to split in a way that they could all go. Some would suggest that this has left the country with two weaker teams than they could otherwise field, but hey, Wizards has been saying all along that all you need is a deck and a friend. The odd man out is Bjorn Jocumsen, who two years ago came in fifth with Nico and Nitter, but that was by design; the wife and kids are keeping him home.

**Anton Jonsson, Mattias Kettil and Thomas Rosholm** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPWARSAW01/888.jpg)

Taking a breather from his full-time job scaring kids out of **Magic** Online queues, Anton Jonsson teams with two of his Punisher teammates in the hopes of making his third PT Sunday in less than a year to go with his Euros Top 8. After a disappointing last round at Worlds, it'll be interesting to see how one of the hottest players in the world rebounds.

**Darwin Kastle, Dave Humphreys and Rob Dougherty - Your Move Games** 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/9b/35/9b352f8d96418a47ae7cb128c5a9ef6d7653d6344219a648b9094165f494da31.jpg)

Three years ago, team YMG won the first team Pro Tour, and they haven't slowed a step. Last year, starting 1-1-1, they were forced to drop to protect their rating in order to ensure their qualification for the Osaka Masters, but this year, with ratings invites no longer a part of such decisions, I'll boldly predict they'll make it to round four. These guys bring two crucial pluses to the table: the lack of a weak link, with over ten individual Top 8s among them, and a familiarity that is unsurpassed on the Pro Tour. YMG is closing in on a decade of gaming together, and they approach PTs in a more professional manner than any team on the Tour.

**Raphael Levy, Franck Canu and Christophe Haim** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/EURO01/897.jpg)

Is it possible that this is France's number three team? All three are strong players, with Canu being potentially the best player in the world to not garner a Top 8 finish while Haim has been thriving over the last year. Raphael is a young veteran of the PT wars, so if they can keep their heads on straight, a strong finish should be in the cards.

**Patrick Mello, Andrew Cuneo and Andrew Johnson** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTSD02/795.jpg)

In the three years of team PT play, only one team has managed two Sunday finishes. Their name? Car Acrobatic Team. Johnson, Cuneo and Aaron Forsythe have been a model of excellence over the last two years, but with Aaron graduating to WotC, a replacement was needed and German powerhouse Mello fit the bill. Pittsburgh is well known as a Team Limited hotbed, but this format seems to be the least breakable yet. If anyone can do it though, it'll probably be these guys.

**Steve O'Mahoney-Schwartz, Dan O'Mahoney-Schwartz and Brian Davis** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/masterssd02/a951.jpg)

DanO hasn't been seen in PT pastures this year and SteveO and Jon Finkel have both faltered, so pro point qualification was no longer theirs to have. That meant a split was necessary and new teammates required, and Brian Davis and his many PT points fit the bill nicely. Davis has been playing strong **Magic** of late, but he's always had problems in the Feature Match spotlight. It'll be interesting to see if Steve's tempering hand will be helpful there.

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTSD02/754.jpg)

William "Baby Huey" Jensen, Brock Parker and Matt Linde - Huey, Brock + Linde Baby Huey's wisdom was questioned when he parted ways with long time partner Ben Rubin a year ago, but being a part of a close knit team is important and they don't come much closer than these three roommates. Parker could also lay claim to that 'best player without a Top 8' crown while the hard-luck Linde is a former U.S. Champ. They know each other backwards and forwards, but how much have they practiced?

**Mike Pustilnik, Gary Krakower and Matt Vienneau - The Ancient Kavus** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPMOS01/967.jpg)

Krakower and Vienneau have dropped out of the Pro Tour picture of late, but fortunately for them, MikeyP and his pro points want to give them one more try in this format. If they don't succeed mightily here, I see Pustilnik heading in a different direction next year.

  

**Ben Rubin, Brian Kibler and Ken Ho** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTOSA02/660.jpg)

At World Championships, Ben Rubin asked me who the best player on this team was, and when I replied that he was, he told me I was wrong. Ken Ho carried the Colony flag all the way to the win in Osaka, but he's only shown up to one tourney since and performed abysmally there. Kibler's been putting up good numbers of late, so if Ben was right in his appraisal, this should be one of the stronger teams out there.

  

**Antoine Ruel, Olivier Ruel and Florent Jeudon - Black Ops** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/international/sideboard/gpcannes00/images/team.blackops.jpg)

Consistently one of the best teams in the world, these guys have been playing together since the first French team leagues started up way before PT-DC. They've had consistently strong finishes at the Pro Tour, not to mention winning the Team Booster Challenge that proved to be the precursor to the Masters. Jeudon took some time off from **Magic** and it looked to some like the Ruels might try to find a new third, but a couple of GP Top 8s from Florent made the decision to keep the team together easy. One of the best.

**Alex Shvartsman, Justin Gary and Zvi Mowshowitz - Illuminati** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/681.jpg)

If it ain't broke, don't fix it. A year ago, these three seasoned free agent teammates cruised through the Swiss only to be upset in the Semifinals. Now united as teammates on Your Move Games, the three of them are together again with a little more experience. 

**Roger Sorino, Andy Stokinger and Ed Fear** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPFLO00/102.jpg)

If its comedy value you want for your dollar, here's where you should be spending it. Sorino is going to sit quietly as the dung flies by his head from every angle, with Team Academy creator Stokinger looking for his first gravy train berth, still seeming reserved when compared to teammate Fear. Ed, for good or bad, is one of the most watchable players on the Tour, oft times putting entertainment ahead of concentration. I'd guess they won't finish as high in the standings as most of the teams listed here, but I'm guessing they'll have a great time applying psychological warfare to anyone put in their path.

**Peter Szigeti, Jon Finkel and Svend Geertsen** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MASTERSBAR01/695.jpg)

Did you ever think you would see the day where Peter Szigeti would carry Jon Finkel to the Pro Tour? Well, that day is here folks. Jon's had a brutal year, leaving him with an anemic fifteen pro points and without enough to reform Antarctica, so he teams with the infamous PTR and four-time Top 8 finisher Svend in trying to capture the Team PT crown he and the OMS boys never got. Originally, Jon and Peter were to play with Dan Clegg, but problems within the ranks caused a parting of ways even before Clegg's suspension. Knowing in hindsight that Geertsen, one of the most naturally talented players on the planet was available, I'm sure they'd have 'reorganized' even sooner.

**Amiel Tenenbaum, Gabriel Nassif and Farid Meranghi.** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptny01/a757.jpg)

France turned into a powerhouse this year, and in these three you'll find the guys who started it all. A year ago, Nassif and Tenenbaum, along with Nicolas Olivieri, took the PT by surprise, dispatching Illuminati before eventually bowing to the Germans in the finals. One Pro Tour later, the rookie Farid took the PT by storm, winning San Diego, and eventually the Rookie of the Year race by the slimmest of margins over Amiel. Now, along with Masters competitor Nassif, they head to this Pro Tour carrying the French flag with hopes of replacing the Black Ops as France's number one team in the minds of the PT community. 

**Jens Thoren, Johan Sadeghpour and Tomi Walamies** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptsd02/a691.jpg)

The most asked question about this team might be 'Who is Johan Sadeghpour, and why isn't Anton on here?', but Sadeg-power's been on Punisher a lot longer than AntonJ and has his own credentials, with more than one strong PT finish and a Gateway victory in San Diego this year. He's flanked by two of the strongest players in the game in Thoren and Walamies, both of who are heading to the Invitational in October. Don't worry about Johan; he'll carry his weight.

**Mike Turian, Eugene Harvey and Osyp Lebedowicz** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/772.jpg)

Well, I can't blame Mike for his decision to leave Potato Nation. I'd moved to England, Scott Johns took a step back from playing on the Pro Tour and it felt like the right time for the mighty Potato, who really wanted to play with roommate and U.S. National Champion Eugene. With a pair like that, they could have picked up just about anyone as their third, and they eventually settled on Osyp Lebedowicz, with Top 8, 16 and 32 finishes in his first three Pro Tours. Osyp lives in New Jersey, giving Mike and Eugene a chance to test when the latter is home from school at CMU, but this format has a soft spot in its heart for Turian, so these guys will get most of their work done in Pittsburgh.

**Jelger Wiegersma, Frank Karsten and Victor Van den Broek** 

![](https://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/668.jpg)

The number two rated team in the world, these guys probably have a better overall record than Phoenix Foundation even if they don't quite have the same reputation. Jelger's the Pro Tour's quietest powerhouse, Frank holds the respect of all his Dutch peers and Victor's been on fire as of late, as evidenced by his 6-0 Day 1 at Worlds and his third place finish at Euros. These guys got eliminated in the Semis of the Osaka Masters, but should rebound well.

This article isn't entirely complete. I know that Diego Ostrovich was likely re-teaming with his Argentine teammates, but I've heard nothing about what's going on with Carlos Romao, likewise the Japanese teams, for which I do apologize, as the format is played more in Japan than anywhere. Regardless, the above should give you a fair idea of who to follow in the coverage come Boston. May your favorites prevail.

Have a good week.
