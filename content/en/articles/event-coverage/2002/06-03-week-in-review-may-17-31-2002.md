### The Extended Rotation

By far the most important bit of news in the world of **Magic** over the last two weeks is the Extended rotation. The DCI announced that, as of six months from now, dual lands and six expansion sets will rotate out of Extended. The new rotation policy was announced - one that will keep the format changing every few years. For now however, few competitive players were pleased.

For one thing, most of us like the current Extended format. Ever since Pro Tour-New Orleans, I've been writing about just how healthy the format is - with plenty of competitive and, more importantly, fun decks to choose from. At the most recent major Extended tournament - the Nice Masters - thirty one players showed up with somewhere around fifteen different deck archetypes. So why the change is a right move? There are several arguments in its favor. As a gamer, I am excited about the fact that every single archetype that is popular in Extended now is slaughtered. This means a brand new metagame in November, which may prove fun to deck build in. As a card dealer, I am aware how Extended card prices have been on the rise over the last 2 years. I've been charging $15-20 per dual land a few weeks ago, as opposed to $10-12 in 1999-2000 season. Force of Will and Morphling shot up to $15+ each, and a number of other cards were also reaching the $10 mark. This was great for dealers, but not so great for players trying to get into Extended for the first time.

One of the main reason people are disappointed with DCI's decision is that they perceive the value of their collections as significantly diminished overnight. I've been buying up dual lands for $3-4 a piece during the Prerelease. However, my advice to those "stuck" with many Extended cards is not to sell off their collections in a panic. At that same Prerelease, I was doing brisk business selling those same dual lands. Plenty of casual and Type 1 players still want them - and the same will be true for a number of other out of print cards as well. Once the panic sellout subsides a few weeks from now, you will see prices for many of those cards rise once again, though not quite as high as they were last month.

### Magic Invitational - Online

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5a/48/5a487069d9fc2c7a1ecece659d5f148fe7e62be20971e10ca589d784c729e925.jpg)

Earlier this week, Mark Rosewater announced the location for this year's **Magic** Invitational. Having traveled around the globe and visited five continents, the event will be held in North America for the first time. Sixteen invitees will be invited to Seattle, to tour Wizards of the Coast corporate offices, and to compete in front of live audiences - online. This year's event will take place on **Magic** Online - which I think is a great way to promote the software. As an avid fan of the program, I am definitely excited about the prospect - and the ability to view each match live is unprecedented and exciting. Let's hope it does not put the *Sideboard* staff out of work!

### National Championships

*Argentina*  
 Diego "Chino" Ostrovich (u-g-b madness) is the National Champion for the second year running. He is joined by Gabriel Michelena (Trenches) and Gabriel Caligaris (u-g-b madness) on the National Team. Ostrovich and Caligaris played the same deck, an original variant on the madness scheme, splashing black instead of red for Duress and Zombie Infestation. 

[Top 8 decks and deck breakdown](https://web.archive.org/web/20020603000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.themagictutor.com/torneos/Nacional_Argentino_2002/nac02_mazos.html&origin=sideboard_wir_20020603)

*England*  
 The online Magic community is quite a bit annoyed at the lack of deck lists from the Englih National Championship. We know that Mark Waterhouse won, using a r-g beatdown deck. Tom Harle made 2nd with an unusual b-g Ichorid deck, featuring cards like Buried Alive, and Sam Gomersall played 'Tog. 

Several of England's better-known players placed in the Top 8 but did not make the team. Ben Ronaldson and John Ormerod (who finished 9th) played Liquid Tempo. Gary Wise and Ollie Schneider splashed white into the deck - but the exact deck lists remain unknown. I assume that they will become available after the U.S. Nationals. In the meantime, I have nothing good to say about event organizers and British web-based press, who made England the only major European country that failed to publish Nationals deck lists in timely fashion.

[Final standings](https://web.archive.org/web/20020603000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://infinity.kirenet.com/~rhorvik/england.htm&origin=sideboard_wir_20020603)

*South Africa*  
 The winner was Christian du Plessis (u-g-r madness). He beat Lourens Scholtz (Zevatog with red). John Wood (Zevatog) placed third. Du Plessis made the National team twice before as a finalist, and finished in 5th place last year.

[Coverage](http://mzone.mweb.co.za/residents/mwwendy/nats2002b.html)

*Turkey*  
 Siyar Erzen won the Nationals with Psychatog, defeating Eda Bilsel's Trenches deck in the finals. Basar Coskunoglu qualified for the National Team, also with Trenches.

TJ Collectibles over at Milford, MA hosted a very fun event last weekend. Last week's question:

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpmil02/948.jpg)*What ex-Magic R&D member competed at Grand Prix: Milwaukee?*

Mons Johnson (of Mons's Goblin Raiders fame) played at the Grand Prix and did very well, fighting for Day 2 in the last round. Like other WotC employees, Johnson could not play in sanctioned tournaments for a while after leaving his job at R&D. Now that the grace period has ended, he can feel free to go pro.

Dan Mackler was playing a mirror match against monoblack control. His opponent cast a lot of discard spells early on, catching mostly the useless creature destruction cards - Innocent Blood, Mutilate, and Chainer's Edict. He ended up with Mind Sludge, Duress, Addle, Corrupt and Soul Burn in his own graveyard. Mackler drew Haunting Echoes with Mirari in play - not only was he able to remove most of his opponent's quality cards that turn, but he also realized it was a good idea to copy Haunting Echoes targeting himself. He got those creature removal spells out of the game and prevented himself from drawing anymore dead cards in this creatureless matchup.

### Bad Play of the Week

Courtesy of Ray Powers:

Okay, to preface this Bad Play of the Week correctly, you will need to see a picture of the event site the San Diego Prerelease: [http://www.sdccc.org/sdconcenter/photographs/exterior/jpgs/cc51.jpg](https://web.archive.org/web/20020603000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.sdccc.org/sdconcenter/photographs/exterior/jpgs/cc51.jpg&origin=sideboard_wir_20020603)

Notice the long slanting Glass part, because it's very important. 

During the Prerelease, two of the younger teen **Magic** players went out to the smoking area (not to smoke), which is kind of sideways and up from the slanted glass. They found it very neat that such a glass existed and decided to 'slide down to the ground' on the outside glass, climbing up over the railing and on to the slanted glass surface. 

Those of you viewing the picture will note that at one point, however, the glass slant... well... stops. As did the players, fortunately not dumping over the edge and splatting. However, they were unable to traverse to a safe place from where they were, and eventually were spotted and a public safety officer had to drive in a cherry picker to rescue them from their location.
