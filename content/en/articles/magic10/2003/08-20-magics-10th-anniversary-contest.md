### Top 5 - The Best Game of Magic You Ever Played and Why It Was So Great

**1st prize**  
*Jeremy Peters, British Columbia, Canada*

When I was 14, I spent a summer with my mom traveling around France. We started off in Marseille, and made our way up to Paris, staying in bed-and-breakfasts along the way. I remember when I was packing my bag I had a really hard time deciding what to bring. One thing I'm glad I brought was my deck of magic cards.

I have been playing magic on and off since 4th edition. I still remember buying my first starter deck and getting a Force of Nature! Boy I loved that card (even though I was 8 or 9 years old, and had no idea how the game worked "Wow, 8/8 - those numbers are really big" I remember thinking. Anyway, I am trying to say that I had grown up with Magic: the Gathering.

My cards spent most of the vacation in my bag. I would be off touring with my mom while my cards would sit alone in their little box. I met a whole bunch of people my age, but none of them even knew what Magic was; I was very disappointed.

When we arrived in Paris early one morning, I begged my Mom to let me go see the Egyptian Tombs in the Louvre. She didn't want to go right then, but she said I could go by myself and she'd meet me in a few hours. Now that I think about it, letting a 15-year-old kid take the metro by himself probably wasn't a reasonable thing to do, but I am glad she did it. For some reason, I had accidentally put my Magic cards in the front of my backpack, and when I sat down on the train, all of my cards fell to the floor. This boy, around my age, jumped off of his seat and helped me pick them up. He said "Je joue la Magic: L'Assemblée, voulez-vous jouer avec moi?" I mumbled "euhhh, pardon, uhh pardon moi? Je, uhhh, Je ne parlez pas Francais." He smiled and pulled a deck of his cards, from his backpack.

We sat on floor and played three or four games of Magic. At first it was really confusing - he had a lot of trouble understanding me, and I had the same trouble understanding him. But since we both knew how to play, and were familiar with Magic cards, it wasn't that hard to figure what a French Lightning Bolt did, or that my English Crusade gave my white-weenies +1/+1. We probably played for an hour and a half - boy did the time fly bye. To this day I do not think that I have had such and amazing experience as my metro-game-of-Magic in Paris. A card game gave two strangers from opposite sides of the world enough common ground to hang out and have an amazing time together.

When I clued into the fact that my mom was probably frantically searching through the biggest museum in the world for me, I quickly said "merci, au revoir" and got off of the train. I really wish I had asked for his email address, or had some way to find out what has happened in the last four years. Even still, I remember that day like it was yesterday, and it truly was the highlight of my tour du France.

**2nd prize**  
*Jeremy Peskoe, Maine, US*

I have played some great games of Magic and in some great locations. The best game I have ever played was a curious one. By curious I mean curious in why it was the best. It was relatively short and I lost.

However, it always serves to remind me why I love this game so much. My wife and I saved up a pile of money over a few years and took a 3 week road trip around the coast of Spain. In our last week we spent a few days in Barcelona and while exploring the city, we stumbled upon a shop called the "Black Lotus". It should tell you how hopelessly lost to the game I am by the simple fact that after 2 and 1/2 weeks in Spain, with all the history and culture, the thing that made my heart stop was a tiny Magic shop. I convinced my wife to give me a little time to myself and I wandered in. There were a few tables and about 10 people hanging out or playing. Someone sitting at a table solo, asked me something (I don't speak much Spanish) but I eventually deciphered that he wanted to play. As I had packed extremely light, I hadn't brought any decks, but there were some pre-constucts (from Invasion I think) so I bought one. I seem to remember he was playing some current type 2 deck and I went down pretty fast. But here I was in a foreign country, where I don't speak the language and I had managed to play a my favorite game with a complete stranger without saying almost a word. Magic breaks down political, racial, gender, language and now geographical (with M:tG OL) barriers and brings people together. Ill never forget that game.

**3rd prize**  
*Jeff Gerstl, Ontario, Canada*

I was at the 1994 Chicago Comic Con. Batman Forever had just come out, and the DC Booth was blaring Seal's "Kiss From a Rose" and U2's "Hold Me Thrill Me" in a never ending loop. Walking around the booths, I noticed tons of people, sitting in groups of twos, threes and fours in the corners of the rooms. Closer inspection revealed a card game, of such complexity and uniqueness that I was intrigued. One of the gamers was kind enough to point me in the direction of the small booth with the Wizards of the Coast sign. They were holding free tournaments, where you got to keep the cards when you were done! I signed up, and played in my first ever tournament, a Rochester Draft using Revised Boosters, run by a guy with the unusual name of Skaff and another gentleman. Listening to the oohs and aahs of the participants as impressive cards like Lord of the Pit were opened was captivating, and I was instantly hooked. My deck was terrible, but the man across from me was kind enough to show me how to tap mana, cast spells and attack. Needless to say, the game was over quickly.

He gave me some of the cards he wasn't going to use from his sideboard, and I went off to buy some booster packs for this crazy new game. Oh, and the other person teaching the game? Some guy by the name of Richard Garfield...

**4th prize**  
*Ben Blanchard, Maine, US*

The Best game of Magic I ever played. My first game.

My older son was my first opponent. My kids taught me the game. They learned to play Magic during lunch at school. What brought me to this best-ever game was a simple trade my younger son negotiated. I would buy the cards and they would teach me to play. I bought a starter set and a pre constructed deck for myself - Way Wild. The starter set had manuals and rules and counters and all the things I needed to play. I got a box of boosters for my kids. At the time, I had no idea how this would change the way we communicate. We went home and I spent the afternoon with them learning how to play. Land, mana, casting cost, counters, spells, flying, trample, first-strike âEUR" all new ideas.

We settled into building our decks and we talked about the cards; different strengths and weaknesses, strategies, and suddenly it occurred to me "I was having a conversation with my kids". We were laughing and talking about setting up traps for our opponents. We were discovering how cards complimented each other and in the midst of it all, each behind our decks, we were instantly equals; but more important "we were talking. We were connecting." And those connections didn't end there.

I was playing a modified 60 card Way Wild "all green deck with real-life cards. I understood green. Green understood me too. For a beginner that's important. Don't be complicated "just bring the creatures out to play and let the rules do the rest." Attack and Don't relent was the only strategy I knew. It's a good strategy for green players. Lose a creature? Don't worry "there's plenty more where that came from". Over-run, over-power, out-number and don't waste your strength on defense. I had supreme confidence in my deck and myself.

I felt a little sorry for anyone that had to face me "but only a Little". Did I mention my Foil Thorn Elemental?

I finished my deck and my son finished his. I won the roll of the dice and I chose to go second. That was the only part of the match I won. My creatures came out but were just as soon Bursted back to my hand, confiscated or put back on top of my deck. My son flew over my defenses and before I knew, I was mustering my dignity for a beating. Regardless of the score at the end of the game, I won. My kids and I created a common interest. We created a place where they were the experts and I was the novice. It was empowering for them and it opened a window for communication that is always open for me.

That first game was the best game because it changed the rules for us. I don't always have to be the expert "and they can give advice that I should listen to. Since then, we've spent many hours talking together about new cards, new strategies and a lot of other real-life issues mixed in. Magic is often the icebreaker when there are sticky issues to discuss between us. My first game of Magic was the best game because it opened a door that connected me with my kids and that is Magic.

**5th prize**  
*Francisco Javier Guillen Olivares, Valencia, Spain*

Some years ago, the summers were the time of the year in that I could be playing rol games, and painting statuettes with my friends. In the terrace of the field house of some of them, the afternoons passed among laughs, snacks, paintbrushes, metal statuettes and dice.

Every day we were approximately at the same hour and in the same place. We played hours and hours. We really passed fun time.

But one day, I arrived my friendâEUR(tm)s home, and there were my friends in the floor, playing to a strange game of cards, a game that I have not never seen. I found the game insipid.

I don't understand anything. They threw cards, other they twist 90 degree another cards, other were retired to a pile, they were fun and I continued without understanding anything.

The following day, my friend Pablo, one of both that was playing the previous afternoon, gave me more than 100 cards of those that were so strange for me. Then he explained to me how that game worked. During some days, instead of to paint statuettes or to play any other games, Pablo continued explaining to me how it was played. I was learning how to play MAGIC!

Two days later I played my first game, I mean, my first one that was not of learning. I will never forget it. My deck was not very good, black monocolor, with really simple cards, practically all common ones because, yes, he gave me 100 cards, but all common and basic lands.

That game allowed me to send my army of the darkness against the angels, knights and lions. I sent the death to destroy celestial creatures. In few minutes, my troops lost the battle. They lost for my inexperience, not for gills neither for enthusiasm in the combat. I lost the battle, but not the war.

I lost my first battle, I lost my first confrontation against another magician, much more experienced that me; it snatched my 20 points of life without too much effort.

From that day, Magic runs for my veins, I began with 100 cards, and now I have more than 4000 different cards, I love to collect them, to classify, to clean, to put them in its cases. I love to read the histories that appear in the wizards web page relating the histories on some cards, how they were designed, etc. I play Magic every day. I organize tournaments. I like to play Magic! I love this game!

That game was the beginning of something important, something that has not ended up still, something that continuous growing day by day, my love for one of the best games that have never known. And, although it seems a paradox, the history it is that, thanks to a friend, I knew Magic: the Gathering, and some years after, thanks to Magic, I have known many friends.

That day the Magic entered in my life, it became a part of me.
