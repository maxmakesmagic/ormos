### Red

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/02/b7022031271bc60e3a9f6cab3cfb1b0385eb357778344880173514d26d4565c4.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1c/07/1c07adccdbee79c82c7d4cc4c5081d331472eb9f6ae3ea4858d79e0c011a131e.jpg)

Mountains were the last land type to settle in. We actually ended where we started, with the visual of these enormous monoliths that recede into the toxic cloud cover. They are the ‘hearts’ of the mountains as they were, the only thing remaining after unimaginable wars and unnatural erosions. People don’t live in these spires; they avoid their imposing shadows, or perhaps worship them as pagan gods. The red-aligned actually live in the dormant volcanoes or blasted craters of what few unshattered mountains remain. If you look closely this is actually represented on Greg Staples’ Mountain art.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/83/fe/83fe51a23483117ce560ca4a3a674c45d82c38ee05d2d4e31d32355c8879eec9.jpg)

![Mountain4_640](https://i.ormos.uk/03/4b/034b2ac05508378e7d37eac8b4f0bf15f862345d61ba0c359a1bc31ce11229d0.jpg)

What about the barbaric peoples tough enough to live in these environments? Cranford loved seeing adaptation worked out visually. How do they live there? His idea was to imply story or world through a sense of cultural adaptation. I was hot to hit the ‘more-toxic-the-higher-you-go’ note hard, and again we ended up with a combination of the two. I quickly latched on to the idea of having red resonate in a similar vein to the [Asaro ‘Mud Men.’](https://web.archive.org/web/20061016000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://images.google.com/images?q=asaro+mud+men&hl=en&btnG=Search+Images&origin=mtgcom_feature_367) Visually it played perfectly into the depersonalization angle, and it could explain a sort of filtering or protective system by which people could breathe in the environment. I pitched the idea of twisted, feral peoples wearing whole-head-enveloping, blobby, coiled forms of flowstone. Creepy, depersonalized, tied into flowstone (which filters the air)… I thought it was brilliant.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/57/ad/57ad5e80cf8dc51c197a9762a89d93e2f5749121a9e95dfd486a466bc0069b54.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/3d/fa3d4bfc5bf513edfefa4e32eeb2954d07263f385fd3fe01f17d850cd91e71d0.jpg)

Much to my dismay, the drawings were immediately dubbed ‘Turd Heads,’ and I had to find a new idea. Still feeling the Asaro direction was right, I simply switched the focus from the stone headgear to the eye and mouth shapes in their clay masks. Red couldn’t have too tech-y a respirator so we plugged a porous filter stone into their masks and voila!

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d1/a7/d1a7bf193d85b2bd01ec9b6a0dc81c044d047588be02fb23368779541f6ea6d6.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e4/d0/e4d0cf40f927e8d887cb88510cbe724f59fdbf648da26c1c203138b85edd917b.jpg)

Anything too simple or unsophisticated to find a way to filter the toxic high-altitude air would wear the results on its face. The soft tissues would recede, and the eyes would become milky, the look we lovingly called ‘crankmouth’.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3d/bf/3dbfa82d378362a024443040d8423ba1632506f8bb0a3eccd2b2457006871dea.jpg)

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/a8/3aa81d65c2d01f8fc8ff45be098c1abd80464883b6924f4c9b6f32809508fd89.jpg)

Red weapons are simply as brutal as the hard-asses that wield them.
