I started writing this report of my experiences at the World Championships while I was still in Sydney, as I was trying to give you a day-by-day account of events as they transpired, but last minute playtesting and sleep got the better of me. The fragments of those writings are lost somewhere on Ben Rubin's laptop, and since now I'm finally back in Atlanta and he's home in San Francisco, I no longer have easy access to those files. It's probably for the better, however, as now this report will be of a consistent perspective, allowing me to reflect on the events of the tournament as a whole rather than one day at a time. Thus - without further ado - the Brian Kibler World Championships 2002 experience.

I left for Sydney mere hours after writing my "Mini Colony - Final Days" article, heading to the Reagan International Airport with my constant companion in all things Magic, Ben Rubin. Our travel itinerary took us to LAX via Chicago-O'Hare, and there we met up with the Froehlichs, who'd flown out of Baltimore. The cross country flight hardly fazed me, as I've gotten so used to flying long distances that anything under ten hours is pretty much routine, not to mention the fact that I managed to finish up the last few books in the Ender series, all of which I'd highly recommend to anyone. I pick up a book by Stephen Hawking on cosmology, but end up disappointed that it doesn't have anything to say that I haven't read before. We run into Eric Franz in LAX, who tells me he's going over to Sydney early and staying in a youth hostel before his roommates arrive - the glorious life of the U.S. team. In any case, the flight from LA to Sydney is exceedingly long, but I manage to sleep through most of it courtesy of my oversized and overly comfortable alpaca pillow (which always draws comments from flight attendants about how soft it looks - and it is!) and we finally arrive in the land down under in the early hours of Friday morning.

At the airport, we run into the Dutch contingent along with their token Belgian, Jan Doise. Ben Seck had arranged all of our accommodations and agreed to meet everyone at the airport to bring us to our hotel, and soon enough everyone's favorite Australian tour guide showed up with a bus to cart us all over at once. To avoid getting exceedingly jet lagged, we try to stay up for the rest of the day doing practice drafts with the Dutch - which don't go overly well for us, with Ben the only Colony member who manages to actually win one. I feel like my decks turn out very well in general, so despite my failure to win any of the drafts outright, I'm still fairly confident in my understanding of the format.

It was during this very first day down under that I fell in love with the country of Australia, or at least the city of Sydney. Most of you are aware, I believe, that I used to be a wrestler, and that I took time off from competitive Magic to pursue that path several years ago. Many of you are also aware, I'm sure, that wrestling is an extremely demanding sport, particularly because it is broken down into divisions by very specific weight designations. For those unfamiliar with the process of "cutting weight" involved in wrestling, I'll just say that I once weighed in at 189 lbs on a Wednesday afternoon - and then 172 lbs the following Friday evening for a tournament. My diet in those days consisted of a bowl of Special K for breakfast, a turkey sandwich for lunch, and egg whites for dinner - that is, when I ate anything at all.

The reason all of this is relevant is because now that I'm back to playing Magic, I can engage in one activity I missed a great deal during wrestling season - eating. If you couldn't tell from my ranting and raving about alpaca blankets, I have a great deal of appreciation for the creature comforts in life, and good food is certainly in that category - and what a place Sydney is to eat. The morning we arrived, Ben Seck took our entire crowd out to a food court at a nearby mall. Now, if you're in the United States, a mall food court generally means something like a McDonalds, a pizza place, a Chinese food restaurant, and a sandwich shop - pretty much all fairly mediocre fast food, without all that much variety. At this mall, however, you could get everything from Thai to Indian to Malasyian food, along with all sorts of European and American dishes. If only I could get sushi and curry at food courts around here - I'd be a truly happy man.

We crashed pretty early that night, exhausted from our trip. I was hoping to just nap during the afternoon and go out on the town that night, but everyone seemed far more set on sleeping than partying, so I had to wait until Saturday to get my fix. I woke Saturday morning at 4 AM, still quite jet lagged from the fourteen hour time difference, and wandered the streets around our hotel for a while to check out the nightlife. I stayed up long enough to watch the sun rise, and finally went back to sleep just about when everyone else was waking up. I didn't arise until everyone was planning on heading out for dinner, so I tag along, and we end up at a nice restaurant down by the harbor. One of the interesting things about Australian cuisine, as Chris Pikula explained in his report from the Invitational a few years back, is the prominence of prawns. I order a pasta and prawn dish, which in the U.S. would consist of a plate full of pasta and maybe a half dozen shrimp, but which in Sydney seems to be made up of a couple noodles and a bucket full of seafood - certainly not a difference I had any intentions of complaining about.

After dinner, we head back to the hotel to meet up with some of Ben Seck's local friends, including Nick Wong, who just recently moved to Sydney for school. With our newfound companions, most of the Dutch and I head out to a nearby pub, where we talk and drink for much of the night. We give the dance floor a shot, but apparently word hasn't gotten out to Australia yet that Right Said Fred's "I'm Too Sexy" isn't exactly classified as "good music". The Australians and most of the Dutch call it an early night for the PTQ the next day, but after the pub closes, I head out with Kamiel and Jelger to a nearby club, where we manage to find better music and stay out till around 6 am. Good times were had by all.

With my itching for nightlife at least temporarily scratched, the next few days involved considerably more preparation for the Standard portion of the tournament. When we left for Sydney, we'd come to the conclusion that the g-w-r beatdown deck I'd dubbed "Red Zone 2k2" was our top Standard deck, but we'd yet to finalize a few important details. We'd waffled back and forth over the Living Wishes, which were initially in the deck but got taken out for a while to see how the deck could perform without them. The reason for this was primarily that we overvalued certain sideboard cards, and we were concerned about having enough room to fit them all. Our testing in Sydney showed that these cards - most notably Seedtime - were significantly less powerful than we'd anticipated, and we concluded that the deck could certainly spare the sideboard space needed to make Living Wish effective.

One notable - and quite frustrating - incident during this period came when Ben and Efro were testing the deck in the room and I'd stepped out for a while. The Dutch managed to get a glimpse of a playtest game when they were let into our room with insufficient warning for Ben and Eric to clean things up, so they saw cards like Sylvan Safekeeper, Glory, and Genesis lying around. Since they hadn't done much playtesting of their own for the Standard portion of the event, and more or less didn't have a deck, they spent a while trying to put together a version of our deck from what they'd seen. While we didn't mind helping them with their decks, which we did - offering up advice on the lists they showed us and the like - we didn't want to compromise our own success by leaking our tech. We were - understandably, I think - rather upset, then, when it seemed to be common knowledge by the first day of Worlds that we were running a g-w-r Living Wish deck.

Regardless of such leaks, we stuck with the deck, as the coverage makes quite clear. We had to scramble at the last minute for cards, as Ben doesn't have any collection to speak of, so I got less sleep the night before the tournament than I might've liked. Of course, I was still quite jet lagged at this point, so once again I woke up around sunrise, and by now I'd begun to develop quite the cough and cold, which just got progressively more miserable as the tournament progressed. Of course, my mind wasn't so much focused on my physical ailments as it was on the tournament to come, and I cued up Prodigy's "Minefields" - the same song I listened to before every round at 2000 Pro Tour-Chicago - and waited for the pairings to go up.

### Round one - Katsuhiro Mori

Some people look at the pairings every round and hope they see a name they've never heard before, signaling an easy matchup where they can cruise to victory. While I certainly don't object to getting a break from playing against other PT regulars now and then, I'm rarely happier at a Pro Tour or Grand Prix than when I'm sitting in the Feature Match area. I love playing in front of a crowd, chatting away with my opponent amicably while Josh Bennett or Toby Wachter type furiously in an attempt to immortalize the banter for the *Sideboard* audience at home. I certainly can't deny that I love the spotlight - I used to be heavily involved in theater, before my **Magic** travels made attending regular rehearsals impossible - and I was happy to hear my name announced over the speakers as a Feature Match for round one.

Game three features an early war of attrition, as my creatures whittle away at his resources, and it looks like I'm winning until Gidi peels a Fact or Fiction followed by a Psychatog from a position of having no cards in hand. Suddenly I can't really attack anymore, and when Gidi draws Upheaval two turns later, suddenly I'm going to lose. He Upheavals with enough mana to play a Nightscape Familiar, and I play a Bird. I know he has a Repulse in hand from his Fact or Fiction, so the following turn I just play an Elf to give me two blockers, rather than tap out and die to his Repulse. Gidi draws and attacks, and I start doing Psychatog math and ask how many cards are in his hand.

"Eight", he answers, and suddenly I stop doing Psychatog math and start doing basic arithmetic. Upheaval, Familiar, discard down to seven - draw to eight, land, Psychatog, leaving six cards - draw step makes seven. I realize there's no legal way for Gidi to have eight cards in his hand at this point in the game, and I call over a judge. Marc Hernandez - one time World Championship finalist himself, now a regular fixture in stripes at PTs - comes over and asks for our explanations of the situation, and Gidi concurs with my description of events and agrees that he has eight cards in hand. Gidi is understandably upset about the situation and asks for the head judge, and Colin and Marc confer on the situation and issue a penalty of a game loss.

I'm fairly certain Gidi wasn't intending to do anything dishonest in this situation, but the World Championships are no place for this level of mistake. As genuine as he seemed, I cannot be entirely certain that Gidi was not attempting to cheat, and the rules need to be strict in this sort of situation to ensure that those whose intentions are nefarious don't cheat honest players out of wins. Sometimes legitimate mistakes happen, as it seems in this case, but Pro Tour players should be careful enough to avoid such things, or be willing to accept the penalties.

So I end the day with an uninspiring record of 3-3. After our impressive 12-3 start, the RedZone2k2 contingent had a terrible slide, ending at 15-15 overall. The two rounds after I did my interview with Josh Bennett, not a single one of us won a match - we were 0-10. Discouraged that I'd done so poorly in the format for which I felt the most prepared, I returned to the hotel to try to get some sleep before the draft day.
