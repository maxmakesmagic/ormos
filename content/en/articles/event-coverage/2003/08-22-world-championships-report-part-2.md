If getting to sleep at 4 AM before Day 1 was harsh, waking up at 3:30 before Day 2 was brutal. With no visible clock in the room, I assumed it had to be closer to a reasonable time to be waking up, so I got out of bed and took a shower before Dave kindly informed me that it was still the middle of the night. Unable to get back to sleep, I made my way to the hotel fitness center to at least make some productive use of my time. After a bit of biking and some brief weight lifting, I head down to the tournament area to see if anyone is afflicted with similar jet lag to my own. I find, to my amusement, Gary Wise and U.S. Champ Josh Wagener involved in a side draft. After Josh loses in the first round and Gary makes some less-than-stellar plays to nearly lose his own match – no, Gary, Pemmin's Aura doesn't automatically give the creature flying – I head up to change and once again enjoy the hotel's free breakfast buffet.

My first draft table is hardly what you want to see waiting for you when you need a good finish to stay in contention. Dirk Baberowski, William Jensen, Gerard Fabiano, Steve Wolfman, and Wolfgang Eder were all names I recognized, and Dirk and Huey are both definitely among the best drafters and players in the game. This draft was covered on the *Sideboard*[here](/en/events/2003/worlds03/draft-1-table-5). I felt coming out of the draft that I had the best or second best deck at the table despite how thin the white had been at the table, and my first pick pretty lady certainly didn't hurt that feeling. Most of the decks at the table seemed downright bad, so I was confident that I could 3-0 or at least 2-1.

### Round 8 – Daniel Brasil

Daniel's deck during the draft looked to me like the typical g-r deck in this format – and by that I mean very, very bad. I made my feelings toward drafting green – and particularly g-r – quite clear in my Nationals report, and Daniel's deck is a textbook case of why. Quite simply, his deck didn't really do anything. And it did it slowly. Both games, Daniel uses Fierce Empath to fetch Needleshot Gourna, which he hides behind until he eventually dies. A thrilling battle, to be sure.

This is one of those matches that makes me happy I studied Buddhist philosophy, because if I weren't able to look upon this match with detachment, I think I'd just about explode in frustration. Yeah. That's what I thought, too.
