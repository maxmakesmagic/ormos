Some **Magic** players who enjoy multiplayer games put a lot of stock in "making friends." Helping out other players wins you allies, they say. You get more time to set up and spring your trap if you can fool people at the table into thinking you're there to help them.

For reasons I won't elaborate here, I don't completely agree with this philosophy. (Regular readers are already well-versed in my neuroses; I'll share with the rest of you some other week.) But when it comes to multiplayer games, I like to play with a certain cycle of creatures from *Mercadian Masques,* and when I was asked to pick a cycle to spotlight this week, my first choice was pretty easy.

### Eureka! It’s a "Show and Tell" Creature!

To understand why, you have to go back to a fairly obscure green *Legends* rare called Eureka, which allowed players to throw all the permanents they liked from their hands to the board. Despite its original wording ("both players"), Eureka strikes me as one of the first **Magic** cards that really captured the essence of the multiplayer game. The players aren't gathering around the table to compare "Sligh" curves and card advantage; they're gathering to get a game going and to set off as many fireworks as possible.

Show and Tell, a blue *Urza's Saga* rare, came a few years later and stole Eureka's mechanic (insert your own favorite blue vs. green rant here). It even came in at a lower cost, though it allowed each player to put into play only one artifact, creature, or enchantment.

In the very next expansion, the spirit of these two cards was shared across all five colors, as *Mercadian Masques* gave us Enslaved Horror in black, Indentured Djinn in blue, Hired Giant in red, Charmed Griffin in white, and most beloved, Hunted Wumpus in green. Wizards of the Coast R&D intended to balance the reduced mana cost of these solid creatures with the drawback of giving your opponent an advantage (recursion, card-drawing, putting permanents in play, and so on). Wumpus aside, the cycle didn't see much tournament play beyond a few block-specific decks, but many casual players latched onto them immediately.

![Show and Tell creatures](https://i.ormos.uk/55/d0/55d0448b82e29a64d8a6e675f4e4021239f30a9ae0a450cf16f1a27e5fcec004.jpg)

Who doesn't enjoy the taste of Hunted Wumpus in the midst of Pandemonium? Who wouldn't put the Indentured Djinn in the service of a Viseling? These two combinations alone continue to be tried worldwide--either that, or the same silly player is traveling from continent to continent under different aliases, emailing me ideas from three blocks ago.

What made decks like these even better for casual play was the possibility of gaining a benefit in team play--the cards say "each other player," not "target opponent." And having a teammate who can take advantage of your "Eureka" creature lessens the drawback considerably. Whenever a reader asks me how to build a team-style deck, I try to add one of these creatures whenever possible.

One of my favorite uses of a Eureka creature, though, is based on an old, inefficient combo deck that four out of five **Magic** players inevitably try. You remember the time you built that deck with Thran Forge to turn all your opponents' creatures into artifacts and enchantments so that you could blow them up with Disenchant? Well, the last few blocks have introduced some intriguing possibilities with such a deck. Charmed Griffin plays a leading role, teasing out artifacts and enchantments for you to destroy, all the while providing a 3/3 airborne body for beatdown. With the flashbacking Ray of Distortion, the reusable Aura Shards, the nifty effects of Orim's Thunder, and the two Mutations, I wouldn't even bother with boring old Disenchant anymore.

### "Each Player May" Be Part of a Larger Cycle

Of course, options for friendly fire isn't limited to those options. Language that says, in effect, "each player may do something cool" can be found in other cards. There's a well-known cycle of *Exodus* cards--the Oath cards--that give each player the chance to do something if he or she meets a certain condition. Other random examples from across the **Magic** ages include Mind Bomb, Truce, Liege of the Hollows, Emberwilde Djinn, Ley Line, Mana Cache, and New Frontiers (Ley Line especially . . . see the *Judgment* Phantoms).

That list, however, isn't exhaustive. Also in *Mercadian Masques* are the 'Mongers (Warmonger and Wishmonger, but not Spiritmonger of *Apocalypse* fame)--Hill Giants that also give each player the opportunity to affect the board.

![Mongers](https://i.ormos.uk/1b/80/1b80e4ac4eb01d0f99e54790b58f3354e70baefbe992c07d8c3810cf2c5eb2d3.jpg)

A cousin to the above deck might illustrate the potential of something as innocuous as Wishmonger. Breaking the circle of fun has never been, well, more fun. Here, you give your own artifact-styled beef the freedom to attack or block at will, while other players have no chance to stop your army. Coalition Honor Guard is terrific alongside Wishmonger (all opponents can target only your Guard with the 'Monger's ability, but you get to choose the color)
