This is the first installment in my series looking at the cards of *Judgment* for the purposes of Limited play.

### Introduction

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f1/f3/f1f33029253dbdbf7bbfc87add35050e1205c815f138629c51e9bb7fd7407c33.jpg)

Okay, after talking with my editors, the decision was made to change the format of the series this time around. Instead of the usual pick guides, with *Judgment* I'm going to try ranking all of the cards in a particular color and then presenting them to you in that order. This is far from a precise process, as I've started this series even before Prerelease day and am making a number of educated guesses, but in all, it should make for an interesting experiment and give you a better idea of where cards rank.

When reading the lists and compiling changes of your own, there are a few things to consider. First, the list assumes a generic decklist, with card values fluctuating with the contents of your draft. Second, I know there will be errors in here, and when I figure out what they are, I'll do a follow up piece letting you all know. The series is being released this early so you might get a better idea about where to start with your O-T-J drafting, so I hope you'll get at least that much from it and enjoy the read in the process.

### White

While white does have a few powerhouses in the form of Glory, Commander Eesha and the like, the thing that really strikes me is how consistently strong it is all the way down. Vigilant Sentry is a card with 1st pick caliber potential, and yet it appears half way down because white has so much strength going for it.

In the completed cycle, white will likely be best paired with either blue or black, blue because of the synergy between the two colors and their ability to control/manipulate, black because you'll be receiving strong white from *Odyssey* and *Judgment*, both of which will be passed left in most drafts, so *Torment's* black can provide strength from the opposing direction.

With its weakness in *Torment* and the strength of blue and green in *Odyssey*, I doubt that white will be the most sought-after color in the format, but it will definitely be popular. Don't force it too hard, but if you see it's still under drafted thanks to *Odyssey-Torment* residuals, don't be afraid to push it hard and hope for the best, especially in late May.

### The Cards

![](https://i.ormos.uk/79/25/79258423a63f9d34ecabd9026388154f1863c7c99b19176bed1e0d678ded6c1f.jpg)**Glory**  
 When I first saw the spoiler for *Judgment*, I read over this card three times and started giggling to myself. If there were a card that just allowed your creatures to be protected from every color in the mid to late game, it would be playable, but throw in the fact Glory provides you with a more than menacing 3/3 flyer and this is a powerhouse. Amazingly, the flyer is often the lesser of two evils for your opponent, and as a result, your opponent will at times be forced to try to keep it alive. Keep attacking with the Incarnation: either your creatures will stay alive, or your opponent will be dead.

**Commander Eesha**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There's a good reason I don't like 2/2 flyers for four: you can fill that same mana slot with this monster. Beloved Chaplain is an annoying little thug, bullying around all the 4/4s of the world until some sort of natural disaster takes it out of action, but imagine what it will do now that it flies and has a toughness of four. Hard to kill and evasive, Eesha is a powerhouse. If you're playing white, pick it. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/be/fd/befda8b795b828415bec64277f5ee69adf2e0885f9da39e41340971e79ae6217.jpg)**Phantom Flock**  
 As good as the phantom mechanic is, I'm not entirely convinced it's a strong quality for an evasion creature to have. Granted, the Flock is a 3/3 flyer that can't be dealt with by Fiery Temper, but at the same time, once it's blocked by anything other than Angelic Wall, the Flock becomes something of a non-threat; a chump blocking Aven Fisher leaves you needing something to stop Aven Windreader next turn. Don't get me wrong, this is definitely one of the better uncommons you're going to find, but understand that in some match ups, you'll really wish it was a regular 3/3. 

**Battlewise Aven**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 2/2 flyers for four mana never excite me, but this old bird shows feathers of a different color later in the game. Early on, you can trade the old codger for other 2/2s and the like or use it to go to the air for a few points while you control the ground, and then the moment your graveyard hits seven cards…well, lets just say you'll likely be in a winning position. How high you pick this Aven should rely completely on your ability to get cards into the graveyard. If you have a couple of Looters though, there's little I'd take over it.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7b/83/7b8388ae7191884cc1fcfdcdde8bed9e8606339d2027cbed796ff0ebb9dc1fa1.jpg)**Unquestioned Authority**  
 Wow do I like this card. Replacing itself when it comes into play by virtue of the card drawn, Unquestioned Authority makes your creature not only unblockable, but also immune to creature abilities. Yes, there is the possibility of card disadvantage inherent in any creature enchantment's need to resolve on the targeted creature, but that minor drawback is far outweighed by a number of benefits, one of which will often be 'you get to win the game'. 

**Spirit Cairn**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A good sideboard card if nothing else, the Cairn can offer you a lot more, providing an army of 1/1 flyers that your opponent must systematically deal with or suffer the consequences. Turn two Mongrel, turn three Cairn, turn four discard a couple, hit for four and bring a couple of 1/1 flyers into play doesn't sound too bad, right? How about activate Looter, discard Rootwalla, cast it, pay white, bring a 1/1 flyer into play. Starting to see the possibilities? The Cairn is a little quirky for most environments, but there are so many ways and reasons to discard in this format that it becomes a serious card. Be sure to treat it that way when you're making your draft selections.

**Valor**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Unlike its cousin Glory, Valor isn't actually all that great while in play. 2/2 first strikers for four mana don't hold much water in this format, so as often as not, you'll find yourself using Valor as an 'instant', discarding it after blockers to give first strike as a fast effect. A strong ability, universal first strike is pretty hard to stop, so I think you'll find yourself wanting to pick this pretty early. I'd have ranked it higher if not for all the damage prevention in the format nullifying first strike at times.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/aa/d7/aad7bc0c7fa9423b6694496958b702c1a7306172c54b89657d9a36414ba95076.jpg)**Guided Strike**  
 When I saw this trick to end all tricks was coming back, I got excited for the prospect. For two mana, Guided Strike can cycle assuming anyone has a creature in play, can provide a surprising offensive boost and most commonly, will shock your opponent as they realize their best creature has died and you've drawn a card in the process. The general rule in Limited states you want your deck to consist primarily of creatures and creature kill, but really, the Strike is creature kill with more versatility, so pick it high and pick it often. 

**Battle Screech**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Wow is there some power here. Back in *Invasion* block, for five mana you could make three Ordered Migration tokens with most decks and that proved to be really good, but now, for less mana and fewer requirements, the Screech goes one better. Yes, it only makes two tokens at first, but the beauty is that those two tokens represent two thirds of what is needed to pay the flashback that same turn, making for a potentially dominant card. Could give your opponent's fits combined with Soulcatcher's Aerie.

**Shieldmage Advocate**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If your opponent has a land in the graveyard, what are they supposed to do against this thing? The advocate provides a 1/3 body while carrying a threat of damage prevention that will more often than not result in your not needing to activate it. That means that opposing forces can't safely attack, burn spells can't be cast and, basically, you can't be killed until your opponent's graveyard is completely empty. That means no threshold for them. I may have these ranked too high as I haven't had a chance to see how much of a drawback the card return will be.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/17/36/17366cbc6663386629d9f5f24150bf8902fbd9c232399abd2e82642c86600d1d.jpg)**Pulsemage Advocate**  
 There's a lot of potential for mayhem here. The 1/3 body is passable as a defender in a blue-white deck, but really, that isn't what makes this thing special. What makes it special is discarding Volcanic Dragon or Ashen Firebeast and reanimating them while keeping your opponent from ever attaining threshold. Granted, three cards is a lot to give back, but the ability to animate at the speed of an instant is ridiculous, often killing an opposing attacker, so just use your best judgment when using this rare. 

**Phantom Nomad**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Nomad, meanwhile, suits the Phantom mechanic well. Providing a fast, easy to cast 2/2 on turn two, the Nomad will deal early damage, then be able to trade one counter for a 3/2 creature. In the least, this is a creature that can chump block twice, so overall you'll find yourself drafting it surprisingly high. 

**Chastise**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
Chastise or Second Thoughts? Both provide a nice benefit in addition to removing a troublesome attacker, Chastise costs one less, and while I prefer the card draw to the life gain, the latter can be better in some situations, so I'd probably take the Chastise if for no other reason because it fits into a lower casting cost slot. In Limited, you want to be thinking aggressively more than defensively, so lower-powered front end creatures may be preferable, but there should always be room in your deck for Chastise if you valued it highly enough to grab it early.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/78/0e/780e38ffc7d3a66fdcb929b67cdaeca75d9c41d3e78a13e723553069abcd576c.jpg)**Silver Seraph**  
 Obviously huge, the Seraph costs a whole lot of mana, but fortunately will win the game more often than not once it hits play. Especially good in a deck where it can be discarded for alternate use in the early game, I wouldn't take this over the set's better commons, but if your deck is bad, this'll definitely give you a way to win.

**Spurnmage Advocate**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 One mana? The Advocate provides the 1/1 body needed to deal some early damage with aggressive decks while at the same time practically ensuring victory in the damage race. You have to pick and choose your activations, as you'll eventually have to start returning the creatures you were originally destroying, but simply, this is a lot of power over creatures and opposing graveyard size for a one mana creature.

**Selfless Exorcist**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If nothing else, the Exorcist provides a large body for a color without a lot of them. That said though, he seems especially good against black players intent on trying to recycle their creatures with Morgue Theft, Gravedigger and the like. Additionally, the Exorcist provides you a way of either keeping your opponent below threshold or moderating the size of your own graveyard, which can be good with cards like Dirty Wererat that have advantages both before and after threshold. Not awesome, but definitely solid. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/56/29/5629e06b1384230d0a2a6ba53d9d57b9fd0748b11ba6bb5b3abf1c4532f186f7.jpg)**Vigilant Sentry**  
 2/2 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif)![White Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/55/78/55785924f8cda9ab2a85364f807ba172c7530e43d94b14aad222ec4bbf2589ca.gif) is definitely less than optimal, but it isn't the end of the world, and wow does this thing make up for it when threshold is achieved. The moment you have threshold, the Sentry is going to dominate the table like few cards can. How high you'll be picking it depends entirely on your deck's ability to gain threshold, but if that prospect looks promising, then you'll be picking this thing pretty high.

**Aven Warcraft**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Warcraft provides a nice little trick effect even before threshold, but the cost is definitely an issue. Yes, it can translate into card advantage in massive combat situations, but as often as not, you'll wish it was a lower costed trick like Pay No Heed. That said, this card had a much higher upside, so if you don't see the mana being a problem, you'll find it in your deck more often than the one mana instant.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/37/eb/37eb4bdc2f4ebf5a322bcb90be72a78ffe5107b0bce10e63c681ad22eab5a667.jpg)**Prismatic Strands**  
 Another interesting trick, the white player may find themselves with more good tricks than their deck has room for. Strands offers you the opportunity to really do over those players relying on one color, preventing Shower of Coals damage and making useless an ensuing red attack or preventing Morbid Hunger's effectiveness, but the really sweet deal here is the flashback cost, with the blocking creature you're saving providing all of the casting cost you need. Because of the number of great white tricks in *Judgment* I wouldn't pick this too high, but it'll win you a game once in a while.

**Ancestor's Chosen**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Okay, 4/4 first strikers are obviously pretty strong once they get into play, and the life gain is a nice little bonus, but seven mana really is a lot to pay for the combination of the two. Keep in mind that reanimation effects can help speed up the Chosen's ascension to 'in play' status, but there really isn't a lot in the format that helps you there. Playable if you need it, but I wouldn't force the issue.

**Border Patrol**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Unless you're playing a lot of creature enhancing enchantments, the fact this thing doesn't tap to attack won't matter too much because in a sea of 2/2s, it just won't attack anyways. That said however, Border Patrol's extraordinary toughness makes it a very solid way for the deck that seeks to control the ground to do so for a cost that, while high, is reasonable in its casting ease. By the time you get to *Judgment*, you'll know how your mana curve is shaping up, and there are better spells out there for this cost, but if you don't have them and you're open to the idea of adding some, this one will do.

**Nomad Mythmaker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Well, there's a lot of power here, but harnessing it could be an issue. This is the kind of card that would be great if it were being passed around in the first pack, because once you have it, you can conceivably build some very powerful interactions into your deck. Instead though, it's a rare in the third pack, and as a result you can't really do anything to really take advantage of the Mythmaker's power, because by the time you have it, it'll probably be too late. Especially good with Elephant Guide, if you get one or two, I'd recommend grabbing a hold of the good father.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/72/35/723587f87fc329723c3aace50e1c3badb52c84cceca8f7eb18dbf96c2d2e4862.jpg)**Benevolent Bodyguard**  
 On the surface, this may look like another one of white's meaningless 1/1 dorks for one, but if you delve a little deeper, I think you'll find the Bodyguard provides a lot more than that. Offering your mana curve a newer, faster starting point, this particular 1/1 will likely get a point or two in during the early game, and then offer your other, more important mid game creatures some measure of protection. Granted, cards like Psionic Gift and Cabal Torturer will nullify the Bodyguard's advantage (though you can still use it as a sorcery style effect before attacking) but your more aggressive white decks will need quick utility creatures, and that's what the Bodyguard is.

**Cagemail**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 I'm not too sure about this one. Seems like Cagemail can be used in all kinds of interesting ways, but the drawback is usually too much to be happy with. You play it on an annoying attacker, it becomes a defensive juggernaut you can't get through. You play it on your own guy that needs protection and it can't attack any more. Seems like the only use you'll be truly satisfied with is to put it on a small utility creature like a Cephalid Looter so your opponent won't be able to deal with it so easily. An interesting utility card in a heavily control-ish deck, but I won't be picking it too high.

**Funeral Pyre**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A low power card, Funeral Pyre still offers enough versatility to be mildly playable. Targeting an opponent's flashback card, the seventh card in their graveyard or a useless graveyard card of your own, the fact this is an instant means it can be used in combat tricks to alter the math your opponent accounted for. The capacity for surprise and evasion, no matter how low a degree of either the Pyre fulfils, is valuable, as is the card's versatility. Not a must have, but you could do worse for your 23rd card. 

**Soulcatcher's Aerie**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 If you really want this, you're going to get it, because there really shouldn't be enough birds going around for there to be two 'bird decks'. Again, it would be amazing if you could draft it in the first pack, but you can't so you'll be forced to regard it as a mediocre, quirky card that can kick posterior for you on the odd occasion. Especially good with Battle Screech.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ab/44/ab4453556fae8fe08db196dae14de4622c0b974a5e72b48414f81a08c011ddb6.jpg)**Suntail Hawk**  
Scryb Sprite was never a card worth getting excited about, and while the Hawk provides a very cheap evasive body, it may not do enough to be consistently playable. At the same time though, it trades with Dusk Imp for less mana at a lower pecking order slot, and that's pretty good. A great target for a fast creature enhancement. The cards you've already drafted will likely decide if you should play it, and therefore whether you should pick it.

**Ray of Revelation**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Another in a series of enchantment kill cards, the lack of versatility of this Ray is acceptable if only because there are so few artifacts that are worth getting rid of in this set. If you're green without white, keep this in mind as a potential sideboard card. If you have a lot of ways to discard it, one mana for targeted enchantment kill is pretty good. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/5c/51/5c51f94d0fcb62f1945497917139ec44ca09c7a8af46f781c19d96fa7e7b34d1.jpg)**Lead Astray**  
 With the environment not yet established, I can't be certain whether or not the super aggressive white decks of *Tempest* block will see a descendant flourish, but there are some pieces there and this is one of them. Lead Astray is not a powerful card, but if you're building a super fast deck designed to do precisely 20 points in the first seven turns, there may be a use, at least after sideboarding against decks that play one or two fat creatures in an attempt to lock down the ground. Don't pick them early, because you'll get Lead Astrays very late, but keep it in mind if you're playing tempo.

**Trained Pronghorn**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Pronghorn isn't an exciting card, with its body lacking mass and its special ability the power to really influence a game on their own. Granted, you make this a 4/4 and its ability becomes a huge problem, but there aren't a lot of cards in the format that can make it that much bigger permanently. If you have those cards, this might be a good target for them. Otherwise, leave it in your sideboard.

**Solitary Confinement**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 There are some very interesting combos for this card, but unlike Constructed, you can't pick and choose the cards that are opened in Limited, meaning this is likely to be relegated to permanent sideboard status. If you happen to get four or five copies of Genesis though, this will become an option.

**Golden Wish**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Sorry folks, but at five mana, this thing costs a lot more than you'll want it to, while any artifacts or enchantments of use will already be in your deck. Not too useful in the Limited arena.

**Test of Endurance**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 What do you have if your deck has Test of Endurance and two Ancestral Tributes? A big pile of awful. Don't play this card if you want to win, I don't care how many Life Bursts you have.
