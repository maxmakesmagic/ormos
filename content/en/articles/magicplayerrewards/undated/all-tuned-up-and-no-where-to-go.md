* * *

You test your deck every day at school, at work on your lunch break, or in your basement with your friends. Your skills have been honed to a fine point like the edge of a katana. You are ready for the challenge.

Players all around the world are finding a new way to play **Magic: The Gathering®**. The DCI has come up with a way for you to enjoy a duel in your local card store and win cool promo cards that you can’t get anywhere else. Best of all, it happens every Friday night. It’s called Friday Night Magic!

**What is FNM?**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/97/43/9743337e50be87b84ce6538b444522c6ad68f3440630771dc0018a6bd11e240e.jpg)

Friday Night Magic (FNM) is a **Magic** tournament program designed to stimulate casual play and help introduce players who have never played in a tournament to the tournament environment. While FNM does use the DCI floor rules that govern sanctioned **Magic** play at all levels, including the Pro Tour, the rules for these weekly events are much more lax than the ones used for professional-level **Magic** play. The focus of this event is to indoctrinate new players into the world of tournaments and teach them what to expect if they should ever try to move on to a higher level. 

One thing that you will need to play in an FNM tournament is a DCI membership number. If you have ever played in a sanctioned tournament like a prerelease or similar event, you probably already have one of these. If not, don’t worry. The organizer for the event will have DCI membership cards ready to get you signed up. DCI membership is free, and it allows you to keep track of how well you are doing by tracking your matches and rating online. For those of you who already have a DCI membership number and follow your ratings, FNM is a good way to gain some extra points. 

**Why is FNM right for me?**

To make it easier for newer players to play FNM, the only formats allowed are Standard, Booster Draft, and Sealed Deck. This means that you don’t have to have all the older card sets to play, so someone that is just starting to learn **Magic** can jump right into the fray. Standard Constructed format uses only the last two stand-alone expansions for **Magic** and the most current core set, as well as the smaller sets that fall in between. The sets legal for Standard right now are *Eighth Edition* (the current **Magic** core set), *Mirrodin*, *Darksteel*, *Fifth Dawn*, *Champions of Kamigawa*, and *Betrayers of Kamigawa*. 

This also makes your local FNM tournament a great place to get an idea of what the local metagame looks like when preparing for big events like your state championship or regionals. FNM provides the perfect proving ground for events like these for more experienced players, as it is a chance to test your latest tech in a real tournament environment.

Stores all around the world have been running these events for years. Every month, the DCI provides participating stores with a new, exclusive, DCI promo card stamped with the FNM seal. The only way a player can get these promos from the DCI is to play in one of these events. Best of all, you don’t even have to win a tournament to get one of these awesome cards. At each FNM event, four of these promos are given away. Two of them are given to the top finishers in the tournament, and the other two are given out randomly as a door prize to participants who did not win one already. All you have to do is show up to have a chance.

The promo cards are foil and have the DCI logo as the expansion symbol (you'll have to win one to see what it actually looks like). The cards chosen for FNM promo cards are cards that are legal for Extended but not currently in the Standard format. If you play in an FNM tournament this April, you will have a chance to leave with one of these FNM Blastoderms!

That’s right, a 5/5 for only four mana! Don’t let the fading distract you—this guy is big, and as long as he is on the board he will have an impact. He is almost guaranteed to deal 10 damage unless your opponent is willing to give up some of his or her own creatures as chump blockers. To maximize the power of this beast, many players used to play it in conjunction with Fires of Yavimaya, giving it haste and sending it at their opponent for 15 points of damage in three turns.

  

**Why run FNM?**

FNM is a great way to get players into your store. A store interested in learning more about how to run FNM should contact Wizards of the Coast Merchant Services at 1-800-821-8028. You don’t have to have a DCI certified judge in order to run these tournaments. Anyone who is familiar with the floor rules for **Magic: The Gathering** can run this type of event. This means that if you have played in several sanctioned tournaments, you probably know all you need to know to be an FNM judge. Your judge could be an employee of the store or a player who is interested in volunteering to do the job or has an interest in becoming a certified judge.

Just for organizing and running one of these events, you will get the four FNM promo cards to give out to your players. These highly sought after cards are sure to keep players coming back for more.

However, more than just the allure of promo cards to distribute to you players, running FNM in your store really makes it the place to be on Friday night. I have worked with and played at a couple of local stores in West Virginia and Ohio that have started running FNM. The quality of the players at these stores really starts to improve once everyone in the area knows that they can show up at a certain time every week and have a chance to play against other local players in this serious-casual format. While running the occasional tournament might boost interest in **Magic** in your area, there is nothing more effective at getting people to come and play at your store than the knowledge that there will be a well-run event with decent prizes every week at a scheduled time. 

**Things to Remember!**

Now that you know a little bit about what Friday Night Magic is, it’s time to get out there and play. To find out where you can play check out the [website](https://web.archive.org/web/20051212112908/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=events/magic/fnm), or email DCI@Wizards.com (in Europe email DCIEurope@Hasbro.co.uk). If there isn’t a store around you that runs FNM, ask the store owner about it the next time you are in there. Maybe once the store sees there is enough interest in these events they will start running them.

As soon as you know where to play, you can pack up that Standard deck you’ve been testing with your friends and challenge the best players in your area. This is your chance to enter the world of competitive Magic and ensure that you will never be without an opponent on a Friday night again!
