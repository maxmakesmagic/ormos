Before I get started on our next deck, I have an announcement to make. When I go to U.S. Nationals this year, I plan on playing a deck from the Deck Clinic series. While I am not going to tell you which deck I am going to choose, or even if it has yet been published, I will play one that has been published by the time Nationals comes around. It may not be exact, card for card, as I continue to find improvements in many of the decks I play, but it will definitely be recognizable as one from the Deck Clinic series.

For this installment of Deck Clinic, I am going to turn my attention to a g-r-b Zombie Infestation deck. This deck has proven to be very refreshing to me. After the last two decks that I looked at, which took quite a bit of effort to get going, this one fell into place very easily. However, it is extremely tuned to beat creature decks, and has a lot of trouble with solid control decks, like Psychatog. Since the deck is designed to play in a casual environment, I consider this to be a plus, as fully tuned Psychatog decks usually don't show up in casual play. Here then, is the original submission:

**Playgroup:** Casual

### Comments:

The point of this deck is to use Wild Mongrel and Zombie Infestation to effectively discard madness and flashback cards with the benefit of producing a desired effect and making my spells cheaper to play, or play as instants in the case of Rootwalla and Arrogant Wurm. It does perform quite well..... I would just like to streamline it a little bit. Any suggestions are welcomed. Thank you!

### Why do I always start with the lands?

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/6f/d7/6fd7071710dce1c801f9658701fc2ba0f9a72c38a913713dc54a1cd728a49b5d.jpg"><br><i>Without a good mana ratio, you might as well scoop.</i>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Whenever I get a deck, the mana ratio is always the first think that I look at. Whether it is at home on my computer or at an event, looking at the mana in a deck that has just been handed to me is important. The reason for this is twofold. First off, the number of lands in a deck is a very easy place to go wrong, and sorting this out can produce large rewards with relatively little effort. Second, you need to know the mana ratio in the deck so you can tell if the curve of the deck is reasonable. For example, if you have very little mana, then I should not be seeing extremely expensive cards like Obliterate in your deck.

For this deck, however, a different reason immediately popped out. The deck is going to be extremely shaky due to the color requirements, and the lack of a solid color base. Currently, the mana base is 14 sources of green, 13 sources of red and 13 sources of black. While having equal, or almost equal chances of getting each color of mana is the best way to reduce not having a color, there is a better way to work your mana. What you really want to do is maximize your chances of having the color that you WANT. To do this, we must look at the deck. We count up the number of times each mana symbol appears. I count them mostly how I expect to cast them, but I keep in mind that I might need more of that color. From here, I can see how heavy a deck is on a particular color. We then add a bias to our mana colors to represent the bias in our requirements. This bias is not a direct ratio. On the low end, you never want less than 8 mana, even if you only have a single spell of that color. For main colors, you need an absolute minimum of 12 mana, and that is just if all the spells require only one mana of that color. It goes up the more spells you have with 2 or more of the same color in the casting cost. Also remember that these numbers are minimums. The more you can get of any color, the better your deck will run.

To solve this problem, I want to get as many multi-lands as I possibly can into the deck. While the deck is extremely aggressive, it wants to play against creature decks, and losing speed against creature decks will not hurt it as bad as not having a color. For this reason, I started with all 8 lands that come into play tapped and produce 2 of green, red or black. However, I didn't stop there. I also added a Shadowblood Ridge and two Mossfire Valleys. Once I had all these additional color sources, I felt that I could remove the 2 Chromatic Spheres, and just rely on my land to do the job. In the end, I wound up with 15 green sources, 18 red sources and 13 black sources. The reason for the red bias here is the additional modifications I will do to the deck.

### First Impressions

![](https://i.ormos.uk/dc/b4/dcb41a2de60b08bbca5b28d17b7bb034595c4ce2f07b224f3a8602fdb8fb9a75.jpg)

When I looked at the deck, I didn't really like the Firebolts or Urza's Rages. I wanted to abuse the Zombie Infestation as much as possible, and these cards didn't seem to belong. However, once I started playing, I realized my mistake on the Firebolts, they definitely belong. However, Urza's Rage immediately got the axe. Another card really started to bug me as I was playing, however: Fiery Temper just wasn't getting the job done. I really wanted it to kill a Bird or an Elf early, to slow them down. However, the earliest it can be cast is turn 3, as I can not get a way to discard it into play until turn 2. At that rate, it is too late for it to really have an important effect on the game. As such, these went onto the chopping block as well.

### What to Bring in?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/63/7c/637c398447f85d22a94efeffa3c0f2d738fdb5f71d2e2a9202cc23fbac442432.jpg)

Since I wasn't casting Fiery Temper until turn 3 anyways, what would be a good replacement? A quick look through the madness cards provided me with an easy answer: Violent Eruption. While this would not have been playable in the original deck, with all the additional sources of red mana, it shouldn't be too bad now. True enough, playtesting showed that the card did the job much more effectively than its predecessor, and that its 3 red mana in its casting cost was generally only problematic if I could not get a Wild Mongrel or a Zombie Infestation on the board. Nevertheless, this card alone was the reason that I need to get so many red mana sources into the deck.

### A Hard Choice

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1a/a0/1aa06258532d7f6f71a996189d6adac7143fefba8ede14d9122c0935fb3cb849.jpg)

I also had to choose which removal spell I liked better: Chainer's Edict or Terminate. Both of these are very reasonable spells. While the deck doesn't always want to play 7 land, once you have a Chainer's Edict in your graveyard, you can often see that it will be worth it. On the other hand, Terminate definitely wins the battle if your opponent has Spiritmonger and another small creature. I thought about changing the removal to Terminate for quite a while, but in the end, it was the Violent Eruption that made the choice for me. With the amount of creatures that the Eruption usually removed, the number of times my opponent actually had small creatures dwindled, and Chainer's Edict seemed like the much better choice.

### Summary

This deck plays very well for the environment it was designed for, and just loves playing against green decks. It destroys creatures with an almost reckless abandon, often gaining a free 2/2 zombie in the process. However, I cannot recommend trying this deck in an environment with lots of control decks, particularly Psychatog decks.
