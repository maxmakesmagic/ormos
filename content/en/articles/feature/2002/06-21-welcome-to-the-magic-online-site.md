### ***Magic** Online* launches with a fresh look.

* * *

<table><tbody><tr><td><p>Hello and welcome to the new <i><b>Magic: The Gathering®</b> Online</i> web site. With the launch of <i><b>Magic®</b> Online,</i> Wizards of the Coast decided the old site needed a little facelift. I’m here to tell you what you can expect to find here in the future.</p>&#10;<p>First off, allow me to introduce myself. My name’s Daniel Myers and I’ll be producing this web site. Unless you’ve read through the credits in the back of the <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> manuals, you’ve probably never heard of me before. I’m not a former Pro Tour player and, in fact, I haven’t played a sanctioned game in well over a year.</p>&#10;<p>So, what the heck am I doing producing this site?</p>&#10;<p>Well, <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> and I have a little history. I spent most of the past year editing the help files and other game text. And, as I mentioned above, I also co-authored the user manual. Since April 2001, I’ve pretty much eaten, slept, and breathed digital <b>Magic</b> cards. Like the rest of the development team, I have a passion for <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i>. (People think <b>Magic</b> players get a little overwrought about the game; they should’ve been at some of the team meetings.)</p>&#10;<p>Other than the online version, I began playing the <b>Magic</b> game itself around the time the <i>Revised Edition<sup>TM</sup></i> set was released. After a five-year hiatus from the game, I got back into it the weekend of the <i>Prophecy<sup>TM</sup></i> prerelease. Add in some time as a <b>Magic</b> editor, shake well, and voila! Here I am.</p>&#10;<p>That break in my playing career has a lot to do with my being here. While I’m not a deck building master nor do I have secret drafting kung fu, I do know what it’s like trying to get back into the game after some time. I’m hoping that those of you rejoining us won’t have to make some of the mistakes I did. For example, I lost a <i>Planeshift<sup>TM</sup></i> prerelease game because I didn’t realize my Shivan Wurm could trample over my opponent’s Vodalian Zombie. (If you have no idea what that means, keep reading. I promise you’re in the right place.)</p>&#10;<p>Sixth Edition Rules aside, I also like teaching other people how to play. For years now I’ve enjoyed that burning sensation in my wallet every time I pass a card shop and I just want to share the warmth. So, here I am to give new and returning players a leg-up on the game.</p>&#10;<p>No, I’m not going to ignore folks who’re already comfortable playing <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> and are looking to improve their skills. Especially all the testers who’re back to join us after spending so much time helping the Wizards and Lizards make this game everything it is. We couldn’t have done it without all you folks and you’ll never get anything but props from me.</p>&#10;<p>However, you’re not going to see much deck tech or card analysis on the site. I’m going to leave that stuff in the capable hands of the folks at <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20050409084428/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/welcome"><i>MagicTheGathering.com</i></a> and <i><a href="/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/sideboard-online">Sideboard<sup>TM</sup> Online</a>.</i> Meanwhile, I’ll be off in the <b>Casual Play</b> area playing a six-person free-for-all with a red Goblin deck.</p>&#10;<p>The goal of the <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> site is to give the community a central place for everything about the game. Testers will recognize some stuff here, while a lot of it is brand new.</p>&#10;<p>One of the more obvious changes is that there’ll be weekly updates. Anyone who’s watched this site for the past year knows how much it changed (about as often as a snail wins a marathon). From now on, each week there’ll be a new article about <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> and the community growing around it.</p>&#10;<p>That also means you’ll find critical information popping up here from time to time. If there’s a newsworthy game change or some rescheduled downtime you’ll find out about it here as fast as I can post it.</p>&#10;<p>There are some new areas on the site, too. First off is <b>The Academy</b>. Inside you’ll find lessons on everything players need to know to enjoy <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i>. I’m starting off with a lot of new-player content and, as time goes on, will continue adding material for all levels of skill.</p>&#10;<p><b>The Academy</b> is also where I’ll post information on sets available in <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i>. Along with an overview of the set, you’ll get a description of how to play online cards with new set mechanics on them.</p>&#10;<p>The <b>Tech Updates</b> section will include the most current list of scheduled downtime. You’ll also get the inside scoop on what the hardware and software folks have planned. If it has to do with development of the game or other tech issues, you’ll find it here.</p>&#10;<p>Patrons of <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20050409084428/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/welcome"><i>MagicTheGathering.com</i></a> will recognize the <b>Ask Wizards</b> section. If you have a burning question about <i><b>Magic</b> Online,</i> send it in. I’ll gather some up on a regular basis and post the answers I get from the experts at Wizards.</p>&#10;<p>And while the testers are no strangers to the message boards, the <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> Message Boards link goes to the new discussion area set up for the community. Whether you have a question to throw out or an opinion to express, this is the place to make your voice heard.</p>&#10;<p>Some old friends are going to stay. Foremost is the <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> Frequently Asked Questions list. If you have a problem you can’t get an answer to in the help files, check here. A brief perusal of the FAQ may save you an email to the support crew.</p>&#10;<p>I’m not going to fool myself by thinking most former testers don’t only come by to check the <b>Server Status</b> box. This little gadget is staying so you can check if the servers are up and how many folks are playing. While you’re passing through, why not stop and take a look at what else is going on?</p>&#10;<p>The last item I’ll mention is the <b>Downloads</b> page. If you have a friend who wants to play, or maybe just aren’t sure if you want to pick up a starter box, this page will have links to our download partner’s sites. You can get the client software there without leaving the comfort of your desk chair.</p>&#10;<p>To sum up, the new <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> site will be a dumping ground for information about the game. If it’s important to the community, I’ll make sure it appears here. I’ll also be relying on all of you to tell me if you think something important isn’t covered here.</p>&#10;<p>In closing, I just want to welcome all the Timmies, Johnnies, and Spikes out there to the <i><b>Magic</b> Online</i> web site. Keep checking back for new items and changes. In the meantime, I’ll see you online.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
