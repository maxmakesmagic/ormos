When evaluating most new cards, we compare them with older, similar, cards in order to approximate their power. Such an approximation for Hollow Specter might leave you thinking “this card is too good”. After all, at ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif)![Black Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/e1/6b/e16bcf75cffdd1079b6ba1f9f43c9e6e7253bb89521acdf6b3e93d3c2e63dbb5.gif) for a 2/2 flier with a comparable ability, this new creature is very close to a card that was banned from a format that included Necropotence, Hymn to Tourach, Demonic Consultation, Force of Will, and Lightning Bolt - all cards that are certainly too powerful for Standard, let alone Block, formats. But looks can be a bit deceiving; let’s start with the difference between these disruptive fliers’ abilities.

Hypnotic Specter forced the opponent to discard a card at random, for free. Hollow Specter requires a payment of ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif). Upon damaging a player with this creature, he or she will show you X cards from hand and you may force her to discard one of your choice. So, the new Specter is a bit weaker - but not *much* weaker. The expensive, Coercion-like, method of discard allows the Specter to avoid madness and flashback cards. It can also take an important combo piece or board sweeper, like Wrath of God or Upheaval, from an opponent with multiple cards in hand. Crucially, like Shadowmage Infiltrator, Thieving Magpie and Hypnotic Specter, once you are getting damage through with it, given a tenable board position, this creature’s ability will, more often than not, decide the game in your favor. But this leaves a few questions outstanding: will he get through? And will forcing him through leave you enough resources to defend yourself from the increasingly aggressive creatures being played in recent decks? In other words, will the board position be tenable *after* you’ve used the Specter early in the game?

Consider another comparison, that between Ophidian and Shadowmage Infiltrator. The snake saw a considerable amount of play, but the Infiltrator is almost certainly superior - so why wasn’t he even good enough for a lot of Block Constructed decks that could cast him? Because opposing creatures have gotten better, *a lot* better, since the days of Ophidian. Decks have, in turn, gotten considerably more aggressive. This has meant that control decks are less viable and it is control decks that such card-advantageous creatures are best against. Additionally, with the increasing number of removal spells needed for these decks to survive against the surge of creature-based strategies, control decks are packing more answers to creatures like Shadowmage Infiltrator and Hollow Specter, who are in turn less likely to accomplish their missions than their older brothers were.

But back to my earlier question, how likely is the Specter to get through? The answer is fairly likely. Black has access to Duress, Mesmeric Fiend and Cabal Therapy to ensure the Specter’s survival. Additionally, some decks don’t even play removal spells, though at least one has Wonder with which to block our new friend. On the other hand, how worthwhile is casting the Specter? Against an aggressive deck, it probably won’t be good enough to spend your third turn playing a 2/2 with almost no defensive value. The fact that you can then use it on your fourth turn to spend mana and take a card from your opponent’s hand is of little consolation. It seems that you will have to wait until the opening rush has been quelled before casting this guy. By then, why not use a Nantuko Shade or Visara? I’m afraid that the Specter is only going to be worth playing in your deck if he is worth casting early in the game. In other words, look to employ him in the sideboard until the Standard and Extended formats slow down.

Of course, I wouldn’t be previewing this card if I thought it had no applications. Although it doesn’t seem like it will be making huge waves, I do see this card being quite useful in monoblack control, as a way to derail combo decks, turn by turn. With Duress to provide an opening, it can also do some serious damage to control decks. Perhaps as part of a hand destruction sideboard plan, it could be even more reliable and thorough when combined with Mesmeric Fiend and Cabal Therapy. With such a plan against most of the decks that previously made monoblack cringe, I can’t help but wonder what will keep this archetype from becoming first tier.

When I think about green-based decks dominating Standard, it seems that monoblack must have some merit. While the Specter doesn’t do much against the cycling decks, he certainly seems to have potential for high impact when playing against Wake and Psychatog decks. Besides decks that use Lightening Rift, this deck now seems well equipped against all of the popular decks in Standard.

So, here’s the new Hypnotic Specter. He’s good at what he does, but what he does isn’t in demand right now. Still, he has applications and will certainly be considered by many a deck builder. But until Constructed slows down, I can’t see him making a huge impact. As we’ve seen, even creatures that look unmistakably dominant, like Shadowmage Infiltrator, can be left out if they aren’t right for the format. Luckily, Wild Mongrel can’t block the Specter. But Firebolt can certainly stop the Specter and won’t do you much good against the decks, like green-blue and green-red, that make a living on emptying their hands in order to kill you before you can get into the game. And don’t think that, just because Hypnotic Specter was good enough to be banned, this guy will be similarly dominant. Dark Ritual, as a way to put this man in action early, is gone. As is Contagion, the old way to keep opposing creatures in check efficiently as the Specter did his thing. Still, in the right matchups, this new Specter will be powerful.
