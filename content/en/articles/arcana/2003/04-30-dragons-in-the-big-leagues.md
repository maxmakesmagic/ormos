![Covetous Dragon](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Covetous+Dragon)

Dragons aren’t just for the young tikes, no sir.

[Pro Tour – Venice](https://web.archive.org/web/20030430000000/http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=sideboard/events/ptven03) reminded us that sometimes the best strategies in **Magic** involve big creatures, and that event was not the first time that large red flyers dominated the skies on the professional level.

David Price immortalized himself in **Magic** lore with his win at Pro Tour LA 1998 with a mono-red deck featuring [Rathi Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20030430000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rathi+Dragon). The great Kai Budde's first Pro Tour win also came using Dragons—this time [Covetous Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20030430000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Covetous+Dragon) in a mono-red artifact-based deck. Pro Tour – Chicago 2000 showcased two Top 8 decks with Dragons: Zvi Mowshowitz's Fires deck employed [Two-Headed Dragon](https://web.archive.org/web/20030430000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Two-Headed+Dragon) and Brian Kibler's Red Zone made use of [Rith, the Awakener](https://web.archive.org/web/20030430000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rith%2C+the+Awakener). And who could forget Darwin Kastle's Dragon deck at Venice?

All five decklists are provided below.
