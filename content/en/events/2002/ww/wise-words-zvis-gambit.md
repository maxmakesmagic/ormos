![](http://www.wizards.com/protour/images/pt_lg.gif)It's almost time. Twice a season, the Pro Tour's finest gather to match wits in the Limited arena hoping to open bombs, cut off colors and just out and out-draft the opposition. The first of these occasions, held on American soil, comes near the beginning of a new draft environment, and using three packs of one set, a lot of new mechanics and a lot of guesswork, pro players attempt to decipher the format, trying to do so with a set of personalized rules by which their drafts should be defined. This year, that was San Diego.

The second of these events has established itself in Europe, and unlike its American cousin, sees the use of multiple sets, with two boosters of the standalone and one of that standalone's first expansion. The trick to this stop is figuring out how the rules one has established for one's self are altered with the introduction of the new set. Two weeks from now in Nice, France, we'll see how well the Pro Tour's finest have adapted to the addition of *Torment* to the *Odyssey* Limited environment.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/95/3a/953aaa26f6d1188de23ebcef4844b80fe703f98308387bc929f179d337890751.jpg)*Torment's* introduction has made a bigger splash then most expansions because of the imbalance of colors. With twice as many black cards as white or green, for the first time ever, drafters aren't competing on a level playing field. The dividends of drafting one color or another were simpler to decipher than in the past. With *Torment* black both containing more cards and a higher caliber of card than the other colors (it could be argued that three of the top five commons in the set are black), my sister probably knows that black is now the strongest color in the format, and she doesn't know what mana does.

It's that reality that made an email I received on April 10th so surprising. It was sent from Zvi Mowshowitz to the entire Godzilla mailing list, and while not all of it is printable here for team security reasons, the last line was really the only one that mattered:

> "I may just show up with a preannounced white gambit for all five pods and just see what happens. :)
> 
> Zvi"

Through the list, I immediately wrote back suggesting this might not be the best idea Zvi has come up with, but he felt that his path was clear, and while his original plan was to announce it himself, I thought it might make an interesting strategy article to discuss the merits of his plan.

Zvi's tried this once before at 1999 Pro Tour-Los Angeles, when the world, drafting Rebels, didn't seem impressed by Zvi's steadfast decision to stick to white, green or blue. More specifically, well-known green drafter Mike Turian didn't comply, sending two strong players spiralling downwards to a fourth round Feature Match where one was 1-2, the other 0-3. So why should things be any different this time?

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/PTOSA02/849.jpg)

Well, first of all, this time white is not the color of choice. While everyone wanted to draft Rebels in 1999, there aren't that many people who will hope to play plains this time around, specifically because *Torment's* white is anaemic while its *Odyssey* predecessor doesn't quite compare to its green counterpart. The logic goes that if few drafters want white, and those few that don't mind it know that the guy sitting next to them is forcing the issue, there should be little resistance. The problem with this mentality is that it assumes that word gets around. Unlike Rochester draft, where you can easily communicate your strategy to those around you, in Booster draft, you can only provide information in the form of the cards you pass. While this problem would be nullified if every player attending Nice knew about Zvi's plan, not all of them will. Not only are there players on the Pro Tour who don't have internet access, but there are also players who wouldn't be able to understand the English Zvi's (and now my) articles are composed in if they have access to them in the first place. And then there are the players who just won't care.

Zvi has other reasons for trying this strategy. Unsure about whether he'd be attending Nice until recently, Zvi feels that with the imbalanced colors, signalling your neighbours is frustratingly irrelevant as some players will start black, then draft black and then draft more black under the assumption it will pay dividends in *Torment*. I'll add that with this environment coming on the heels of *Invasion* block, three-color drafting and the decision to splash are more common now than they've ever been, all of which means that there will be more fighting over colors than any regimented drafter would be pleased to see. 

The most important reason aside from the above for Zvi's gambit is simple: this is the color Zvi prefers. In the limited number of drafts he's done in the format, Zvi has found that he enjoys playing the white deck, and with little time left, this gives him the opportunity to focus his playtesting on one draft style. Because red and green don't match up well with white, he's working with the understanding that he'll likely draft blue or black, hopes his neighbors will avoid those colors, and hopes that they'll respect his announcement enough to do themselves a favor and not get into a war over white scraps.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f2/97/f297e95678e39ca17445c494667d43c97ec137c462ab80c815ea384d340e78bf.jpg)

There are some merits to Zvi's plan. For those players who know what he intends to do, the prospect of sharing white is not a good one. If there's any color in this set that you don't want to share, white's it. With essentially two packs worth of cards available, you really want to make sure the paths to the best white cards are clear on both sides, as you really need to get the good stuff in *Odyssey* to make up for the time you'll waste picking *Torment* cards. This means that the white player should be cutting white off from the get-go, perhaps even taking inferior white cards over superior blue or black ones to ensure that no one behind you is in your color. Also important is the possibility that those people behind Zvi will know of his plan, meaning he may not need to cut so hard. If Zvi is sitting at a table full of IRC regulars, he won't have a lot of fighting on his hands.

There are, however a number of problems with Zvi's approach. First and foremost among these is communicating his plan to those players who don't stay as attuned to the goings on of the internet community as you and I. Unless Zvi is planning on walking around the morning of with a poster screaming ‘I'M THE WHITE MAGE' (or a t-shirt perhaps?), some players won't comply simply by virtue of their ignorance towards the gambit. Worse even, there will be other players who have found themselves preferring the white deck in their playtesting who won't give up the fight that easily, especially if they're sitting on his right (actually, where they sit won't matter all that much as either way they're getting one pack's worth of white cards. Unless they read this though, they'll likely be in the usually-correct mindset that the player on the right controls the draft), and last but not least, there will be those who open Wayward Angel or Kirtar's Wrath, and those who get passed third pick Mystic Zealots and Shelters. Simply, regardless of one's proximity to Zvi, if player X is searching diligently for a second color and gets passed Mystic Zealot and Morgue Theft as the best cards available, can they really be expected to go with the weaker card when the drafters in front of them are so obviously not white?

The answer is no. I've often preached that one of the key skills required to be a well-rounded drafter is the ability to adjust to every draft situation, and while sitting next to Zvi will require a like-minded adjustment, Zvi's hope is that his presence will trigger an off-white valve which just can't exist. If for example, his neighbour has decided going into the Pro Tour that this best bet is to start blue and look for white as a fallback plan (a realistic possibility), if the black doesn't come, that player will have to choose between drafting white or complying with Zvi's desires and abandoning everything they learned from their playtesting. Seems like a pretty easy choice to me.

For all that can go wrong though, there is a lot that can go right. If Zvi gets favorable seating arrangements next to players who hope to capitalize on the extremely likely possibility of Mowshowitz staying out of green and red, he should be able to proceed virtually scot-free. If I were sitting down for the Top 8 draft next to a guy who had sworn to draft white heavily with either blue or black as a compliment color, my thought process would have to account heavily for his presence when I was ready to make each pick. Problem is though, the Top 8 is single elimination and you need to approach that kind of draft with a 3-0 mentality. Is that the right way to go about a Pro Tour?

My educated guess is that the answer is no, if only because you need to have four solid drafts in order to reach the Top 8, and when you're as established a player as Zvi, that really is the only goal heading into a Pro Tour. Assuming 11-3 is the mark that one will need to get to the tournament's fifth draft, not only will one have to be able to take advantage of those drafts where everything goes right, but they almost must be able to adjust when things aren't going as well. If Zvi sits behind the player who opens Kirtar's Wrath in the first draft, he'll likely end up drafting a mediocre deck and go 2-2 if lucky, leaving him with a mountain to climb to make Day 2, let alone Day 3. Additionally, where most players would have the opportunity to switch colors if it felt like their color of choice was being drafted in front of them, Zvi's proclamation suggests this won't be an option for him, as those players behind him will be working on the assumption that he's in white. If he makes the color switch, those drafters around him will feel the sting of it, meaning that any similar announcements he makes in the future will not be taken seriously, and the man has too much reputation to throw away on one Pro Tour draft. This all means that if this captain's ship goes down, he won't have a life raft available. Now consider that nothing this substantial can go wrong in any of four drafts and you still have a very risky proposition.

Not that Zvi doesn't understand that. He's doing this for the sake of **Magic** theory as much as anything else. Zvi is proceeding as planned despite all the negatives because he's always found himself enjoying the game when assured of staying in archetypical form (see Turboland). Mostly though, I think he's just curious to see how it'll come out on the heels of the Shvartsman experiment from an observational point of view. In essence, Zvi is conducting an experiment in which he's cast himself in the role of scientist, financier and guinea pig. I for will be waiting for the experiment's findings with perked ears: we don't know if the little fella will get the piece of cheese or have its spine cracked by the trap. 

My prediction: Zvi's going to have three pretty good drafts and one catastrophic one. He'll lose one or two matches with one of his good-draft decks to mana problems and another one or two to poor deck match ups (I'd suggest blue-black if you want to beat blue-white specifically), leaving his final record somewhere around 9-5 (I would say 8-5-1, but I get the feeling Zvi would rather play his way into Day 2 than draw because of the aforementioned Top 8 mindset). In the greater scheme of things, 9-5 is never a bad record, but is it good enough for a Pro Tour champion?

It'll have to be.

Unless he proves me wrong.

Have a good week.
