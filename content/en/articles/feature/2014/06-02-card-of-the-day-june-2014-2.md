<table><tbody><tr><td colspan="2"><b>Card of the Day - Friday, June 13, 2014</b></td>&#10;		</tr><tr><td colspan="2">&#10;			<div>&#10;			<p></p>&#10;&#10;			<div>&#10;			<div><a><img alt="Pernicious Deed" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Pernicious%20Deed&amp;options="></a></div>&#10;			</div>&#10;			<br><a>Pernicious Deed</a> – <i>Conspiracy</i>. English picked up <i>pernicious</i> in the early fifteenth century from Middle French, which took it from Latin's <i>perniciosus</i> (&quot;destructive&quot;), which in turn developed from <i>pernicies</i> (&quot;ruin&quot;). Both parts of the word almost certainly came from<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language">Proto-Indo-European</a>. <i>Per-</i> just meant &quot;forward&quot; or &quot;through,&quot; but <i>necis</i> and its originator <i>nex</i> probably look familiar to <b>Magic</b> players, as they gave us <a>nausea</a> and<a>necro-</a>, <a>for example</a>.&#10;&#10;			<p></p>&#10;			</div>&#10;			</td>&#10;		</tr></tbody></table>

<table><tbody><tr><td colspan="2"><b>Card of the Day - Thursday, June 12, 2014</b></td>&#10;		</tr><tr><td colspan="2">&#10;			<div>&#10;			<p></p>&#10;&#10;			<div>&#10;			<div><a><img alt="Grenzo's Rebuttal" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Grenzo%27s%20Rebuttal&amp;options="></a></div>&#10;			</div>&#10;			<br><a>Grenzo's Rebuttal</a> – <i>Conspiracy</i>. We are perhaps more familiar with the legal and political form of <i>rebut</i>, meaning to contradict or refute, but that meaning traces back only to the early nineteenth century. Originally, rebut had a more violent meaning. From Old French through Middle English in the fourteenth century, it originally meant &quot;to drive or beat back,&quot; or &quot;to repulse.&quot;&#10;&#10;			<p></p>&#10;			</div>&#10;			</td>&#10;		</tr></tbody></table>

<table><tbody><tr><td colspan="2"><b>Card of the Day - Wednesday, June 11, 2014</b></td>&#10;		</tr><tr><td colspan="2">&#10;			<div>&#10;			<p></p>&#10;&#10;			<div>&#10;			<div><a><img alt="Bite of the Black Rose" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=Bite%20of%20the%20Black%20Rose&amp;options="></a></div>&#10;			</div>&#10;			<br><a>Bite of the Black Rose</a> – <i>Conspiracy</i>. Short words that describe concrete, observable phenomena (like <i>bite</i>, <i>black</i>, and <i>rose</i>) tend to be pretty old. Both <i>bite</i> and <i>black</i> trace back easily via Old English and <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language">Proto-Germanic</a> to <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language">Proto-Indo-European</a>. <i>Rose</i> traces back via Latin and Ancient Greek to Persian (which, in turn, might have taken it from<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language">PIE</a>).&#10;&#10;			<p></p>&#10;			</div>&#10;			</td>&#10;		</tr></tbody></table>

<table><tbody><tr><td colspan="2"><b>Card of the Day - Tuesday, June 10, 2014</b></td>&#10;		</tr><tr><td colspan="2">&#10;			<div>&#10;			<p></p>&#10;&#10;			<div>&#10;			<div><a><img alt="Æther Tradewinds" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&amp;type=card&amp;name=%C3%86ther%20Tradewinds&amp;options="></a></div>&#10;			</div>&#10;			<br><a>Æther Tradewinds</a> – <i>Conspiracy</i>. <a href="/en/articles/arcana/2014/03-21-in-ther-words">This Arcana</a> explains <i>Æther</i>. <i>Trade wind</i> originated in the mid-seventeenth century. As explained by the OED (<i>Oxford English Dictionary</i>), it is &quot;from the phrase <i>blow trade</i>,&quot; which means &quot;blow steadily in the same direction.&quot; The OED continues: &quot;Because of the importance of these winds to navigation, 18th-century etymologists were led erroneously to connect the word <i>trade</i> with 'commerce.'&quot;&#10;&#10;			<p></p>&#10;			</div>&#10;			</td>&#10;		</tr></tbody></table>
