304 competitors from 56 different countries descended upon San Francisco for the right to be called a world champion. Now there are 8 at the top of the standings. Camille Cornelson from the Netherlands has put in an impressive performance all week long. Also from the Netherlands, Julia Newton, 15 years old, a rookie making it to the top 8 from Malaysia, Terry San Juan Chen from Canada, the rookie Ato Paquette from France is on fire. Gabriel Nassif from Japan, Rio Ogura Murray, the Mueller Evans from Canada and Manuel Bhavan from French round out the top 8. Do you notice anything missing from there? There were no Americans for the first time in the history of the World Championships anywhere near the top 8. Not only are the Americans outside of the individual competition, but the USA is practically eliminated from making the final two of the team competition. Is the United States dominance over the game done for? Have the Europeans and other countries taken over? Magic Online seems to have equalized the scales, but now it may be more than equal and the United States has a lot to make up for. The French are leading the pack in the team race with 104 points, but Japan, the Netherlands and Belgium are close behind with 99 points and it goes all the way down to the 7th place. South Korea are only one match away tomorrow from being at the top of the standings. It's gonna be a close race to see who makes it to Sunday and plays out for the right to bring the championship home to their country. Also the player of the year race all but decided Gabriel Nassif making the top eight in the individual standings. Being a member of the French team at the top of the team standings need only make top 12 tomorrow to guarantee himself the player of the year title. Camille Cornelsen could get it all by winning the tournament and could have knocked Nasif out of the top 8 if he had played him and beat him. Nasif talked him into a draw and almost guaranteed himself that slot. Nikolai Herzog, the leader in the player of the year race coming into the World Championships, put in an abysmal performance, failing to make top 1/28. His cries of anguish could be heard across the hall when he found out that Nasief made it into the top 8, thereby locking him out of his chance at the title. Day three was mirrored in Block constructed and as expected, Affinity was the deck to beat. Eugene Harvey went six and O to win the day with his version of Affinity, although only making about 40% of the field. The rest of the field was there to try to take him down, and some people, like Manuel Befand, built decks to try to beat the decks that were trying to beat Affinity. It was considerably more diverse than expected. The Rookie of the Year title is also up for grabs. Alexandra Bassett has the lead but being in the top 8. Ayo Paquette and Julian Newton can make it if they win. The title of World Champion will also become Rookie of the Year. The team title, the Player of the Year title, the Rookie of the Year title and the all important World Champion title are yet to be decided. Stay tuned through Saturday for the team competition, all culminating in the finals on Sunday as we find out who the 2004 Magic the Gathering World Champion will be.
