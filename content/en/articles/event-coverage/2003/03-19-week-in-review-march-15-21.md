### Coming Up: Pro Tour-Venice

Several years after Pro Tour-Rome, the PT circuit returns to Italy – visiting its most picturesque city. 

Venice – or Venezia – is one of the most interesting places in the world to visit, renowned for its history, architecture and especially its canals. The Pro Tour itself will take place on the island of Lido – which, as I understand it, is sort of a suburb, one short boat ride away from Venice itself. 

Competitors will be called upon to construct decks using *Onslaught* and *Legions* cards only. The last two Block events were held in Japan, which decreased the attendance somewhat, as a number of U.S. and European pros chose not to travel so far for a Pro Tour. The new location should bolster attendance.

The best known – or simply the best? – deck in the format is Astral Slide. Very similar to its Standard version, this red-white deck abuses some of the most powerful cards in the set: Lightning Rift, Astral Slide and Exalted Angel. Although the deck dominated the field early in our testing, there are a number of other viable archetypes. You will excuse me if I do not disclose any details until next week. In fact, this may actually be the most interesting Block format in a few years.

Although a number of players have expressed concerns over traveling during what most almost certainly is going to be war time, it is highly unlikely that any of those qualified will actually choose not to attend because of it. 

### No More Masters

![](http://www.wizards.com/protour/images/pt_lg.gif)Starting next season, there will be no more Masters Series, as announced here on the Sideboard recently. Starting with the 2003 season, Wizards of the Coast will pay out $635,000 per year to the Top 50 players in the world, ranked by pro points. 

Most pro players were happy with the change. First of all, there are now less major tournaments to prepare for – relieving pressure for those pro players with full-time jobs and other time-consuming commitments. Second, the better players are likely to earn more money. Single elimination tournaments are governed by luck far more than Swiss events, especially a year’s worth of Swiss events. Thus, better players are likely to make more money at the end of the year. The change will also save Wizards of the Coast time and money. 

### Gama Trade Show

![](https://i.ormos.uk/28/40/2840ce1a14feb5b02851fd9892902fe87d76dcd0b8468759641bd8d8a3274d51.gif)While Pro players compete in Venice, many storeowners, tournament organizers and Wizards of the Coast staff will attend the GAMA Trade Show in Las Vegas. An event organized by Game Manufacturer’s Association, GAMA is perhaps the largest and most important convention for industry professionals. WOTC hosted its annual TO (Tournament Organizer) conference last weekend and will undoubtedly advertise its new products, including *Eighth Edition* and the upcoming **Simpsons** TCG. You can find out more about GAMA at [www.gama.org](https://web.archive.org/web/20030319000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.gama.org&origin=sideboard_wir_20030319).

### North East Challenge

Several years ago, Tom Shea, owner of TJ Collectibles in Milford, MA, had a vision. He wanted to create a tournament circuit that would link and cross-promote many of the smaller stores in the area that offered play space, but were perhaps too small to host a PTQ or JSS tournaments and so were a bit under the radar. The event was called New England Challenge and from the first year, it was a confirmed success. The event gained in popularity rapidly and was even sponsored by the DCI. Last year, the New England Challenge gave away several thousand dollars in prizes and the final tournament was covered (by yours truly) on the *Sideboard*. See 2002 NEC coverage [here](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/06-12-2002-new-england-challenge). 

This year, Tom’s event is taking another leap forward and becomes North East Challenge – inviting stores from New York, New Jersey and Connecticut to join New England stores and the total cash purse for the finals will exceed $3,000. Here’s how this event will work:

There are a total of thirteen participating stores this year (though there may be one or two last-minute additions). Each store will host five qualifier tournaments. The winners of these tournaments, as well as one additional player per store (with best overall record) will qualify for the Finals event, to be held at TJ’s on June 14th. The winner will earn $1250, with cash prizes paid out down to 40th place. 

For additional information, please visit [http://www.tjcollect.com/north\_east\_challenge\_2003.htm](https://web.archive.org/web/20030319000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.tjcollect.com/north_east_challenge_2003.htm&origin=sideboard_wir_20030319), or contact Tom at tjcollect@aol.com
