As we wrap up 2012 and start 2013, I'll begin my term as author of Daily Decks with a look back on some of the most successful decks of 2012. As my way of introducing myself, I think it's most fitting to lead off with a deck I helped design, and since this is the first day of a "year in review"–style retrospective, I think it's most fitting to start with the first Pro Tour of the year, [Pro Tour *Dark Ascension*](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628235750/http://magic.wizards.com/en/events/coverage/kibler-triumphs-epic-pro-tour), in February. 

![Lingering Souls](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Lingering+Souls)

[Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) and [Snapcaster Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Snapcaster+Mage) had been dominating Standard, and the big question was whether *Dark Ascension* could change that. [Lingering Souls](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lingering+Souls) was the hottest new card, and I was confident it would be a major player in and against [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets). 

In a time when the traditional way to go bigger than the opponent was to play a [Sword of War and Peace](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sword+of+War+and+Peace) and fight to stop your opponent's Sword, I decided to try to avoid the artifact battle by using [Drogskol Captain](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drogskol+Captain) and [Phantasmal Image](https://web.archive.org/web/20121231000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Phantasmal+Image) to create much larger fliers than my opponent. 

The resulting deck helped Jon Finkel make his way to the finals and Jelger Wiegersma earn a Top 8 finish.
