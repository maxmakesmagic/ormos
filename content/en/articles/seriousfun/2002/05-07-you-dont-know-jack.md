Sometimes countering a spell just isn’t enough.

You’ve had that feeling, haven’t you? A bomb gets announced, you feel lucky that you had the ![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) and the Counterspell handy -- but as you watch that Penumbra Wurm or Spinal Embrace hit the graveyard, you think to yourself…

*I could have done a better job than him with that spell.*

![Spelljack](https://i.ormos.uk/48/f1/48f1879c347c50d61f55e8f2ab6fd00b2a268a0ff51e5bc786107689643df046.jpg)

I mean, really, who is more qualified than *you* to cast the best spells in the game? You work hard. You play hard. Then you shower, and you smell nice. You’ve got that certain *nous ne sayons quoi* that makes people recklessly modify established foreign-language clichés. Isn’t it time you got your chance to run the world?

“…Pernicious Deed? Why, I’ll bet you don’t even know how dangerous that thing is. You could set it off for six and hurt my creatures, even though you wouldn’t mean to. I’ll tell you what. Why don’t *I* take that from you, and set it off at four, where it belongs. There. Much safer.”

“…Fact or Fiction? Why would we want to see *your* next five cards? Mine are much more important. Here, I’ll show you…”

“…Flametongue Kavu? I’m not sure you’d aim that thing right.”

“…Morphling? Why, that card must have cost you at *least* $12. I *never* get to play $12 cards. Let’s see what the world would be like if I could. Hey, wow. That *does* feel good. Just having it removed from the game, ready to play… that’s enough for me.”

In short, Spelljack is a trash-talker’s dream. A bit too expensive to see play in tournament constructed formats, the ’Jack (as Wizards now officially allows everyone to call it) has unlimited in-your-face potential just about anywhere else. More flexible than Desertion, more tenacious than Grinning Totem, the ’Jack steals all, threatens all. And so it has a bit of an ego to it. Never have six open mana talked so much…

“…I really, really hope you have something cool in your hand. Like a Sengir Vampire. No, wait, I don’t want one of those. Play Multani, Maro-Sorcerer instead. *That* would be cool.”

“…I will now take *Mark’s* Maro, and attack *Mark* with it. *Mark*, take 6 damage from *your* Maro. Ah, the air is thick with irony.”

Beyond its potential to help you be more obnoxious, though, Spelljack can do other things. First, some rules clarification.

### ’JACK BE NIMBLE

First thing to know: unless the ability specifically refers to events that happen before entering play (e.g., “this card cannot be countered”), no abilities on the targeted spell can stop Spelljack. So while Blurred Mongoose is safe from Spelljack, Nimble Mongoose is not. Scragnoth? Safe. Guma? Not.

Second thing to know: the spell you counter with Spelljack remains removed from the game until you’re ready to play it. This isn’t like madness, with a window of only 1.7 seconds after the tournament judge’s sister-in-law passes priority. Just take the card, put it in your pocket (or, more appropriately, in the owner’s removed-from-game zone), and pretend it’s in your hand for purposes of casting it.

### ’JACK BE QUICK

Of course, that means that you don’t *immediately* get a creature, sorcery, artifact, or enchantment. You’ll have to wait until your turn (your first main phase, to be precise) to add the insult to the injury. Instants, however, are good to go as soon as you are.

So the ’Jack works best in those groups where the players are seasoned enough to know how powerful instants are in multiplayer, and **Magic** in general. From Swords to Plowshares to Terminate to Mystic Snake, you want something that you can threaten with as quickly as possible.

Even a complex spell like Radiate can be bent to your will. You have two options here. First, you can Spelljack the whole thing, and save the Radiate for a spell that you’d rather see copied *ad nauseum.* Or, you can single out a copy generated by Radiate (or Mirari, or Fork), in which case the copy is lost forever and cannot be recast. (A "copy" cannot be stored in the removed-from-game zone.)

Given Spelljack’s insulting nature, continual threat will be the name of your game. And you’ll need to back up those threats when your opponents start to come after you. If you build a deck around Spelljack, you will need to be able to do things at instant speed, just about all the time. Like stealing -- Legacy's Allure, Dominate, Dominating Licid, and Overtaker can all do this at instant speed. Misdirection will deflect the inevitable direct damage and Terminates.

Tell you what. Since we have a bunch of cards already, maybe we can build an entire deck based on *other* people’s spells. Now, fair warning, this deck goes fairly heavy on the rares. The fact is, spells that steal other people’s cards are almost never common, or even uncommon. If you want to build a “theme” around Spelljack, and do it whole-heartedly, you’ll need something like I suggest below. For a lower-cost version, use bounce-oriented creatures like Wall of Tears and Man-o'-War, as well as simple countermagic, to hold the fort until the theft cards you can afford have time to arrive.

### ’JACK, JUMP OVER THE…URZA'S RAGE?

The question will come up, at some point -- for your opponents, if not you -- how does one stop Spelljack? Well, in most cases, you can’t; that’s why the spell costs ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) instead of, say, ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Blue Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/5d/b75de4f4cec208d24e23ce112a4bb89540d55aa95fe1ef377d1c7c5d2b6bdb50.gif) and gain five life. This is probably the best multiplayer spell in the set -- you want *answers*?!? Killjoy.

Well, I suppose there are solutions, or at least ways of minimizing Spelljack’s effectiveness. For the sake of those players who don’t like blue (I run a bit hot and cold myself), here is what I would recommend.

*Uncounterability:* If she can’t counter it, she can’t steal it. Urza's Rage is the obvious example, but there are plenty of less dramatic (and slightly cheaper) options, ranging from Kavu Chameleon to Overmaster to Gaea's Herald.

*Spells with flashback:* If you try to flash a spell back from its graveyard, and it is countered by Spelljack (and presumably cast later by the offending blue mage), it will *not* be removed from the game. It will go back to your graveyard, where you can try flashing it back again. This is because the Spelljack mage is casting it without flashback… as if it were from her hand, remember? This doesn’t stop Spelljack; but then again, Spelljack doesn’t really stop you in your tracks, either.

*X spells:* To a Fireball, Drain Life, or Hurricane, Spelljack is just an expensive Counterspell. While you may be disappointed that your finisher may not go off, believe me: the guy who had to blow three times as much mana as necessary to stop it is not pleased, either. You’ve also got a valuable piece of knowledge -- he’s out of Counterspells. Play accordingly.

*Spells that affect everyone heavily:* These look an awful lot like X spells to the guy with the ’Jack. Living Death is never going to look pretty, no matter who casts it or when. Ditto Jokulhaups, Armageddon, or other board-clearers. If such a card is the foundation of your deck, you can still expect to see it countered -- but it can never be used against you like a dragon legend can.

*Spells with kicker:* The option is there for the Spelljack mage to pay kicker when she plays your spell; but the chances are low that she can. A better deal for her than an X spell, but still somewhat disappointing.

*Small, efficient, unspectacular spells:* Following the same theory that a spelljacker wants the most for her money, she is never going to try that hard to stop a Soul Warden, or even a Gaea's Skyfolk. If she does, you have her in a corner and that’s a good sign. If she doesn’t, that Spelljack will go somewhere else -- which may not be fun, especially if your friends are uncooperatively playing with huge bombs, but at least you didn’t lose the card yourself.

*Incarnations:* I’ll talk about these more next week.

*Spelljack:* Probably the best solution. Honestly, what could be more fun than having a free Spelljack available whenever you wanted it? And talk about your trash talk…

“…That was a nice try at taking that Balance. But you know, I think that’s the kind of spell that deserves to happen right now. Why don’t I just hold on to *your* Spelljack, right here, and I’ll show you a spell that *really* deserves to get stolen…”
