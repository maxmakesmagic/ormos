One of many new mechanics introduced in *Odyssey* is the "punisher" mechanic given to red cards. Punisher offers an opponent the ability to counter your spell by using another resource. Blazing Salvo and Longhorn Firebeast are examples both of the punisher mechanic.

Various punisher cards have made their way into players' draft decks, but none were really powerful enough to make an impact on Constructed play. The trouble with these cards is that when your opponent is ahead, the punisher card does absolutely nothing. If you are staring down an army of your opponent's creatures and cast Blazing Salvo at one of them, he will gladly pay five life and consider you to have taken a mulligan by drawing the Salvo. It becomes far better when you are on the offensive, and five points of life are a lot more precious - but when you are already winning, almost any card can be good.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cb/2f/cb2f9262f1927a6e0bfa5f71beef5b15e94f887b7f03b7f7802565f02751cc1d.jpg)

Until now, Skullscorch has been the only punisher card to see some tournament play. *Judgment* offers what I consider to be the best punisher spell yet, and the one that will see play in *Odyssey* Block, Standard and Extended environments - Browbeat. Instead of giving your opponent an option to pay life and counter Browbeat, sending five points of flaming damage at your opponent's head is its main purpose. And while it is possible for an opponent to counter it via the punisher mechanic, even a player at a low life total will think long and hard before allowing you to draw three extra cards.

In order to take full advantage of the card, you must play it in a deck capable of dealing a lot of damage very quickly. Extended Sligh decks are a perfect example of this. Between a horde of very fast creatures, a load of burn, and Ball Lightnings, Sligh offers a perfect opportunity to take advantage of the latest weapon in red's arsenal. Actually, Browbeat is very similar to Ball Lightning - which alternates in dealing six damage, removing a nasty creature in combat when it is blocked, or gaining you six life if an opponent happens to have a Swords to Plowshares in hand. Ever since Bob Maher added Chimeric Idols to his version of Sligh at Grand Prix-Houston, that has been the three casting cost spell of choice to add to the Ball Lightning, superceding Seth Burn's original Viashino Sandstalker. I dare say that Browbeat is better than either, and present the following version for your perusal:

In Standard, there likely aren't enough quality red cards to put any kind of a Sligh deck together that would be competitive in tournament play at the moment - though I may certainly be proven wrong, as a few Burning Bridge variants succeeded at Regionals. Instead, one can combine red's direct damage with green's quality creatures to make a competitive deck. One way of going about it is to play larger monsters such as Skizzik or Beast Attack. Such a deck is a little slower and does not gain as much a benefit from playing with Browbeat over some of the other potential selections. With fewer early creatures available to it, such a deck also has a harder time handling control builds with Standstill or Goblin Trenches. The version I propose plays enough early damage sources to make Browbeat really count - whatever option your opponent would choose.

This is only a rough draft, and there are a lot of decisions to be made when building this deck. With so many cards available to choose from, do you replace something in the deck with Call of the Herd? Do you cut some creatures and find room for more direct damage to make Browbeat even more of a threat? Extensive playtesting and a search through the rest of the *Judgment* cards will help each player find the build they can be satisfied with.
