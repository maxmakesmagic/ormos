### 

**Sideboard:** Some people might see your success and, in a greater sense, the success of Latin America, as a fluke. Do you feel like you have to follow up strong to prove that isn't the case, and that the you are the region are for real?

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I don't think it's a fluke.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

**Romao:** I don't think it's a fluke, but we have to prove this by doing a great job in Houston, a great job in Chicago, and a great job in Copenhagen. We can do this. We have the people and the skills to do it. I don't think it's a fluke.

**Sideboard:** How about you personally? Do you feel any pressure that you have to show that you are the World Champion, and that you really are on that top level?

**Romao:** No. I think I had more pressure when I never won anything, because I had other things to worry about, like how I'm going to pay my travel expenses. Now, I'm the World Champion! I'll play, and if I win more tournaments, that's very good. But if I don't… well, I'll still try to win.

**Sideboard:** What does Latin American **Magic** need to do to reach the next level?

**Romao:** Now, **Magic** is like work. It involves teamwork. I think the most important thing about **Magic** is practice.

**Galimbertti:** I think it's important to have a lot of players at the Pro Tour. If there is a Pro Tour that only Romao and Ostrovich go to, we'll help them practice, but we won't try as hard because we're not going. So, the more people who are going to play, the better.

**Sideboard:** Do you think that there's anything you can do to help **Magic** grow in Latin America? A lot of players will be looking up to you now, so do you feel a responsibility to help them?

**Romao:** Yeah, I think my image can be used to show what **Magic** can mean to a person. I think that if I play in tournaments, other people will see me and say, "He's playing, we want to play too!" I'm not going to be the great salvation of **Magic** in Brazil and Latin America, but I can help a lot.

**Sideboard:** What is it like to be the World Champion?

![](http://wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds02/814.jpg)

**Romao:** Very good. It's amazing. Before Worlds, I came to Pro Tours and I was just another player. Now, I am in Feature Matches, and I think Feature Matches for the Latins is a great thing. I always dreamed of being in Feature Matches. People ask me to sign their cards now; it's great.

**Sideboard:** What do you think are the differences in attitude between Latin American players and the rest of the world, if there are any?

**Romao:** An attitude for playing, or in general?

**Sideboard:** Both. I'm also guessing that one would influence the other.

**Romao:** I don't know about other Latin countries, but in Brazil for example, when you play in a tournament and you call the judge and it causes a game loss, it's very ridiculous. You'd be called a cheater. A Brazilian would say "You have to win the game by playing!" I think it's good, but also the rules are the rules. In Brazil, people don't like to do this, but other people don't care. Also, now that (Diego) Ostrovich and me are going to play in several Pro Tours in a row, we're going to be around other Pros more and we can learn from each other. 

**Sideboard:** If that's the case, what do you think you could teach non-Latin players?

**Romao:** Well, we're in a position where we just have to learn. They're the old, and we're the young. We are learning, so I don't think we have anything to teach yet.

**Sideboard:** What's the future for Latin American **Magic**?

**Romao:** I don't know. Maybe the financial crisis will get better. Argentina and Brazil are having a lot of problems with that, and if it's fixed **Magic** will start to grow. 

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>When you have a financial crisis, the first thing you cut is entertainment.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

**Galimbertti:** It's very troublesome. When you have a financial crisis, the first thing you cut is entertainment. So, the parents aren't going to buy as many boosters for their kids, and the kids aren't going to play as much.

**Sideboard:** Do you find that frustrating in the sense that it's not fair because people from other countries don't have that disadvantage, so you have to work harder?

**Romao:** I don't want to say **Magic** will ever die off in Brazil or Latin America, because that's a strong word. But over the last year or two, it's gotten a lot weaker. The translation to Portuguese happened in '98 or '99, and then **Magic** kept growing. In the last two years it stopped growing, and actually fell. I think there's a small chance **Magic** will end in Brazil. Two years ago, you'd go to the **Magic** stores and they'd all be full. There used to be five or six stores in Sao Paolo, and now there's only one store left. 

**Sideboard:** Do you think it is good timing that you won the World Championships now while you're area is struggling, and maybe this will bring more attention to the situation?

**Romao:** I think if they promote Latin America as an area that can play **Magic** well, it will help a lot. 

**Sideboard:** What's in your future? More Top 8s? More Pro Tour victories?

**Romao:** I expect more Top 8s. I expect more Pro Tour victories. I expect to try to… not to make a living off the game, but I'd like to make it more than a hobby. I stopped university for a year to play **Magic**, and I expect to earn a lot of money. I think my future is good. It depends only on me. If I work hard with the other Latin Americans, I think we have a good future.

**Sideboard:** Do you think you can be on the level of players like Kai and Finkel? It's a tall order, but you are the World Champion.

**Romao:** I don't know. It's very difficult to get to their level.

**Galimbertti:** In Brazil, the talk was that Jabaiano - that's his nickname - is the best Brazilian player. Even though he never made a national team, the common talk was "Jabaiano is the best Brazilian player". This was before he won Worlds.

**Sideboard:** Now that you are the World Champion, do you feel the need to prove that you are on the level of a Kai or a Finkel, or is there no pressure like that?

**Romao:** No, I don't feel any pressure. I think if I play against a good player, the pressure is on him. I'm a new person on the Pro Tour, so I don't think there's any pressure to be on the same level as Kai or Finkel. I'll try to get there.

**Sideboard:** What advice would you give to any player who wants to be a World Champion some day?

**Romao:** I think the best advice is to work hard, and like the game. I like the game, and I think I can say I love the game. I've played the game for four years, and I never thought about quitting. 

**Sideboard:** Anything else you want to add?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/75/04/7504d7ec205070221b678cb7946c48a1f9be092944e0389c9bb127341c76479e.jpg)

**Romao:** My parents will read this, and I need to thank them because when I started to play in Pro Tours, they helped me a lot since I didn't have money to go. They never stopped believing in me. Before Worlds, I played in six Pro Tours and one Worlds, and the whole time they kept saying "You're going to win!" 

**Sideboard:** Now that you've won $35,000, I'm sure they're very happy!

**Romao:** Yeah, my father is very happy. He tends to show his emotions a lot while my mother is more reserved. 

**Galimbertti:** A lot of friends of ours came to the airport to receive him when he came back from Worlds, and when he came out of the door, his father was there and took the trophy and stood up and shouted, "My son is the World Champion!" 

**Sideboard:** Was that embarrassing?

**Romao:** Yeah, a little. He kept shouting, "My son is the World Champion! My son is the World Champion!" But, I can understand. He's very happy.
