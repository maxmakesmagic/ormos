Consumptive Goo, a flashy and potentially dominating utility creature, comfortably wins the poll with nearly a third of the votes. The other considerable forms of removal occupy the next three slots, with the vast majority preferring Lingering Death or Skirk Volcanist. Rush of Knowledge, despite requiring the addition of a third color, rounds out the noteworthy (more than 5% of the total) votes on sheer power alone. The professionals unanimously agree that the deck wants a removal effect, but a relatively ill-favored one floats to the top.

**Kai Budde:** The choice comes down to Torrent of Fire and Skirk Volcanist. Consumptive Goo and Lingering Death aren't really in the same league and Lingering Death is especially bad in an aggressive black-red deck as you want to attack each turn. The Goo looks good on paper but in reality is not very exciting, surely not even close to the other three cards. Torrent of Fire and Skirk Volcanist are both good cards, while I've never played with the goblin myself so far. Given the card pool, I'd go with Skirk Volcanist anyway as there aren't too many permanents available that can power up Torrent of Fire. A 'free' creature removal morph works also quite well in a fast deck as you hopefully can play him as one of your last spells and losing the lands doesn't hurt all that much if you get rid of some important blockers and attack for the win.

**Ed Fear:** I'm hoping for a fair amount of unanimity among the pros on this pick; the correct pick is absolutely Skirk Volcanist. Your deck is an offensive machine, removing opposing blockers and killing your opponent as quickly as possible; Skirk Volcanist simply fits this plan best. First, we can easily discount any defensive pick. Besides the quantity of excellent cards in our colors, the best non-red, non-black card is Rush of Knowledge, which could not matter less to our deck. Within our colors several cards do not merit consideration. Goblin War Strike, Unspeakable Symbol, Spark Spray, Death's-Head Buzzard, and Unburden are simply not options because of their relatively low power level. This leaves four cards: Skirk Volcanist, Lingering Death, Torrent of Fire, and Consumptive Goo. Torrent of Fire is likely the worst of these four options, not only is it quite expensive to cast, but it is far less useful than average in our deck due to our aggressive curve, much like our Erratic Explosion. Consumptive Goo is simply far too slow for the kind of deck we are trying to build. To generate the same kind of effect as a Volcanist, you would likely have to spend a dozen mana over several turns. The Goo simply can not generate the kind of tempo advantage the Volcanist can. The Lingering Death is definitely our second best option, it is cheap and efficient; it will almost always remove a blocker. Skirk Volcanist just does the job better, and leaves a three-power goblin to attack with. The instant speed of the Volcanist's ability also allows for significant usage once damage is on the stack. Turn 5, unmorph my Skirk Marauder, unmorph my Skirk Volcanist, cycle my Gempalm Incinerator, attack. Turn 5, Cruel Revival, unmorph my Skirk Volcanist, attack. Take the Volcanist; hope to get Spark Spray back after a lap.

**Ken Krouner:** Wizards likes to make bomb rares; they have their reasons. These are cards that you slam when you open them or sigh when they are not in your colors. I am a firm believer that Consumptive Goo is one of these cards. Skirk Volcanist is a powerful card in certain decks, however not in this one. You are light on red cards as is and you are going into the third pack which as we all know is extremely heavy black. On top of that, you have a Forgotten Cave further reducing your mountain count. As such, I don't even think Volcanist is second; I would take Torrent of Lava for this deck, as you have morphs with high casting costs and red-black is always looking for a way to go to the dome. This red-black is already great at being aggressive, what it needs is a card that can dominate the late game in the unfortunate event it comes to that. Goo not only fills this slot, but it also acts as a lightning rod earlier in the game to keep focus off your more effective attackers.

**Antonino de Rosa:** Red-black is my favorite color combination. I think the only 3 pick-able cards are Lingering Death, Skirk Volcanist, and Consuptive Goo. In this color combination, cards that will allow you to win the early game are more important than cards that will allow you to win the long attrition game. Consumptive Goo falls into the latter category. Looking at the Goo, you might be very impressed but in reality, it is just above average and is clunky and slow. Lingering Death might be one of my favorite cards. For two mana, you take down the biggest or baddest creature your opponent has and it helps this color combination a whole lot because it gives it a way to deal with 4 toughness creatures (red-black's worst nightmare). But I think the pick is Skirk Volcanist. It gives you a turn 3 drop if needed and around turn 6 when you unmorph it, it has a huge effect that will often win you games. The Arc Lighting ability is quite nice. I have seen it take down 3 creatures who just survived combat, I have seen it take down 2 creatures, and I have seen it just be good by taking down one creature. All in all, Lingering Death and Skirk Volcanist are very close and since your ways to take down small critters are very limited, I would go with the Volcanist.

Skirk Volcanist dominated the professional vote, with seventy percent preferring removal in the form of an efficient and surprising morph-trigger. Lingering Death received about the same percentage of votes as in the public poll (20%). The public seems to have vastly overrated Consumptive Goo, which received only Ken Krouner's vote in the professional ballot. Despite none of the professionals choosing Torrent of Fire, it was mentioned throughout their explanations and merits some consideration. 

None of the other red-black cards are in the same league as these four, all of which are powerful enough to dissuade one from adding a color for Rush of Knowledge. Splashing for a card requires it to be significantly better than the other options, and Rush is actually weaker in this deck even without the mana issues it creates. A black-red deck does not seek to stabilize the board and break parity with a spell such as this. The deck would vastly prefer a removal effect, as its game plan is to keep attacking before the opponent gets started. Waiting for five mana plus an island would just give your opponent time you don't want them to have. Death's-Head Buzzard is color-intensive, and its effect is potentially symmetrical or even self-destructive. It's not that great even when it is good, so one can rule it out immediately. Unspeakable Symbol can actually be quite powerful, and surely deserved to place higher than Death's-Head Buzzard or Rush of Knowledge. In an aggressive deck, one can use the Symbol to trade life for both damage and superior board position. While the trade of three life for +1/+1 might not seem advantageous at first glance, you are pressing the temporal advantage that a deck like black-red needs to win. Perhaps more importantly, Unspeakable Symbol is a prime example of threat vs. execution. As long as your life total, board position, and game plan lead your opponent to believe that activating Unspeakable Symbol is beneficial for you, you can continue to make otherwise unthinkable attacks, without ever activating the Symbol at all. With all that said, quality removal effects can yield the same temporal advantage and also have the added benefit of being more consistent. They are useful when your plan for a given game must shift to a defensive posture, or when there is a creature that will win your opponent the game if not nullified. Spark Spray is a fine removal spell, but despite its cycling-driven consistency and low casting cost, it just lacks the power of the other removal.

Consumptive Goo, Lingering Death, Skirk Volcanist, and Torrent of Fire all offer unique forms of removal for a cost, and this is where the debate really lies. My experience indicates that the Goo is the weakest among them. It's not at all surprising that it won the popular vote, as nearly all who first look at the card fall victim to the allure of possibility. It can kill a one toughness creature relatively quickly, has a similar effect to Smokespew Invoker in the late game, and all the while grows bigger for your trouble. Additionally, second turn Consumptive Goo, third turn morph leads to a great threat that parallels the aforementioned Unspeakable Symbol theory. To borrow from Zvi Mowshowitz, this line of thinking is "romantic". Realistically, Consumptive Goo rarely turns out to be so effective. To use it as an adequate combat threat in the early game, you must invest two mana and then have four mana open, so you basically need to draw both it and two black sources by the second turn. If you are just using the four mana to pump up the Goo, you could do worse, but again this is a subpar use of resources. It's been referred to as the best Invoker, and this is actually quite accurate. It's in the same categorical power level, but has some additional traits going for it. Just like Smokespew Invoker, its best use is in a deck that seeks to stabilize the board and obtain a lot of mana, such as black-white clerics or perhaps certain black-green decks. Despite this black-red deck's heavy black commitment (about 60% thus far with *Scourge* coming) and penchant for removal, Consumptive Goo offers a trade of short-term tempo loss for long-term board control, and therefore does not find a home in this aggressive deck.

Perhaps the most interesting question this pack poses is Lingering Death vs. Torrent of Fire. While I imagine most players would have immediately chosen Lingering Death when *Scourge* first came out, most have realized since that Lingering Death was initially overrated and Torrent of Fire underrated. Upon first glance, Lingering Death's cost appears minimal by comparison, but the value of each can really depend on the deck archetype. Lingering Death has a great set of advantages, killing any creature your opponent can produce for only two mana. This seems to have everything going for it: versatility, power, and speed. Unfortunately, it's not all that simple. Your opponent keeps the creature for both your turn and his next turn, meaning that if it is keeping you from attacking, you lose your combat phase. Additionally, if you are on the defensive but have no answer to it, you will be forced to take another hit from it. A Nantuko Vigilante, Daru Sanctifier, or similar effect truly foils this removal spell as well. Lingering Death, while always a good addition to your deck as a removal spell that can always kill otherwise dominating utility creatures, is usually similar to Consumptive Goo in that its value goes up in black-white clerics or perhaps slow black-green. It lends itself well to these archetypes because waiting a turn for its effect is not nearly as relevant on average. Clerics in particular can usually handle a large man for another turn with ease, but might otherwise succumb to an unanswered Wellwisher, Sparksmith, or Timberwatch Elf. I've found that in aggressive black-red, a card that would be much weaker in certain decks such as Carrion Feeder is actually a better pick because it performs the correct function.

Torrent of Fire is similar to Lingering Death in that there is wild variance in its effectiveness, despite the fact that it is without question a solid card. Normally thought of as a red-green card (an archetype that will have plenty of high casting cost permanents), it costs a lot more, but the return is often there. It costs three more mana than Lingering Death, and requires ![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif). It can only deal damage equal to the highest casting cost of a permanent you control, often a real pain in the morph format. If you need to kill a Sparksmith, an Aven Redeemer, or a Grassland Crusader, you will be regretting not taking Lingering Death when you had a chance. While I imagine most would assume Lingering Death is a better pick for black-red as Torrent of Fire seems slower and more difficult to manipulate, I'm not so certain. While it may cost five mana, losing a combat phase in an aggressive deck seems far worse to me. Also, don't forget that Lingering Death is rarely going to be cast on turn two, particularly if you are the aggressor. Assuming that turns two through four are spent casting creatures and attacking, Torrent of Fire's weakness (three less mana on turn five, which would presumably be spent playing a morph) seems far less relevant. While this also takes a high-casting cost permanent for granted, Thrashing Mudspawn has a high casting cost compared to its morph cost, and Skirk Outrider or Infernal Caretaker could help a lot as well. When used as a creature removal spell, oftentimes two or three will be enough to do the job anyway. Speaking of its other application, its ability to do damage to your opponent is what catapults it beyond Lingering Death. Despite its cost of seven mana and its narrow purpose, Searing Flesh has been winning more than its share of games since *Onslaught* came out. Lessons learned from Searing Flesh and the ![X Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/b1/6bb1152b0a3305d0a7c2e2eb725b8ebd18620607efc62f6f38b0642b97c8501f.gif)-spells of old demonstrate how powerful Torrent of Fire's flexibility can be. I believe I would prefer this over Lingering Death with the expensive spells of *Scourge* yet to come, but this is really close.

Fortunately, in this situation one not need bother determining the relative power of Lingering Death and Torrent of Fire, because the correct pick is Skirk Volcanist. Ken Krouner points out some problems with adding it to this deck, namely the Forgotten Cave, the low red percentage, and the weakness of red in *Scourge*. This design was deliberate, as otherwise I felt Skirk Volcanist would be the correct pick by too far a margin. Ken may have underestimated the percentage of red in the deck, as it is 60% black 40% red going into this pick, with the Skirk Volcanist adding another red card. Though Gabe Walls makes excellent points, I believe that his point concerning Skirk Volcanist's tempo loss is countered by many of the supporters of the goblin. While sacrificing two mountains does set you back on resources substantially, the return for this investment is huge. The morph-trigger will either devastate your opponent by effectively removing 2, 3, or even 4 cards, or will be the last effect used to clear the way for an attack that allows for such gains in tempo and board position that your opponent is effectively dead and the loss of mountains is irrelevant. So just like the other removal effects, Skirk Volcanist offers its own tradeoff: long-term resources for short-term gain. This is precisely the effect for which this aggressive red-black deck has been searching, and as Ben Rubin says, the Goblin Turncoat and Gempalm Incinerator make the choice even easier. While a red-blue or red-green deck needs its resources longer and Skirk Volcanist would be weaker inherently, a red-black deck containing such mindless aggression as Wretched Anurid and Embalmed Brawler needs to end the game quickly. All of this analysis only looks at Volcanist's ability as well. It can be the often critical 3 mana 2/2 when necessary, providing consistency none of the other three debated cards have. When the time comes, be prepared to lose two mountains and place your bets on the Volcanist, it will rarely let you down.
