**Sideboard: How did you earn “The Great One” nickname?**

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>You can’t look at his eyes; he’s The Great One!</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Maher: Back in the day, when I was talking about how all the guys used to get together and we’d play random teams, there was one weekend when I had a very good record and my decks weren’t necessarily that great because I always hate drafted a lot. I was just winning and I was coming out of awful situations, time after time. Nate Clarke started joking around about, “don’t look in his eyes!” and things like that. Everyone used to joke around and look down at the table and wouldn’t look me in the eyes because Nate was saying that was the key. And then Feming said, “yeah, you can’t look at his eyes; he’s The Great One!” and it just kind of stuck with those guys and spread out from there.

**Sideboard: Are there any problems with having a nickname like that?**

Maher: I don’t really refer to myself like that. That would be ridiculous. Courtney was watching a movie once, and they referred to someone as “The Great One” and I spun around, because I just heard someone saying something like that and I think they were talking about Wayne Gretzky. It was just one of those things where that’s the only time it’s strange, because I do get referred to as that a lot among players. It is weird. It’s quite the nickname. It’s really neat to have and I’m glad people think of me that way.

**Sideboard: It must be an ego boost too.**

Maher: It is a bit, but I’ve always made sure to not really refer to myself that way. Even on ***Magic** Online*, the only reason my nickname is “TGO” is so that people know who I am. They don’t have to find my nickname and add me to their buddy list or things like that, they just know who I am. That’s just about the only time I’ve referred to myself as “The Great One”.

**Sideboard: What’s your side of the story as to why you were suspended?**

Maher: I moved to Madison for school and I didn’t know anyone there. There was one kid from my high school who I was friends with, who also went to school in Madison. Once college started, we went our separate ways. I played **Magic** very competitively and I played **Magic** at a store that also played **Magic** very competitively. I was there very often, probably four or five nights a week and I became very good friends with the owner and the manager who were Chad Butterfield and Jason Moungey. They were also very competitive people who weren’t having the same success I was. A couple of years into our friendship - I mean this was the core group of friends I had - I found out that Jason, while he was running the tournaments that Chad was organizing, was also organizing false tournaments. Not only was he boosting his own rating, but since we were very good friends, he also decided that he was going to boost my rating. 

**Sideboard: So you didn’t know about it?**

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I was very surprised.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Maher: No and when I did find out about it, I was very surprised. But, then it turned into something where I was looking to go into a new store as a business partner with Chad, and this was my group of friends in Madison. I didn’t really have much else. Even the guy I was living with was a very good friend of Chad’s. So, I really had no idea of what to do and I just kind of let it go. When I found out about it, another player in the store there had also found out about it, because his rating had gone up too. He was a regular customer at a store and Chad Butterfield made sure that even though they were using his DCI number, they wanted to make sure he didn’t lose rating points, because they liked him also. There were a few other people besides me that were gaining ratings points who were just regular customers, people who they liked. They were also vastly punishing some DCI numbers of people they didn’t like. It was just one of those things that kind of blew up. They decided that they weren’t going to do it anymore and I just left it at that. I didn’t know what to do because it was my core, close group of friends. The 1999 Pro Tour rolled around in December and I won, so as soon as I won, I made flight arrangements to go to the Grand Prix and stay two extra days.

**Sideboard: That was Grand Prix-Seattle right?**

Maher: Yeah, Grand Prix-Seattle. Since I was a Pro Tour winner, I felt a lot more of an obligation… I wasn’t necessarily turning them in, but I really felt that if Wizards of the Coast was going to give me $25,000… So I went out there for the Grand Prix and ended up winning and on that Monday I went out to Wizards of the Coast, sat down with the DCI and gave them whole story and everything that was going on, because I felt that they needed the information. It had already blown up in Madison and all the players in Madison knew what happened and what was going on and that it was Chad and Jason. So I wanted to give them the information, because if something was going to happen to me, I didn’t want it looming over my head. I was contemplating trying to play the game professionally after I won a Pro Tour and had $25,000 and I couldn’t make that life decision with this thing looming out there that could drastically affect me if it ever came out. So, I told them about it, they started an investigation and I was basically told not to worry about it too much until the investigation was done.

I decided to play professionally and ended up Player of the Year that year and everything and they brought in Chris Zantides who reopened the investigation. There was a lot more going on and it went back and forth for a long time and they gave me a six-month suspension because I knew about a ratings scandal and didn’t bring it forth when I found out about it. I knew about it for a matter of months before I said anything. Had I not won the Pro Tour, I probably never would have come forward. I lost a lot of friends in Madison when I did that. 

**Sideboard: The other two guys got five years, right? You got less because you were the “whistle blower” and brought the matter to the attention of the DCI?**

Maher: Yeah. 

**Sideboard: Do they still run a store?**

Maher: One of them is in Madison, the other one has moved on with his life. He doesn’t play **Magic** and he moved out of Madison. 

**Sideboard: So it doesn’t matter to him then.**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/Worlds2001/699.jpg)

Maher: Not too much. He had a high enough rating that he could randomly qualify for a Pro Tour here and there and if he wanted to go he could have and now he no longer has that option. But no, it didn’t really affect him much. But Chad still owns and operates the store in Madison. It’s just one of those things where with the six month penalty; I was upset that it came so long after they got the information. I just thought it was unfair because I had made some of my life decisions based on the fact that I was going to be playing **Magic** and become a full-time professional **Magic** player. And then a year after I had gone to the DCI and told them what happened, so that I could make that decision, I felt like that part of it was behind me. Then they suspended me, so I was upset about that. I thought it took far too long, and if they were going to do that it should have happened a long time ago. My life may have gone in a completely different direction. 

Also, I do agree that some sort of penalty was in line. I mean I did find out about a ratings scandal, I did nothing about it and it did help me. It’s debatable whether I qualified for anything off it or not. Quite technically, I qualified for Worlds in ’99 off it, but I played a funny deck in my Regionals that year and went 2-3. If I didn’t have extra rating points to play with, I just wouldn’t have played in Regionals and I still would have qualified anyway. So, it’s debatable because I got very few points from the tournament but it did boost me up. And, I only qualified for Worlds by one rating point. It’s one of those things where people can take whichever view of it they want. It’s all in the eye of the beholder on that one. Something certainly should have happened, because if I hadn’t won the Pro Tour, I wouldn’t have come forward and that would have been in violation of the DCI policy. But, I never did anything maliciously. I never went out of my way to do something. So, it was roughly accurate. It was very hard for me to accept at the beginning, but just looking back on it, they were as fair as they could be once they decided they were going to do something about it.

**Sideboard: Prior to that you had a solid reputation as an honest guy. Did you sense that people changed their attitudes towards you after the suspension?**

Maher: A lot of people have talked to me about the situation, and once they’ve heard me explain it or heard other people in Madison explain it - because it was a very well-known thing in Madison… Everyone in Madison assumed that Wizards knew what was going on, since it was an open topic of conversation. Some people on the internet were less than thrilled with me and some people could have cared less. But as far as people walking up to me at Pro Tours and people on the Pro Tour, everything has been the same since I left. I haven’t really felt any backlash from it.

**Sideboard: Do you have any thoughts on your friends Dave Williams and Ed Fear getting suspended around the same time?** 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/GPMOS01/944.jpg)

Maher: It was an unfortunate coincidence. The things Dave and I got suspended for were very different and both Dave and Ed have always said they were innocent. I don’t have an opinion on it. I know what happened to me and that’s all I can say.

**Sideboard: Are you worried that being friends with two guys who were suspended, along with your suspension makes you look bad?** 

Maher: People are allowed to have whatever opinion of me they want to have. I would hope that people would like me, I hope people would reserve an opinion until they’ve actually had some interaction with me. But, everyone is entitled to their own opinion and I’d never try to take that away from anyone. If someone solely believes in guilt by association, then that’s probably not someone I’d be interested in spending a lot of time with anyway. 

**Sideboard: Did the suspension motivate you in any way? When you came back, you started a pretty impressive run.**

Maher: It did to a point, but I had also made a decision that, when I came back, within a year, I would be on the Masters or I would look for a new career. I was going to give it one year and if I wasn’t a professional again… and really, I think you need to be on the Masters to be a professional **Magic** player, then that would be it. I would move onto something else in my life, but still play in whatever I was qualified for. I wouldn’t schedule my life around **Magic** events.

**Sideboard: Kai’s been the unstoppable force on the Pro Tour for the past two years and you’re not ahead of him anymore in the Player of the Year race, but if you do get that title for the second time at the end of the season, what would it mean to you?**

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/pthou02/595.jpg)

Maher: It means that there’s room for other people. He has been an unstoppable force, but he’s certainly not flawless. I’ve gone to a lot of tournaments with Kai and I’ve seen him make mistakes, just like anyone else. A lot of Kai’s dominance has come from the fact that less experienced players, when they play him, they realize it’s Kai Budde and they just outthink themselves. People do not play their top games against Kai most of the time because it’s Kai and they’re intimidated.

**Sideboard: What would it mean on a personal level to be Player of the Year again?**

Maher: It would mean that I’m still on top of my game, and that the six-month suspension didn’t take anything away from me. I practiced a lot during that suspension. I still drafted as often as I could.

**Sideboard: ***Magic** Online* too?**

Maher: ***Magic** Online* and in real life. The guys in Madison were great with me, because I never had to explain a thing to 99% of them. They knew the whole story and they were all very upset that I got suspended, because they all had personal interaction with Jason and Chad and the rest of the people in the store and the rest of the people whose ratings got affected. They had a very firm grasp on the situation and they were all very surprised that I got suspended. But, there wasn’t anything they could do anything about. They were always wonderful to me. No one ever said a negative word to me about it.

**Sideboard: Is that a part of Madison, that it’s very tight-kit and everyone is really helpful towards each other?**

Maher: I wouldn’t say tight-knit is necessarily the right word, because any time someone new decides to go to school in Madison or comes out to draft with us, it’s very easy to work into the flow. So, I would just say they’re a very understanding, open-minded group of people that will make their own decisions about people. They’re just wonderful to be around.

**Sideboard: A lot of people like to mention your private plane and automatically assume you have access to lots of money, but I remember in an interview a few years back you said that you have money problems like any other student. Has that changed at all?**

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>I have bills to pay and I have budget problems.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Maher: It hasn’t changed too much. My family doesn’t have a plane anymore. My dad retired and that was just something he used because he traveled a lot for work. I’ve certainly had a lot of privileges other people don’t have, but I myself am not wealthy. Even growing up, my parents didn’t have money until I was out of high school. I grew up, the car I drove was a 1982 Oldsmobile with 190,000 miles on it and I was lucky if I got to where I was going. My sisters growing up, the three of them were in high school at the same time and they had to share a car.

**Sideboard: They were older?**

Maher: They were about eight years older than me. I haven’t necessarily been skewed by the fact that my dad has been very successful in business in the last ten years or so, because I mostly grew up without the money and I don’t have it right now. My parents have it and they’re very nice to us and I’m sure they’d make sure I never ended up on the street or anything like that and they help me with school. But, I have bills to pay and I have budget problems. I live on a tight budget and the money I get on the Pro Tour is very important to me. If the money wasn’t on the Pro Tour, I wouldn’t be able to devote the time to it that I do. I mean I have mortgage payments, I have tuition to pay and I have books to buy. 

**Sideboard: So what’s your education and employment status at the moment?**

Maher: I’m finishing up my undergrad in Madison. My major is Communications. I took a semester off and that’s when I won Pro Tour-Chicago and then I took the following semester off and won Player of the Year, so I took some more time off. I’ve taken about two and a half years off intermittently. Now I have about a year left to finish my undergrad.

**Sideboard: Why go back?**

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i> I have certain goals for myself that I don’t think I can accomplish being a professional Magic player for the rest of my life.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Maher: I want to have my degree, because I don’t know what I’m doing with my life yet. Whatever I end up doing, I think it’s going to be easier to accomplish with a degree. I’m able to do school and **Magic** at the same time, both to a level that I’m satisfied with. So, I know I want to play **Magic** and as long as I can do school while I can do that, I figure why not?

**Sideboard: And what will happen when you graduate?**

Maher: Then I’ll reassess where I am with **Magic**. I’ll certainly start looking for some sort of job. I have to make a decision about whether to become a business professional and play **Magic**, or be a professional **Magic** player and also hold down another job.

**Sideboard: So you’re definitely going to get a job outside of Magic no matter what?**

Maher: Yes. I have a lot of goals in my life and unless we start to have large corporate sponsors in **Magic**… the money on the Tour is great while I’m a student, but I have certain goals for myself that I don’t think I can accomplish being a professional **Magic** player for the rest of my life.

**Sideboard: What are your goals for Magic for the present and future?**

Maher: I’d like to win another title. It’s just one of those things where it’s been a while.

**Sideboard: Grand Prix, Pro Tour?**

![](http://www.wizards.com/protour/images/pt_lg.gif)Maher: Pro Tour, Invitational, Worlds, something like that. The Grand Prix are great. I just want to get into the upper echelon. I don’t want to stop until I’m further up that money list. I’d like to break the Top 5 on the all-time money list. I’d like to be in the Top 5 of all-time Grand Prix Top 8s or Pro Tour Top 8s. I don’t necessarily have to be first, but I certainly want to be remembered as one of the better players.

**Sideboard: Do you want to be mentioned in the same light as a Kai or a Finkel?**

Maher: Ideally, that’s what I’d be looking for. I don’t know if I’ll ever attain that, but it’s what I’m shooting for.

**Sideboard: Do you believe that you’re on their level?**

Maher: There’s no one in the world that I play against and think I’m at a disadvantage. I think in general, yeah, I have the play skill of Kai or Dirk or Jon or anybody like that.

**Sideboard: What advice would you give to a player who is looking to improve his game?**

<table><tbody><tr><td>&#10;<h3><i>Most Magic players love to give their opinion.</i></h3>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Maher: I would recommend getting someone who they know is better than them to mentor them. It doesn’t have to be consistently or taking classes from them or anything, but just get them to watch a game or two, get them to find one flaw in your game and actually give a good shot at fixing it. If you ask someone to look at your draft deck and they say, “oh, your mana curve is a little off”. If you don’t know what a mana curve is, find out. If you want them to elaborate on it, ask them. Most **Magic** players love to give their opinion. Just give them a chance and you don’t have to take it as gospel, but just try to take it in with an open mind and see how many different people’s ideas you can work into your playing. Eventually, it’ll work itself out.

**Sideboard: What advice would you give to someone who just qualified for their first Pro Tour?**

Maher: For the first Pro Tour, I’d say set a goal, but certainly make sure it’s attainable. For my first Pro Tour, I wouldn’t go in with a goal higher than Top 64. You’re just starting to set yourself up for a letdown. Your first time on the Pro Tour is stressful. It’s not just **Magic**. There’s a lot to deal with - travel, the hotel, dealing with jetlag and time change and sitting down against someone like Kai or Jon for the first time. You may very well make a mistake and you can’t beat yourself up about it. Being successful on the Pro Tour, highly successful, is usually something that just comes with time. Most of the people who do really well in their first Pro Tour aren’t necessarily the people who stick around and are successful in the long term. 

**Sideboard: Anything else you want to add?**

Maher: I’d just like to thank everybody. Everyone has been wonderful since I came back from my suspension. I spent six months away, wondering what people were going to be like. When I came back, everyone was wonderful to be honest. I was really happy, and it felt like everyone took the time to find out exactly what happened, and really just felt like they had nothing to be upset with me about.
