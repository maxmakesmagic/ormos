Grand Prix-Boston was the setting for the final showdown of Neutral Ground and Your Move Games champions in the fifth installment of the New York/New England rivalry: The Grudge Match. Four times, YMG had sent up their best against Neutral Ground and four times they had come away defeated. With YMG’s participation in the series coming to an end, this would be their last chance to break Neutral Ground’s stranglehold on the event.

YMG’s Lucas Galvin and Neutral Ground’s David Chung had never been in the spotlight before and both were well-aware of the expectations placed on them. Lucas was the hometown favorite and was keenly aware of his pressure to win. Chung, on the other hand, did not want to go down as the player that allowed to the title to slip away. “I don’t want that hanging over my head when I go back to New York!”

The format for the showdown was Standard. Each player brought three decks built under Unified Format rules. That meant that there could be no more than four copies of any single card — other than basic lands — between all three of each player’s decks. The players would play three matches and the winner of the best two out of those three would be declared the winner — and win $1,000 in the process! 

Originally, the showdown was scheduled to take place on Saturday after Day 1, but Lucas was preparing himself for an impressive Top 16 finish and it was postponed until Sunday evening. Both players agreed before the event to not use any Legions cards, although they were welcome to do so. Neither player felt that they had enough time with the new set to prepare.

Each player submitted their decklists and the deck they played for each match would be determined randomly. For the first match the randomization process yielded…

…‘Tog on ‘Tog.
