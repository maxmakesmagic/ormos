After the San Diego Masters Series, I did not feel like playing Standard for a while. I needed the time to let the emotional scars heal. I lost in the Masters. In Round 1, which I've never done before. To a Battle of Wits deck. Who loses to Battle of Wits? Curse that Toby Wachter character.

So anyway, my Constructed play time was taken up with Extended, and then *Odyssey* Block. Like a villain in an awful horror flick, the urge to play Standard always comes back in the end. And so it did a few weeks ago, when I agreed to join my friend Jay in a trip to a local Friday Night **Magic** tournament. I now had two days to figure out exactly what I should play and how *Torment* affects the Standard metagame.

I don't want to claim to have done any serious testing - or know what the best decks in the format are - or even truly care. All I wanted at a time is a fun to play deck that had a chance to win the tournament. My initial impulse was to play Kibler's b-r deck posted on the *Sideboard* in his recent [article](/en/articles/event-coverage/2002/02-22-top-notch-tech-black-in-standard). It was endorsed by several of my friends, as well as by Kibler himself, who told me via IRC chat that he is very satisfied with the deck. So I loaded up **Magic** Online to play a dozen random games and familiarize myself with this deck.

The deck was indeed pretty good. Its greatest problem was that everyone knew about it, and were ready and willing to dedicate sideboard slots to it. Having won a number of games, I also managed to lose to such cards as Teferi's Moat, CoP: Black, and every red-green deck that came along. Granted, red-green is not the most common archetype these days - but it's out there, and I did not feel like losing to it. I began to ponder various changes that could improve things both against control and against beatdown decks. Would adding Urza's Rage help get around Circle of Protection and the Moat? And how can I solve the red-green problem?

![](https://i.ormos.uk/43/20/43209367b3938a9ed37ee06bd08de476ecc362746df21d65a434d4f57bc3106a.jpg)

Sam Ward, a friend from Australia who I met when he lived in New York and played at Neutral Ground for a while, offered an answer. "Replace red with white" he said, showing me a deck build similar to what I would end up with. Unlike the mighty Flametongue Kavi, Vindicate does not tend to serve for four. However, it can kill enchantments - which is pretty important - kill larger creatures, and help out Braids by taking out one of your opponent's lands on turn 3. Another excellent white card is Spectral Lynx. It helps fight off the rampaging Elephant hordes. But what finally sold me, was the far better sideboard options including the mighty Worship. Along with a bunch of regenerating creatures, it suddenly turned the red-green matchup upside down. 

I named the deck Noir after the artwork on many of the black cards in it - dark and bleak. After a little bit of testing, I arrived at the following deck list:

I took the deck to that tournament and went 6-1, winning a foil Mystic Enforcer. I played in two smaller tournaments since and have gone 6-0 for the total of 12-1 record. Several of my friends played the deck in tournaments around the world and the worst record reported so far was 3-1-1. Granted, it has not been tried in anything more competitive that a local store tournament - but that is where most of you, gentle readers, play anyway - so enjoy!

Here is a quick run down of the cards in this deck, what they do, and why they are there.

### The Not-So-Deep Analysis

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ba/71/ba71d140bd2ff1b8e63e2ce5c40f104688efae4179b778581514ea04548c9951.jpg)

**Nantuko Shade** - This is likely the best beatdown creature available in Standard. In a monoblack or nearly monoblack deck, Shade can grow to gargantuan proportions, forcing even Dragons to chump-block it. At two mana, it also sneaks into play before most countermagic can stop it. Numerous games were won with a turn 2 Shade that went all the way. Generally, you pump this guy with whatever mana you have available on your turn rather than play around a direct damage spell they can cast on their own turn. Against control decks however, it may often be wise to leave two lands untapped - so that you can recast the Shade if they bounce it after you are done pumping it up.

**Ravenous Rats** - In a close battle of 1/1s, Rats defeated Mesmeric Fiends to earn a place in this deck. The limited about of testing I've done shows that Rats are better. I could be wrong, so feel free to test either creature. There are several advantages for playing this creature, which is not often seen in Standard. First of all, it takes out a card from your opponent's hand as it comes into play - contributing to the overall discard/disruption theme of the deck. If your opponent is forced to use a removal spell on the Rat, you have forced him into a 2-for-1 trade that is the trademark of this archetype. Second, the Rat and its Rager cousin protect Braids from an unfortunate incident involving your opponent's Chainer's Edict. Finally, the Rat will very often "take one for the team" when Braids is in play and demands a sacrifice. Since it already yanked a card out of your opponent's hand, you won't miss it that much.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f2/fe/f2feaac81270a5122424eba69de5d2a61336dc30077c5a81b0682a80c0180194.jpg)

**Spectral Lynx** - While the 2-for-1 creatures are there to wreck the control decks, Spectral Lynx helps defeat the aggressive decks. Its protection from green ability will come in very handy when fighting off anything from Basking Rootwalla to a Wurm token, and their regeneration ability is key to defeating aggressive decks in combination with Worship. When facing a control deck, Lynx is no dead draw - a 2/1 for one will always feel at home in an aggressive deck, even when it's special abilities are not needed for the matchup.

**Phyrexian Rager** - Like the Rat, it provides 2-for-1 card economy, and often attacks more than the Shade. I mean, who will want to Aether Burst or Repulse this guy? Rager helps smooth out the mana draw, and it is 2/2 so it can attack for a respectable amount of damage unlike those wimpy Ravenous Rats.

**Braids, Cabal Minion** - This is the card this deck is all about. The plan is simple - get Braids into play. By the time you did, hopefully you have already disrupted your opponent with Duress, Verdict, Vindicate or Rats. With less cards available to them to work with, they will suffer from sacrificing permanents to Braids worse than you will. Control decks are especially hard-hit in this scenario since they've got no or few non-land permanents. Since Braids is Legendary, you can cast one, let your opponent lose a permanent, sacrifice it and then cast another. The trick to playing this deck well is to know when you should keep Braids around and when let it go. This is achieved by practicing. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3e/82/3e82b8e8ecdac950bed12e90749bfc8817ab37a895af8300d2b48fb1d2f5d1a3.jpg)

**Mortivore** - An excellent finisher in a mirror match and in creature matchups, Mortivore can get pretty large even if your opponent does not play creatures. In that scenario Mortivore is playable but suboptimal - I will talk more about that when discussing the sideboard options. 

**Chainer's Edict** - The only creature removal spell in the deck besides Vindicate, it is especially good against control decks with a low count of creatures. Psychatog and Mystic Enforcer can both be quite troublesome, and Edict helps you out a lot in those matchups. Against creature-heavy decks, you will want to use your Edicts early, since killing that Birds of Paradise on turn 2 is far more effective than killing it on turn 10. Even in matchups where your opponent has many creatures, Edict is fine as it will eventually take out two of them. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/3a/12/3a12c80ee6f3d6a8124dbef0789a1e918377f5dc221c39224bdddc479ab8ec2a.jpg)

**Duress** - One of the best black cards of all time, Duress is a perfect way to disrupt your opponent's strategy, especially if you cast it early in the game. With card-replenishing spells like Fact or Fiction in the format, Duress will rarely fail and, in fact, will work overtime to take out your opponent's most powerful cards.

**Gerrard's Verdict** - Effective against both control decks where any card advantage engine counts, and creature decks that will be looking to play out their hand quickly, Verdict is as close as anything in Standard comes to a Hymn to Tourach currently. Its ability to net you a few life points can also be relevant on occasion. I have won a few games by targeting myself in order to discard two lands and gain six life points.

**Vindicate** - An all-purpose removal spell, it is pretty apparent why a card that can kill any permanent for just three mana makes the cut.

**Tainted Field and Caves of Koilos** - These allow for a very stable mana base, providing white mana and not detracting from the power of Nantuko Shade. 

### Matchups and Sideboarding

![](https://i.ormos.uk/fc/d3/fcd303e216c832a96e5657bf356203e657af86193465daa8f87aac71517049b4.jpg)

**Psychatog** - Chainer's Edict is very annoying for this archetype, yet it remains one of the best decks in Standard. This matchup is generally favorable and there are not a lot of cards you want to board out. Chainer's Edict, although not as useful early in the game, needs to stay in ensuring that they are unlikely to win without casting Upheaval. Take out 1 Edict, 2 Verdicts and 2 Mortivore to add 3 Phyrexian Arena and 2 Persecute. 

**U-w Millstone Control** - Likewise, this matchup is definitely favorable. U-w player will likely rely on enchantments to win it for them - but Aura Blasts and Vindicates take care of that. -4 Spectral Lynx, -2 Mortivore, +3 Phyrexian Arena, +3 Aura Blast. Depending on the version you face, you might replace a pair of Verdicts with Persecutes.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f6/cc/f6cc10e9a82f8b2f3435983c817e9e13687963d9e931d19e333c41fc79903285.jpg)

**Monoblack** - There are many various forms of monoblack decks out there, some aggressive, similar to Kibler's deck - other pure control taking advantage of cards like Mutilate and Mind Sludge. You can shuffle the sideboard options a bit here, though the key is to bring in Persecutes. Persecute is a card this deck fears just as much as they do, so the game becomes a bit dumb as it can often come down to who gets up to four mana first. 

**Red-green** and U-g, Aggressive Matchups - The key here is to replace discard with Worship and more removal. A common strategy is to do this: -2 Verdict, -4 Braids, -1 Ravenous Rats, +3 Worship, +4 Slay
