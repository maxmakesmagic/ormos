Congratulations to Lee Shi Tian, winner of 2008 Grand Prix–Birmingham!

He and his Kithkin deck first defeated Matthias Künzler’s Faeries 2-1 in the quarterfinals, then dispatched Raphael Levy’s elemental deck in three games, and in the finals against Remi Fortier’s Faeries the combination of [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) and [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) swept to a clean 2-0 triumph!

As far as the metagame of this relatively young format is concerned, we did get some very clear answers from this weekend’s results. Faeries and Kithkin are the two most succesful decks, with both placing three players in the final eight, and will be the decks to gun for in the upcoming qualifier season. In addition there are two distinct rogue strategies, which both employ all five colors of **Magic**, but then take off into completely different directions. Manuel Bucher’s Quick ‘n Toast update is arguably the most control-oriented deck in the format, while Raphael Levy’s elemental concoction is definitely a blast to play and akin to some sort of creature-combo deck, like Goblins or Madness in years past.

## Blog: 9:50 a.m. - Deck Tech?

*by Tobias Henke*

When doing the metagame breakdown for day one, we stumbled upon one very interesting deck we couldn't really classify. If there was any doubt that this Block format is definitely on par with *Ravnica* in the mana-fixing department, take a look at Omar Rohner's list -- the Spanish player went 6-2 yesterday and only missed out on day two on tie breaks!



## Blog: 10:00 a.m. - Deck Tech: The Actually Only Eight Commandments

*by Tobias Henke*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/05/4f/054fa0fe1cb2a5811cf37c8f5483ef6cf68410193019d5cfd78dde8ac34b8bdb.jpg)*Manuel Bucher*Both Guillaume Wafo-Tapa and Manuel Bucher have been regular guests in the Feature Match area throughout this weekend so far (and are 3-1 in those matches). Currently the French Wafo-Tapa is 8-1, while Manuel Bucher is 7-1-1. We sat down with the young Swiss for an inside look on their concoction, a Block-restricted update on their impressive Standard deck, that had put a lot of people into day two there and only narrowly missed out on a top 8 berth.

"The most unusual card in the deck probably is the one [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil)", Bucher said. "It's in the place of a fourth [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout), because one Spout and one [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil) is always better than two [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout)s."

But that's only one minor detail that sets this list apart from similar decks in the field. "All the other Command decks are running [Fertile Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fertile+Ground) and [Garruk Wildspeaker](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Garruk+Wildspeaker), but the deck does have so many three-drops, that [Fertile Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fertile+Ground) often is just a waste of cardboard -- especially with [Pestermite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Pestermite), [Fulminator Mage](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fulminator+Mage), [Primal Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primal+Command), and [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) that tap, bounce, or even destroy the enchanted land."

When asked about Planeswalkers, the young Swiss stated, "Garruk isn't any good, really. Even in the mirror one just attacks with [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) or [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) and then it's gone. You can even get one of those into play at the end of their turn via [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin). One simply doesn't lose against Garruk..." Then he added, "But we do have [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) in the sideboard."

"We are now 4-1 in matches against Faeries", Bucher said happily. "Seeing as that is the most played deck, this should be pretty good. The match-up against White Weenie is positive as well, and the mirror match too. They have dead cards like Garruk and [Fertile Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fertile+Ground), while we got [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) and [Mind Spring](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mind+Spring). [Mind Spring](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mind+Spring) probably is the absolute best addition to the deck."

"A bad match-up, however, is the Elemental deck with [Reveillark](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reveillark)", added Bucher. "The Feature Match against Levy was testament to this weakness. If you kill the Lark, they return something like [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) and possibly Elemental Harbinger and just go get another one. It's really frustrating... and one of the reasons we have [Primal Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primal+Command) in the sideboard. We're not particularly happy with the card, but in this match-up we desperately need to get rid of their graveyard, before applying any sort of mass removal."

"Another problem is that this list was build for 60-minutes rounds", the Swiss mused. "With only 50 minutes, one really should add a copy of [Twilight Shepherd](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Twilight+Shepherd)."

## Feature Match: Round 11 - Air Force vs. Army

*by Tobias Henke*

It's the Scandinavian battle here between Danish Rasmus Sibast and Antti Malin of Finland. Also, it's the epic clash of Fae (Malin) against Kith (Sibast), a very frequent match-up today.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e8/82/e8820080c71eed660961704ffa349d809ee931cf1a4ccc2a42ea0b3b96bc6dcb.jpg)*Antti Malin analyzes the sitiuation.*Malin won the die roll and kept his opening seven, while Sibast took one... two... three mulligans! "I got 26 lands, but they refuse to appear", Sibast complained. "I think now I'm going to keep whatever I get."

He did and even had a passable draw with [Windbrisk Heights](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windbrisk+Heights) on turn one, followed by [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) on the next. The lord was countered by Malin's [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions), however, as was the next [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) -- curiously with the help of [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) once again.

Sibast soon was out of cards and his deck could only muster one lowly [Burrenton Forge-Tender](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Burrenton+Forge-Tender), as multiple [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger)s accumulated in his hand without the mana to cast them. ## Blog: 12:45 p.m. - Judge Stuff & Staff

*by Tobias Henke*

Traditionally Constructed GPs are less taxing on the judge staff than Limited events, because less cards are played and those are known better. Of course, that doesn't hold true as soon as we're reaching the deep-sea areas of the Comprehensive Rules, where not many players have gone before.

 

![Mirrorweave](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Mirrorweave)

That surely is the case with the rules surrounding layers and copy effects. Quick question: [Mirrorweave](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirrorweave) is played targeting [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher), while there is an activated [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) in play. What happens to the poor 'Vault?

Well, apparently it turns into a green 2/2 creature with all creature types. Why? Because first [Mirrorweave](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirrorweave)'s copy effect is applied and then all of the [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)'s own effects, like the type changing and power and toughness setting.

How about this one: What will happen to the other creatures, when an animated [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) itself is targeted with [Mirrorweave](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirrorweave)? Now, in that case, all of the other creatures stop being creatures entirely. Sure, they can be activated for one mana, but the copy effect as such does not only take into account the copied cards basic stats (the so-called "copiable values"), roughly speaking the card as it is printed without any other effects applying to it.

Of course, we got competent judges all over the place to figure out even the most tricky situations. Here they are, headlined by head judge Adam Cetnerowski. Say hello!

## Finals:

*by Tobias Henke*

So this is it: the final battle. Lee Shi Tian from Hongkong is living the dream. He went into this Top 8, probably being the least expected to end up in the finals, and now he’s here, one match away from his first title. Remi Fortier of France, sitting across from him, is the exact opposite. He already made a name for himself by winning Pro Tour–Valencia last year, and clearly is the favorite to win here.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/47/07/4707317fb063144982f5303a656e6b861885b7275831468f1458474e81e29185.jpg)*Lee Shi Tian, putting on the pressure.*Tian won the die roll and kept a hand with [Surge of Thoughtweft](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Surge+of+Thoughtweft), [Goldmeadow Harrier](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Harrier), [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn), and four [Plains](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plains) -- far from perfect, but potentially very powerful, if the more expensive spells like [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger) come along. On the other side of the table Fortier mulliganed to six.

Tian had [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) for his turn two, Fortier [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom). The Wizened Cernn came down and Fortier went to fourteen, then thirteen due to [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom). A second [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) was countered via [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite), but still Tian’s attack brought Fortier down to six. Tian also added [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) to the board.

When he attacked with Stalwart, Cenn, Knight, and Harrier on his next turn, Fortier could only block three of them and the [Surge of Thoughtweft](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Surge+of+Thoughtweft) in combination with the impending lifeloss from [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) ended that game.

**Lee Shi Tian 1 - 0 Remi Fortier**

Both players had again good draws for the second game, Tian with [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) into [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) into [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) -- Fortier with [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) into [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona).

![](https://i.ormos.uk/e7/0e/e70e969025ff525e104a048f56e7b2a6b7b67eaa124799e870894dcbd267d568.jpg)*Remi Fortier, the pressure bearing down on him.*The Stalwart and Knight charged into the red zone, but before declaring any blocks he would regret later, Fortier decided to first take a look at Tian’s hand via [Vendilion Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vendilion+Clique). And indeed, in reponse [Thistledown Liege](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thistledown+Liege) came down to further strengthen the white attackers. When the smoke cleared the Clique had traded with [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) and one [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) token chumped. Fortier went to nine.

Now things started getting interested in a match that had so far been rather lop-sided in favor of the white deck. [Incremental Blight](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Incremental+Blight) got rid of [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn), and shrinked the Liege and Knight.

But still, the Liege was in play and Tian threatened to get some good mileage out of it, when he cast [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession). Definitely trhe Liege had to die -- [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) took care of that. Tian’s next attack got Fortier down to six anyway and [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) was constantly nibbling at his life total. Especially with two of his [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique)s neatly tugged away in his sideboard, the black enchantment turned into a real threat for his owner.

Another attack (this time with three spirit tokens and a [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)) got him down to four, then three.While Fortier did indeed have more and better creatures by now, racing would certainly not win him this game, with Tian sitting safely at 27 life due to the many attacks he got in earlier with [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain). Fortier tried anyway.

He also had [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder) to maybe try and find an answer to his bittersweet blossom. But even shuffling didn’t turn up the much-needed champion or [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192203/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command). When on one life he drew his card, the crowd drew its breath... then errupted into applause, as Fortier reached out in defat for the final handshake.
