### How quickly can *you* get six 6/6 wurms?

In *Odyssey* block, the wurms have certainly stopped messing around.

![Crush of Wurms](https://i.ormos.uk/a8/5a/a85a593f558fee193f49a22b72ba115795645d8a73a5fe5a4244ab6a867260b6.jpg)

Remember back when wurms were vanilla flavors like Scaled Wurm and Craw Wurm, or intricate arithmatic jokes like Johtull Wurm and Warping Wurm? *Invasion* block brought us more serious entries like Penumbra Wurm and Shivan Wurm, and now the last three expansions have brought wurms fully into the mainstream. *Judgment*'s offering is perhaps the most frightening of all for opponents. While Roar of the Wurm is the most efficient wurm creature to date (when flashed back), and Arrogant Wurm the first wurm to enjoy instant speed, throwing out multiple huge wurms as if they were confetti has still never been possible.

Until now.

The **Magic** cards that can *possibly* give you 36 power and 36 toughness in a single piece of cardboard are very few and far between. Minion of the Wastes, the original Lhurgoyf and its *Odyssey* block kindred, Keldon Warlord, and Sutured Ghoul top the list -- and all of these do, in fact, require one or more other cards (the lifegain that puts you at 37 to survive the Minion's payment, the 36 instants you need in your graveyard to power your Cognivore, and so on).

But if you want that 36/36 to be a sure thing, and you want it in just one card, and you also figure you ought to be able to split all that power and toughness among six different creatures so that Terminate doesn't look so hot…well, there's a Crush of Wurms coming to a table near you.

With *Judgment* releasing officially about now, many people have had plenty of time to dismiss this card as an overpriced "kiddie card" unworthy of serious **Magic** players. Okay, mana efficiency, tempo, whatever, blah blah blah. *Do you want the damn wurms or not?*

Then let's get to it.

### THE WURMS' TURNS

Here's the goal: **cast *and* flashback the Crush of Wurms** quickly enough that everybody else around your kitchen table gapes at you. (If you're doing this during an actual family dinner, and no one else in your family plays **Magic**, you can take as many turns as you want to get this same reaction.) We'll start with the slightly accelerated model, then a moderately brisk model, then a blindingly fast model. But be warned: the faster you try to go, the less certain you can be that you'll get the consistency and speed you're looking for.

I measure the speed of these options by defining when the flashback ought to happen. If you can make the flashback happen quicker, of course you're probably making the original cast happen earlier, as well.

**By Turn 9 or 10:** The flashback cost of Crush of Wurms is ![9 Mana](http://www.wizards.com/images/Symbols/Symbol_9_mana.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif), or twelve mana. Heck, that's the same as Urza's Rage with kicker. People play Urza's Rage before their twelfth turn all the time. *They're* supposedly really smart.

You can be virtually guaranteed of a tenth-turn flashback by putting between eight and twelve simple mana accelerants in your deck. These would include: Birds of Paradise, Llanowar Elves, Moss Diamond, and Emerald Medallion.

An elf and a diamond early on would allow the first cast of the Crush to come on turn seven -- very respectable for three 6/6 creatures. Plopping down a medallion the turn afterward (when has a turn-eight medallion ever been so useful?) would give you the flashback on turn nine.

A slight acceleration like this gives your deck plenty of room for cards that will make for a rigorous, consistent deck that may actually protect the wurms. In fact, we could give ourselves enough time to cast an Anarchist so that we can put out an additional trio of wurms before flashing the card out of the game…

A deck I did here at MagicTheGathering.com for Burning Wish a [few weeks ago](/en/articles/seriousfun/2002/04-30-i-wish-i-wish-i-wish-i-had-perish) could certainly use the Crush of Wurms at a similar pace and fashion.

**By Turn 5 or 6:** The High Tide - Palinchron decks of a few years ago that generated infinite mana (for a Capsize or Stroke of Genius) always bothered me tremendously. Hey, buddy, you want to win? Win with some guts. Wurm guts, preferably.

A blue-green wurm deck has several advantages: first, you can use the Quiet Speculation and Roar of the Wurm trick that everyone's been talking about. That should give you an early wurm to catch the bird, so to speak.

Second, you will equip yourself with a bit of countermagic to prevent the most awful enemies of this deck -- Evacuation, Upheaval, Repercussion, and so on.

Third, you will have enough self-discard capability to take advantage of Wonder, which will give all of your wurms flying. Which makes perfect sense, I guess. (You could also use Brawn, of course; but flying wurms are inherently funnier than trampling wurms.)

So your ideal game looks like this: turn one Tropical Island; turn two Tropical Island and Wild Mongrel (and then discard Wonder whenever you feel frisky); turn three Island, Frantic Search or Quiet Speculation to land a Roar of the Wurm in your graveyard; turn four Island and send an emissary wurm to herald your next turn; turn five High Tide, High Tide again (if you don't want to wait a turn), Palinchron, return Palinchron, Palinchron, return Palinchron, etc. until you can cast Crush of Wurms from both hand and graveyard.

![High Tide + Palinchron = WURMS!](https://i.ormos.uk/57/a2/57a2bdb97a6cd5d3fe83ff013bcc5fac40bfad11c6a4a702e691b8c296ff8417.jpg)

That's fairly exact and requires the right cards to be fairly close to your graveyard -- but you have some leeway since we've pushed the Wonder and Roar of the Wurm tricks in there, too.

**By Turn 3:** You have at least two different ways to go, here. Both are fairly frightening, and require improbable draws and topdecks.

The first path would be a green-blue deck with the following base:

**FairlyAlarmedWurms.deq**  
 4 Crush of Wurms  
 4 Llanowar Elves  
 4 Fyndhorn Elves  
 4 Priest of Titania  
 4 Concordant Crossroads  
 4 Intruder Alarm

…and hey, add whatever else you think will help. Non-basic lands are not necessary; but would probably be of some use.

Here's what you're shooting for: on turn one, you drop a Forest and an elf. Turn two, play an Island and Intruder Alarm. Perhaps one of your opponents will see that disturbing enchantment and swing for, what, one with a goblin? Okay. As long as that goblin isn't a Mogg Fanatic, you're fine. You respond on turn three with the following:

Tap Forest for Concordant Crossroads, tap your elf for… another elf. (First elf untaps.) Tap elves for Priest of Titania (then untap them), tap elves for another Priest of Titania (then untap them), then play Crush of Wurms. Put each wurm's entry on the stack, and tap elves for mana in response to each. (Untap them each entry, as well.) You will have enough mana to flash back the Crush on the same turn. Now, don't forget -- they have haste! So swing away for 36. Add your elves to the assault, if you like, to kill off two different, completely healthy players if they don't have any blockers.

That uses up nine cards over three turns, which doesn't give you a lot of breathing room. Even a tiny bit of burn can ruin your day, and heaven help you if someone has a Counterspell. (You opponent may even Spelljack it, if he loves combos as much as you do and manages to get a Dream Halls out on his second turn.)

The second path is a potentially faster cast, but you leave yourself even less room for error since you spend life through the restricted Channel, and give your opponents mana to develop their own board position:

![The fastest Crush in the West](https://i.ormos.uk/4d/24/4d249d44df132d64b1b56516187de2f655991abcdf41b2b6c39337c791cb4856.jpg)

**ReelingDrunkWurms.deq**  
 4 Crush of Wurms  
 4 Exploration  
 4 Eladamri's Vineyard  
 4 Mana Flare  
 1 Channel  
 4 Taiga

…and so on (you can also work Fastbond into it).

On the first turn, you play Taiga, Exploration, a Forest, and Eladamri's Vineyard.

On the second turn, you play two forests, drink up the Vineyard mana for Channel (you'll be spending 17 before you're done), tap Taiga and 2 life for Mana Flare, then tap three forests (![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif)) and 15 life to cast your six wurms.

There. We're all very impressed with how clever you are. Now stop annoying your friends. Brisk wurms are fun all the time; but sudden wurms seldom are, more than once.
