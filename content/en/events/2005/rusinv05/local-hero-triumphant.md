This weekend millions of players across the globe will be tearing open brand new booster packs from the new Ravnica: City of Guilds expansion. In Russia things are a little different. Over here we have the 9th Edition release. This is not just any old release. This tournament marks the first expansion of Magic to be released using the Cyrillic alphabet. To commemorate the occasion sixteen of the World's best players have been invited over to take on the best that Russia, Ukraine and Belarus can offer in an exhibition tournament. For the full player list and profiles see here.

In the end the Local Heroes were not enough to stand up to the might of the Best of the Rest. The Best ran out 129-95 winners. But there was still the matter of the rotisserie draft for the top four players of each team.

Julien Nuijten was the top ranked player from Saturday and had the honor of taking the first pick in the Rotisserie draft. This draft featured every card in 9th Edition, in foil. Unsurprisingly, the World Champion took Loxodon Warhammer. It was not enough to save him from Antoine Ruel's clever, almost creature-less, blue-red deck in the semi-finals.

On the other side of the bracket Matvey Linov, perhaps better known as DuSheLov from Magic Online, was dismantling his opponents with an almost mono-blue deck. In the end it was the Russian player who caused the upset in the finals. His Mahamoti Djinn proved to be too tough to kill for the experienced French pro. The Local Heroes team might have been defeated by the Best of the Rest, but they were able to salvage some pride as Matvey Linov came out on top of the Rotisserie draft.

Overall the event was a massive success with many local players showing up to play in the 9th Edition release or to test their skills against the pro's on the gunslinging tables.

Given the choice of every card in 9th Edition, which card should you take first? Well there weren't too many surprises. Julien Nuijten, as the top ranked player in the swiss, got to choose first and rather predictably chose <a>Loxodon Warhammer</a>. As an indication of how powerful equipment is in the post-<i>Mirrodin</i> environment Gerasimenko immediately took <a>Vulshok Morningstar</a>. Antoine Ruel also plumped for an artifact, <a>Icy Manipulator</a>, before some people's top pick, <a>Wrath of God</a>, went fourth. Cornelissen opted for <a>Royal Assassin</a>; Alieksieiev, <a>Worship</a>; Olivier Ruel, <a>Plague Wind</a> and then Linov wheeled <a>Confiscate</a> and <a>Mahamoti Djinn</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Before the draft Olivier Ruel had said he'd wanted to build around <a>Hell's Caretaker</a>/<a>Nekrataal</a> Combo and also pick up Pague Wind and <a>Grave Pact</a>. I didn't think he'd get all four but he did. He also got <a>Gravedigger</a>, Rats, <a>Highway Robber</a> … you get the idea where this going. <a>Hell's Caretaker</a> would be a kill on sight card against his deck.</p>&#10;      <p>His brother Antoine, and quarter-final opponent grabbed a <a>Shivan Dragon</a> second pick, but then steered away from creatures. He built a blue-red deck with burn, counters and card drawing and grabbed support from artifacts. With <a>Traumatize</a> and <a>Aladdin's Ring</a> it was interesting looking deck.</p>&#10;      <p>Julien Nuijten looked to capitalize on the Warhammer with a very fast red-white deck with weenies and <a>Glorious Anthem</a>. He also got <a>Flame Wave</a> and <a>Shard Phoenix</a>, but left <a>Seething Song</a> too late and saw someone else snatch it. I only mention <a>Seething Song</a> because Nuijten had drafted three of then in a deck that featured <a>Flame Wave</a> and <a>Form of the Dragon</a> earlier this week, which only goes to show that the good players can always find a use for cards that don't immediately seem good. Fellow Dutchmen Cornelissen assembled a solid black deck.</p>&#10;      <p>Denis Tagunov seemed to have a solid plan when he started with <a>Wrath of God</a> and <a>Millstone</a>. I thought he was going for a &quot;stall the game out, deck you&quot; kind of deck but then seemed to lose focus and get pulled into both green and blue.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/rusinv05/1pick.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Nuijten secures the first pick of the draft</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Green seemed to be universally shunned until several players moved into it late and even then I was surprised at the order of the picks. The draft was interesting as Olivier got to form the virtually mono-black <a>Hell's Caretaker</a>, <a>Nekrataal</a>, <a>Grave Pact</a> deck. Antoine responded by not drafting any creatures other than a <a>Shivan Dragon</a> and <a>Anarchist</a>, relying instead on burn or <a>Traumatize</a> backed up with counters to win his games.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine won the coin flip and opted to play first. His deck doesn't really do a great deal early. Olivier started with a <a>Festering Goblin</a> and then dropped a dangerous card in this matchup, <a>Nantuko Husk</a>. Antoine tried to burn it with first Guerilla Tactics and then <a>Shock</a>. The Husk munched down on a <a>Festering Goblin</a> and <a>Looming Shade</a> instead as Olivier kept a Will-O-the-Wisp in reserve.</p>&#10;      <p>A <a>Quicksand</a> was thrown at the Husk. The Husk ate some <a>Ravenous Rats</a> and just kept right on coming.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine drew for some answers with <a>Counsel of the Soratami</a>. He was down to four life. </p>&#10;      <p>Ruel went for a <a>Serpent Warrior</a> and Antoine had to <a>Rewind</a> it. Olivier swung with the Husk but didn't try to end the game immediately as his only remaining creature was the Wisp and he didn't want to risk a trick in response.</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine got him to sacrifice the Wisp to keep the Husk alive after it took a <a>Hammer</a> on the chin. A <a>Shivan Dragon</a> wasn't good enough as Olivier was holding onto a <a>Consume Spirit</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>Olivier 1-0 Antoine</p>&#10;      <p>Olivier led off with <a>Ravenous Rats</a> and then <a>Drudge Skeletons</a> as Antoine dropped a <a>Disrupting Scepter</a> and then ramped up his mana with <a>Ur-Golem's Eye</a>. Antoine Rewound a <a>Horror of Horrors</a> and got to work on Olivier's hand with the Scepter as Olivier only had the two one power beaters.</p>&#10;      <p>A <a>Nekrataal</a> came out as an expensive 2/1 first striker and took a <a>Blaze</a> on the chin.</p>&#10;      <p>The pace of the match was fairly breath taking. Either that or I'm way too slow a typist.</p>&#10;      <p>The game state was Olivier with just some <a>Ravenous Rats</a> after <a>Quicksand</a> sucked down the Skeletons. Antoine was firmly in command now with <a>Treasure Trove</a> and then a <a>Rod of Ruin</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>&#10;        <a>Traumatize</a> smashed Olivier's library. However, a <a>Grave Pact</a> meant <a>Shivan Dragon</a> wouldn't be administering the coup de grace. With barely any library left Antoine <a>Annex</a>ed Olivier's sole <a>Plains</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>A <a>Serpent Warrior</a> was big enough to survive the Rod so Antoine buried it under a <a>Flowstone Slide</a>.</p>&#10;      <p>A <a>Nightmare</a> showed up late but Antoine was able to bring back <a>Blaze</a> with an <a>Anarchist</a> and then Olivier ran out of library.</p>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/rusinv05/qf1_2.jpg">&#10;            </div>&#10;            <div>&#10;              <i>Olivier Ruel</i>&#10;            </div>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;      <p>Olivier 1-1 Antoine</p>&#10;      <p>Antoine led off and <a>Mana Leak</a>ed Olivier's turn two <a>Ravenous Rats</a>.
