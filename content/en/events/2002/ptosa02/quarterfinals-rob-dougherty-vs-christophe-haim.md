![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a700.jpg)This quarterfinals match pits Rob Dougherty, seasoned Pro Tour veteran with his 3rd individual top 8 and 4th Pro Tour top 8 including the team competition, against rookie Christophe Haim from France, in his first Pro Tournament ever. Rob Dougherty is playing a control black deck with Nantuko Shade, Shambling Swarm, and Stalking Bloodsucker for the kill, filled with standard black creature control like Innocent Blood, Chainer's Edict, and Mutilate, as well as Diabolic Tutor, Mind Sludge, and Haunting Echoes. Rob's deck is very similar to many mono-black control decks that filled the PT-Osaka, with the exception of the Stalking Bloodsucker, used to fly over worrisome token generating cards like Squirrel Nest and Bearscape. Christophe Haim, on the other hand, is using an aggressive Blue/Green deck with Nimble Mongoose, Basking Rootwalla, Wild Mongrel, Aquamoeba, Werebear, and Arrogant Wurm for beatdown, as well as Circular Logic, Standstill, Careful Study, and Breakthrough. These two deck archetypes were the most popular and successful decks during the swiss rounds of PT-Osaka and it is unclear which deck type has the advantage. That largely depends on the individual sideboard strategies that each deck employs.

Christophe Haim wins the die roll and chooses to play first.

### Game 1

Christophe got a reasonably aggressive start with first turn Nimble Mongoose, second turn Wild Mongrel and Basking Rootwalla, and third turn Nimble Mongoose. Christophe was able to get Rob down to 9 life before Rob could cast a Mutilate, which cleared the board of everything but Christophe's Wild Mongrel. Over the next few turns, Christophe was able to get Rob down to 6 life before Rob stabilized and cast Haunting Echoes, leaving Christophe with only Werebears, Circular Logics, Aether Bursts, card drawing, and land in the deck.

It appeared that this would be the end for Christophe Haim, with so few routes to victory left against a deck that is packed full of creature kill. Yet, he managed an amazing comeback, top decking a Deep Analysis to draw four cards, using two Standstills to draw six cards, and using a Breakthrough to rip through most of the rest of his deck. He was eventually able to exhaust Dougherty's creature kill with well played creatures, Aether Bursts, and Circular Logics, finishing Rob off with his final Werebear.

Dougherty 0, Haim 1

### Game 2

Rob Dougherty brought in Faceless Butcher, Mesmeric Fiend, and Ghastly Demise and sideboarded out Mind Sludge, Skeletal Scrying, and Rancid Earth. Christophe Haim brought in Anurid Scavenger, the 3/3 protection from black creature which puts cards from the graveyard back in to the library, which served a dual function. Not only is it good at blocking and racing past Nantuko Shades and Shambling Swarms, but it provides protection against Haunting Echoes.

Rob chose to play first and gets an aggressive draw with second turn Nantuko Shade, third turn Mesmeric Fiend (nabbing a Circular Logic from Christophe's hand), and a fourth turn Faceless Butcher, putting Christophe's Wild Mongrel on ice for a while. Without Stupefying Touch or Persuasion in his sideboard, Christophe's deck has a hard time dealing with Nantuko Shade. Eventually, Christophe is able to get enough creatures in play to allow him to triple block and kill the Nantuko Shade, but Christophe lost all three of his creatures, a 4/4 Werebear, a Basking Rootwalla, and an Aquamoeba. Dougherty quickly finished him off with his horde of Mesmeric Fiends and Faceless Butchers.

Dougherty 1, Haim 1

### Game 3

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a701.jpg)Christophe Haim tried to get in some early beats in Game 3 with a second turn Werebear and a third turn Anurid Scavenger, but both died quickly to Rob's Chainer's Edicts, leaving both players at 20 life. Christophe, who was having no success at keeping a creature on the board, was soon being stared down by a Stalking Bloodsucker. First blood was drawn by Rob, with four points from the Bloodsucker before it was Aether Bursted back to his hand. Christophe responded with a Nimble Mongoose and an Anurid Scavenger and managed to knock Dougherty down to 14 before his board was killed by Mutilate. Dougherty replayed the Stalking Bloodsucker and attempted to go on offensive, racing an Arrogant Wurm on Haim's side of the table. After knocking Haim down to 10, Rob attempted to Chainer's Edict the Arrogant Wurm, resulting in both the Wurm and the Bloodsucker getting bounced with Aether Burst. Luckily, Rob had drawn a Nantuko Shade that turn and still had the mana to cast it, putting Christophe on the defensive once again. Christophe replayed the Arrogant Wurm, cast Circular Logic to stop a Ghastly Demise, but died on the next turn as Rob Dougherty cast a Faceless Butcher to clear the way for an enormous Nantuko Shade.

Dougherty 2, Haim 1

### Game 4

Christophe got off to a quick start with two Basking Rootwallas and an Aquamoeba in the first few turns. On the fourth turn, he had mana up in order to counter Rob Dougherty's Faceless Butcher with Circular Logic, and on the next turn Christophe knocked Rob down to 6 life. Things were looking bad for Rob Dougherty, but he was determined not to give up. On the next turn, Rob cast another Faceless Butcher, which resolved and took out a Basking Rootwalla. Christophe attacked with his other Basking Rootwalla on the next turn, Rob blocked with Faceless Butcher, and Christophe attempted to pump up the Rootwalla only to find that Rob had a Ghastly Demise waiting for just such an occasion.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a702.jpg)Things are looking worse for Christophe Haim, but he still had a few tricks left up his sleeve. Rob Dougherty cast a Mesmeric Fiend on his turn and Christophe responded by discarding Arrogant Wurm to his Aquamoeba and paying the madness cost to get a 4/4 trampler into play. Rob followed up his Mesmeric Fiend with a Nantuko Shade, which is forced to block and trade with the Arrogant Wurm on the following turn. With the death of his first Nantuko Shade, Rob decided to cast Diabolic Tutor to get another one and play it in the same turn. Despite having a potentially huge Nantuko Shade on his side of the board, Rob Dougherty had to be wary because he was still low on life. Christophe then cast a Werebear, which he can make 4/4 on the following turn by discarding cards to Aquamoeba, and waited to see what Rob's next move would be. On his next turn, Rob attacked with the Nantuko Shade and dealt 12 to Haim, leaving two mana up in case of an Aether Burst so he could replay the shade. No Aether Burst comes and Rob is forced to throw his Mesmeric Fiend in front of a 4/4 Werebear on the following turn. Rob untapped, drew, and discovered a welcome Faceless Butcher in his hand which he could use to remove Haim's last blocker, an Aquamoeba, and come across with the Nantuko Shade for the kill.

**Final Result:** Doughtery 3 Haim 1

In the end, Rob Dougherty and his control black deck were victorious over Christophe Haim. For his first Pro Tour appearance, Christophe Haim had to have been happy with his top 8 finish and Rob Dougherty was on his way to the semifinals.
