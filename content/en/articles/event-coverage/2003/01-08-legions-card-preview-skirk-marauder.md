*Odyssey* Block Constructed was fun, but after a while, all of those Wild Mongrels and Psychatogs got a bit tiring to look at. You could see it on the faces of the players at your local store, I’m sure, and perhaps you were even one of them. You were trying to find something with which to compete and the rest of the pack was still playing the same old tried and true deck. It’s amazing that those players could keep from yawning as they once again cast Upheaval and Psychatog.

Lucky for some of us, *Onslaught* brought back a fun deck archetype that doesn’t have a single card that says “counter target spell” on it. That’s right, its time for the goblins! *Onslaught* brought **Magic** players a brand new goblin champion: the *Goblin Piledriver*. While only a 1/2 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), he has protection from blue built in, allowing him to race past Psychatogs all day long, and when he can convince his goblin buddies to join him, he gets +2/+0 for each other attacking goblin. Not a bad guy to breathe life into this classic deck type. In addition to Goblin Piledriver, *Onslaught* brought us Sparksmith, a 1/1 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) who can kill the opposing player’s creatures with the flick of a wrist (and a bit of damage to its controller’s face). Last but not least, we got Goblin Sledder, a remake of the old favorite, Mogg Raider, from *Tempest*. Recently, goblin decks have had to struggle to find enough one casting cost guys to fit into the deck. Now, with both Goblin Sledder and Raging Goblin available, as well as Grim Lavamancer, if you are inclined to branch out into the non-goblin world, that shouldn’t be a problem for red beatdown.

I don’t know about you, but I’ve been sitting at home dreaming of more good goblins in *Legions*. Well, dream no longer, because the goblins are almost here. Skirk Marauder, in particular, looks like he packs quite a punch.

Skirk Marauder is a 2/1 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), which is a good power-to-casting cost ratio. Creatures like this have been used in red beatdown before, even without useable special abilities, like Firebrand Ranger and Rage Weaver in monored decks. On top of its efficiency, however, Skirk Marauder has two additional aspects that can come in quite handy. First of all, it is equipped with the morph mechanic, meaning that it can be played as a 2/2 colorless creature for ![3 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/7f/5d/7f5d2f1e55eaa9d19a9a23027b63bfbb5b39dbf8642160dda1a477a03df23a6a.gif). This ability can be useful for a number of reasons. Perhaps the opponent has a creature with protection from red on the board and you need a creature to trade it or get through to damage the opponent. In this case, a 2/2 colorless creature might be just what you need. Or maybe the opponent has a Circle of Protection: Red or an Engineered Plague on goblins that would make a 2/1 red goblin essentially useless. In addition, red beatdown is now the first deck that has two Constructed-viable morph creatures, making it difficult for your opponent to determine what exactly the face down creature is. Is it a Skirk Marauder or a 7/1 trampling Blistering Firecat? There is no good way to determine the answer, except waiting and seeing. The same advantage that red decks have in Limited, forcing opponents to choose whether to block an unknown creature that could be either a Skirk Commando, punishing the player for not blocking, or a Battering Craghorn, a 3/1 first striker that will punish the player for blocking, is now seen for the first time in Constructed.

The other aspect of Skirk Marauder that is so useful is perhaps the most important one and is intimately related to its ability to morph. For the morph cost of ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif), Skirk Marauder’s controller can flip it over to reveal it and have it deal 2 damage to target creature or player. This card is reminiscent of old utility creatures like Ghitu Slinger, a 2/2 creature for ![2 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/f9/cc/f9cc8a15b009997919d58ad79964384351495d1b6c9231f938316577453038a8.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) that deals 2 damage to a creature or player when it comes into play. Skirk Marauder can provide card advantage by being able to kill an opposing creature and still keep attacking or trade with yet another of the opponent’s creatures. Not only that, but after beating down as a 2/2 colorless creature, Skirk Marauder can be revealed to deal the final two points of damage to the opposing player. Let’s see them counterspell that.

Even if Skirk Marauder doesn’t make you want to play monored beatdown, don’t give up on the card quite yet. Because Skirk Marauder only has one red mana symbol in the casting cost and only one red mana symbol in the morph cost, it can easily be played in two color decks, like green-red or any other color combination, for that matter, as long as red is one of the colors.

Rarely do you see a creature of this quality for red beatdown decks. To make up for the flexibility and power of burn spells, the creatures are often a bit weaker than guys in other colors. Not so with this one; the Skirk Marauder is the complete package. He is a 2/1 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Red Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/86/2f/862ffc3d21299d18f0315a3f77721a10737fb394e147b0987204ef0496ce1060.gif) in the early game, he can be used to work around popular countermeasures like protection from red creatures, Circles of Protection and goblin killers like Engineered Plague, he can be used to deceive the opponent by pretending to be a Blistering Firecat and he can kill opposing creatures when revealed or deal 2 points directly to the opponent. What a deal!
