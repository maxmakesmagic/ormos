This is the first installment of my series evaluating the cards of *Legions* for the purposes of Limited play.

### Black

It isn’t until you look at the list that you realize just how solid black is in *Legions*. Not only is the set’s best card, Bane of the Living, found here, but the sheer number of powerful uncommons and rares is staggering, while Skinthinner is one of the best commons in the set, with its fellows running wide and deep and all but a few proving playable.

With so many strong red commons in *Onslaught* and the newfound theory that you don’t necessarily have to draft black or red any more, the black player is more likely to benefit from their color going underdrafted than in triple *Onslaught*. In order to benefit though, one must draft with the foresight and understanding that tells us that things will get better pack three. If you aren’t the kind of person who can stay true for two whole packs, you may just want to grab the red card.

Black is strong in any color combination, providing ground stability for blue, Deacon and other clerics for white, fast, large creatures for red and kill for green. One thing you need to consider when drafting with *Legions* is that *Legions* rares are almost as common as *Onslaught* uncommons and *Legions* uncommons not unlike *Onslaught* commons, so now you have to take every card seriously. You may not want to presume your opponent has Silent Specter because the math is just so stacked against it, but Bane shows up one in five drafts, so that face down creature could just be waiting to wreck you.

### The Cards

**Bane of the Living**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The first time I heard about Bane of the Living, I went to Randy Buehler and informed him that if I ever lost a Limited match on the Pro Tour to the card, I’d find him again and punch him in the face. It needs to be noted here that I am generally not a violent man and that means said actions could be defined as extreme measures. Why the intolerant reaction? Simple. For the cost, Bane is a ludicrous card in Limited play. If you’re black, you take it, no questions asked. The best card in the set, by a long, long way.

**Graveborn Muse**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The card that some say is number two, this Muse is the best of its ilk, a 3/3 body for four mana proving pretty strong in a format full of 2/2s for three, leaving the ability as a pretty ridiculous bonus. The Muse can single-handedly win a game, even if it’s the only zombie in your deck, with the Phyrexian Arena effect proving none too shabby. Just be careful: this isn’t a “may” ability, so if you get low enough on life, the Muse could prove to be your undoing.

**Aphetto Exterminator**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Compared by many to Flametongue Kavu, while the Exterminator loses out in those comparisons, to even be mentioned in that company suggests power. If nothing else, the Exterminator can act as a 3/1 for three, capable of stealing opposing Primocs or doing two a turn to creatures once enchanted with Lavamancer’s Skill, but that’s the last resort: more often than not, the card provides card advantage without help aside from the mechanical need to be flipped over. That makes for pretty powerful stuff.

**Hollow Specter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 2/2 flyers for three mana are always a precious commodity, so when they have a potentially dominating activated ability without drawbacks, they become first-pick caliber cards. The Specter can prove to be insane on turn three, allowing either the full peek or the one mana discard accompanied by a three mana creature on turn four. Don’t feel like you always have to spend the maximum here. If the Specter costs you your own development, a good part of its inherent advantage is nullified.

**Noxious Ghoul**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 At 3/3 for five, the Ghoul had better have one great ability, but it doesn’t disappoint. When it comes into play, Sparksmith dies, not to mention any creatures that are close to death after sustaining damage in combat. If you have a lot of zombies, the Ghoul can provide an Infest effect for opposing creatures every couple of turns pretty easily. If you have almost no zombies, the Ghoul could fall in value a bit, but if you’re black, you won’t be in that situation very often. A great card.

**Skinthinner**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Likely the number three common in the set, the ‘Thinner’s potency later in the game is fairly obvious, but what makes it so good is its ability to function as a two-drop when your mana appears to be lacking. Combining ridiculously well with Cruel Revival, the Thinner is more often than not a first-pick caliber card: that it’s this far down the list serves as testimony to black’s depth.

**Havoc Demon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A tough card to rate, it’s slow-to-pay casting cost is what leaves it here, though I considered placing it higher. The Demon’s value can rise with any of a number of ways to control its demise, like Nantuko Husk or Corpse Harvester in your pile of cards, not to mention six- or seven-toughness creatures that will survive its death. If nothing else though, this is a 5/5 flyer: those are still pretty good if I remember correctly.

**Corpse Harvester**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The Harvester loses out on the Skinthinner/Noxious Ghoul debate because its ability takes longer to manifest itself, but when it does, it usually has the most lasting effect. Its ability to create three-for-one trades is ridiculous and the fact it thins your library of land while providing one per turn is nuts. Amazing with a Smokespew Invoker in your deck, the Harvester will fetch it for you and provide the mana to activate. A very strong card.

**Toxin Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 On its own, Toxin Sliver is something of a powerhouse, usually taking a couple of cards out at a very reasonable price for its size and shape. Throw in a Crypt Sliver or two though and you have a regenerating basilisk (I’ve been playing for a long time now) that hits for more. Black’s sliver’s are ridiculous when compared to those of the other colors.

**Spectral Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Case in point: in Spectral Sliver, you get a creature that fits the parameters of a morph except for the fact it can also be pumped repeatedly with colorless mana. That means it will trade for two morphs on turn four if gang blocked and otherwise prove pretty much unblockable until late in the game when your opponent’s creatures will start lining up for a chance get slaughtered one per turn.

**Scion of Darkness**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Sorry kids, he’d be higher up, but black’s depth is ridiculous, relegating it to non-top ten status. Scion of Darkness is a massive creature with a powerful effect, but it is slow enough that many will overvalue it. The cycling is nice, but any cycling effect that costs more than two can not be considered too powerful: of course, if you have a Doomed Necromancer, getting this little fellow out on turn four makes for a nice possibility…

**Smokespew Invoker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 At 3/1 for three mana, the Invoker provides you with a solid body in the early goings, but it’s later in the game when it truly flourishes. When you reach eight mana, this little beauty becomes a must-kill card that, if unchecked, will dominate the table and usually win you the game. The best of the Invokers, that the Spew is a zombie means that you’ll have ways of getting it back from the graveyard a lot of the time. That means they’ll have to kill it twice. How ridiculous.

**Deathmark Prelate**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 When I first saw the Prelate, I told people I thought it was pretty good. They questioned my assertion, but now I’m sure. The simple fact is that the Prelate solves all kinds of problems, from fat green men to rares with the word ‘bomb’ written across their foreheads, they all fall one at a time if you have the zombies to spare. If nothing else, it blocks a morph and survives, making it eminently playable.

**Withered Wretch**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Black gets a true bear and it rates this low? The Wretch fills the two-slot admirably, establishing tempo and trading with a more-to-cost morph or like-sized creature, all the while nullifying cards like Aphetto Dredging and Entrails Feaster that rely on the graveyard to bloom into full usage. That’s a whole lot of positive for a two mana creature, even if it is a little color intensive.

**Sootfeather Flock**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 This is the halfway mark. A three-power flyer that can arrive on turn three. How ridiculous. The Flock pales by comparison to the cards higher on the list due to mana inefficiency and a boring lack of activated abilities, but it’s certainly a better-than solid card. Just understand that while it provides a path to victory, the Flock isn’t going to do too much until you’ve stabilized the table.

**Embalmed Brawler**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The bottom half starts with the equivalent of Trained Armodon. The Brawler should, more often than not, be made a 3/3, even if you have more zombies than one in hand, because no matter how big you make it, the damage difference is always two and later in the game you’ll want it to be available for blocking without pain. Of course, if you have a lot of removal in your opening hand, you may be able to ride a 5/5 to victory…

**Vile Deacon**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Moving way up in the cleric deck, if you’re playing with aggression on your mind, then the Deacon should always prove playable in that it’s a minimum of 3/3 on offense. Of course, if your deck permits, the Deacon can be a lot more than that, proving dominant once a couple more clerics hit play. If they’re your own, well played. If they’re your opponents… well, just don’t be complaining.

**Gempalm Polluter**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 A total beating with Unholy Grotto, the Polluter is always good, cycling for two, providing a 4/3 body and potentially winning the game with unpreventable pain to the opposing head. If you have five or fewer zombies, it starts becoming a card you might consider not playing, but even then, unless your deck is really good, it’s making the cut.

**Crypt Sliver**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Best in blue-black where it can hold down the ground while making Illusions regenerate and allowing your flyers the time they need to finish off your opponent, Crypt Sliver doesn’t always make the cut in your more aggressive decks because it just isn’t an aggressive card. That said, this little thing is a beating when combined with black’s other slivers and an annoyance for attacking ground creatures regardless.

**Infernal Caretaker**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 An off-color morph more often than not, the Caretaker can be a game winner in later turns, providing a lot of card advantage against nonblack decks. Remember: the Caretaker returns all zombie cards to owners’ hands and that includes those in your opponent’s graveyard, so be careful with its activation if your opponent has swamps.

**Phage, the Untouchable**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Phage, age 27, has been out of school for four years and is now looking for someone to spend her life with. She enjoys walks on the beach, nights by the fire, angering 6/6 flying haste creatures and killing everything she touches. If you like large women with an affinity for dread, this may be the one for you.

**Goblin Turncoat**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 The importance of two-power creatures for two mana should not be underestimated, but without other goblins in your deck, the Turncoat is wildly unspectacular any time after turn two. Great with Festering Goblin, the Turncoat can function as a Nantuko Husk of sorts in the right deck, proving very powerful in double blocks with other goblins. No card in the set wants to be a zombie more.

**Drinker of Sorrow**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Not unplayable, the Drinker is very large for its casting cost and, if it gets to attack, can sacrifice itself, trading card-for-card. It can’t block though, and sometimes the five points won’t be worth the loss of tempo, so it shouldn’t always make your deck. Good in an aggressive black-red, remember it only triggers when damage resolves, so it’s the optimal sacrifice for Husk or Harvester.

**Zombie Brute**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Honestly, how many zombies do you expect to have in your hand when you reach seven mana? If the answer is more than none, you’re playing the game wrong. Granted, this could be huge with the Harvester/Caretaker combo, but planning for turn thirty while drafting feels like a big mistake to me. 

**Ghastly Remains**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  
 Does anyone else find it sad that R&D so obviously put so much thought into making this card interesting only to have it turn out to be virtually unplayable? The Remains could be very big on turn three, but the mana requirements are unrealistic, and later on you’ll have no zombies in hand, so it’s seldom going to prove playable.
