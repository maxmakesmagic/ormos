### Getting Inside Mark Gottlieb's Head for Prismatic

Dabbling in Prismatic was fun last week, and I thought it might be even more fun to dabble with the Prismatic decks Mark Gottlieb posted last year (see [last week's article](/en/articles/intotheaether/2005/08-11-prismatic-with-comedy-al) for the links). Kill 'em All seemed a bit too brutal and nasty, so I decided to give his Prismatic Singleton deck a try.

The one problem with MaGo's decks is that they're pre-banning, which means they're chock full of no-no's. Thankfully, MTGO's deckbuilder is pretty good at pointing out what's wrong with your deck, and I quickly found the problem children. Spoiled brats, all of them, so I gave them the boot and replaced them with kids who know the meaning of the word fun:

*   Bribery became Overwhelming Intellect
*   Eladamri's Call became Genju of the Realm
*   Diabolic Tutor became One With Nothing
*   Fabricate became Jushi Apprentice
*   Skullclamp became Miren the Moaning Well
*   Sundering Titan became That Which Was Taken
*   Living Wish became Kodama's Reach (okay, not exactly fun but quite handy)

Yes, I picked One With Nothing purely for silly style points, because as far as I know MaGo's deck isn't exactly built around discarding your hand. Then I realized that I would probably have One With Nothing in every single opening hand, or top deck it when I was desperate for an answer card, and then it would cease being cute and silly. So I actually replaced Diabolic Tutor with Choice of Damnations, which I think is perfectly in keeping with the spirit of MaGo and could actually do something useful.

Hopefully when or if Mark reads this, he'll approve of my (admittedly casual) changes!

Thus armed with the Mindspawn of Gottlieb, I staked out the casual room looking for a game and found one at Arcept's table.

*A goofy deck. A random deck. A deck that contained every card I could ever want to see in my hand. A deck that would play differently every time the shuffler shuffled it up.* The words of MaGo echo in my head as I look at my opening hand. 

Our game begins with him Terroring my Diligent Farmhand with no green mana available to sacrifice him. Curses! I play Explosive Vegetation, and then next turn a Crosis the Purger! Who meets the wrong end of a Rend Flesh. I rip a Genju of the Realm though and have enough mana to play it and attack.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/aether/bs22_pris1.jpg)

I thoroughly expect Arcept to handle the land beats but it gets in two smacks before a Moment's Peace buys him some time, which he uses to summon a Frostwielder to go alongside Tim, slowly pinging away small bits of life every turn. After the Peace has been flashed back I swing in again, curious whether Arcept has found another answer.

He has.

![](http://www.wizards.com/magic/images/mtgcom/fcpics/aether/bs22_pris2.jpg)

Pinging the animated land with Frostwielder, he then Dark Banishes it. The Frostwielder's ability removes the land from the game, and since it doesn't hit the graveyard the enchantment doesn't return to my hand. A mighty fine play! My mutant elf is coming too though, so Prodigal Tim takes one for the team.

Arcept handles the Elf next turn, but I managed to rip Eternal Dragon, enchant him with Dragon Breath to give him haste, and swing in for the win. In the classic battle of questions versus answers, I managed to finally ask a question that went unanswered. Fortunately for me, Arcept didn't run across anything to refuel his hand with cards.

Arcept was a pleasant opponent and the first game was a lot of fun, so I asked if he'd be willing to be our featured Player of the Week this time.

This deck is the answer that came to what I saw in prismatic games.

I start a prismatic deck with my white cards. Since large creatures were thumping me, I went with Holy Day and Haze to give me more time to draw my answers, and I went with Wing Shards, Vengeance and Second Thoughts as more permanent answers. I don't have a lot of creatures, but I like Beloved Chaplain, who can block really well and also has evasion once I have control of the board.

Blue gave me counterspells, card draw, and Tim to handle Elves. Black gave me a ton of creature kill and a couple of my best creatures. 

I chose some Red spells mainly as stall tactics while waiting for better answers, like Temporary Insanity and Blind with Anger. Green pretty much has a dedicated job in prismatic, fixing mana and acceleration. The low costs on most of my cards helps green not need this as much but still, the more mana the better.

### Tips and Tricks

![](https://i.ormos.uk/7b/f2/7bf229d0c6a55f610c06b3d05f77d0f9d1ea728de6fbb151de4caf1f1e8334dd.jpg)Programmerman wrote in with this tip that many are probably familiar with, but if you aren't it's a critical one:

> *“The undo feature (in game, Alt + U or Ctrl + Z) is used to "untap" land and other mana sources. It works in almost all circumstances, up until you pass or play something. So, if you have two Forests and a Swamp, and want to cast an Elvish Warrior, if you accidentally tap Forest and Swamp, you can use the Undo function to untap the Swamp and start over. This work on almost all mana sources, except Charmed Pendant type mana sources (which involve moving cards from non-revealed zones) and mana abilities that trigger non-mana abilities (such as City of Brass, Forbidden Orchard, or any time another thing will trigger, like Price of Glory).”*

### Survivor Magic IV: Week 2 (Round 3)

The session started off with the Reward Challenge: the Casting Cost Search. Tribes had to search a 10x10 grid of mana symbols and find 20 cards with unique casting costs in it and list them. The first tribe to list 20 cards first would win the reward, being able to chose 2 commons to their card pool, from any set, as long as they didn't already have that common. The Kashi Tribe won.

Next came the Immunity Games. The deck format was 100 card, 5 color decks, with a minimum of 10 cards in each color. The game format was three player FFA, with one member of each tribe at each of the five tables. Whichever tribe had the fewest wins would go to Tribal Council and vote off a member of their tribe.

Kashi Tribe won 4 of the games, Matsu Tribe won 1 game. This sent Sakura Tribe to Tribal Council.

After giving Sakura Tribe ten minutes to figure out what they wanted to do, they joined Tribal Council and after some comments regarding them not liking the FFA format and their suspicions that the other two tribes allied together to make sure they lost, they were asked to vote. The votes were:

1 vote for Ka Tet  
 1 vote for superpriest  
 3 votes for Jade Idol

Jade Idol was the first person voted out of the Sakura Tribe.
