It's 2:30AM EDT on Wednesday, August 7th. I'm not sure what time that makes it in Australia right now, but what I do know is that I'm leaving for my flight to Sydney in about twelve hours now, and that makes me very happy. I'm definitely ready to go to Worlds right now, and I mean more than just being satisfied with our testing results. A month straight of playtesting certainly has its perks - I don't think I've ever felt this thoroughly prepared for a multi-format tournament before - but it also unfortunately has its downsides.

Yesterday morning, for instance, I woke up at 8AM lying on a pile of decks and scattered cards on the couch in the computer room. I'd fallen asleep without even realizing it, completely drained from playing mind-numbing mirror matches over and over for hours on end, and Ben had been kind enough to toss me a blanket as I passed out on top of our stacks of proxied test decks. When I finally managed to extricate myself from the mess of sharpie-covered Barbarian Bullies and the like, Ben was just going to bed, and EFro had long since been asleep.

I got on the computer and loaded up **Magic** Online. Yes, I realize I said in the last Colony update that I'd given up it to concentrate on playtesting, but the release of *Judgment* made drafting online actually somewhat worthwhile practice - not to mention the fact that I was the only gamer in the house not sleeping soundly. Hegstad had left on Sunday to take care of some things at home before leaving for his own flight, which is out of Los Angeles later in the week. By "taking care of things at home", however, it seems that he meant "have my own computer to play **Magic** Online on", as he's been drafting almost constantly since he's left, by my reckoning. Better than falling asleep on piles of cards, I suppose.

All those hours of testing that left me passed out, tossing and turning with nightmares of agonizingly boring mirror matches, however, have certainly paid off. I'm extremely happy with our deck for the Standard portion of the event and certainly feel that we've explored most of the possibilities for *Odyssey* Block. I'm relatively convinced, however, that Day 3 of Worlds won't be all that much more interesting than Pro Tour-Osaka despite the inclusion of *Judgment* in the mix, but I'm hoping that perhaps the German contingent of our testing group can prove me wrong in the scant week we have left.

More than looking forward to the actual competition at Worlds, however, right now I'm very much grateful for the chance to get out and experience the city of Sydney. As I've said, a month of playtesting certainly has its upsides, but one of the downsides for this particular experiment has been a decided lack of human contact besides the other residents of the Colony. Outside of our obvious mutual interest in Magic, EFro, Ben, Hegstad, and I don't share that many other common pastimes, and with a single car between the four of us - and Ben without a license, and me not trusted behind the wheel of anything without a roll bar - managing to go out and do anything was a bit of a rarity. Our trips to Origins and Michigan managed to break up the monotony of playtesting day in and day out, but still, the routine took its toll. I may be a gamer, but I'm also a brother of the Alpha Tau Omega fraternity, and every once in a while that side of me needs a bit of release. I've already talked to some of the Dutch who are staying in the same complex as us about going out as soon as we get to Sydney, and I'm looking forward to re-living our wild times at GP-Heidelberg.

These Colony reports are meant to give something of a "behind the scenes" look at the life of a pro, and while I could discuss playtesting and networking and strategy for pages on end, such a glimpse truly wouldn't be complete without a look at the community that makes up the Pro Tour itself. I'm looking forward to my trip to Australia today nearly as much simply for the opportunity to hang out with those people I only see at major events as I am for the tournament itself. While all the time spent preparing is certainly intended to produce results, none of it would be nearly as worthwhile if it weren't for the atmosphere that surrounds the Pro Tour. 

Most of what is written about Pro Tour players and their relationships focuses on teams, rivalries, and controversy. In my Invitational report this year, I lamented the death of the art of the tournament report, because I believe that it is this body of literature that truly tells the story of the game - the story that you can't find in the coverage online. The Pro Tour is more than wins and losses - more than dollars and pro points. It's a community of players who come together because of a common love for the game, but for many - myself included - it's the community and not just the game that keeps them coming back. Obviously, the allure of bright lights and big prizes is a huge incentive - if I said that I didn't think about what I'd do with the 35K from winning Worlds every now and then, I'd be lying through my teeth (and let me tell you - a good deal of that would go toward financing the alpaca room) - but for me, at least, the money isn't everything. I keep coming back to the Pro Tour because I have such a great time with the people even when I'm not winning that it's worth the trip, even if that trip happens to take me all the way to the other side of the planet. Whether it's karaoke and clubbing with Huey, Linde, Hegstad and company at last year's Worlds in Toronto, "sightseeing" with Ben Seck, Chris Pikula, Benafel, and David Price at the Invitational in Cape Town, late night Werewolf games at the Borteh manor at Origins, or all-night Blackjack at the Bellagio with Bob Maher and Dave Williams at GP-Vegas, Pro Magic events are a nearly endless source of good people and good times. I didn't come to Virginia to spend a month of my life living, breathing, and sleeping Magic (quite literally, it would seem, on the latter account) just because I'm looking to pick up a few dollars. I did it because I love to be a part of the story that is the **Magic** Pro Tour, and there's few better ways to do that than to be World Champion. I mean c'mon, $35,000 has got to go a long way in buying rounds of drinks.

Keep watching here at Sideboard.com for the final pre-Worlds update from the Mini-Colony, live from Sydney. Hopefully I can manage to borrow Ben's laptop long enough to write something before the tournament - if not, though, you can at least follow the coverage to see how we all do. See if the Mini-Colony can win the National Team competition! In any case, I'll have a post-Worlds wrap-up for you all once everything is through.
