From Osyp Lebedowicz’s report:

*“He did help me get ready for Worlds in Berlin though. He taught me how to say ‘I’ll cast Quicksilver Dragon’, ‘I’ll cast Bucther Orgg’, and ‘are you dead yet?’ all in German.”*

These are quotes from the Sideboard coverage of Pro Tour-Chicago:

*“Zvi Mowshowitz and Kai Budde are two of the biggest names in the ‘this format stinks’ camp.”*

*“Kai agrees. ‘If you are at a table with seven other competent players, it just comes down to choosing of colors and then distributing the cards. Nobody hate drafts until they have enough cards for their own deck and that's it,’ said the German.”*

*“Das ist ein Butcher Orgg.” -Kai Budde*

*“So it seems Budde has proven his own theories about Rochester Draft wrong. Humble pie probably goes down easier with thirty grand on the side.”*

Does anyone else see a problem in the last sentence posted on the front side of the PT-Chicago coverage?  
 Sure, I said I don’t like the format all that much, as it has similarities to sealed deck, as soon as everyone found its colors. In quite a few drafts, a judge could’ve distributed the cards and no one would have noticed at all. But not liking the format doesn’t mean that I don’t understand it and it certainly doesn’t exclude me from getting lucky. I can’t really deny that a lot of my match wins came, to some extent, from playing cards with gold expansion symbols…

While I did a lot of booster drafts on **Magic Online**, most my preparation came from real-life drafts. Using the regular Rochester queue on **Magic Online** just doesn’t work at all. Some people who play there are just too bad for real testing, but the more serious issue is that, for some reason, you are allowed to chat during the draft. I tried it a few times and every single time, some guys would just discuss their picks. Most of the time, it didn’t really help them, because they were just arguing that the other guy should leave one color while that guy obviously went into the color first (according to him that is) and, in his opinion, the other guy should draft something else. Blah blah blah…

A few times, they worked it out though and it was no real draft any more. We organised a few Rochesters with high-profile players, but those don’t make a lot of sense either. If everyone knows what to do, it looks way too much like sealed deck. The easy solution was to go back to real-life drafting. Dirk, Marco and I were going to play some Standard between Christmas and New Years at Dirk’s place, to prepare for Masters and Dirk mailed a few people to see if they wanted to show up and do some drafts. We didn’t really expect many people to have time, but we gave it a try. Well, apparently gamers don’t have much to do on those days as, in the end, fifteen people showed up. Eleven of them, I think, were qualified for PT-Chicago. That gave us two draft tables and lots of gaming took place that weekend. Back in Hamburg, it wasn’t all that difficult to organize drafts either and we did roughly two drafts a week the last month before the PT. That definitely helped a lot.

In the end, my plan going into Chicago was to draft blue-red, as that color combination is easily the most powerful archetype in *Onslaught*-only Limited. The backup plan in case a blue-red mage was sitting in front of me was green-black. The huge flaw with the green-black plan is that at a lot of tables, the players split red and black, because everyone wants some removal. But the red commons are a ‘little’ bit better then the black ones and when there are four red and four black mages, the black decks are a lot worse on average. I don’t have a problem fighting for red if the guy in front of me is, for example, green-red though. A red-blue deck wants slightly different cards anyway, especially Lavamancer’s Skill, which is pretty awful in any non-blue deck.

I have to add here that when I said that Bram drafts unconventionally, that does not mean that I thought it was bad. He had multiple good reasons to draft a green card when the Tusker got to him. The most important one was that he was afraid I wouldn’t take it and that would result in five players in front of Bram’s quarterfinal opponent Nicolai drafting non-green decks. After all, he had to beat Nicolai round 1 and he tried to prevent Nico’s deck from becoming utterly insane. The other reasoning is that the rule to draft red or black exists because you have to kill Sparksmith. Nicolai was a green-black mage at this point and with both players next to him drafting red cards, it was highly unlikely that he would go into red. Also Bram’s whole bracket was non-red which meant the first time he could encounter the annoying goblin was in the finals. To me, it sounds like a good plan to draft a deck to beat your quarterfinal and semifinal opponent. Making the finals is tough enough; drafting to win the finals doesn’t seem to be all that necessary to me…

 We didn’t get decklists this time and I thought Nicolai’s deck was a lot better against my deck then it actually turned out to be. His Wellwisher had been keeping him alive for a few turns as my Shepherd would have finished him off otherwise. Eventually, I got down to 4 and all he needed was to shoot me end of my turn with the Cannon, untap, shoot me again to win the game. I slowly drew my card and it was – Searing Flesh! Yay!  
 To be honest, I had quite a few outs there. My second Cabal Archon would’ve won and any of two Shocks or the two Misery Charms would’ve forced a draw with the Shepherd, but Searing Flesh was just the right card at the right time.  
 The second game was a brutal beating as I went turn 1 Festering Goblin and turn 2 Sparksmith. Nico didn’t draw too many spells and the few creatures he drew couldn’t really compete with the goblin. His draw in game 3 was pretty slow and he missed his fifth land for a critical turn. I had a nice aggressive draw with Shepherd, Severed Legion and Cabal Archon and finished him off with Dirge of Dread.
