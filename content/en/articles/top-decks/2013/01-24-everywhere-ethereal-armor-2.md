So between the end of last year and the beginnings of *Gatecrash* it has been some time since Top Decks has been dedicated to reporting the relevant formats' actual *top decks*. So with all respect to Domri Rade, Gyre Sage, and Duskmantle Seer... it's time to talk *Ethereal Armor*. 

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/td/td231_etherealarmor.jpg) 

Ethereal Armor | Art by [Daarken](https://web.archive.org/web/20130124000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/pages/search/default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&artist=[%22daarken%22])

(Relatively) recent developments in both Standard and Modern (the current PTQ format) have revealed some very different successful strategies than the last time we touched on them in this column.

### Ethereal Armor in Standard—Hexproof Mirror Match at Grand Prix Atlantic City

The top *two* decks in the finals of the recent [Standard Grand Prix](/en/events/2013/gpatl13/sterns-bant-auras-take-atlantic-city) were both aggressive decks based on hexproof creatures loaded with enhancements. Reminiscent of the Team SCGBlack "combo" deck that put two players into the Top 8 of [Pro Tour *Avacyn Restored*](/en/events/2012/ptavr12/angels-carry-hayne-to-miraculous-victory), these hexproof decks are designed for minimum interaction and maximum impact (with the opponent's face). 

Jon Stern's Bant Auras 

Standard – Winner, Grand Prix Atlantic City

The Grand Prix–winning deck is a good example of the Standard Bant Auras.

As you can see, it has eight solid hexproof creatures—Invisible Stalker and Geist of Saint Traft—and a ton of ways to make them big. Bant Auras can start the game by fixing its mana with Abundant Growth, and that Abundant Growth can boost an Ethereal Armor down the line. Rancor is a top-flight enhancer "good enough for *green* decks" and obviously quite effective on Invisible Stalker, which can neither be blocked nor targeted.

The most obvious "combo" in the deck is Geist of Saint Traft + Spectral Flight. Geist of Saint Traft is one of the most powerful and frustrating offensive creatures across a variety of formats. If you drop a Geist of Saint Traft turn two off an Avacyn's Pilgrim it isn't even vulnerable to Liliana of the Veil! Pretty much the only thing an opponent can do about the Geist is to put a creature in front of it (generally taking the 4/4 on the jaw). But if you apply Spectral Flight to Geist of Saint Traft, it not only gets big and puts the opponent on a three-turn clock, but it takes away the principle option for interaction. Flying creatures, quite simply, are tough to block.

Silverblade Paladin is quite the deadly assassin in this strategy, but maybe not in the most obvious ways. Sure, if you have a huge creature wearing a hundred Rancors and Ethereal Armors, perhaps the manyfold bounties of an Increasing Savagery, doubling damage will be attractive... but what about not pairing Silverblade Paladin when you have a Geist of Saint Traft already on board? You can instead pair the Silverblade Paladin with the 4/4 Angel token, allowing you to hit for 8 in the sky... 2 + 8 is 10, or half the other player's life total—might or might not be better than a more obvious double strike route. Just know that—especially with the Angel's evasion—this is an important option for the deck.

The much-decorated Joshua Utter-Leyton put some interesting twists on his Bant Auras; in the main deck we see Fencing Ace—which starts off with double strike—as well as Curiosity. *Just* putting Curiosity on a Fencing Ace can give you quite the little card-drawing engine, and Rancor on a Fencing Ace can strike down a Thragtusk, live, and hit the opponent. Curiosity in general not only works synergistically with Ethereal Armor, but—Fencing Ace or no—it can keep the deck flowing, especially when working with evasion creatures. Although the deck is fast (and many of the creatures are quite clearly hard to deal with, or at least target) it still has to commit tons of permanents to the battlefield. Having a backup—a hand full of Plan B—reduces the deck style's reliance on a single dimension.

Utter-Leyton's sideboard was built to beat creature removal—and sweepers more specifically. Strangleroot Geist might be targetable, but its point is to be good against cards like Supreme Verdict... the kind of cards where Invisible Stalker, and especially Geist of Saint Traft, are by nature exposed. Wolfir Avenger not only lives through quite a few kinds of commonly played creature removal, but Josh could play it at the end of the opponent's turn (ideally a turn where the opponent just tapped down or out for a big defensive card).

It is probably fair to say that Jund is still THE defining strategy of the Modern format, but if you plan to win a [Pro Tour *Dragon's Maze* qualifier](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/qualifiers-for-pro-tour-dragons-maze) tournament, likely you will have to be prepared to beat the Bogle deck. 

Actually predating the Standard version by a short time, this deck is based on similar principles: Play a hexproof creature that the opponent can't target. Load it up with Ethereal Armor and other synergistic creature enhancements. Bang, bash, kill quickly. 

Now, the Modern format gives you access to quite a few more cards than Standard, so the card choices will be different other than Ethereal Armor (a card that exists at the perfect intersection of card power and being one mana). Two-thirds of the creatures in this deck are one mana, which can set you up for some immediate beatdown with Rancor or [totem armor Umbras](https://web.archive.org/web/20130124000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/pages/search/default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&name=+[umbra]&type=+[%22enchantment%22]). 

Because a favorite play of the dominant Jund decks in Modern is to flip Liliana of the Veil off their Bloodbraid Elfs, the Bogle deck—hexproof that it may be—has to find a way around the Cruel Edict ability on that powerful Planeswalker.

Red-White-Blue can potentially have the upper hand, regardless of who is sitting across the table.

Modern Birthing Pod decks can chain up with value from none-to-five; they can block well, accelerate mana, and find solutions to any number of the format's problems. But these decks tend to kill with infinite Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker combinations. 

 ![Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Kiki-Jiki,%20Mirror%20Breaker&options=) 

  

Interestingly, Kiki-Jiki can combine with creatures at the three, four, or five. 

 ![Deceiver Exarch](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Deceiver%20Exarch&options=) 

*   Deceiver Exarch—Kiki-Jiki copies Deceiver Exarch, which untaps Kiki-Jiki, setting up another Exarch copy.

  
  

 ![Restoration Angel](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Restoration%20Angel&options=) 

*   Restoration Angel—Kiki-Jiki copies Restoration Angel; its token "blinks" Kiki-Jiki, which of course leaves us with an untapped Mirror Breaker (ready to do another copy, blink, and re-copy).

  
  

 ![Zealous Conscripts](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Zealous%20Conscripts&options=) 

*   Zealous Conscripts—Like Deceiver Exarch, Zealous Conscripts can untap Kiki-Jiki, as can each copy.

  
 

While most successfully navigated Pod games end up with Kiki-Jiki setting up an attack for essentially infinite damage, how you get there is most of the fun. If your opponent has a Birthing Pod and a persist creature... watch out!

If you want options... Gifts Ungiven has got them for you. 

The deck can play big creature or lockdown... or play a grinding progressive game based on card advantage and a slow Planeswalker march to inevitability.

You can play Gifts Ungiven for your fatty of choice (Grand Cenobite against small creatures, Shield of Emeria against combo decks, say) and the one Unburial Rites. You find two cards, fail to find two, and "have to" dump both cards into the graveyard. Then you untap and pay the flashback on Unburial Rites. It is in that way a one-card combo setting up the big white hater of your choice.
