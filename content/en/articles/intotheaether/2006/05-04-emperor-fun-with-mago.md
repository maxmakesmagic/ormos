I was really looking forward to Days of Emperor Fun II this past weekend. My minivan's transmission went belly up and, facing a rather large hit in order to get it rebuilt, I really needed to have some fun. Thankfully, Saturday night there were plenty of other players looking for the same thing. The 2/2/all Standard format seemed to shake things up a bit and led to quite a bit more interaction than the more typically played 1/1/1 format seems to bring. I managed to get in about a half-dozen emperor games and by-and-large enjoyed them all.

I decided this would be a good time to try out an Ink-Treader Nephilim deck. I used to have some fun around the game shop tables in multiplayer games with Radiate and Fire // Ice (using the Ice side of course), and I was itching to try the newest incarnation of it. Electrolyze made the update complete, nicely playing the role of Fire // Ice. When searching for another cantrip effect, I ran across Wildsize, which fit perfectly by being able to help my flankers in the middle of combat if necessary.

Of course, with all the card drawing I had to put Niv Mizzet in the deck. In fact, he insisted on calling this a “Niv Mizzet” deck with just a side order of Nephilim magic. You and I know how things really came together, but there's no need to ruffle a dragon's… er, scales. Here's the deck I initially decided to run.

Since I was running green already, I thought I'd run Moldervine Cloaks as a fine enhancement to my flankers' creatures, and with the card-drawing they would be easy to get back (though I would need to keep in mind not to deck myself between Dredge and the mad card-drawing). Spectral Searchlight played double duty, helping to fix my gross mana requirements or lending an extra mana hand to my flankers who might need it. Oh, and they can also sometimes ping my opponents at inopportune times.

Overall, I was fairly happy with the deck... until GL2270 demonstrated a serious flaw in my plan.

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_bs59_picMain_en.jpg)

When he tossed a Char at my Nephilim, my first thought was “Hm, isn't that going to kill him?” Then with horror I quickly realized that *I* would control the copies of Char that Ink-Treader Nephilim spawned, each hitting me for 2 points of damage. A quick count of the available creature targets around the board and I knew I was toast, literally (some of the creatures in play aren't in the view here because they wouldn't fit on the screen).

Check out my left flanker for this game: limitedinformation! Yes, the newest member of the weekly Featured writers on **magicthegathering.com**, [Noah Weil](https://web.archive.org/web/20070902111727/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/authorarchive&author=NoahWeil) came out to get in a couple games of emperor. Like a lot of us who came out to play this weekend, he was new to the format. I tried to get him to send me his decklist to share, but he declined. “It was literally a five minute concoction,” he said. It looked like a nice Green/White deck and he was about to get medieval with Tolsimir Wolfblood when I had my run-in with a very painful stack. 

Anyway, Char was some serious bad times for me. I debated on whether I should cut the Nephilim altogether, but that would require a major retooling of the deck and, honestly, I really wanted to play with Ink-Treader. Should I just ignore The Fear and keep going with the game plan?

The goblin with the funny hat was my compromise. Hidetsugu's Second Rite didn't seem to be doing anything but sit in my hand, so I cut those and one Shadow of Doubt to make room for my hero, Goblin Flectomancer.

Unfortunately for this game, opposing emperor HurricaneKnapp had taken a page from Two-Headed Giant Constructed's banned list by playing Erayo, Soratami Ascendant. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/global/images/mtgcom_daily_bs59_pic2_en.jpg)

Ironically, Mark himself [wrote back in September](/en/articles/latestdevelopments/2005/09-02-septembers-minor-tweaks): “The first card to be honored with the distinction of being banned in 2HG is Erayo, Soratami Ascendant. This little sucker is quite easy to flip when two players are casting enough cheap spells to make it happen, and once it has “ascended,” it does double-duty by countering the first spell played on each turn by each player on the opposing team. Teams can essentially get “locked out” of the game on turn 2 or so with startling regularity. As 2HG is mainly a format for fun, we don't want to encourage turn-two locks.”

Well, if it's easy to pull off in two-player teams, in three-player teams it is obviously a breeze! On top of that, Gekko450's flanker deck was apparently designed to mostly ignore the opposing flanker and just toss flame directly at the dome of the opposing emperor. If both opposing flankers utilized that strategy the game would be a short, brutal, and most un-fun affair.

Just like any “fun” format, a Spike approach can spoil it, but by and large, most people I had the privilege of playing with over the weekend brought decks to have fun with. I think the second Days of Emperor Fun II was a rousing success and I hope that people continue playing the format and having a good time with it. I know I will keep a 2/2/all emperor and flanker deck handy when I get the urge for something different.

One nice side benefit of Days of Emperor Fun II seemed to be a lot of “regular” games of emperor 1/1/1 being played. No matter what style of play you prefer, I think the Emperor format offers a nice change of pace.

### *Visions* Release Champion – schtuffy Wins!

The *Visions* Release Championship was bumped back a week due to the *Dissension* prerelease tournaments, so it ran this past weekend. I tracked down the winner to share his winning decks and tell us a little bit about himself. 

> “My name is Eric Smith A.K.A. schtuffy online. I'm 20 years old, and I'm currently studying physics as an undergraduate student at Penn State University. 
> 
> “I haven't played that many premier events online, but I have been fairly successful in the ones that I have played. I'm currently 7 for 12 in making Top 8s online. All of these events have been Limited, and most (all but 1) have been release events.
> 
> “I've been playing **Magic** since 1997, but I only started playing it regularly my senior year of high school. I have yet to play in a sanctioned tournament with paper cards, but I hope to remedy that this summer.
> 
> “Building your *Mirage-Visions* sealed deck is very dependant on what you open of course, but in general there are a few good things to note. The first is that most games will not go into a complete stall in sealed. The format has good, maindeckable landwalkers in the common slots as well as flyers to break stalls.
> 
> “I also don't like splashing in the format, as there are a lot of cards that have double color costs, but it's usually a good idea to splash red for the Torch or Rock Slide that you opened, especially if you have a couple of color fixers. In my sealed pool I was lucky enough to be able to play a great deck with only two colors - this made my deck very consistent.
> 
> “I've only drafted MMV 3 times, so I don't feel all that qualified to comment on the format in particular. I do have an anecdote I'd like to add. In my draft, I first picked Enfeeblement in a weak pack and then was passed Volcanic Geyser, with a common missing. This meant to me that the guy on my right had probably taken a Torch. I went for the Geyser anyway (over Pacifism); red is just that good.”
