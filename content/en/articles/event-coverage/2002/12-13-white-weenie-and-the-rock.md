Christoph pulled out at least one win against Sligh with turn 2 Absolute Law, turn 3 morph, turn 4 unmorph Exalted Angel, but that is a pretty unlikely draw and will still lose against a goblin deck with a good draw. One blocker and +4 life on turn 4 just doesn’t race Goblin Piledriver and his friends. Even though Sligh was everywhere, Fiends did quite well. Christoph made Top 8, but lost to eventual winner Mack while Delere ended up being one win short and finished 16th. Blech, on the other hand, walked into a lot of bad matchups and found himself at 1-5 when the day ended. While this would have been an excellent choice for Day 2 at PT-Houston, I can’t recommend running this in a regular qualifier. It may be playable if you know that Reanimator and Oath are going to be 40-50% of the field, but I just can’t see that happening. The Rock is also not the best matchup. It largely depends on the version being played. If they don’t have sufficient removal for Meddling Mage, you can just lock Pernicious Deed and win without too much trouble. If they do have some sort of targeted removal, it becomes a lot worse. The deck isn’t really fast enough to defeat them before they get to that Vampiric Tutor and a Deed wrecks your team.

The card that really surprised me in this deck was True Believer. Dirk played a few games with his goblin deck against the Believer and it was a pain. Usually, he would deal some early damage and then try to burn the white player out, but he had to get rid of the cleric first. This could prove quite difficult at times. Burn isn’t the only thing it stops, though. Edicts, Duress and Cabal Therapy don’t work if this little guy is around. There are only few decks that don’t run some of those cards.

This list is already a slightly updated version. At the GP, there were 2 Topples and 2 Reprisals in the deck, but Topple is clearly better. If tier 1 right now include Sligh, Rock, Reanimator and g-u madness, it is useful against three of those four and it is playable, if unspectacular, against Sligh. A card I liked a lot in Lippert’s deck was Exalted Angel. Christoph was winning games left and right with it. If it lives, it’s great against Sligh and Rock. It provides you with a fast, evasive clock that makes racing pretty much impossible and the six casting cost is a nice defense against Deed.

The Interdicts are there to combat Pernicious Deed. It sounds strange to board in three cards that do pretty much nothing if they don’t draw a Deed (countering the abilities of Yavimaya Elder and Ravenous Baloth just doesn’t cut it), but as that is their best card in the match, they will use Vampiric Tutor to fetch it, making the three slots worthwhile. The Worship is okay against goblin decks, but not great against Sligh. Facing Cursed Scrolls, you can’t rely on having a creature on the board. It is very good against green-blue madness though. The Masticore comes in against red and black decks and in the mirror. The Tutors obviously get boarded in whenever you need one of the silver bullets. The Drakes are only against Reanimator, although I would board them in the mirror if your opponent can search the rebel chain up to Lin Sivvi. The third Angel comes in against Rock and Suicide Black.

While this deck doesn’t have the brute force of Angry Hermit or Reanimator, all those small guys are surprisingly effective. Justin Gary’s Oath deck can hardly handle a Meddling Mage blocking Pernicious Deed. Sligh has major trouble against Divine Sacrament and the goblin deck just doesn’t like decent blockers at all. I haven’t played enough with this to claim that it is a tier 1 deck, but I think it can compete with the big guys.

The three losses I had against red decks here were due to the same reason I lost on Day 1 – I didn’t cast spells. My round 1 opponent drew a combination of Ports and Wastelands in game 1 and never allowed me to get up to double green while he slowly burned me out. In round 2, I met eventual finalist Benjamin Caumes. I won game 1 pretty easily with three Walls, but he drew three wastelands in game 2 and I didn’t cast any spells. Game 3, he drew three Ports and, well, I didn’t cast any spells.  
 I won the mirror in round 12 again on the strength of Buried Alive. In round 13, I played against Sam Gomersdall, who definitely lucked out. He completely slaughtered me in the first game, but he had only five or six counterspells in his deck, so he couldn’t counter my Buried Alive in game 2. Genesis and Thrull Surgeon got to work and he didn’t really have a chance. The same thing happened in game 3. He was at 2 life, I had various creatures on the table and Genesis with Surgeon going. He had seven lands in play and zero other permanents or cards in hand, while there were two minutes left in the round. He drew a card and passed. Me: attack – Sam: Moment’s Peace

Sam played a land and passed.  
 I got something and attacked, he flashed back.  
 He drew a card and ended his turn.  
 I activated Surgeon. He played a Brainstorm in response, put two cards back and then used Cunning Wish to get a Moment’s Peace and played it.  
 On his turn, he drew the first card he put back with the ‘Storm and passed.  
 I used Vampiric Tutor to get my Crypt Creeper. I drew and attacked. Instead of flashing the one in his graveyard back, he played the second Moment’s Peace the ‘Storm gave him. This is when time ran out. I used the Creeper to get rid of one of his two Peaces and passed.  
 He drew the land that was the third card he got with the Brainstorm and played it.  
 I attacked and he flashed back the last Fog he had left. He drew his card and passed. This was the last turn I had.  
 I attacked and he turned over the Moment’s Peace he just drew – the first card he didn’t know from the Brainstorm! What a lucky man.

Game 3 turned out to be another draw, but as I saw before the round that I would most likely finish in the Top 32 with a loss, while Ruud had a very small chance to make Top 8 with a win and as I was fortunate enough to receive a concession myself on Day 1, I scooped and gave Ruud the win.

After all was said and done (and without concessions!), the deck had a record of 6-3-3 which was alright, as I had 3 byes early on and played against good decks/players the whole tournament. All three losses were unnecessary, but that is how this game works sometimes. If you plan on running the red version, I’d cut one Village and one with to add two more basic forests. But in general, I don’t think this version is a good call. It has the same problem Kastle’s version had – the decks you are gunning at with the Terminates just aren’t around any more. I got rid of some nonbasics and added a 24th land. I wanted to have some removal, but I don’t like Edicts very much. They don’t do much against Sligh, Suicide Black, White Weenie or the mirror. Instead, I moved the Butcher into the main deck so I can Tutor it up against Reanimator and I also added a Terror. The Terror is there to fight Meddling Mage and g-u madness. It’s also good against Reanimator and Oath. I liked Buried Alive so much at the GP that I moved one into the main deck. It’s certainly a bad draw against Sligh, but against the burn version, it is not all that bad. That deck isn’t fast enough to overwhelm you that easily and if you manage to activate a Deed and stall your opponent, you need to win somehow. If you have the Buried Alive at that time, it provides you with Genesis/Ravenous Baloth and if you get that going, the red mage will scoop. I would like to have more Smothers in the board, as those are great tools against madness. You really need to get rid of Bouncer and killing a Wild Mongrel or a Wurm token doesn’t really hurt either. The problem is that all the other cards in the board are pretty essential and I wouldn’t want to leave without those. In that tournament, I also had a Visara the Dreadful in the board in place of the Taskmaster, as it was a league thing and I was pretty sure that I wouldn’t be playing against Aluren. If Aluren doesn’t appear in your metagame either, I’d recommend making that switch. Visara is good in the mirror, against White Weenie and against madness. The problem with madness is Gilded Drake. But if you can back her up with Therapy/Duress, she’ll dominate the table.

Sadly, this deck has one general problem and that is the 50 minute round. The mirror match is painfully slow. I am a really fast player and I can’t really think of the last time I got an unintentional draw in Constructed. It surely wasn’t in Standard during Nationals/Euros/Worlds last year when I played Psychatog the whole time. That deck isn’t exactly the fastest either. I got three draws in one tournament with Rock. I’d suggest you take that as a warning. 

In the mirror match, it is actually a good plan to just win game 1. The Creeper in the board allows you to deal with your opponent’s Genesis while he hopefully won’t be able to remove yours. If that plan fails, the only thing that speeds the matchup along is one player not drawing lands …

Hopefully that won’t be you though!
