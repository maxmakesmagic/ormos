That means that yesterday's 5-3's will have to put on their best game, both in Draft and Legacy. Unfortunately, the fifteen-point field is saturated with talent.</p>&#10;<p>You want Hall of Fame members? We have five for you: Mowshowitz, Dougherty, Levy, Kastle, and The Great One. Since early Thursday morning, players have been competing a whole host of events, from drafts and 8-man Constructed queues to PTQs and special tournaments that have lured players in not just with their prize pools of fancy gadgetry but with the opportunity to win the <b>Magic</b>-branded 2007 Toyota FJ Cruiser available in the aptly titled &quot;Win a Car&quot; tournament held here in New York. I'm pretty sure that by English standards, the &quot;car&quot; is more like a small house, but however you look at it, it's a pretty cool extra reward for playing your best <b>Magic</b>.</p>&#10;<p>&#10;<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/worlds07/blog_sides.jpg"><br><i>The view from the besieged Side Events booth</i></td></tr></tbody></table>Thus far, there have been 3 events classified as &quot;Vroom!&quot; events, in Sealed Deck, Legacy, and Two Headed Giant formats, earning eight lucky players slots in the exclusive 32-player finals. Tomorrow there is the North-East Standard challenge, which will qualify another eight players, and there are still some events today drawing people in attracted by trophies of the four-wheeled variety. </p>&#10;<p>There are some big names that have been seen around the side events area thus far. Winners of PT–LA 1998 (Dave Price) and 2001 (Mike Pustilnik), have both been spotted at the side events stage, and MikeyP was quick to confirm that he would be around all weekend playing in PTQs and other events as long as they were running. As I was at the side events stage today I was met by a cheery looking Shingo Kurihara, who having lost out in the main event was signing up for some 2HG. He simply grinned at me and said the word &quot;Car!&quot; (exclamation point very much included).</p>&#10;<p>There is definitely plenty of excitement about the idea of driving the trophy home, and it has brought all sorts of names from around the history of the game back to our World Championships. </p>&#10;<p>As Mark Rosewater put it &quot;The World Championships 2007 are to the Pro Tour as Time Spiral is to Magic.&quot;</p><br><a name="5"></a><li><b>Friday, December 7: 3:25 p.m. – A Glance at Legacy!</b><div><i>by Josh Bennett</i></div>&#10;</li>&#10;<p>Legacy was the biggest unknown coming into this World Championships, and the pros seem to have risen to the challenge and dedicated a lot of playtesting to it. There's a strong contingent of Enchantress players, some <a>Counterbalance</a>, and blue-green threshold and non-threshold. The <a>Manabond</a> deck is also making a splash. </p>&#10;<p>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td>&#10;<td></td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td>&#10;<td><a><img alt="Manabond" src="https://i.ormos.uk/3e/f1/3ef1b24ba985d4b7c84fb10b8ac508dd5cd8856907c7ed457e972411d1b18d8a.jpg"></a></td>&#10;<td></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>The idea of a turn-one kill has proven irresistable to many players, who have dug out their <a>Goblin Charbelchers</a>. More than a few have found there to be many more <a>Force of Will</a>s in attendance than they had hoped. A glum look, an empty hand, and a board of <a>Lion's Eye Diamond</a> tells the whole story.</p>&#10;<p>Osamu Fujita is playing an extreme take on the Smokestack control deck. In addition to the usual suite of Trinisphere, <a>Smokestack</a>, and <a>Crucible of Worlds</a>, he has the full eight <a>Armageddon</a>s (some labeled <a>Ravages of War</a> to prevent disqualification), <a>Pendrell Mists</a>, and just two win conditions: A single <a>Mishra's Factory</a> and <a>Academy Ruins</a>.</p>&#10;<p>Walking past one table, a player had out three <a>Chalice of the Void</a>, on zero, one, and two. This would be fine, if his opponent wasn't with Affinity and playing out free Frogmites and Myr Enforcers.</p>&#10;<p>The French have turned up with hate-filled package based around <a>Blood Moon</a>s and the <a>Magus</a> thereof. Dubbed Dragon Stompy, it powers out punishing spells and big monsters on the back of acceleration like <a>Ancient Tomb</a>, <a>City of Traitors</a>, <a>Chrome Mox</a>, and <a>Seething Song</a>.</p>&#10;<p>With such a diverse field, it looks like versatility will be the order of the day. Those decks that can adapt to a variety of situations will find themselves on top.</p><br><a name="6"></a><li><b>Friday, December 7: 4:40 p.m. – Player of the Year Updates</b><div><i>by Tim Willoughby</i></div>&#10;</li>&#10;<p>This year's Player of the Year race is now looking likely to become a sprint finish. Going into the World Championships, all eyes were on Tomoharu Saito. With a seven-point lead on Kenji Tsumura, garnered over a series of strong Grand Prix finishes, Saito went in with the title to lose. If he finished high enough, there would simply be nothing that any of the competition could do to stop him finishing on top.</p>&#10;<p>While Saito was the favourite, there were plenty still in with a chance. Typically this chance would involve a Top 8 finish, where the Pro Points payout becomes high enough to catch up on Saito's lead. Kenji Tsumura has had his sights set on capturing a second Player of the Year for some time, and when I bumped into him on Thursday, he had already launched into the mantra of P – O – Y. </p>&#10;<p>What is interesting is that with just four rounds left to go, Raphael Levy, Guillaume Wafo-Tapa, and Kenji Tsumura are all within reaching distance of Top 8, while Saito picked up a draw that almost certainly means he now cannot make it.
