Back in 1994, in the first summer I played **Magic**, I became convinced I had the secret pass codes to the universe about a month into playing. The card that gave me my first inkling at superior understanding to this game was [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape).

![Kird Ape](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Kird+Ape)

 

Like [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) in our opening anecdote, [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) was a vanilla 1/1 creature for one mana. Except when it wasn't.

If I just played with Forests, [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) would be a 2/3 creature for that same one mana! I don't know if I yet knew that [Taiga](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Taiga) existed, but I knew enough to realize I should be giving some thought to how my [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) decks should look (you know, other than just playing every [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt) I owned).

Ideally, [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) has *synergy* with Forests.

We started this article on synergy with an Aristotle quote. *Synergy*, the word, comes from a Greek root meaning "working together."

In **Magic**, synergy is simply the realization and concept that certain cards are more effective when played with certain other cards. The cards, generally speaking, can function all right in and of themselves, but because of this synergy, produce more together than what they could when taken in isolation.

The undefeated 6–0 Tsapko played a straight RW deck that started on [Savannah Lions](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savannah+Lions).

You'll note that even though his deck was only red and white, Tsapko was willing to play [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire) (black), [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand) (blue), [Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath) (green), and [Wooded Foothills](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wooded+Foothills) (green) in his mana mix. Tsapko's deck didn't make black, blue, or green mana, so these various lands really just served to get one of his two basic Mountains or two basic Plains... or, of course, one of his four copies of [Sacred Foundry](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sacred+Foundry).

All of these cards are arguably fine, but they become increasingly compelling in a deck with [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer).

![Grim Lavamancer](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Grim+Lavamancer)

 

If Tsapko played a straight mono-white beatdown deck instead of a red one with [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer), we might not see all of these fancy lands. Maybe he wouldn't have wanted to invest a life point in getting his actual mana-producing lands onto the battlefield. But putting any cards into his graveyard "for free" is highly synergistic with [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer). [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) is a powerful card in general, and historically has proved playable just by getting a little extra oomph out of a spent burn spell. But here, the prospect of getting lands into his graveyard "for free" really put [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) over the top. We can say that these lands had great synergy with [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer).

Plus, this is a deck with both [Savannah Lions](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savannah+Lions) (white) and [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) (red) for one-drops. A single [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand) can therefore—if at a cost of 3 life points—transform into a first-turn [Sacred Foundry](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sacred+Foundry) that can tap for either card on the first turn.

In sum, though, I just wanted to highlight that a straight RW deck—for all the reasons noted—would be happy to play with these seemingly off-color black-, blue-, and green-finding lands in a deck with no black, blue, or green mana-producing lands. Tsapko's RW deck played ten such finders.

Pro Tour Champion Remie only went 5–1 with his RW version, but his deck did something a little more special than Tsapko's. Can you see the extra synergy that would have made 1994 Mike Flores so happy?

Like Tsapko, Remie played [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire), [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand), [Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath), and [Wooded Foothills](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wooded+Foothills) in a RW deck. And like Tsapko, Remie was able to ride the synergy between these cards to put [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) over the top. But unlike Tsapko, Remie shifted his count to *eleven*, playing four copies of both [Wooded Foothills](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wooded+Foothills) and [Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath)—the green-finding ones.

Do you see it yet?

Remie added [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) to the RW creature count!

It's a "red" creature...right?

Right!

Remie didn't play a lot of green cards in his main deck or anything, he just realized that as long as a RW deck *is already willing to play [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire), [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand), [Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath), and [Wooded Foothills](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wooded+Foothills)* (as Tsapko's straight RW was), it might as well get paid off.

So as an *additional* synergy, he added [Stomping Ground](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stomping+Ground) and [Temple Garden](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Temple+Garden) to get an essentially free upgrade to [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape).

The upgrade wasn't 100% free, however. Remie would have to pay 3 life when Tsapko would only have to pay 1 life in some scenarios, but the ability to run the powerful [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) was compelling. [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape) both reinforced the concept of going for a red mana on turn one (as with [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer)) *and* gave Remie [redundancy](/en/articles/makingmagic/2014/03-10-redundancy) on the concept of 2-power one-drop creatures, adding [Kird Ape](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kird+Ape)'s 2 power to [Savannah Lions](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Savannah+Lions)'s.

Of course, once you have a little green mana (Remie's access to those two nonbasic Forests actually counted thirteen of his actual lands), you might as well run a sideboard card like [Unflinching Courage](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unflinching+Courage) [Armadillo Cloak](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Armadillo+Cloak).

I hope you can see that [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) is an outstanding card that gets online much faster when played with [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire)/[Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand)/[Windswept Heath](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Windswept+Heath)/[Wooded Foothills](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wooded+Foothills). For example:

> **Remie:** [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire) (pay 1) → Mountain, [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer).  
> **Opponent:** Forest, [Llanowar Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Llanowar+Elves)  
> **Remie:** [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand) (pay 1) → (tapped) [Sacred Foundry](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sacred+Foundry); tap Mountain for ![](https://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/red.gif), exile [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire) and [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand) from the game, tap [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) to deal 2 to [Llanowar Elves](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Llanowar+Elves).

All of [Bloodstained Mire](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bloodstained+Mire), [Flooded Strand](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flooded+Strand), and [Grim Lavamancer](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grim+Lavamancer) are fine cards, but playing them together allows Remie a "free [Shock](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shock)" by turn two, and, in this hypothetical, a clear route to [card advantage](/en/articles/makingmagic/2014/02-10-card-advantage).

[Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) was a contributor to this PTQ Top 8 deck. It was fine in the way that self-contained card-advantage machine for two mana (that could, separately, counter an opposing [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom)) is fine.

But the dominant [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) deck of the era was Faeries.

[Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) was "fine" in my deck, but in Faeries, [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) rode a variety of tribal synergies to over-perform. To wit:

*   **2/2-making machine:** With the help of [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona), [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom)'s tokens would be free 2/2 creatures instead of free 1/1 creatures.
*   **[Volcanic Fallout](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Fallout) insurance:** [Volcanic Fallout](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Fallout) was a bane not just to Faeries in general (a deck made up mostly of small creatures), but a well-placed [Volcanic Fallout](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Fallout) could kill a [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) in a humiliating fashion. Kill all the Faeries, and [champion](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&text=+%5BChampion%5D&format=+%5B%22Lorwyn-Shadowmoor+Block%22%5D) will fail. Well, not with [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) on the battlefield! [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) itself was an enchantment, and therefore would not be destroyed by [Volcanic Fallout](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Volcanic+Fallout); so [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) would still be left with a Faerie (the enchantment itself) to champion.
*   **Permission setup:** Invest two mana in a [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) on turn two and you'll never have to spend mana on your turn again! The Faeries deck could rely on that initial two-mana investment to produce an army, leaving mana open for [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command), or [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite).

[Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom): Good in a black-red deck, but a centerpiece of over-performance in a blue-black one!

We started this article with a familiar scenario for anyone playing Standard two years ago. [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) blind-flipping on a turn-two [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak). [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) has been a staple in basically any Standard it has been legal; [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) was, surprisingly, not an initially popular card.

[Insectile Aberration](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insectile+Aberration), the flip side of [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?&name=Delver%2Bof%2BSecrets), would get along great in Mono-Blue Devotion! As a cheap evasion creature, it has a great deal of synergy with [Bident of Thassa](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bident+of+Thassa), and seems redundant to one-drops like [Judge's Familiar](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Judge%27s+Familiar) and [Cloudfin Raptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudfin+Raptor).

But [Insectile Aberration](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insectile+Aberration) would basically never show up. With only two copies of [Cyclonic Rift](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cyclonic+Rift) and one copy of [Disperse](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Disperse) for instants (no sorceries whatsoever), [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) just wouldn't have any buddies to help make [Insectile Aberration](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Insectile+Aberration) appear! A 1/1 for one that never becomes a 3/2 flier just doesn't have synergy with the rest of Mono-Blue Devotion.

But in a deck that was already happy to play [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), [Dismember](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dismember), and [Vapor Snag](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vapor+Snag)—more than one-third instants and sorceries, including the reveal-fixing [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder)—[Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) had an irresistible upside.

It is important to differentiate synergy—essentially "playable" cards and tools over-performing when played together—from actual *combinations* of cards. [Delver of Secrets](https://web.archive.org/web/20140616000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Delver+of+Secrets) revealing Mana Leak is one thing...but an actual two-card combo is something else entirely.
