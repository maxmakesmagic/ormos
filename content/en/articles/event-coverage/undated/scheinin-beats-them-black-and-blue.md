## Grand Prix Barcelona 2011

![](https://i.ormos.uk/da/b3/dab3668430f08db27a3a697799411b4a005315ea4bca79413e88658201a8c98d.jpg)

 

Grand Prix Barcelona is at an end, and what a tournament it has been. 1,201 players started out on Saturday, and after 18 rounds of play, it was Spaniard Martin Scheinin who emerged victorious, over his countryman Toni Ramis Pascual in a lopsided final where Scheinin's blue/black control deck maintained a firm enough grip on things that it proved a shock that Pascual, in his first ever Grand Prix, won an extraordinary game two.

While the field was littered with variations on the Caw-Blade archetype, the top eight showed that there are plenty of ways to attack the format, with a variety of decks represented, both aggro and control. Given some of the explosive [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) turns seen in the elimination stage of the event, one could almost call some of the decks combo decks.

As the sun goes down on the Spanish leg of the Grand Prix circuit, we look forward to Grand Prix Dallas Fort Worth, which will also be in the Standard format. We've seen a few new archetypes and variations on existing builds here in Spain, and it will be exciting to see how the metagame adapts as a result.

For now though, the fiesta will go long into the night, as we celebrate our victor for Grand Prix Barcelona 2011, Martin Scheinin.

## Quarterfinals - Toni Ramis Pascual vs Guillaume Wafo-Tapa

*by Tim Willoughby*

At Grand Prix Barcelona, as with at each tournament that Guillaume Wafo-Tapa has shown up at, the little Frenchman has coolly and calmly gone about the business of winning. There is little fanfare about Wafo-Tapa's persona, but that doesn't make him any less dangerous a player, especially when equipped with a blue/black control deck – his weapon of choice for this tournament.

Guillaume's opponent in the quarterfinals is Toni Ramis Pascual. The young Spaniard is equipped with Valakut, a deck against which Wafo-Tapa confided that he felt he had the edge. That said, he was still wary of the powerful starts that the deck can have, and knew that his priority had to be countering the early mana ramp that Valakut can present.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/91/bd/91bd4fbc39a0e98a9204c2fb438fc4264f3fb1b17f6ef25a9e765470b1986b8d.jpg)

*Toni Ramis Pascual*

Ramis Pascual won the roll, meaning that he had the potential to sneak at least some mana ramping past the Pro Tour Yokohama champion. His turn one [Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Valakut%2C+the+Molten+Pinnacle) was followed by a [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest) to enable [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra). As acceleration goes, the snake is among the most powerful of options, but also the most fragile – a point evidenced by the casual way in which Wafo-Tapa killed it off with a [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade).

Toni's next play was a Khalni Heart [Exploration](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Exploration), followed by a [Terramorphic Expanse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terramorphic+Expanse). It looked that Wafo-Tapa would not be able to cut Ramis Pascual off green mana (one of the ways that Valakut decks can be vulnerable), so Wafo-Tapa cast [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) on Valakut itself, making the business of winning a little more complicated.

Toni was not without big plans though. He cast a [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra), followed by a [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow), sacrificing his enchanted Valakut to fetch two copies of [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain). Next Khalni Heart [Exploration](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Exploration) got more lands, and [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) enabled a [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan). The lands it fetched (two copies of Valakut) in turn allowed an [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore). To finish off his very good turn, Toni played a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), and hit Wafo-Tapa for six. None too shabby.

Wafo Tapa's fight back began with a [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) on Valakut, and then a second, leaving Toni without any active copies of the powerful land. He did have 8 power of creatures to attack with though, and on attacks fetched up his final Valakut, and a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) for 3 more damage. A [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) finished things.

Toni Ramis Pascual 1, Guillaume Wafo-Tapa 0

After a lightning fast game one, there was plenty of time for sideboarding. Guillaume, on the play and knowing that this game could be his last of the tournament, led off with a [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit). He allowed an [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement) from Ramis Pascual on turn two, and played a timely [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) to remove one of two copies of [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap) from the Spaniard's hand. Once that was gone there were two more copies of [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement), and two fetchlands left. One way or another, it seemed likely that Ramis Pascual would have plenty of mana available to him.

Toni only played one of his walls the following turn, content to sit back with mana up. This is what Guillaume did as well, but without playing a land it was not clear that Wafo-Tapa's reasoning wasn't simply that he didn't have a play.

An [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore) from Ramis Pascual was followed by [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement). This still left mana for an [Acidic Slime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Acidic+Slime), which threatened to leave Wafo-Tapa high and dry. He had to counter, and did so with a [Flashfreeze](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flashfreeze), turning on Ramis Pascual's [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap). The trap found [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan). To say that Wafo-Tapa was in bad shape would have been an embarrassing level of understatement. A [Doom Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doom+Blade) despatched [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan), but not before it had fetched up Valakut and a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), meaning that Toni had four mountains in total.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b0/60/b06049a362251bd33da2ad2ad34fdef2b2f8d193e6fb87a2483175d7994e58f9.jpg)

*Guillaume Wafo-Tapa*

Wafo-Tapa used [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) on Valakut. After a little thought he cast [Jace, the Mind Sculptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+the+Mind+Sculptor), leaving one [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit) up. He used Jace to fateseal his opponent. Seeing an [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan), he barely flinched, thinking a little before putting it on the bottom of his opponent's deck.

Toni couldn't help but laugh when he drew his card. A [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan) was an ideal draw, and found two copies of [Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Valakut%2C+the+Molten+Pinnacle). Wafo-Tapa used Jace to bounce the titan, suggesting he had a counter ready for its return visit. This proved to be the case, as [Flashfreeze](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flashfreeze) despatched the Titan. Unfortunately for Wafo-Tapa though, it seemed that Ramis Pascual was having the draw of his life, as he cast a [Green Sun's Zenith](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Green+Sun%27s+Zenith) for six off his [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement) mana factory, getting a [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan) that way. His active Valakuts left the Frenchman very close to being knocked out of the tournament. A land would mean six damage. A spell would almost certainly mean similar amounts if not more. An attack step would do it too. Wafo-Tapa dug as best he could with Jace and [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain), but it was to no avail. He extended his hand, and the Spanish crowd went wild. A Spaniard would be in the semi-finals.

Toni Ramis Pascual wins, advancing to the semi-finals!

 

  
 

 

## Quarterfinals – Eduardo Sajgalik versus Jonas Köstler

*by Rich Hagon*

It's fair to say we're going to see some [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest)s and [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain)s in this match. Sajgalik is playing Valakut, while Köstler adds blue to be Red-Blue-Green, or RUG.

Sajgalik won the die roll, and opened with a mulligan, not surprisingly given the three Titans that sat in his opening grip. Six cards were every bit as poor, and Köstler was looking a strong favorite even before picking up his seven. Two words – Gabriel Nassif.

That's the last guy who memorably went to four cards on the play before pulling out a stunning victory, on that occasion against Patrick Chapin in the semi-final of Worlds in 2007.

## Sunday, 6:30 p.m. – Cards of the weekend

*by Tim Willoughby*

Perhaps you haven't had a chance to enjoy all the sparkling prose of myself and my esteemed colleagues on the coverage team this weekend. We won't judge you. That's what judges are for. We'll simply reiterate some of the key stories, so that if you do want to catch up, you can do so at speed. Simply put, here are some of the cards that have made this weekend at Grand Prix Barcelona what it is.

 

![Lotus Cobra](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Lotus%20Cobra&options=)

 

It seems that just about every story that has started 'you'll never guess what I just saw' from this weekend has related to a powerful start from [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra). Full four of the top eight decks are running the little snake from Zendikar, and if it survives on turn two, it enables some pretty incredible sequences of plays.

Richard Bland thought he'd had a good one with a turn three [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan). The top seed in the Swiss got beaten though by another top 8 competitor – Eduardo Sajgalik. Having started turn three with just the snake and a couple of lands in play, Sajgalik managed to finish up with both a [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan) and an [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan) in play, along with a pair of [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra)s, and a whole mess of lands.

Even when **Magic**al Christmas land is not happening, [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) is a valuable addition to both RUG and Valakut, as incidental acceleration that can attack in the meantime. Its interaction with fetchlands and with [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore) makes is a painless addition that can bring a lot of pain to opponents

 

![Inferno Titan](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Inferno%20Titan&options=)

 

In a metagame sporting a large amount of 1/1 and 1/2 creatures, there is a lot to be said for [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan), who can single handedly turn around games like no other. When there are creatures to kill, he'll happily do so. When there aren't creatures to kill, he represents a terrifyingly fast clock.

There have been rumours around the room about him gaining a [Basilisk Collar](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Basilisk+Collar), and annihilating whole teams. That said, there is also talk about what happens when he gets enchanted with [Corrupted Conscience](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Corrupted+Conscience). Apparently if you play with fire, sometimes you get burned.

 

![Mortarpod](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Mortarpod&options=)

 

While [Sword of Feast and Famine](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sword+of+Feast+and+Famine) is clearly very powerful indeed, [Mortarpod](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mortarpod) has impressed me in its versatility and strength this weekend. It allows [Stoneforge Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoneforge+Mystic) to fetch removal when removal is needed, to fetch a blocker when a blocker is needed, and even sometimes (just sometimes) to make your [Squadron Hawk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Squadron+Hawk) just a tiny bit bigger than opposing [Squadron Hawk](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Squadron+Hawk)s.

I think that the interaction that impressed me most with [Mortarpod](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mortarpod) is with [Sun Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sun+Titan). [Sun Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sun+Titan) shows up in some Caw-Blade decks, along with the mono-white control deck featured today, and in Simon Bertiou's innovative UBW Tezzeret/Caw-Blade deck that is in the top eight. Between [Mortarpod](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mortarpod) and [Sun Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sun+Titan), coming into play abilities can be used again and again, while problematic creatures can be dealt with cleanly and easily. Not the most powerful card of the weekend, but one of the ones with the most utility.

 

![Inquisition of Kozilek](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Inquisition%20of%20Kozilek&options=)

 

The latest iteration of the [Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress)/[Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) school of targeted discard is a very powerful element of just about any deck running black, be it poison, Dark Blade, Vampires or traditional blue/black control. As a turn one play, it provides a huge amount of information, and can frequently turn a risky keep into a bad one. Against [Stoneforge Mystic](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoneforge+Mystic) decks, it can break the matchup wide open either by pre-emptively stopping the Mystic, or taking the equipment that a turn two Mystic has fetched.

While the Spanish [Inquisition](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition) was not particularly surprising, but that didn't stop it being powerful, in spite of the fact that this is a format where most decks have some pretty terrifying expensive spells.

 

![Creeping Tar Pit](http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?size=small&type=card&name=Creeping%20Tar%20Pit&options=)

 

Half of the top eight here in Barcelona is packing a full set of copies of [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit). Being able to have lands that function as 'spells' is always a good way for any deck to get extra value, and the blue/black decks have a dangerous weapon in the pit. Fixing mana is just the beginning for this man-land, which can consistently attack planeswalkers without fear of being blocked, and can pick up and wield [Sword of Feast and Famine](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sword+of+Feast+and+Famine) very effectively indeed. Not only is it cheaper to use than [Celestial Colonnade](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Celestial+Colonnade), its synergy with the sword goes further, as it will always get through blockers, ensuring that the sword triggers to untap lands.

The top eight is still going on here in Barcelona, but suffice to say that without these, my top five, it really wouldn't have been the same event.

 

  
 

 

## Semifinals – Eduardo Sajgalik versus Toni Ramis Pascual

*by Rich Hagon*

The Spaniard Pascual has made it thus far with just a single Bye to his name, and at his very first Grand Prix attempt. Sajgalik, meanwhile, has been around the block a few times, having dual French-Canadian nationality, played for the Canadian team at Worlds, and twice reaching the top 8 of Great Britain Nationals, where he currently lives.

Pascual opened with Valakut, indicating a mirror match, with Sajgalik going with [Terramorphic Expanse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terramorphic+Expanse). The first of what could be many fetchlands, a [Misty Rainforest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Misty+Rainforest), saw Pascual get both colors of mana, with the optimal turn two [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) moments away. [Terramorphic Expanse](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Terramorphic+Expanse) became [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest) for Sajgalik. [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement) was his French/Canadian/British response on turn two.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c3/d5/c3d563142b32ff81808b10a533c3c5e1de2578567ba76a4cee30289f2f357c59.jpg)

*Toni Ramis Pascual*

Pascual had the first go at an explosive turn three, with a second [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra), a Valakut, an [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore), a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), and [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement). It was a big turn, but not crippling for Sajgalik. [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) found him a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) and a [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest), replacing the green source he'd sacrificed as part of the the [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) cost, leaving him three mana to play with. ## Semifinal – Richard Bland vs. Martin Scheinin

*by Tobi Henke*

England's Richard Bland has made his second Grand Prix Top 8 here in Barcelona and was looking to improve on his previous finish—third at GP Madrid last year, which was exactly the same spot he would end up in with another semfinal loss today. His opponent, Martin Scheinin from Spain came close to the Top 8 before, but never made it this far. Now he was playing with the finals on the line.

Bland won the die-roll and both players kept their opening seven. Bland's didn't include the signature turn-two play of RUG, [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra), but it did have [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain) which simply found one. Scheinin had an [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island) and [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain) himself.

The Cobra was summoned, but didn't do anything just yet. On turn three Bland played a land, attacked, and simply passed back to Scheinin. Over the next couple of turns, Bland developed his mana and hand further with [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore) and two [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain)s, but he was in no hurry to cast an actual threat, as his opponent was stuck on two [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island)s and two [Tectonic Edge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tectonic+Edge)s as his only lands. When Scheinin discarded [Grave Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grave+Titan) at the end of his turn, Bland knew he was in a pretty good position. He tried for [Jace, the Mind Sculptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+the+Mind+Sculptor) but lost it to two [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak)s.

Scheinin finally drew a [Swamp](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Swamp) and peeked at Bland's hand with [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek). He saw two [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan)s, [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak), and a [Lightning Bolt](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lightning+Bolt) which he took away. Then he tapped out for [Jace, the Mind Sculptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+the+Mind+Sculptor) and used the Planeswalker's [Brainstorm](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Brainstorm) ability, clearly not afraid of the [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan) that was sure to enter the battlefield on Bland's next turn. It did and Jace died. Scheinin untapped, killed the Titan via [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat), played a [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit), and once again tapped out for Jace, this time for [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren).

All this time, [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) had slowly depleted Scheinin's life, who was down to 9. Bland's second [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan) made that 6, while the Cobra attacked [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren). Now the game was close—either to its end or to a turnaround in Scheinin's favor. The Spaniard summoned [Wurmcoil Engine](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wurmcoil+Engine). The crowd held its breath ...

## The Final – Martin Scheinin versus Toni Ramis Pascual

*by Rich Hagon*

Spain v Spain. UB versus Valakut. Who will prevail?

With the winning die roll, Scheinin began with [Drowned Catacomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drowned+Catacomb)s, with [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek) revealing two [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap), [Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Valakut%2C+the+Molten+Pinnacle), [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow), [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra), and [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore). The [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) hit the graveyard, with [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit) completing turn two for Scheinin. He knew [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) would arrive for Pascual, and it did. Scheinin was ready with [Black Sun's Zenith](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Black+Sun%27s+Zenith) for one, shuffling it back in, as he must.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/64/3d/643d2a0483e117099659c736caeebc95dc4d116d8d78f7682c15ea5b49f46cb9.jpg)

*A crowd watches the final game.*

The [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore), which again Scheinin knew about, led to [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) and Valakut from Pascual, but that was all. With double [Tectonic Edge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tectonic+Edge) now among his four lands, it looked like the early game was belonging to Scheinin, a thought compounded when he cast [Jace, the Mind Sculptor](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace%2C+the+Mind+Sculptor) on turn four. Straight into Fateseal mode went the Planeswalker, and Pascual, who had made his way to the final off just a single Bye at this, his very first Grand Prix, was in trouble.

[Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) found him a pair of [Forest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest)s, before he laid [Khalni Heart Expedition](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Khalni+Heart+Expedition). Not exactly stellar. [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) denied Pascual a second green source, as Jace continued his work, Fatesealing a card to the bottom. [Tectonic Edge](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tectonic+Edge) killed Valakut before it could do anything nasty, although that seemed a long way off. Up Jace went again, still with Pascual seeing nothing good, despite Scheinin sending cards to the bottom, meaning Pascual at least had a shot at something decent.

[Duress](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Duress) showed [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap), [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap), and [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan), with a Trap heading for the graveyard.

Jace to eleven, and this time the top card could stay right there.

[Tumble Magnet](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Tumble+Magnet) joined the Scheinin side, leaving Pascual even fewer meaningful options. [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement) certainly wasn't an issue.

Jace to thirteen – now that's Loyal. [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat) killed off [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement), returning Pascual to just one green source, and that was that.

Scheinin 1 – 0 Pascual.

After an opening land from Pascual, Scheinin had the turn one [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek), which is strange, because it's what I expected, and apparently no-one expects the Spanish [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek). Pascual revealed [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra),two [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement), [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap), [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore), and a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), with one of the [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement)s vanishing.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/36/05/360592fe7d1b1a55dd41b7bd8232987112d41873abbb4a0c86896f8cf3f1f1c4.jpg)

*Sheinin Happy People.*

Pascual got [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) into play, but found [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) waiting for his attempted [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore). Still, he had Valakut in play, a Cobra in play, and that was already more than he'd essentially accomplished in the whole of game one. He offered [Explore](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Explore) again on the following turn, with Scheinin using [Stoic Rebuttal](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stoic+Rebuttal) to say no for a second time. [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) attacked, followed by a land and [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement) to end Pascual's turn.

At four mana, Scheinin cast [Memoricide](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Memoricide), naming [Primeval Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Primeval+Titan). Away went the giants of the Valakut deck, leaving Pascual significantly lower on potential threats. His [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra) attacked once more, but all the body language for Pascual was of disaster waiting to happen.

With a resigned shrug he reached six mana, and cast [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap) for full price, knowing that he was unlikely to hit anything significant.

[Acidic Slime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Acidic+Slime) at least meant he didn't miss entirely, destroying Scheinin's [Drowned Catacomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Drowned+Catacomb)s.

Although Pascual had a Valakut in play, Scheinin used [Spreading Seas](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spreading+Seas) to 'turn off' a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain), leaving Pascual back at zero in that department. [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat) killed [Lotus Cobra](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Lotus+Cobra), with Scheinin still at twelve life.

[Acidic Slime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Acidic+Slime) continued to fight the good fight for Pascual, dropping Scheinin to ten, who found his first [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain) of the game.

[Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek) revealed only a [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain) in Pascual's hand, so it was back to [Acidic Slime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Acidic+Slime) beatdown. [Green Sun's Zenith](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Green+Sun%27s+Zenith) for five resolved, much to Pascual's apparent surprise, allowing him to serve up a fresh [Acidic Slime](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Acidic+Slime) – if Slimes that are Acidic could ever be said to be fresh. Another Scheinin land bit the dust, with him falling to seven as he sacrificed [Marsh Flats](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Marsh+Flats). After a horrible, horrible game, Pascual was now just two turns from sending the final into a decider.

Scheinin simply passed. Seriously, was Pascual about to pull this one out of the fire? [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat) stemmed some of the bleeding, but Scheinin was still at five. [Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain) sent him in search of more goodness, which his deck was failing to deliver. Two more land stared back at him.

In came the Slime, dropping Scheinin to three. And...

INFERNO TITAN!

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b5/24/b5246a46066c88e4b3d25b41a86a6ca0e4187353685130f42f47c5ec1a4a3a32.jpg)

*Toni looking laid back before the crowd.*

Crazy game, **Magic**. Just crazy.

Scheinin 1 – 1 Pascual

[Preordain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Preordain) began the final duel of Grand Prix Barcelona 2011. [Evolving Wilds](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Evolving+Wilds) replied for Pascual, who spent an age over his mulligan choice.

That choice was made clearer by [Inquisition of Kozilek](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inquisition+of+Kozilek) from Scheinin, revealing [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow), Summoning trap, [Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement), [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan), two [Mountain](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mountain)s, and a [Misty Rainforest](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Misty+Rainforest). The [Harrow](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Harrow) duly disappeared.

[Overgrown Battlement](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Overgrown+Battlement) arrived on schedule turn two, but for the third game running this was no explosive Valakut start. [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat) added to Pascual's problems. End of turn three: three basic lands. Not the ideal Valakut start. [Khalni Heart Expedition](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Khalni+Heart+Expedition) was met with [Mana Leak](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Leak) from Scheinin, who seemed under immense pressure, with at least one hundred spectators crowding round the one remaining table.

At five mana, he elected to turn [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit) 'live', and begin the beats. Pascual, meanwhile, could lay nothing but land. At six mana, Scheinin had something more exciting to do with his mana than activate his [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit) – [Grave Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Grave+Titan), plus friends.Pascual had a tiny window, with Scheinin tapped out. Could he do anything relevant with that slim chance?

Pascual: sixth land, [Inferno Titan](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inferno+Titan), kill a Zombie, and one to Scheinin.

Pretty good.

Scheinin: [Go for the Throat](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Go+for+the+Throat) on Pascual's Titan. Activate [Creeping Tar Pit](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Creeping+Tar+Pit). Attack for eleven, make two Zombies.

Really good.

Pascual passed the turn, and so began what seemed certain to be the final act of the tournament. In came the team, Pascual revealed a lonely [Summoning Trap](https://web.archive.org/web/20220808192759/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Summoning+Trap) which could do nothing, and Spain had a new Champion.

Martin Scheinin 2 – 1 Toni Ramis Pascual

Congratulations to Martin Scheinin, Champion of Grand Prix Barcelona 2011!
