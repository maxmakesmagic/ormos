It's 2007, and I've just learned how to play *Magic*. I ask my friend what I should buy first, and he asks me what colors I like. I say, "I don't know, red and blue?"

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Stitch+in+Time)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stitch+in+Time)

It's 2019, and I'm a contractor for Wizards of the Coast. I send an email to Blake Rasmussen, Editor-in-Chief of DailyMTG. I ask him if I can preview a weird card from *War of the Spark* on Twitter. He responds, "Would you like to write a weird article instead?"

I'm Chris Mooney, and I love Weirds! Join me today as (in true Izzet fashion) we jump forward, backward, and sideways through time to learn about *Magic*'s weirdest creatures.

* * *

It's 2006, and *Guildpact* has just been released. Players around the world are being introduced to the haunting Orzhov, the bloodthirsty Gruul, and the . . . replicating . . . Izzet. Among the cool and quirky creatures of the Izzet league are Goblins, Faeries, Giants, Dragons, and . . . Weirds?

What the heck is a Weird?

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Steamcore+Weird)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steamcore+Weird)

A Weird is an Izzet creature created by combining two (or more) elementals into one being. For instance, our friend [Steamcore Weird](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Steamcore+Weird) is a fire elemental encased inside a water elemental. Weirds are wild contradictions that somehow manage to work, which, to be honest, is how I feel about myself a lot of the time.

Weirds represent the core of what I love about the Izzet: they're deterministically unpredictable, made both by logic and in spite of it, a living embodiment of creativity. Plus, they've got sweet art!

* * *

It's 2018, and I've just been told I made it to the [Top 8 of *Great Designer Search 3*](/en/articles/feature/2018/03-09-great-designer-search-3-meet-the-top-8). I need to choose a piece of card art to represent myself. I ask all my friends to pick the card that they most associate with me. Every single one of them says [Gelectrode](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gelectrode).

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Gelectrode)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gelectrode)

[Gelectrode](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gelectrode) was an all-star of my first deck, a *Guildpact* theme deck named "[Izzet Gizmometry](https://web.archive.org/web/20191121065322/http://www.wizards.com/magic/displaythemedeck.asp?set=guildpact&decknum=3&lang=en)." I owe a lot to [Gelectrode](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gelectrode). It's the card that helped me learn how the stack works. It's the card that taught me the power of building a deck around a specific theme like instants and sorceries. And, perhaps most importantly of all, it established that *Magic* card names could be puns.

Honestly, there was no turning back after that last part.

* * *

It's 2005, and artist [Hideaki Takamura](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoiler&method=visual&action=advanced&artist=%5b%22Hideaki+Takamura%22%5d) turns in the final piece of art for the card [Thunderheads](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thunderheads). He also turns in art for a matching Weird token.

[Thunderheads](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thunderheads) gets printed the following year. The token won't be printed for another thirteen.

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&multiverseid=96946)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=96946)

![MTGO Weird Token](https://i.ormos.uk/ff/7d/ff7df7864627e15b4a77d691359428e87d974e7e82509d5d66fb6b555cf93f96.jpg)

You see, back in 2005, creature tokens weren't included in booster packs like they are today. For over a decade, the only way to see Takamura's awesome Weird token art was on *Magic Online*.

These weird tokens lived online for many years, seen only by the most diehard of Weird fans (like me) and blue *Guildpact* drafters desperate for a 23rd card (also me). I dreamt of owning a real copy of this token, but I had pretty much written it off as impossible. After all, what kind of product would want to reprint [Thunderheads](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thunderheads)?

* * *

It's 2018, and the Guild Kits arrive to answer my prayers! These preconstructed decks included cards from all three Ravnica sets, as well as matching tokens. Now, thirteen years later, the wonder of these physical Weird tokens are finally available for us Weird fans to behold (and, you know, actually hold).

![Weird Token](https://media.wizards.com/2019/images/daily/en_A72EqLcaDu.png)

* * *

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Frostburn+Weird)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frostburn+Weird)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Blistercoil+Weird)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blistercoil+Weird)

It's 2012, and *Return to Ravnica* also brought us a return to Weirds! This time, the Weirds came as a vertical cycle of hybrid creatures. At common, we had [Frostburn Weird](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Frostburn+Weird), which was a solid card in RTR Draft with a lot of flexibility. At uncommon, we had [Blistercoil Weird](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Blistercoil+Weird), which was part of this set's uncommon one-drop hybrid cycle alongside some more memorable cards like [Dryad Militant](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Dryad+Militant) and [Rakdos Cackler](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rakdos+Cackler). And at rare, we had [Nivmagus Elemental](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nivmagus+Elemental).

Wait . . . Elemental?!

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Nivmagus+Elemental)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nivmagus+Elemental)

Why, that's no Weird at all! What gives? Did the Izzet not have another elemental on hand they could fuse with this one before it escaped?

The purpose of [Nivmagus Elemental](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nivmagus+Elemental)'s non-Weirdness has haunted me for years. My only solace comes from the fact that [Nivmagus Elemental](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nivmagus+Elemental)'s Elemental-ness did actually matter when, later that year, Team StarCityGames tutored it up with [Flamekin Harbinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flamekin+Harbinger) in the Modern Pro Tour.

* * *

It's 2019, and I'm still confused by [Petrahydrox](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Petrahydrox).

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Petrahydrox)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Petrahydrox)

* * *

It's 2013, and the world gets to meet the first-ever legendary Weird, Melek!

In the story of *Dragon's Maze*, Melek was created to be the Izzet's maze runner. Unfortunately, he didn't have much of a chance to show off his skills, since [Ral Zarek](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ral+Zarek) sabotaged Melek and took his place in the race. But Melek has lived on in Commander, where his build-around potential can really be maximized. But I'm sure that one day we'll have enough Weird creatures to build an entire Weird-themed Commander deck, and Melek will be ready to go! (And yes, I've already tried to do this. No, it did not work.)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&multiverseid=369062)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=369062)

*Dragon's Maze* also brought us two other Weird cards. One is [Fluxcharger](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fluxcharger), but the other is a bit harder to find.

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&name=Fluxcharger)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Fluxcharger)

[![](https://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&multiverseid=369080)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=369080)

That's right, the Turn half of [Turn // Burn](https://web.archive.org/web/20190404000000/https://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Turn+%2F%2F+Burn) transforms a creature into a Weird! I enjoy how the fuse mechanic connects to the Weirds' identity of being multiple elementals fused together.

* * *

It's 2019, and you open a Weird creature in your *War of the Spark* booster pack. Wait a minute, I think we went a little too far into the future . . .

And now, without any further ado, please give a very warm, very weird welcome to . . . Spellgorger Weird!

![Spellgorger Weird](https://media.wizards.com/2019/war/en_CGVqJbTWnr.png)

Spellgorger Weird might start out small, but like any good Izzet creation, it can get out of hand pretty quickly. Normally, you'd want to grow your Spellgorger Weird with a healthy diet of instants and sorceries, and maybe a few artifacts and enchantments for flavor. But *War of the Spark* is *not* a normal set, and, personally, I look forward to using my planeswalker cards to give this little guy a boost.

Wait, what's that in the background? Does the flavor text say "two weirds"?

![Spellkeeper Weird](https://media.wizards.com/2019/war/en_V7XJoOWzNZ.png)

That's right, Weird fans! *War of the Spark* brings us not one, but *two* new Weird cards! What Spellkeeper Weird lacks in raw power it makes up for in flexibility. Early in the game, it can help protect you and any of your planeswalker friends. Later on, you can cash it in to get back your most exciting instant or sorcery.

* * *

Weirds will always have a special place in my heart. They're one of *Magic*'s many unique quirks that add to its charm, and I hope that all of you out there will enjoy playing with our two newest friends.
