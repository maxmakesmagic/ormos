### Game 1

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a703.jpg)Lebedowicz kept a hand of double Psychatog, Concentrate and four lands – a mediocre hand in the matchup where you never really want to cast the 'Tog till after Upheaval has resolved, but not bad enough to be thrown away. Thoren's hand consisted of six lands and Innocent Blood, so he was forced to mulligan. The new hand was acceptable, featuring a pair of Swamps, Innocent Blood, Chainer's Edict, Haunting Echoes and Rancid Earth. 

Lebedowicz cast Peek at the end of Thoren's second turn, finding the abovementioned cards as well as Innocent Blood and the third land. On his turn 3, Lebedowicz made a gambit of casting one of his Psychatogs, hoping to get Thoren to cast Innocent Blood instead of Rancid Earth – which would allow him to cast Concentrate on turn 4. Thoren did not bite – he killed a Swamp and proceeded to cast Nantuko Shade and use Chainer's Edict targeting Osyp. Lebedowicz saved his 'Tog with one of the two Aether Bursts in his hand.

Down to thirteen life points from Nantuko Shade, Lebedowicz chose to re-cast Psychatog rather than use Chainer's Edict in his hand. With another Aether Burst in hand, he was hoping to bait out some of Thoren's Innocent Bloods – the only card that could save Thoren if Upheaval/Psychatog combo resolved for Lebedowicz. Of course, Thoren was not about to cast one of those with Nantuko Shade in play. After a second Concentrate, Lebedowicz used Edict to take out the Shade and attacked with Psychatog, removing six cards from his graveyard in order to play around Haunting Echoes he knew were in Thoren's hand.

On his turn, Thoren cast Innocent Blood and then Echoes, with Psychatog being the only card in his opponent's graveyard. Catching the other Psychatogs would pretty much win the game for Thoren by default but, of course, they were in Lebedowicz's hand else he would not have tapped out leaving his only path to victory defenseless. Thoren took out the third Psychatog with Chainer's Edict, and Lebedowicz summoned the fourth, knowing via Peek that his opponent's hand contained Innocent Blood and Rancid Earth. Lebedowicz had a pair of counte via Peek that his opponent's hand contained Innocent Blood and Rancid Earth. Lebedowicz had a pair of counterspells and Upheaval, so barring Thoren draw something very good on the next turn, he should be able to win. 

When Lebedowicz summoned the fourth Psychatog, Thoren opted for Innocent Blood rather than tap out for an Edict. Lebedowicz bounced the 'Tog with Aether Burst and Thoren took out a land, preventing the Upheaval combo for another turn. Thoren attempted a Mutilate a turn later, but it was countered leaving him with no cards in hand. At fifteen life, Thoren was low enough to where Lebedowicz could kill him by discarding and removing all of his cards, after casting the third Concentrate. Thoren conceded in response to Concentrate being placed on the stack.

 Out: 4 Psychatog, 4 Aether Burst, 2 Shadowmage Infiltrator

**Thoren**  
 In: 4 Mesmeric Fiend, 2 Haunting Echoes, 2 Last Rites, 2 Skeletal Scrying, 2 Laquaus' Champion  
 Out: 4 Mutilate, 4 Chainer's Edict, 4 Innocent Blood

### Game 2

Thoren kept a hand of 4 Swamp, Last Rites, Nantuko Shade and Diabolic Tutor – nearly as good a draw as he could have hoped for in the matchup. On the other hand, it was Lebedowicz's turn to take a mulligan and kept a hand of three lands, Circular Logic, Obsessive Search and Zombie Infestation.

Thoren got a turn 2 Shade into play and proceeded to cast Last Rites. As he was ready to pitch the cards, Lebedowicz stopped him. He had three spells in his hand and had an option to discard some of them to Zombie Infestation. Of course, if he did, he Thoren would be likely to discard less cards to Infestation. Lebedowicz chose to pitch two of his spells and Thoren discarded Mind Sludge to get the last remaining non-land card out of his opponent's hand. 

Lebedowicz chump-blocked with the Zombie token next turn and cast Skeletal Scrying for three. Thoren cast another Last Rites, presenting the same dilemma to his opponent as the turn before. This time Lebedowicz had two spells and two lands. He discarded both spells. Thoren pitched a Swamp but was unable to take anything from his opponent's hand. 

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a704.jpg)The next few turns had a distinct feeling of déjà vu to them. Once again, Lebedowicz chump blocked and drew three cards via Scrying. Innocent Blood finally took out the shade, but Toren drew another one, and Lebedowicz cast another Edict. Thoren finally used his Diabolic Tutor. At the end of his turn, Lebedowicz cast Peek to learn that the card the Swede went after was Laquatus' Champion. Down to six life, Lebedowicz would need to find countermagic before his opponent drew his sixth land. To make matters worse for the American, the other card in Thoren's hand was Haunting Echoes. 

Thoren drew and played Mesmeric Fiend, stealing away Predict and leaving Lebedowicz with Obsessive Search – which was enough to find a Syncopate for next turn's Champion. Haunting Echoes resolved though, taking out a substantial chunk of Lebedowicz's library. It was a mixed blessing, since it also ensured that he was much closer to Upheaval – which he had plenty of mana to combo with Zombie Infestation already in play. Of course, he would probably have to top-deck it, as his search spells – Peek, Concentrate, Peek, were leaving the game.

Two turns later, Lebedowicz drew his best card. He attacked with the Zombie. Thoren, who saw the writing on the wall, blocked with his two Mesmeric Fiends (he cast the second one a turn before but found only lands in his opponent's hand. Four Zombie tokens took out Thoren in just 3 turns.

**Sideboarding**

Both players tried to bluff each other out by adding a number of cards from their sideboard to the deck, then taking the same cards out. Neither succeeded at convincing his opponent to switch his strategy for Game 3.

### Game 3

**Thoren's opening hand:**

2 Swamp, 2 Skeletal Scrying, 2 Nantuko Shade, 2 Diabolic Tutor

**Lebedowicz' opening hand:**

Island, Swamp, 2 Darkwater Catacombs, Predict, Chainer's Edict, Innocent Blood

As easily predicted from the players' opening hands, the Shades did not live to attack. Thoren tutored for Mind Sludge, but chose not to cast it against Lebedowicz' four untapped lands. Instead, he cast Skeletal Scrying to draw three cards at the end of his opponent's turn. 

With Cabal Coffers in play, Thoren cast Last Rites with access to five more mana. Lebedowicz could not counter with Circular Logic and chose not to cast Skeletal Scrying to draw up to four more cards. Thoren yanked Logic and Scrying out of his opponent's hand and took the rest of the cards via Mind Sludge. Lebedowicz could not stop Thoren when he cast Skeletal Scrying for five and then used his remaining four mana to cast Diabolic Tutor, searching out Haunting Echoes. On the following turn Thoren cast another Sludge, followed by Haunting Echoes. Although there were no Zombie Infestations in Lebedowicz's graveyard, Upheaval was there and with no cards in hand he had no chance to win that game. Thoren's deck finally showed its true power in this game.

### Game 4

Lebedowicz's opening hand:

4 Islands, Swamp, Darkwater Catacombs

Thoren's opening hand:

3 Swamp, Cabal Coffers, 2 Diabolic Tutor

After taking one mulligan (throwing away a 1 land hand), Lebedowicz chose to keep a very poor hand of all land rather than going down to five cards. Fortunately for him, Thoren also had to take a mulligan, as his hand featured a pair of Cabal Coffers as the only land. 

Lebedowicz peeled a Zombie Infestation off the top, dropping it into play on turn 2 in order to protect it from opponent's discard effects. Thoren cast a turn 3 Mesmeric Fiend but Lebedowicz chose to make a Zombie, pitching a land and his only spell – a Chainer's Edict. Thoren tutored for Nantuko Shade, which would be very good with Cabal Coffers he played next turn. Another Tutor net Thoren a second Nantuko Shade, as the Swede decided to go for an all-out aggressive strategy in this game.

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/ptosa02/a689.jpg)Lebedowicz cast Concentrate and found Chainer's Edict. He cast it, killing a Mesmeric Fiend, with 2 Edicts in the graveyard and enough mana to cast one of them on the following turn. Meanwhile, the Zombie token he made was dealing damage, slowly chipping away at Thoren's life total. 

Thoren top-decked yet another Shade, cast two of them and cast Last Rites, forcing Lebedowicz to make another Zombie and keep just a land in hand. On his turn Lebedowicz tapped out to Edict away one of the shades and chump-blocked the other two with the two Zombie tokens he controlled. Thoren's luck held out as he top-decked his last Shade on the next turn! Lebedowicz failed to draw Upheaval, which was the only possible answer he could have had at this point, sending this exciting match to its final game.

### Game 5

Lebedowicz' opening hand:

3 Island, 2 Peek, Skeletal Scrying, Chainer's Edict.

Thoren's opening hand:

4 Swamp, Skeletal Scrying, Haunting Echoes, Shambling Swarm

Lebedowicz cast two Peeks and a Predict, yet still failed to find a fourth land. When he finally did, after missing a land drop – it was yet another Island. When Thoren cast Haunting Echoes on him, it was almost a favor. Lebedowicz, whose hand featured both Zombie Infestation and Upheaval strongly contemplated letting it resolve. It would make the Circular Logic in his hand temporarily useless, but greatly increase his chances of drawing three more lands he needed to win this game before anything really bad happened.

Despite over ten cards being yanked out of the game, Lebedowitz failed to draw a land for another several turns. Thoren cast Skeletal Scrying for three and used Mind Sludge to take out Lebedowicz' hand. Diabolic Tutor sealed the fate of this game, as Thoren got his second Haunting Echoes to draw a concession and win the match. 

**Final Result:** Thoren 3 – Lebedowicz 2
