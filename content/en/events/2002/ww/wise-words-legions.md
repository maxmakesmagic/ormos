Now that the *Onslaught* Limited review is finally coming to a close, I can finally move on. It’s a good thing too: time was ceasing to exist. I was caught in a vortex of “you must play red or black”, “Sparksmith is so good” and complaints of strewn bodies cast aside by angered pit fighters, Angels, Starstorms and other of the set’s oft-maligned bombs. The world of **Magic** was closing in on itself and I was trapped on the inside. No longer.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b7/8e/b78eeeac205d206f91ee01c7f2929458343f990c2d2d0e3a076951e600530cf9.jpg)Now, I’m finally out, rescued by a 145-strong group of humans, demi-humans, animals and monsters who banded together to provide me new material and with it new thoughts. *Legions* is here, I’ve drafted it day and night for weeks and while my understanding of the set isn’t quite complete, it had better be close; I’m playing a Limited Masters with it in a week.

What follows is a look as some of my favorite and least favorite things about *Legions*, the effect the set has had on the *Onslaught* Block Limited environment and a few conclusions you may not have come to. Maybe this deviation on the *Onslaught* norm for me will provide you a new insight on the set we’ve all been drafting for the last month and a bit. Sorry this took so long, but hey, I had work to do.

### Three Things I Hate About *Legions*

**1) Timberwatch Elf** – The set’s strongest common is once again a dorky little tap creature and after Bane of the Living, T-Elf may be the set’s #2 card (though you could definitely put up arguments for Graveborn Muse or Imperial Hellkite). That it even merits consideration for that distinction feels sort of ridiculous; even Faceless Butcher was further down the list, while Wild Mongrel and Cephalid Looter weren’t even close to the top of the *Odyssey* rankings.

Timberwatch Elf’s strength is derived from a few sources: a brand new selection of bigger, faster elves, the lack of kill in the format and the fact that while untapped, it’s virtually impossible for opposing creatures to attack or block, a massive Pacifism for all living things as it were. Eventually, creatures will start blocking, but only in a damage prevention capacity, sacrificing themselves for the greater good. I hate to break it to you, but the elf is greater still.

**2) There’s Not Enough Red** – Everyone’s opening red cards in *Onslaught* and that means everyone’s drafting them, but after the first two packs, a funny thing happens: there’s not enough to go ‘round in pack three. Red is *Legions’s* weakest color, despite Skirk Marauder’s strength and with at least four red drafters per table, the cards just can’t meet demands. So what do you do about it?

Simple: don’t draft the red card. Green is the strongest color in *Legions*. While it lacks a distinguishable number of first pick caliber cards in *Onslaught*, that means there’s almost too much green come pack three. Sometimes it’ll be worth drafting the weaker cards early on in order to receive the dividends pack three: you’ll understand what I mean the first time you get Timberwatch Elf third.

**3) Mana is Too Important Now** – Simply put, if you don’t play a third land on your third turn, you will not win in this format, barring the casting of a 1/1 goblin with a tap ability or a 1/2 elf that produces mana. Furthermore, failure to play a land in each of your colors by turn five is almost certainly death.

The format, as it stands, with nineteen creatures or more per deck, is so based on getting the head start that a mana induced hiccup turns fatal. What can you do about it? Draft fast creatures, make sure you have enough morphs (6-7) that a three land mana stall can be survived by casting a generic 2/2 every turn and live a good life outside **Magic**: maybe the peace that last one helps you realize will help you deal with the stress and frustration involved with getting run over.

### One Pick that isn’t Close

Infest over Blistering Firecat. Zvi Mowshowitz is one of the best players on the planet, but his draft strategies have always been somewhat controversial. When I read that in the Top 8 of Grand Prix-Boston he’d taken Firecat over Infest, explaining it as a toss up whose decision came down to personal preference, I was shocked.

I like the Firecat more than most, for its mana efficiency, explosiveness and surprise value as a rare morph that will seldom be accounted for in opposing calculations, but it’s not even close to the black spell. With the increase in creatures, non-creature spells stand to gain strength and Infest is one of the best around.

The new environment’s more popular decks include green-red elf, based around the Timberwatch and white-black clerics, whose damage prevention makes it tough to penetrate with the severe lack of kill inherent in *Legions’s* all-creature nature and both are pretty much torn to shreds by giving all creatures –2/-2, but that’s just the bonus. The simple fact is that the set is based around the generic 2/2 for three mana. Simple fact #2 is that hordes of those guys die in an instant and you can hold yours back until after they do.

**1) Crown of Fury** – Cast on Krosan Vorine, the Crown transforms the beast into a black Legends enchant world. Each turn, you choose a creature you don’t like and attack, killing it. Do this two or three times you’re likely to win the game: after that it’s just fun.

This isn’t to say that the Vorine’s the only target. Crested Craghorn, while still easy to kill, gives you a ton of first strike damage your opponent has to try to stop before their army is whittled away and even the small provoke creatures become machine-like once Crowned. Throw in the little bonus of the more tribally-aligned *On-On-Lg* draft environment making the sacrifice ability a little stronger and this thing is very playable now.

**2) Threaten** – How cool is this card now? Before, you had the Husk trick, but other than that, there weren’t too many ways of abusing it, but now… well, think about it. You Threaten their provoker, attack, force them to block and kill both creatures. Corpse Harvester, Drinker of Sorrow, Skirk Alarmist and host of tribal-oriented creatures allow for the sacrifice of the controlled creature, so your opponent doesn’t have to have a creature to take real advantage of Threaten: think of the opposing provoke creature as a bonus.

  

### Three *Legions* Morphs that are Better than You Might Think

**1) Daru Mender** – Okay, take a second look at this card. **3) Defender of the Order** – I’d guess people generally recognize the Defender as good, but I don’t think its really getting the credit it deserves. ### Skinthinner, Corpse Harvester or Noxious Ghoul?

Thus far, this seems to be the most-asked question inspired by *Legions* drafting. The answer isn’t straightforward, but let me try to provide a guide to the individual choices you’ll have to make.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td><td></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td><td><a><img alt="Noxious Ghoul" src="https://i.ormos.uk/32/c8/32c860b222a25202a0650e42fe7744450be51c2e1f8b6ab4a87097e631667381.jpg"></a></td><td></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

The Ghoul is the most powerful of the three cards, ridding the world of those annoying Sparksmiths and Wellwishers, at the same time offering the possibility of a selective Infest of sorts, killing all the little non-zombies of the world with the casting of two zombies in a turn. Of course, that means you need two zombies to cast, which means you’re looking at a lot of zombies in your deck. If it’s early in the pack and you’re choosing between Ghoul and Skinthinner, I’d guess you want a minimum of four maindeck zombies to that point to make the Ghoul over Thinner pick.

As for Skinthinner vs. Corpse Harvester, the big problem with the very powerful Harvester is that it’s slow and on top of that, you really want to have a decent number of good targets, so generally, I’d be picking that first ‘Thinner over the Harvester. Once you have Skinthinner though, Harvester is definitely the pick, as you’ll essentially have the same chances of drawing a Skinthinner, as you would with two in your deck and the Harvester can lead to so much more. My favorite Harvester targets are Festering Goblin, which you should then sacrifice on the next turn to kill their little guy and grab some additional card advantage in the form of that searched-for swamp and Smokespew Invoker, for whom the Harvester provides the mana needed for true effectiveness.

Akroma. Don’t get me wrong, we all like big women, but this one’s high maintenance and frankly, that’s something I could do without. Yes, Akroma is bomby and will totally dominate the table once there, but her mana requirements are so extraordinary that she loses out to a fairer maiden.

Windborn Muse looks innocent enough, but with tempo being such a big deal and every mana valuable as a result, her Propaganda effect as a throw-in on a 2/3 flying body for the same cost as Dive Bomber makes her a deceptive powerhouse. If you see Akroma, you’re going to want to take her over any other white card you see, but the Muse is the babe you want to open.

**2) Sunstrike Legionnaire** – It looks like an inconsistent Whipcorder, but in actuality, sometimes the Legionnaire does twice the job, especially now that around half the cards in your (yours and your opponent’s) two decks combined are going to be creatures. Best in a super-aggressive deck, the Legionnaire can also serve capably in a preventative capacity while you get your team set up.
