### U.S. Regionals

![](http://www.wizards.com/regionals/images/regionals.gif)Much like last year, U.S. Regionals did not disappoint with the turnout. Ohio Valley, usually the leader when it comes to attendance, had to accommodate nearly 700 players. North East and New England - some of the most important regions when it comes to the metagame - boasted just over 300 competitors each. As of the time this article is being written, about half of the tournaments results have made it to *The Sideboard* staff and are posted [here](/en/events/2002/2002regionals/u-s-and-canada-regionals-decklists). More results will be posted as they arrive from the tournament organizers.

The surprising victor in the metagame wars was red-green. Despite the high numbers of this deck in the Top 8, I do not believe it to be the best deck in the format. Rather, I attribute its success to several factors - it is easy to play, and an above-average number of players showed up with it at Regionals - so it was inevitable that some players broke through. However, the archetype also cannot be discounted totally - it did, after all, hold up in New York and Chicago. 

The second most popular deck was Psychatog - this time deservedly so. A difficult archetype to play, it did not show up in high numbers but it was used by many of the more experienced players and for many of them the choice did not disappoint. 

Blue-red-green made a comeback this weekend. Most of these decks were not Snaketongue variants. Rather, they heavily utilized the madness mechanic, splashing red for Flametongue Kavu, madness cards and Fire/Ice. Almost as successful were the straight blue-green decks that made their transition almost unchanged from the OBC decks seen at Pro Tour-Osaka. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/04/d7/04d72164c3fe7ac43dc3b5a578612b446f34892be4c724744ab9a27d16fb796d.jpg)Braids variants were in prominent, but did not perform quite as well as I thought. I know of great many people who finished within just one match win out of the Top 8 with Braids decks, but it qualified a lesser number of people for Nationals that I would have expected. A variety of other decks broke into the Top 8s of various regions, but they either succeeded in a single region (such as team TOGIT's black-blue-green deck in New York) or did not show up in sufficient numbers to warrant a second look (such as blue-red Wild Research decks). 

Some of those who finished just out of Top 8 were rewarded for their performance when rating-based invitations were announced this week. It allowed a number of players who picked up the extra 20-30 points they needed at Regionals to qualify for the National championship.

### Norway Nationals

Thomas Johansen is the new Norwegian National Champion, having defeated the internationally recognized Oyvind Odegaard in the finals. Outside of the National team but in the Top 8 were such players as Oyvind Harding, Nicolai Herzog, and "Go-Mar" creator Lovre Crnobori. 

![](https://i.ormos.uk/d8/8f/d88f40ae484c8b378ef6e83cc6240953cce5a4bab379eaf97390063dcecb3ee3.jpg)While the winner used a very weird variant of a blue-red-green "Junta" deck, copied from the Online Championships, it was a blue-green variant with unorthodox maindeck cards such as Unsummon and Thought Devourer. Four Oslo players used this deck to place in the Top 8, while two more used a very similar deck, but splashed red for Flametongue Kavu. Although they gave up much in a way of mana consistency, this provided a considerable advantage in the mirror match. The deck is attributed to European champion Eivind Nitter. Of all the Oslo players, Nitter ironically had one of the worst records with the deck, unable to follow a very strong draft day finish with a good enough record to break into the Top 8. 

You can find the results and coverage of this tournament on head judge Rune Horvik's web site at [http://infinity.kirenet.com/~rhorvik/](http://infinity.kirenet.com/~rhorvik/). The coverage is in Norwegian, but the deck lists are in English.

### Magic Trivia

Last week's question:

*How is it possible to shuffle a token into your library in Extended, and which cards do you need to do it?*

Copy a Serra Avatar with a Dance of Many. If the token goes to the graveyard, it is shuffled into the library instead, then when priority is passed, state-based effects are checked, and you remove the token from the game.

### Play of the Week

Courtesy of Mason Peatross:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/95/f4/95f4d9734e18f7212f545fa54f6e2d94ffffedc77978ad7aa9c21e9f554b6f82.jpg)*"From Regionals this weekend:  
 James Stroud was playing Wild Research and his opponent, who was playing black-white Braids, had out Worship and a Spectral Lynx, which really had James on a clock. He couldn't keep Research in play long enough to do anything with it, but he got his opponent down to 4 life. His opponent cast a Phyrexian Rager when James had only one card in his hand. Stroud thought for a minute and then cast his spell in response to the Rager - Fiery Temper, targeting his opponent and dealing three points of damage. Then the Rager's life loss ability took his opponent to zero, circumventing the Worship effect."*

### Bad Play of the Week

Courtesy of Raphael Levy:

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b3/bc/b3bce611ac4f5b71c5feada50d905b9c6ea6f9bd6e109606b806231fbe3d1e1b.jpg)*"I was playing a b-u deck in the sealed deck portion (day 1) of Grand Prix-Barcelona. The situation was looking pretty bad for me: My opponent had Chainer, Dementia Master, and a few other creatures, and was at 11. I had a few creatures on the board (none of them could handle the situation), 4 swamps, and a Whispering Shade in my graveyard (I was also very low on life). Note that I had no way to deal him any damage.* 

*During his turn, he proceeded to reanimate my Whispering Shade. During mine, I cast a Butcher targeting the Shade. Back to his turn, he reanimates (he's at 5 life now) a Butcher of his own, targeting... my Butcher, setting the Shade free... on my Side! (creatures come back under their owner's control).*

*He couldn't attack as I had my Shade ready to block, and he couldn't deal enough damage to me this turn. He passed the turn, and I just attacked with the Shade that he kindly reanimated for me, dealing the 5 damage I needed to finish him off."*
