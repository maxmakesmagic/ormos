### Grand Prix-Cleveland

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/gpcle02/870.jpg)

An American of Russian descent won Grand Prix-Cleveland with a monoblack control deck... and, no, it wasn't me. Amateur Valentin Moskovich played out the entire tournament (he had zero byes), taking down big name players and other amateurs alike and winning any number of seemingly unfavorable matchups to earn the trophy. I watched several of Moskovich's matches while helping *Sideboard* with the coverage and must say that his play impressed me. I would not be surprised to see him more and more often at pro events.

Ever since Kibler won GP-Toronto, Canadian players seem to love retaliating by taking top prize at U.S. events - well, Matt Vienneau does anyway. It came very close to happening again this time. Canada's Gab "The Juggernaut" Tsang took a blue-green tempo deck all the way to the finals. Prior to Cleveland, Tsang hadn't even shuffled an OBC deck - but extensive practice drafting on **Magic** Online and natural skill overcame that obstacle. 

This Top 8 also featured several other well-known names. Rob Dougherty, who dropped out of Grand Prix-London Day 2 to make his flight home, made up for it by placing in the Top 8. He was joined there by Brian Davis and by David Williams, for whom this is the first Premiere-level tournament after being suspended by the DCI for a year. Williams is clearly eager and able to play now, and I foresee more Top 8's for him this season.

Ed Fear missed the tournament, because he was trying out for a lead role in an upcoming major motion picture. If he gets the gig, he will have to play less **Magic** for a while, but we wish him the best of luck anyway!

The most important thing about GP-Cleveland to me was that it proved OBC to be a much better format than we suspected at Worlds. Whereas two archetypes dominated the metagame early in PTQ season, at Worlds and at Sapporo, and new archetypes only began emerging at GP-London, Cleveland showcased a variety of new ideas. There was a Zombie Infestation deck which did very well overall in the tournament. There were a number of combo decks based around Mirari's Wake. There was Billy Jensen's land destruction deck. There was even a monored deck, though it hardly succeeded. True, it was good old monoblack and blue-green tempo that met in the finals anyway, but I believe the PTQ players will take away much from reading the coverage of this tournament and subsequent OBC tournaments will be far more interesting than I previously expected.

### Magic Invitational

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/MASTERSBAR01/944.jpg)

The votes are in! Surprising no one, Jon Finkel was the most popular player with the voters. Shadowmage Inflitrator helped, and so did the "Finkel vs. Garfield" gift box that came out last Christmas. Finkel may have had the worst season of his career, but he more than deserves these votes and the opportunity to play at the Invitational.

None of the other votes were particularly surprising. Zvi Mowshowitz came in a comfortable second, followed by Gary Wise and Dave Price. Chris Pikula came in fifth on the vote, missing the invitation for the first time in years - but it looks very likely that he will get to compete anyway. It seems Kai Budde will not be able to attend the event. If that is the case, Chris gets his spot. To the best of my knowledge, no one else has declined the invitation so far.

Last week's trivia question was about Soraya the Falconer whose ability was made obsolete by the Oracle changes that turned all falcons into birds. Oops! I completely missed the fact that Soraya itself has been changed (and in fact made better) via Oracle as its ability now affects birds. There were plenty of bird fans out there to email me and point this out. I apologize for the mistake.

I am not always wrong however. Many of you also emailed me about the Worldgorger Dragon play from two weeks ago. They did not understand exactly why the permanents never came back into play. So here is a brief explanation of how it works:

1) Worldgorger Dragon comes into play. Its "remove all other permanents from the game" ability is placed on the stack.  
 2) In response, an opponent casts Repulse on Worldgorger Dragon.  
 3) Worldgorger Dragon leaves play and "return all other permanents to play" ability is placed on the stack.  
 4) "Return all permanents" ability resolves. As there are no permanents currently removed from the game via the Dragon, nothing happens.  
 5) "Remove all other permanents from the game" ability resolves. All permanents are removed from the game and will never return now that Dragon isn't in play.

The same trick works with Faceless Butcher. You can cast Butcher, target a creature with it and, before the ability resolves, Aether Burst Butcher back to your hand. You just removed that creature from the game permanently and still have the best common in *Torment* in your hand.

"Chris Conroy was in a tough spot in a draft. His opponent's red-white deck was faster and more aggressive, and he did not have the greatest threats in his deck. However, his opponent also had no way to deal with a large early creature. Conroy had a plan - he cast Aquamoeba early in the game and pitched his entire hand to it, keeping only a Stitch Together. ### Play of the Week #2

(Courtesy of Josh Staudenraous)

"At a local Extended tournament a while back, my two friends were playing Sligh vs. Slivers. My friend with the Sligh deck had out 3 Jackal Pups and a Mogg Flunkies, and was going to deal the death blow that turn because the Sliver player only had a Gemstone Mine out due to being land hosed. The friend playing Sligh decided to add insult to injury and cast a Furnace of Rath before he attacked. Sliver player then tapped his Gemstone Mine for red, and cast Firestorm discarding 4 cards and dealing 8 to each creature and a total of 48 damage to my friend due to the Jackal Pups' abilities. That's a Firestorm for the ages."
