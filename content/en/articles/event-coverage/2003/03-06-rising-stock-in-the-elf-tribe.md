You know that kid who gets picked last for dodge ball in the playground? The one who is skulking off to the side of the school building as even that one girl with glasses who wandered accidentally onto the boys' side of the schoolyard gets chosen before him? Unless you were home schooled like 1999 Pro Tour-Chicago finalist Brian Davis, or perhaps Frankie Muniz of tee vee's *Malcolm in the Middle*, of course you know who I am talking about. As a hard core gamer, it is quite possible that you yourself were that kid at some point in your educational career. In fact, you may even have associated your self-worth as a person by your popularity in the picking order at the beginning of recess in the fourth or fifth grade. 

Later on, I'd hope, you may have found some measure of acceptance in non-traditional endeavors. You may have found some artistic outlet, like drawing pretty pictures (or pretty pixels, I suppose, these days), or perhaps writing down your thoughts for others to see. You may have hit life's pinnacle that one day that girls started "liking you back" after all those years of sweaty palms and that stuttering inability to formulate complete sentences (despite the fact that you've always considered yourself fairly bright); heck, if you're like my friend the recently victorious Dragonmaster, some girl may have even devoted a corner of cyberspace to your adoration. If you're reading this, you have probably found a different outlet for your competitive urges than the mid-morning or post-lunch dodge ball game: tournament **Magic: the Gathering**.

You know that kid, skulking off to the side of the school building, staring at his shoelaces, waiting to be picked? Wirewood Herald was that kid.

Now he may just be that other kid, who, though fairly geeky, seems all right at dodge ball and should get picked over the guy with elevator shoes and inch-thick horn rims, if not the captain of the grammar school soccer team.

In my first ever *Onslaught* draft, I actually forced two or three Wirewood Heralds, but that was because my overall first pick was Voice of the Woods; I wanted to make sure I had enough elves and that I could find my bomb.

At the time I first looked at real, live, *Onslaught* cards, I was barred from being able to play in the Prerelease because I had written preview articles for *Sideboard* and may have had an unfair advantage. Andrew Cuneo nevertheless pulled me aside and asked if I wanted to do a team draft with the new cards. I figured that with one of history's best team drafters as one of my partners, I would have a good shot of winning and took him up on the offer. Then again, every one of our opponents had Sunday Pro Tour experience: the similarly Car Acrobatic Andy Johnson, Chris Pikula, that blue-white bear of a thousand long ago victories and from way back when Randy Buehler was a rookie-come-champion, Adrian Sayers of Austin, Texas.

Anyway, I cracked that Voice of the Woods, decided that making 7/7 creatures must be a good ability and started my draft pile with him. Over the course of three packs, I had built a powerful monogreen elf-beast hybrid deck and eventually clawed my way to 3-0 with my first time out with the *Onslaught* cards. Despite the fact that our team nevertheless managed to lose this draft, I felt good about my performance, and have had a strong preference for the green spells ever since.

Late in my third match, I tapped out for Spitting Gourna against Adrian. I could have played my Voice of the Woods, but in order to do so, I would have had to tap my Wirewood Elf anyway, leaving only four total untapped elves in play. At the time, it seemed that drawing a card from my Wirewood Savage would be, you know, more savage... but then the guy across the table showed me a Blackmail, taking the Voice of the Woods that had beaten his two teammates. He followed up with a Crown of Suspicion on my Wellwisher and things started to look grim as his Severed Legion threatened to poke my life away, two points at a time.

I untapped, and sent in my lonely Wirewood Herald. 

Adrian looked to the Meddling Mage and Andy J and asked what kind of elves I had. They said the Voice; he was down. They said the "guy who gains life"; similarly dead. Okay, said Adrian, I'll block that 1/1 elf. I searched through my deck and found Heedless One, and eventually won the game with her, on the back of the elf tribe, and multiple Wirewood Prides.

Despite my rookie 3-0 performance with the set, you are probably shaking your head at the quality of some of the cards I mentioned that I had played. Like me, you have probably filed away Wirewood Pride entirely and relegated Wirewood Herald to "fat kid at the end of the line" pick status. If you are like my friend Scott McCord of Slay, Pillage, Gerrard, you may not even like Wellwisher (for shame, for shame). As I just admitted, for a long time, until serious *Legions* drafting actually, I shunted Wirewood Herald off to the "cut" pile, if I ever even drafted him, or played him only as a twenty-first or twenty-second card.

But in this new format, where a potential Sparksmith pack had gone by the wayside, where a pack of all creatures presents itself in the number three spot, I have had a complete reversal on my opinion of this fine 1/1 for ![1 Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/6b/4c/6b4cf558eab279fb9414f146ebdd0e0705ec92984fec530597dd2a3bce43ce24.gif)![Green Mana](https://i.ormos.uk/d9/d5/d9d556256faf839099c06019302393fab57e6e9a77769d6f6813a7c961c4cb59.gif).

When packs one and two start getting a little thin, I try to pick up one or two Wirewood Heralds. They can be absolutely devastating in the right decks, going for Timberwatch Elf, a common that many players consider to be the defining card of *Legions* Limited. Because of the effectiveness of Wellwisher in the green-on-green matchup, having the Wirewood Heralds can not only improve your chances to win games where the green opponent doesn't have an active Sparksmith, but restrict the opponent's chances to beat you by finding Wellwisher himself.

Though he seems generally disrespected, some opponents will actually *over*value your Wirewood Herald. At Grand Prix-Boston last week, I attacked with my Heralds repeatedly and would reduce my opponents to six or fewer life on a regular basis before they would deign to block. In a deck with the right elf cards, Wirewood Herald can play Beloved Chaplain on offense and hold off entire teams in the early game. No one wants to see Elvish Riders appear out of nowhere and even fewer players want to go up against an elf tribal deck with Gempalm Strider waiting in the grip. Against most b-r decks, Wirewood Herald can go toe-to-toe with a good number of the other guy's creatures in actual trades and the ability to search up even humble picks like Symbiotic Elf can be a real deterrent against the archetype of dangerous, but tiny, commons.

Speaking of humble picks, that kid picking his nose, standing next to the kid skulking off to the side of the school building, has also gone up in draft priority. Yes, I'm talking about Taunting Elf. Taunting Elf, easily the worst of the *Onslaught* Falter cycle, both probably cost me Top 8 in Boston and has skyrocketed in importance to the average green mage. The ability to kill a random 0/1 creature in the midgame has not surprisingly gone down with the all-creature pack, while the importance of breaking ground stalls, conversely, has gone way up. That kid who no one wanted to play with just a few weeks ago will be deciding an increased number of Limited games over the next few pre-*Scourge* months... don't be surprised if you lose to an oncoming Taunting Elf...

And don't be surprised if your opponent sacrifices his Wirewood Herald to go find one.

As far as the rest of the elf tribe goes, many of the other family members have also gone up in value. *Onslaught* green, in the past, was all about beast dominance. My baseline strategy revolved around the fact that even the late beasts - the Treespring Lorians and so forth - would often trade with more than one of the opponent's solid commons. Factor in the Wirewood Savage and you could avalanche your opponent with a veritable zoo of janky, but enormous, common creatures. That strategy has not changed, per se, but its implementation has, with the introduction of *Legions*.

A large percentage of the games played by any competently drafted green deck will develop the exact same way. Somebody might get some beats in with an Elvish Warrior or morph, or somebody might get down Sparksmith and de facto win the game on turn two, but for the most part, if a green mage is involved, the ground will muck up terribly through the midgame. Gournas of all sorts will stand guard over the Red Zone and neither flyer nor ground-pounder will dare attack. Timberwatch Elf will create problems and the average player will not challenge his incredible ability. There are a couple of things that can change this... if the green player doesn't have any Gournas in play, he might get killed by a white or blue player's mid-range flyers, black's Severed Legion and company are always dangerous and being on the wrong end of the wrong creature with provoke (especially Krosan Vorine with some sort of pants on) can also complicate matters, but don't be surprised when the vast majority of your games turn into ground stalls.

These kinds of stalls will generally be decided by one the following factors:

### The Falter

The Falter is the most important, or at least the most common, way to break a ground stall. Basically, one player generates critical mass and makes an all-out alpha strike to win the game. The reason he breaks the ground stall is because all of a sudden his green men can only be blocked by black men, or all his opponent's potential blockers get lazy, or Mark Zadjner runs out into the Red Zone waving his arms and everyone decides they would rather chase after him with pitchforks than, you know, defend their local planeswalker.

  

### The Counter-Falter

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td><td></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td><td><a><img alt="Choking Tethers" src="https://i.ormos.uk/3d/60/3d60377e3695a485e3446729f068f6b894fcb9b432612ba3b43b1980ee5389ab.jpg"></a></td><td></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

I told Bob Maher last weekend that I would normally take an Elvish Warrior over a Wellwisher because the Wellwisher would be more likely to come back, but that, ultimately, I thought that Wellwisher was the better card and would decide more games. He replied that he had, in fact, many times run the Wellwisher ahead of the Warrior, and for the same reason I valued it more highly. For a while, many pros went into turbo-aggro mode with a single-minded goal of winning the morph war in the first five turns of the game. Cards like Elvish Warrior were supposed to be the second coming and some players even went out and said they liked the Battering Craghorn more than the Skirk Commando. The thing is, getting beats in early is all well and good, but it will probably not prevent the ground stall. Once the ground stall is in place, you really want a Wellwisher to ramp your life back up for the sole reason that, with enough life, you can survive a single Falter-driven alpha strike. Your opponent, should he be foolish enough to send all his fellas for a futile non-kill anyway, will have his pants down for you to alpha strike him back. Wellwisher keeps you from dying to a Severed Legion and is absolute gold in the green-on-green matchup where there are elves aplenty in play. A card that falls into a similar role is Choking Tethers. I cannot communicate the number of times I have searched and searched for my Wave of Indifference only to waste it and be answered with a Choking Tethers that reduced my potential attack to non-lethal.

### The Syreen Penetrator

Speaking to both the flyers and scary monsters that have already been noted as evasive exceptions to the ground stall, this category whose name will be loved by Mr. Zvi "Arilou" Mowshowitz, also describes incidental evasion creatures (like the mighty and mighty unstoppable Anurid Murkdiver against a g-b deck) and cards like Goblin Dynamo or Searing Flesh that can punish opponents who haven't been stingy enough with their life totals, trusting in their ground stalls to keep them from that dreaded zero.

  

### Savage Card Advantage

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td><td></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td><td><a><img alt="Wirewood Savage" src="https://i.ormos.uk/70/ac/70acdf90fdce0248b887e64a50e504253b03e18452c46a649fb2df19f10784cc.jpg"></a></td><td></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Here is another reason why today's subject the Wirewood Herald is so important: he goes looking for the best green common. Wirewood Savage is incredible in ground stall situations. While not particularly strong against that unknown morph turn three when going second, he builds towards a tide of card advantage where each powerful card flows into another powerful card and card drawing fuels more card drawing. Sure, he's vulnerable to basically every removal spell and most players think he's empirically weaker than Snarling Undorak, but for the purposes of setting up the ability to win over the long game that inevitably follows the ground stall, there are few cards better at generating enough of an advantage to start attacking than this little Grey Ogre. Wirewood Savage isn't actually alone in the category... we can list any number of cards that break ground stalls by promoting a decisive positional advantage before attacking: Centaur Glade and Arcanis the Omnipotent are just two cards that fall under this category, possibly straddling the next.

### The Insane Bomb

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/65/b4/65b44cec90bda938bbb278a79acba1f455a38a19b714807bea68c253b77de30d.jpg"></td><td></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/dd/0a/dd0a617788694ee0e82eed58b0746ab9dbdbdb6e709ee5390dcfb32556a6990e.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/96/f5/96f5d53e3ef958055c4fdf5bf835be99797bd18449a68a882c96ab3801bae612.jpg"></td><td><a><img alt="Silvos, Rogue Elemental" src="https://i.ormos.uk/50/39/50398dde7d4a66cac33cee8ab38f72e06f19c3a00994ae9122de449a557913c9.jpg"></a></td><td></td></tr>&#10;<tr><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/a0/a9/a0a91e69ef087e17e689f758224146196f0a29fad934eb2639d2fb73d4cf3c75.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/c0/e2/c0e24bff2fa206011911cb4b5cb20b4559cb82301a77e757b6e86de3c1ae659a.jpg"></td><td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/fa/ab/faab219b94a5d6a8405ff77aefe6f815ba2bba52fc447652a633eec85e227836.jpg"></td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

If there is any format where this comes into play, *Onslaught*-led Limited is that format. Dreadful girl gorgons, Rogue Elementals, Exalted Angels and incredibly angry angels with chips on their shoulders (or even just their bad attitudes) will all break a ground stall. Did you draft a rare creature first pick? It is probably eligible for this role.

Looking back at these categories, you should be able to see how Wirewood Herald can find a Taunting Elf, a Wellwisher, Elven Riders or a Wirewood Savage. At the least, it can thin and shuffle your deck to increase the chances of finding that Havoc Demon or other incredible bomb you need to find to win the game. Don't take him higher than his buddy the Wirewood Elf, and certainly don't value him over one of the cards you plan to find with him, but respect this little nerd... he'll win you more games and block more fatties than you might expect.
