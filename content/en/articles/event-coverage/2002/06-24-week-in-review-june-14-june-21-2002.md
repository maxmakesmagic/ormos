### Magic Online Launch

[![](https://i.ormos.uk/5a/48/5a487069d9fc2c7a1ecece659d5f148fe7e62be20971e10ca589d784c729e925.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20051201024129/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=magic/magiconline)***Magic** Online* launches officially today, June 24th. After about a year of beta and free drafts for those who participated in it, Wizards of the Coast finally expects us to pay up for the privilege. There has been a considerable amount of grumbling in the PTQ/PT communities about the price - at $3.29 a pack and $1 per event ticket, a draft costs $12. However, it is well worth it. Even though you can find a draft at your local store for $7-10, you will have spent those extra few dollars on food and transportation anyway. And the ability to just pick up a draft when you have the urge - day or night - is well worth the additional expense too. 

***Magic** Online* will not replace the store or club you play at - as well it shouldn't - but it is an excellent addition to our **Magic** experience. If you did not get a chance to try it during beta, do certainly give it a go now.

In related news, Mike Turian accepted a position as editor-in-chief of [www.Grimmoire.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20020624000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://www.Grimmoire.com&origin=sideboard_wir_20020624) - a strategy web site heavily geared toward the ***Magic** Online* community. 

### Canada Nationals

![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/canat02/883.jpg)The metagame at this tournament followed a simple formula. "Dominance of Psychatog and Opposition at U.S. Nationals equals red-green Anti-Tog being a good deck." Three of the Top 8 decks belonged to this archetype, with two Opposition and one Psychatog deck also making the cut. But it was the blue-red-green archetype, represented by two different decks in the Top 8, that ended up at the top. 

Jurgen Hahn's deck takes a step back several months, when red-blue-green first emerged as an archetype around the time of U.S. Regionals. It had what it takes to take down two Anti-Tog decks and an Opposition, to earn Hahn $3200 and the title of the National Champion. Hahn defeated Jon Stern in the finals. Stern only managed a 3-3 performance with his r-g deck on Day 1, but made Top 8 thanks to a strong showing in the draft portion of the tournament, and fought his way into the finals, and onto the national team. Ray Weiss and David Rood duked it out for the remaining spot on the team. Weiss defeated Rood, but knew he would be unable to attend Worlds, and forfeited the slot on his team to Rood. His victory over Rood netted him an additional $600 in prize money.

A wildcard team represents Canada this year. Can they live up to the tradition of solid finishes set by their country's team in year's past? We'll only know in August. Among the better-known pro players top finishers included Jeff Cunningham (5th place), Terry Tsang (13th), and Peter Radonjic (14th).

### Coming Up: Team Grand Prix-New Jersey

![](https://i.ormos.uk/41/ec/41eca202de4e516b06754ce34fd4d4dcf551260fd3dedaf603f6585fafc3bbf2.jpg)East Coast players are getting ready for what may become yet another record-breaking Grand Prix this season. On top of the pro points and prize money, this tournament is a one-slot qualified for next year's Team Masters - which means every pro team worth its salt will at least consider attending. Several of the top European players - including Kai Budde - have considered attending this tournament over the European Championship, but were unable to find inexpensive airfare.

With a large competitive player base in the tri-state area, there will be plenty of amateur teams as well. Just about every competitive player here in Brooklyn plans to attend - even the ones who tend to show up to only half or so of New York qualifiers. 

New Jersey is the last title tournament in North America this season. Only two Grand Prix will remain afterward - Standard events in Taipei and Sao Paulo.

### Coming Up: European Championship

![](http://www.wizards.com/international/images/logos/ec.gif)Top players from all over Europe will gather to sing the last hoorah of the pre-Judgment Standard. For the pros, there are money and prestige on the line. For those struggling to qualify, this is the last opportunity to get invited to Worlds. As the Top 8 uninvited players get in, this means a player finishing in 20th place still has a chance to go to Worlds. Then there is always the tournament's location - Amsterdam is best known for its "side events", which lure in plenty of **Magic** players as proven by the several Grand Prix tournaments held there.

### New Magic Web Site

Several of the Finnish players have launched a new strategy web site, covering their community. Although there is no English language content at present, they are hopeful of offering English coverage of upcoming events such as the next GP to he held in their country. For now, those who speak the language should visit [mtg.bore.org](https://web.archive.org/web/20020624000000/http://www.wizards.com/leaving.asp?url=http://mtg.bore.org&origin=sideboard_wir_20020624).

I got over a dozen letters from Type 1 enthusiasts, begging for an explanation. So here is what took place. A number of us (including R&D's Mark Rosewater and Randy Buehler, as well as [MagicTheGathering.com's](https://web.archive.org/web/20051212103504/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/welcome) Aaron Forsythe) were taking a lunch break during the final day coverage of U.S. Nationals. The topic of conversation happened upon Type 1. Pat Chapin, who is probably the world's best Type 1 player at the moment, had some strong opinions about the format.

Pat argued that Miracle Grow is extremely powerful in the format, as the only deck that beats both "Good Stuff" control decks, and the beatdown decks designed to beat the former. When asked as to what beats Miracle Grow, he declared that the Illusionary Mask-Phyrexian Dreadnought archetype is the only deck that keeps Miracle Grow in check, thus keeping this already broken format at least somewhat fair. With Illusionary Mask in play, Phyrexian Dreadnought becomes a 12/12 creature for 1 mana with no disabilities at all. That is when Chapin dropped that very quotable line.

An outspoken advocate of the format, Chapin went on to say many quotable things that afternoon. How about: "Gush is as good as Ancestral Recall right now." Why? Because no one can Misdirect Gush.

### Play of the Week

Courtesy of Marcel Vroegrijk:

This is a real blast from the past - a play that was "techy" about five years ago, and apparently is still very good today.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img src="https://i.ormos.uk/88/f3/88f3033b3959f8eae15705560baa7f22e09965938a2f18fd08d0ff0e21e9d354.jpg"><br><i>Ancestral Armageddon?</i>&#10;</td></tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

Vroegrijk was playing a casual Type 1 game with a Junk-style deck against Stasis. At some point in the game his opponent cast Balance to get rid of Vroegrijk's creatures. In response, Marcel sacrificed all of his lands to Zuran Orb. With Elves and Birds in his deck, he figured he'd recover much sooner than his opponent. However, his opponent countered his own Balance after all of Marcel's lands hit the graveyard. On top of that, he countered it with Arcane Denial, able to draw a few cards off the cantrip effect.

### Bad Play of the Week

Courtesy of DeQuan Watson

William Herderman was playing against Akil Steele at the U.S. Regionals. The game was being played quite casually. At a certain point in it, both players were mana-flooded badly. Herderman revealed his hand to his opponent and the spectators at some point in the game, showing off the five lands he was holding. Steele thought this funny and joined the bandwagon, revealing his hand of four lands and an Addle. This was during Herderman's turn. On his own turn, Steele drew a card, thought for a moment, and... cast Addle! Of course, he got to see those same five lands he saw less than thirty seconds ago. He lost the game a few turns later, after his opponent drew several key cards. Some of which could have been removed with that Addle.
