There were two PTQs within striking distance this past weekend: one in Boston and the other in Harrisburg. The plan was to hook with either Paul Jordan or Justin Polin and attend both. This would have involved leaving NY/NJ Friday night (or so early on Saturday morning that it would still have been Friday night) and making the 4-5 hour trek up to Boston. From there, the plan was to drive to Harrisburg which was South and West of our point of origin.

Bleah.

I was planning on running virtually the same deck as the previous week with more care given to the sideboard. I playtested quite a bit at Neutral Ground during the week and noticed a growing number of mirror matches. Not just g-u but my threshold version complete with Grizzly Fate and Centaur Chieftain. I quickly figured out that my Week One column had been posted. While I was happy that the deck was catching on, I began to question whether or not I should have been so open with the decklist.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2f/88/2f888d4536b43740814fb54aa2184b17e2e7bd1f0b98e4722ca74a9693d50be1.jpg)

One person I played more than a few matches against is Jeremy Cash. I have no idea where this kid came from, but suddenly he is at Neutral Ground whenever I'm there (which I am sad to say is *waaay* too often) playing a good deck. He's smart, with a quick sense of humor and little respect for his elders (at least this elder, anyway). All in all he has the makings of a pretty good **Magic** player. He is playing a version of my deck that came to him via Matt Boccio - another up and coming Neutral Grounder. I won way more than I lost but he seemed unfamiliar with the deck and that may have contributed to the numbers. We discussed the idea of playing some sideboarded matches but the mirror grew tiresome and I played a bunch of games against Roy Badillo's monoblack control instead with positive results.

I also let Eric Kesselman-known for his unnatural love of single blue casting cost cantrips-take the deck for a test drive. He fell in love with the speed and raw power of the deck but disagreed with the numbers of the deck. He hated four copies of Grizzly Fate and wanted to find room for Aether Burst. He would eventually cut a land, a Wonder and two Grizzly Fates. Somehow he managed to squeeze four Bursts AND four Standstills into their place.

Somewhere around the time that Grizzly Fates made the Neutral Ground buy list it was announced that the Team Tournament that was originally scheduled for the upcoming Saturday had been replaced by a Grand Prix Trial for Grand Prix-Cleveland. I had been on the fence about attending GP-Cleveland since I found out I would be doing this column. There is a comic convention being held that same weekend in Bethesda, Maryland which is also the same weekend that my comic book - The B-Sides - is scheduled for release by Marvel Comics. 

Boston seemed even further away than before. I spent the next couple of days looking for an out so I could stay in NY on Saturday, play in the Trial (I would decide whether or not to attend Cleveland based on whether or not I had 3-byes Saturday evening) and leave for Harrisburg on Sunday. Two extra nights in my own bed, an internal conflict resolved… Alas, everyone going to Harrisburg wanted to go to Boston as well. I eventually made plans to meet Justin Polin and Scott McCord at the Chelsea Diner (an after hours game space that is cleverly hidden behind the facade of a 24 hour greasy spoon) on Saturday at 4:30 AM!

Friday rolled around and I head into Neutral Ground for some last-minute testing and sideboard tuning. I want to say that sideboarding is crucial in this format. As I struggled with how to phrase this paragraph, I realized that this was my relative inexperience with Constructed play speaking. In the past I only played in Constructed formats to showcase a cool deck or bide my time until a Limited season. Now, I am actually trying to have sustained success over the course of a Constructed season. The real importance of a sideboard - and more importantly - a strategy to implement from the sideboard was hitting home for the first time.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/1e/a8/1ea8daa1f3747af31cb1150410b0ac5c9028760f8cef2d55e932ca818f7df995.jpg)

Dave Price was shuffling up his monoblack control deck that he was thinking about taking to Worlds and we play a bunch of games before and after sideboarding. I settle on bringing in Envelops and Upheavals - four each - in for Mental Notes and Roars of the Wurm. This seemed to swing the matchup considerably in my favor and I found the session very useful. It seemed even more useful when I started talking to Dave Price outside while he and Mike Short smoked. I told Mike about my desire to play in the Trial and then go to Harrisburg on Sunday and he enthusiastically signed on to drive with me to Harrisburg.

Yay!

I spoke with Justin later that night and informed him of the change in plans and went to sleep comforted by the thought that I didn't need to be awake until 11:00 am. Things were working out perfect… 

Too perfect.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/4a/67/4a67249037eb6546940914936d4c311f0323c4a5db6d7768548e30dab4165d97.jpg)

I arrived at Neutral Ground that morning to find a better than expected turnout considering the Boston PTQ and the last minute announcement of the Trial. Among the 40 odd players waiting to play were Seth Burn and Adam Rubens. Just like the previous week in Providence, both of them are playing Wonderboys - a deck similar to mine but featuring more mana acceleration with Millikins. We were all discussing sideboard strategy and Adam and I argued for Krosan Reclamation while Seth did not value it as highly. I don't believe Seth was playing Quiet Speculation while Adam featured two or three. I don't run Spec either, but still liked the Reclamation for the Wonder wars. Eventually I settled on running my deck from the previous weekend with the following sideboard:

### Round One: Mike Reilly playing u-g Madness

I enjoy talking to Mike quite a bit. He's smart and funny and is very interested in comic books. He is also one of the few gamers I have met who has a broader taste in comics than the traditional superhero fare and we have had some lively discussions about comics and movies that I always enjoy.

**Game one**  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/62/ba/62ba6bfea2a628d249343a4ea15ccb2ced42b0cae4e8c8fb98108ba6b87a3b56.jpg)I had to mulligan my opening hand after I won the die roll. It contained three Grizzly Fates, no one casting cost spells and an island. My second had a Rootwalla, a Careful Study and another island. I Studied the Rootwalla into a forest and not much else. I was able to do about six points of damage with the Rootwalla before Mike's Wild Mongrels made flying Arrogant Wurms and I shuffled up my cards and began sideboarding.

Brian - 0 Mike -1 

**Game two**  
 I brought in 4 Aether Bursts and 3 Reclamations and I took out - I think - one of everything that cost more than one mana. I got a fast Werebear/threshold draw and was able to attack for four with the Bear on turn three thanks to Mental Note, Mental Note and Aether Burst. Mike had what would traditionally be a very good Speculation draw but another two Aether Bursts and a timely Reclamation allowed the Werebear five attacks for the win.

Brian - 1 Mike - 1

**Game three**  
 Mike never drew an island and his life total reads: 18, 15, 7…dead. He didn't deal any damage to me in the last two games. 

Brian - 2 Mike - 1

Seth had a bye this round and reported that the field is all g-u with most of them running something that looked similar to my deck. I think that out of 43 decks there were 26 u-g, 10 monoblack and the rest fell under other.

Gian is a Forbidden Planet regular with a fair amount of success in their Friday Night **Magic** Standard tournaments. He aspires to do well in larger events but his burgeoning baseball career interferes with **Magic** during the summer. This is a rare free Saturday for him to compete in a higher level event.

**Game one**  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/fa/17/fa178d5a343515f726502f16d05fa6f4caabbdca2207e69f13d6b1c45b0c69d7.jpg)I got to go first and kept another one land/Careful Study/Basking Rootwalla hand. I didn't draw into anything but a forest and tried to stabilize against a board of Anurid Brushhoppers and Patrol Hounds. Just when it looked like I might have had a chance, he discarded Glory and killed me.

Brian - 0 Gian - 1

**Game two**  
 I sideboarded badly this game and brought in Aether Bursts but not Reclamations, completely forgetting that Glory was the most threatening card in his deck. Despite this error, a turn two Wild Mongrel and a Basking Rootwalla outraced an Anurid Brushhopper and not much else. I did use an Aether Burst in this game but realized it was a sideboarding mistake after remembering his Glory from game one.

Brian - 1 Gian - 1

**Game three**  
 I took out the Bursts and brought in 3 Reclamations and one Upheaval. Unfortunately, Gian had a very slow draw and I had an ideal one. Turn 1 Rootwalla… attack for 1… play Wild Mongrel… discard Roar of the Wurm and Wonder attack for 7…

Brian - 2 Gian - 1

### Round three: Jeremy Cash playing u-g threshold

As I mentioned earlier, I have played a number of test games with Jeremy during the week and I know that he is playing a version of my deck that has been slightly altered by the inclusion of Quiet Speculation over Breakthrough and Millikins over the much maligned Basking Rootwallas.

**Game one**  
![](https://i.ormos.uk/c8/4d/c84dfa7bd7631cf0867194f0ce39fe86b5a0b510e1c073273166997c906cea38.jpg)I lost game one and I have no memory of what happened although I am pretty sure that he finished me off with Centaur Chieftain.

Brian - 0 Jeremy - 1

**Game two**  
 I decided to side in four Upheavals and four Envelops, once again taking out one of almost each other card. I knew that Jeremy was also bringing in Envelops (from our playtesting) but I was confident I could bait him with Roars, Fates and Breakthroughs to get my Upheaval to resolve. This is more or less how game two went. I got a pretty fast threshold draw with multiple Werebears. His draw involved a Quiet Speculation for multiple Roar of the Wurms and a Krosan Reclamation. I drew into Upheaval and started to bait counters. I cast a Grizzly Fate which was Enveloped and the next turn I cast Breakthrough. Jeremy oversold his bluff as he pretended to think about countering it and I knew he did not have one. Next turn I cast Upheaval and two 4/4 fliers in that order.

Brian -1 Jeremy - 1

Jeremy had a very slow draw this game and I came out of the gates very fast. It was starting to look like he would stabilize. Jeremy didn't have anything that was putting cards in his yard this game and tapped out to hard cast a Roar of the Wurm. I untapped and Upheavaled for the win.

After the game I told Jeremy that he oversold the counterspell. He looked me straight in the eye and said, "As far as I'm concerned, you misplayed and then you top-decked Upheaval for the win. That's my story." Funny kid.

Brian - 2 Jeremy - 1

### Round Four: Mike Ricciardi (aka Chunk) playing u-g threshold

**Game one**  
 Chunk is a Neutral Ground fixture and former JSS-er. His main claim to fame is that he made the finals of the Neutral Ground half of the second Grudge Match against Zvi Mowshowitz. I admire his fearlessness. He will draft against anyone at anytime (providing he has packs, of course) in his pursuit of becoming a better **Magic** player. We have playtested countless matches against each other and were playing very similar decks-let's face it, better than 2/3 of the room were playing similar decks.

He doesn't seem to get much going in game one and I have him down to eleven with a Wild Mongrel, a Werebear without threshold, two cards in hand, and five land in play. I draw a Breakthrough and do some math. I pitch the other cards in my hand to Wild Mongrel making it a 4/4. I cast Breakthrough for 4 keeping all 4 cards that I drew and pitched them all to Wild Mongrel making my Werebear a 4/4 in the process and killing him that turn.

Brian - 1 Chunk - 0

**Game two**  
 Once again I sided in Upheavals and Envelops. At this point I was thinking that I should run all eight cards in my main deck since I would side in the same package against u-g and MBC - which felt like 99% of the field at that point.

Chunk had to mulligan his opening hand but still managed to bash my face with a Rootwalla and a Wild Mongrel.

Brian - 1 Chunk - 1

**Game three**  
 We both know that the winner of this match can double ID into the Top 8 and we took a little longer than usual shuffling not wanting to lose that opportunity to a mulligan. Fortunately we both draw into playable hands. Chunk got an early advantage and took me down to 15 pretty quickly. Unfortunately for him he tapped all of his blue one turn and gave me a window of opportunity to cast Upheaval and a Werebear.

Brian - 2 Chunk - 1
