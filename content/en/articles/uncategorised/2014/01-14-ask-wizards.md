<table>&#10;  <tbody><tr>&#10;    <td>&#10;      <table>&#10;        <tbody><tr>&#10;          <td>&#10;                 <b> Q: Can you please elaborate on how hybrid works with devotion? And what about the new God cards that are two colors? </b></td>&#10;          <td> </td>&#10;          <td>&#10;            <img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg">&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;        <tr>&#10;          <td colspan="3">&#10;            <table>&#10;              <tbody><tr>&#10;                <td>&#10;                  <p>&#10;                    <b> A: Matt Tabak, Magic rules manager: </b>&#10;                  </p>&#10;                  <p> When counting your devotion to either a single color or a pair of colors, each mana symbol counts only once. A hybrid mana symbol is the color(s) of its component parts. So, for example, if you control just a <a>Azor's Elocutors</a>, your devotion to white is two, your devotion to blue is two, and your devotion to white and blue is two. If you control just a <a>Reaper King</a>, your devotion to any one color is one and your devotion to any pair of colors is two. If you control <a>Azor's Elocutors</a> and Ephara, God of the Polis, your devotion to white and blue is four (and Ephara isn't a creature… yet!). </p>&#10;                  <p> Another way to think about it is if you're calculating your devotion to white and blue, for each mana symbol among the mana costs of permanents you control, ask yourself, &quot;Is this mana symbol white and/or blue?&quot; If the answer is yes, add one to your devotion. </p>&#10;                </td>&#10;              </tr>&#10;            </tbody></table>&#10;          </td>&#10;        </tr>&#10;      </tbody></table>&#10;    </td>&#10;  </tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
