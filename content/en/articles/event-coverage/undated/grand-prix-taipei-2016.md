## GRAND PRIX TAIPEI 2016

TOP 8 COVERAGE

This might be the last Grand Prix before Eldritch Moon changes the face of Standard forever, that doesn't mean it should be taken lightly. Yuuki Ichikawa arrived in Taipei with that very attitude, and emerged victorious from among a field of 793.

Citing Collected Company as the best card in Standard and Bant Company as the best deck, Ichikawa's unwavering belief secured him a second Grand Prix title.

Finalist Huang Yung-Ming from Chinese Taipei had hoped to win it all for his community, but fell short due to mana problems at the very end. However, seeing how he won a World Magic Cup Qualifier last week and made it all the way to the finals in his very first Grand Prix Top 8, Huang is certainly more than ecstatic.

Lee Shi Tian makes his 7th Grand Prix Top 8 and Yuta Takahashi is up to 10, all incredible milestones worthy of remembrance. Rounding off the Top 8 are the four quarterfinalists Liu Yuchen, Lien Chien-Hung, Setsompop Mingrerk and Shawn McNeace.

Congratulations once again to Yuuki Ichikawa! Your Grand Prix Shanghai 2015 trophy has got a new “companion”!

DAY 1 COVERAGE

793 players came to compete at Grand Prix Taipei but only so many can return tomorrow. 266 to be exact. Among them are four undefeated players, three at 9-0 and one at 8-0-1!

Platinum Pro Lee Shi Tian sits atop the standings, along with Huang Wen Che and Kenichiro Omori. Shawn McNeace is the fourth undefeated player, with an 8-0-1 record.

Close behind at 8-1 are household names such as Yuuki Ichikawa, Ken Yukuhiro, Yuta Takahashi, and Ryiochi Tamada, while Jason Chung, Kelvin Chew, Kenji Tsumura, Yuuya Watanabe, Huang Hao Shan, and Makihito Mihara have also made the cut.

Green-White Tokens remains the most popular deck, owing to the dynamic duo of Nissa, Voice of Zendikar and Gideon, Ally of Zendikar, but various Collected Company archetypes are also strong contenders. Humans are threatening to steamroll across the field, while 4-Color Rites, Seasons Past and assorted midrange and control decks will try to halt the assault. As can be seen by the complete Day 1 metagame breakdown, we're at a culmination of a two-month-long Standard format that remains as diverse as ever.

With just two additional Grand Prix before Pro Tour *Eldritch Moon*, the world is also paying great attention to the three-way Grand Prix Master race between Tomoharu Saito, Brian Braun-Duin and (16) Reid Duke. Saito squeaked in at 6-3, while the other two are just getting ready for Grand Prix Pittsburgh. If you're still hungering for more Standard action, be sure to stay tuned as we swing over to the other side of the globe!
