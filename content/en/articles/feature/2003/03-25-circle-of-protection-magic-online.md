### Addendum: Trading Tournament Spots

* * *

While the tournament this weekend brought up many issues that need resolved, I'm going to address one unforseen consequence--trading for tournament spots.

Eventually, this will be part of an updated ["Circle of Protection: ***Magic** Online*"](/en/articles/feature/2002/08-19-circle-of-protection-magic-online), but for now I'm putting it down to make players aware of this situation. Especially since it's a fairly simple issue from a player perspective--don't do it.

Trading for a spot in a tournament is an bad idea for many reasons:

### The Player May Not Give Up A Spot

While it's possible to look at a tournament list to see if the player actually *is* in the tournament to begin with, what's to say he or she is actually going to relinquish that spot? It's not like you can do this via a **Trade** screen--there's no visible evidence and no confirmation of the trade. You're taking this person's word that if you trade a ticket (or whatever), you'll get that spot.

Yes, I know: "Well, I wouldn't trade anything until I was in the tournament." Good for you. However, this brings up another issue.

### Something For Nothing

So you got into the tournament and traded whatever to the player. How do you know that player actually dropped? You may've clicked **Join** at the same time someone else dropped. If you trade a ticket to the player at this point, that player can go off again to trade the same spot to a different player.

On the flip side, a player trading a tournament spot *is* getting something for nothing. Even if there was an entry fee, the player will get that back when he or she drops. If you trade something for that spot, the player is coming out ahead of you no matter what your deal was.

You may or may not care, as long as you got in. Waiting for the "trading" player to drop isn't a solution, either.

### You Aren't Guaranteed A Spot

A person giving up his or her spot in a tournament **cannot** guarantee who gets it. In a situation like Saturday, there were plenty of players hanging out in the tournament rooms clicking **Join** just in case someone did drop. If another person clicks before you do, you don't get in.

Tournament spots aren't digital objects--you can't "own" and "trade" them. If you get into a tournament, you're guaranteed that place when it starts. If you drop from a tournament before it starts, you get back any entry fee and that's that--you don't get to choose the person who gets in when you drop.

In the end, there's no way to enforce tournament spot trading, and you do so at your own risk. It's better to wait and try to get in when another player drops, or join a different tournament. While I'll grant you it was particularly difficult to get into some of the free tournaments this weekend, I hope this isn't a trend that continues.

Remember, you're not guaranteed anything by the player who trades for his or her spot. Most players with good intentions wouldn't be trading a spot, anyway, so it's more likely a scam waiting to happen. Don't take the bait.
