Wizards of the Coast welcomes Hall of Famer Raphaël Lévy as the featured player here at "Ask the Pro." A fixture on the Pro Tour for the last eight years, Raphaël is uniquely positioned to answer your questions about the life of a professional **Magic** player, give a historical perspective on the game and high-level tournament scene, share stories about travelling the world, and talk about the role **Magic** plays in his life.

Fifth on the lifetime Pro Points list, Raphaël began his Pro Tour career back at Pro Tour-Paris in 1997. He became a regular on the tour starting at the 1998 World Championships. Since then he hasn't missed a Pro Tour, an astounding streak cresting 50 consecutive events. He was recently honored as a member of the [**Magic** Pro Tour Hall of Fame](/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/tournaments-events-trading-card-game-magic-the-gathering-2)'s 2006 class and was inducted at the 2006 World Championships in Paris. He was recently named the 2007 [Road Warrior](/en/events/2007/mi07/raphael-levy-is-the-2007-road-warrior) by a vote of **Magicthegathering.com** readers, earning a spot at the [2007 Magic Invitational](/en/events/2007/mi07/2007-magic-invitational-voting) in October. 

Send your question, along with your name and location, via this email form. Answers will be posted every Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday. 

Note: This feature isn't intended for rules-related questions. For those questions, try our [Saturday School archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20070810075256/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=mtgcom/columnarchive&column=SaturdaySchool), the [Rules Q&A message board](https://web.archive.org/web/20070701000000/http://boards1.wizards.com/forumdisplay.php?f=154), or visit irc.efnet #mtgjudge.

<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;                 <b>July 28, 2007</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<p><b>Q</b>: Do you regularly find people freely offering you deck designs, or decklists, to use at the events you participate in? Even if you don't know the person? How do you typically handle people that want to offer their 'services' for your own personal benefit?<br> -Christopher <br></p>&#10;<p><b>A:</b> Hi Christopher,</p>&#10;<p>Actually, it's very rare that someone offers me a deck design or a decklist. I have had a few emails from <i>Ask the Pro</i> readers, with decklists in them, but I never got to credit them. Some have fine ideas but could not really work out in a Grand Prix or a Pro Tour. </p>&#10;<p>The situation you describe rarely happens for a reason. As my father used to say: &quot;Nothing is free,&quot; and people &quot;offering&quot; you services rarely do it for nothing. Credits aren't usually what they are looking for... </p>&#10;<p>... and the thing is, that it's totally normal and natural, that if the service they offer you would earn you something, it should earn them something too (by &quot;something,&quot; understand... well, money). It often cost them time or resources to end up with the information they are giving you and it's a way to make profit off something that would have ended up fruitless for them. </p>&#10;<p>So when you accept someone's help, you usually have to negotiate something. A share of your winnings is the most common deal. </p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;                 <b>July 26, 2007</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<p><b>Q</b>: I'm trying to build an efficient discard deck, mainly black-blue. I've tried Standard, Extended and even Legacy but finally I'm always stacked with the same situation: after a wonderful third or fourth turn, my opponent has no cards left and it seems the game is almost over. But then I continue drawing discard cards and my opponent plays every turn his top library card, with no option for me to get rid of them. I would like you to give me some tips or strategy to solve the problem...an example decklist will be helpful. Thanks.<br> -Aitor <br></p>&#10;<p><b>A:</b> Hello Aitor,</p>&#10;<p>The problem with fully oriented discard decks is that you have very few ways or no way to deal with what's coming from the top of your opponent's library. It may seem frustrating but it's just the way it works, you can't expect him to draw blanks every turn. </p>&#10;<p>Take the problem from another angle. What are your discard spells for? Discarding your opponent's hand doesn't actually do anything in terms of &quot;winning the game&quot;—understand by that, that you can have your opponent discard 50 cards, you haven't achieved any goal. Discard is a way to disrupt your opponent while building up your strategy, denying him possible resources from his hand.</p>&#10;<p>The best way to use discard deck is to put as much pressure on your opponent as possible while destroying his hand. Isn't killing him first the best way to deny him extra draws? The deck I played at Nationals uses this strategy (scroll down to the July 19 answer to see my deck): efficient, aggressive and hard-to-kill creatures, backed up with discard spells. I used a similar strategy at <a href="/en/articles/event-coverage/undated/raphael-levy-magic-pro-tour-hall-of-fame">PT LA '05 with Aggro Rock</a> (click on the decklists tab).</p>&#10;<p>You shouldn't use discard as the only strategy of your deck, but as a support to another, more aggressive strategy...may it be creatures or a combo you want to protect.</p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

  

<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;                 <b>July 24, 2007</b></td>&#10;<td> </td>&#10;<td><img alt="" src="https://i.ormos.uk/7d/ea/7deab367d2187ba6dc0e8758afb21d975578affe86883947e65fa45de83cb2e0.jpg"></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;<tr>&#10;<td colspan="3">&#10;<table>&#10;<tbody><tr>&#10;<td>&#10;<p><b>Q</b>: Recently it was announced that the Legacy format would be replacing Extended at Worlds. How do you feel about this change? Are you excited or do you feel that this is a bad change? Why? <br> - Eli Pace <br></p>&#10;<p><b>A:</b> Hello Eli,</p>&#10;<p>I have to admit, that I wasn't aware of that until I received your question. I immediately went to check in the Tournament Center and <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070810030031/http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=events/magic/worlds">there it was</a>: </p>&#10;<p><i>The 2007 World Championships is a four-day event featuring Standard, Lorwyn Booster Draft, <b>Legacy </b>and National Team Two-Headed Giant Lorwyn Booster Draft.</i></p>&#10;<p>This announcement did surprise me, a lot. I've written a couple of times what I thought about Legacy and the reason why it wasn't popular among pros. The first problem was the fact that no high-level tournaments were played in that format. With Legacy being played at Worlds, we will have to look at the format. </p>&#10;<p>The second issue, and the one that is worrying most of us, is the availability of the cards. When I tell you that finding cards is usually not a problem for PTs, it's because everyone has them. Every player comes with his load of Extended, Standard or Block cards along with him and can lend them around, or cards are to be sold at the shops (and you can still have some spare money to pay for your food). </p>&#10;<p>I can see us being in trouble in December. The schedule in October and November is going to be really tight. With Grand Prix in Standard and Extended, it will leave us very little time to playtest Legacy. What usually happens in cases like this is some of us decide on our decks at the last possible moment, when we have as much information as possible on the metagame (you can read more about choosing a Constructed deck in <a href="/en/articles/askthepro/2007/06-01-ask-the-pro-june">my June 12 answer</a>). Gathering cards at that point is not really an issue...except in Legacy; and I can't really imagine arriving in New York with four of each of the major cards in the format because I don't know what to play yet.</p>&#10;<p>I have nothing against the format itself. I haven't played it much, and maybe it's an interesting format. But without any help, players—specially the ones without connections—will have a hard time building a deck with actual cards. My favourite team format is what we all play outside the Pro Tour—three on three drafting. The way teammates cheer for each other is a lot more relevant in three on three. High-fiving your teammate in 2HG before a match is like wishing yourself good luck. Coaching in between games to help each other sideboarding would be half way between the full coaching and no coaching at all, and would make sense in terms of teamplay. Maybe there could be some kind of &quot;timeout&quot; during games. A player would be allowed to call a couple one-minute timeouts during a match, to ask for assistance to his teammates. Playing team <b>Magic</b> is like playing team tennis. If one messes up, then the team suffers. If one can't serve, the other one can't serve for him. And that's also part of the &quot;team spirit.&quot; You don't only play for yourself, but for your friends too. </p>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>&#10;</td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>
