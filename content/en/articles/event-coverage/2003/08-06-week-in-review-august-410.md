### The Return of Week in Review

Welcome back to Week in Review. As many of you remember, it was once a weekly column. Then I got sidetracked with opening my store, and many other non-**Magic**-related endeavors. Week in Review would still appear, but less frequently. Eventually, I just could not keep up with the amount of information I would have to process in order to produce a quality news article every week, and gave up on it. Jeff Cunningham picked up the flag and wrote several issues, but he too could not keep up with the demands of the column.

Well, I am going to take another stab at it. I have a little more free time now that my store is operating smoothly and I am once again playing more competitive **Magic**. Will I be able to do it weekly? I hope so, but only time will tell. 

### Eighth Edition Rollout Weekend

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f0/6b/f06bbbcb5989f9a6a41b413dd9943b9a8bdb2313634d48b72296db76edec2e35.gif)Dozens of stores around the world got to run their own small version of a Prerelease this weekend, as Wizards of the Coast unveiled the latest core set for **Magic**: *Eighth Edition*. The big news, of course, is the new card face. **Magic** players are reluctantly learning to accept the new card face. The biggest complaint I've heard so far is that the white and artifact cards look too similar. The new foil cards are far better accepted. The new design is truly awesome, and collectors are snatching up the new design.

Another cool innovation introduced with *Eighth Edition* is a set of oversized cards – one included with each display box. There is one card for every **Magic** expansion, celebrating the highlights of what has been reprinted in *Eighth Edition*. This is a new **Magic** collectible and once again, it provides collectors with extra incentive to pick up those *Eighth Edition* displays.

To the best of my knowledge, Wizards of the Coast has not yet posted the total numbers of players competing in the Rollout Weekend tournament. However, it is a safe bet that they've succeeded in creating the largest simultaneous card tournament event ever.

### Coming Up: World Championships

The worlds' best **Magic** players are in Berlin this week to compete in the World Championship – a five day long marathon tournament that challenges players to compete across four different formats.

The tournament will begin with a day of Standard. A special exception has been made for this tournament and *Eighth Edition* is legal instead of Seventh. This will give everyone a chance to preview what the new metagame is going to be like in September. On Day 2, we will play Rochester draft with *Onslaught*, *Legions*, and *Scourge*. Then the new Extended will be played at a high level for the first time since the last Masters Series tournament. *Scourge* and *Eighth Edition* have rotated in since then, and it remains to be seen just how they will affect the format. Mind's Desire is the most talked about card, but will it dominate the tournament or prove to be over-hyped? We will know on Friday. 

Finally, only those on the National Teams will compete in Team Rochester Draft, previewing the format of Pro Tour-Boston. Points accumulated on Saturday will be added to their individual performances over the three previous days of competition to determine the top seeded teams. Finally, the single elimination rounds for both single and team play. The new champions will be crowned on Sunday afternoon.

### Player of the Year Race – Endgame

While it is impossible to know who will take the crown, we have a lot more information as to what is going on in the Player of the Year and Rookie of the Year races.

To no one's surprise, Kai Budde is once again leading the pack at 73 points. He's got a significant lead on Mattias Jorstedt, who is in second place with 60 points. In turn, Mattias is also ahead of the pack – Dirk Baberowski, Justin Gary and Bob Maher are tied for third at 53 points with Osyp Lebedowicz just a single point behind them. Here is a list of players that technically have a shot at the title:

If Finkel wins the World Championships \*and\* Kai fails to place in Top 128, they will be tied at seventy five points. No one below ninth has a shot at the title at all. A chance to play at the Invitational however, is another story. In addition to the World Champion and Player of the Year, top point finishers from every continent will also get to attend. 

Assuming that Kai is able to defend the title, Jorstedt is in a solid lead for the European invitation spot. Dirk may be able to catch him, but it will be a tough job. The toughest competition is going to be among the three Americans. Justin Gary has an advantage in that he will be the only one among them earning points for the team portion of Worlds – which is likely going to be a good chunk of points. Masashi Ooiso is in a solid lead for the Asia-Pacific invitation, ahead of Ikeda by 11 points. South America has not put up as impressive a performance as last year so far, with Diego Ostrovich leading the pack at 25 points. He is followed by teammate Carlos Romao at 20 points – a significant but not insurmountable gap. 

Perhaps this year's Player of the Year race is not as lopsided as last year, but Rookie of the Year is almost a lock.

Masashi Ooiso is leading by a whopping 20 points. Short of making Top 2 at Worlds, Krempels – the ONLY competitor that is mathematically able to challenge him – cannot overtake the Japanese player. Ooiso's dominance may be good news for either Krempels or Ikeda – depending on how exactly tiebreakers are going to be done for the **Magi**c Invitational. ### Play of the Week

Mike “Chunks” Ricciardi was playing against Matt Boccio in the finals of Grand Prix-Atlanta trial at Kings Games in New York last weekend. Ricciardi played a fairly standard u-g build, while Boccio brought a monoblack zombie-cleric deck to the table.

Boccio was at seven life and had five swamps, two Withered Wretches and a Graveborn Muse in play. He was banking on casting a Corrupt next turn to turn the game around. Ricciardi had an Arrogant Wurm, Wild Mongrel, Basking Rootwalla, and several cards in hand. He attacked with all of his creatures and Boccio double blocked the Rootwalla with a pair of Wretches and blocked Mongrel with his Graveborn Muse. Arrogant Wurm was not blocked.

Most players would have jumped at the chance to remove most of the opponent's creatures – but Ricciardi immediately saw a better play. He chose not to pump either Rootwalla or Wild Mongrel. The green creatures died, but none of the three zombies left play. Arrogant Wurm dealt four damage to Boccio, who was forced to lose three life to his Graveborn Muse during the next upkeep and lose the game. Ricciardi went on to win the Trial and earn three byes in Atlanta.
