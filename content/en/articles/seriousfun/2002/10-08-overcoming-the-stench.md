Goblins are bad in multiplayer.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="Relentless Assault" src="https://i.ormos.uk/f6/ea/f6ea82cda4313b5e583693935d73dc2747321113d950b624aee4d7af00c01656.jpg"><br><i>Goblins are not at their best in free-for-alls.</i></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

If you look at the other colors' weenies in group play and see how they suffer or survive mass removal, you'll understand why I say this.

White first. Historically, white's creatures and decks have been built around Wrath of God, so this color is better suited than most for recovery. Rebels (and their champion Lin Sivvi, Defiant Hero) recruit their way out of a clean board; en-Kor creatures, such as Warrior en-Kor, deflect Earthquakes; and half the creatures come with protection from black and/or red. So white does okay, here.

Green's weenies -- typically Elves or token creatures, such as Squirrels or Saprolings -- go through mixed emotions when mass removal hits. On one hand, green's regeneration or pump usually isn't sufficient to protect an entire army of Ant tokens from a Mutilate or even a Pernicious Deed. (A new, efficient mass-regeneration spell for green might not hurt, eh?) On the other hand, green can probably rebuild its forces faster than any other color, thanks to Priest of Titania and her congregation. Green can also do a pretty good job of replenishing your hand through cantrip effects.

Blue also has good hand replenishment -- but the color rarely, if ever, plays weenies. The Wizards in the *Onslaught* block may change that over the coming year, but for now, Camarid, Kelp, and Starfish tokens won't cut it. (Wizards says it'll produce a token card -- for ***Magic** Online,* at least -- for every creature type ever named as a token, so I'm looking forward to the fruits of Spiny Starfish and Homarid Spawning Bed! If only the older card sets were programmed . . .) 

Finally, black has a lot of really good weenies that die in a good stiff wind and won't take you anywhere in multiplayer.

Which brings us back to red, and Goblins.

As the colors of "buy now, pay later," red and black don't make a strong multiplayer combination, especially if they take the beatdown route. There's simply not enough gas -- you spend all of your resources taking out a single player, and then find yourself with three creatures, a single card in hand, and four fairly alarmed (and well-armed) opponents still alive.

Goblins don't scream "late game." To illustrate, I searched a card database for any Goblin card (or card with "goblin" in the text) that also lets you draw a card. There's Goblin Artisans that flips coins whenever you play an artifact, or something like that. You may or may not end up drawing a card -- I can't remember, as I closed the computer window in disgust.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="Arms Dealer, Goblin Welder, Goblin Marshal" src="https://i.ormos.uk/16/81/16819d8b8fe49ad1387afb5f6a5143efbeac5ce072ded196554c4511cbf05085.jpg"><br><i>To handle many opponents, you need to choose &quot;utility&quot; Goblins--like <a>Arms Dealer</a>, <a>Goblin Welder</a>, and <a>Goblin Marshal</a>--instead of pure beatdown.</i></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

There are certainly multiplayer decks that can make use of certain Goblins -- Mogg Fanatic and Goblin Legionnaire are excellent early plays in group; Goblin Welder can play really subtle tricks; Squee, Goblin Nabob can keep your Masticore going forever; and so on -- but these aren't typically found in "pure Goblin" decks. Being a Goblin is a happy (or unfortunate) side effect of the card.

For those who insist on Goblin theme decks, there are some existing Goblin cards that automatically improve as a result of the sheer contribution of more Goblins in *Onslaught --* see Arms Dealer, Goblin Matron, Goblin Recruiter, and Goblin Lackey. If you have a deck that already uses these cards, you may be well on your way to improving it with Reckless One or Goblin Taskmaster by the time you read this. But then, you're pretty hardcore, and nothing I tell you is going to convince you that you're still better off playing, oh say, Dragons if you *must* play red.

For the skeptics among you who want to play Goblins but are afraid of running out of cards or Goblins, or both, I need to provide some firmer ground to stand on.

### FIVE TO STAY ALIVE

The following are suggestions for combinations capable of sustaining you through a group game -- or at least long enough to see more of your opponents out of the game. (Mono-red has other natural disadvantages that will work against you, so splash another color for greater success!) Each combination blends an *Onslaught* card with an old favorite. (So, yes, Kyren Negotiations and Goblin Offensive are good *together;* but that's not exactly fresh information.) 

**Goblin Bombardment/Goblin Pyromancer**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  

A +3/+0 boost to a large enough army of Goblins is enough to take out one opponent, maybe two. Goblin Bombardment (or similar damage for sacrifice, such as Last-Ditch Effort) gives you just enough gas to finish off the last fellow. Not exactly nine-opponent material, but it makes your Goblin deck group-ready on a very basic level.

**Goblin Offensive/Risky Move**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  

Not so risky after all. After all, who'll be playing with creatures that are worse than Goblins? The sheer number of cheap permanents you can create with the Offensive, and the chance to get a terrific creature with the Move, offsets some of the card disadvantage Goblin decks traditionally suffer.

**Brawl/Gratuitous Violence**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  

This combination technically doesn't use Goblins per se, but who are we kidding -- Goblins will be easiest to play in a mono-red deck. Like the Goblin Pyromancer combination above, this is about getting more punch for your swing. This will make for some crazy fights, but your team hits harder.

<table><tbody><tr><td><img alt="Skirk Fire Marshal" src="https://i.ormos.uk/60/85/6085027c51e56f699eacb52c7ee3dde674e1ee8aa4af74ac75ff5ea638f99987.jpg"><br>Onslaught<i>'s <a>Skirk Fire Marshal</a> is one Goblin that can bring the house down on the whole table.</i></td>&#10;</tr>&#10;</tbody></table>

**Goblin Marshal/Skirk Fire Marshal**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  

Yes, you will lose four of your Goblins (that's why the Goblin Marshal is kinda neat). And you'll deal damage to yourself, too. But it's a great way to threaten an eight-way tie, and with a Bottle Gnomes or two, you might even come out ahead of the game. Goblin Medics also goes in this deck.

**Mogg Infestation/Mana Echoes**  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  ![](http://www.wizards.com/sideboard/images/cardback.jpg)  

For my last suggestion, I wanted to hark back to a mass-removal card that actually helps Goblin decks in multiplayer -- Mogg Infestation. Whether you use it on yourself or another player's army (preferably one with large, flying, pro-red, untargetable creatures), you are giving yourself fuel for your next trick with Mana Echoes or Brightstone Ritual.

What's that next trick? It could be an X spell like Rolling Thunder, I suppose. Or maybe it's an Insurrection or other face-smasher. My advice is Dragon Roost. No matter how good Goblins get, Dragons will *always* rule red at the kitchen table.
