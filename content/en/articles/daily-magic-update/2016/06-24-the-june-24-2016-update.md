The Daily *Magic* Update is a roundup of everything *Magic* you should know on June 24, 2016. Today's Update is brought to you by BoREXIT—the Boros guild's decision to leave the Guildpact. Also, by terrible and overwrought political jokes.

# Today's Must

1.  [Choosing Your Mode](http://magic.tcgplayer.com/db/article.asp?ID=13352&writer=Craig+Wescoe&articledate=6-24-2016) | TCGPlayer | Craig Wescoe

Wescoe is all-in on this teaching thing, and he delivers another fine piece today that discusses the different "modes" of *Magic* and how you should play and act in each one. The right play in one scenario might not be the right play in another.

Except playing [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter). That's always the correct play.

# Deck of the Day

1.  [Vintage Unpowered Eldrazi](http://www.mtggoldfish.com/articles/vintage-101-eternally-extravagant)

I don't normally like to step on decks featured in articles that are also in the update, but this one just felt like it needed to be highlighted even more, because it's the Holy Grail of people who want to play Vintage but can't seem to find a [](https://twitter.com/mtgworth/status/743853827493814273)[Black Lotus](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Black+Lotus) just lying around—the truly unpowered, competitive Vintage deck.

There's one catch: You have to side with the Eldrazi.

Mark Hornung recently took second place at the Eternal Extravaganza ([as detailed on MTGGoldfish](http://www.mtggoldfish.com/articles/vintage-101-eternally-extravagant)) with a deck that played zero Moxen, blue cards, [Sol Ring](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sol+Ring), [Mana Crypt](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mana+Crypt)—nothing. Instead, the deck is all [Strip Mine](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Strip+Mine)/[Wasteland](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wasteland)s and multilands to help cast Eldrazi—[Ancient Tomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ancient+Tomb), [Eldrazi Temple](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eldrazi+Temple), and [Eye of Ugin](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Eye+of+Ugin).

So how does it get away without playing the artifact mana that defines the format? With [Null Rod](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Null+Rod), the ultimate equalizer (and also some of the best flavor text around). [Warping Wail](https://web.archive.org/web/20160624000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Warping+Wail) helps keep things under control, while the rest of the Eldrazi crew gets to smash hard.

It just goes to show how much Vintage has to offer if you look for it. And if you have reality-warping, otherworldly creatures on your side.
