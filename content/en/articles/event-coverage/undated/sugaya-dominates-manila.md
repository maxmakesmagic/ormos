## Grand Prix Manila 2008

![](https://i.ormos.uk/60/77/6077e37e6ccbb15e3520923bc6ca28b3bccb39eb8619080736321e95b3616210.jpg)  
 Again the Faeries came, but this time, they did not conquer. Sugaya Hironobu was one of two Kithkin players entering this star-studded top 8 amid a swarm of the Fae, and despite being severely outnumbered, he lined them up and knocked them down. And the crowds, oh the crowds. They were the most enthusiastic I think I’ve I have ever seen at a Magic event. From chanting the name of the semifinalist and local favorite, Luis Magisa, to the heartfelt cheers during the award ceremony, this was an event we’ll all remember for a long time to come.

And looking ahead to the new standard format once Shards of Alara hits the scene, it looks like Faeries will be leading the way, which will probably surprise nobody, but by the looks of things, the Kithkin may well give them a good run for their money. Then again, who knows what Shards will bring? Keep it locked here at Magicthegathering.com and I’m sure we’ll all know soon enough.

## Sunday, August 31: 9.10 a.m. – Round 9 Ken Lim vs Makihito Mihara

*by Noel Neo*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/b4/f2/b4f2cd15f3421a60e313e697d76314f99b6cfb209b05bed2b2b6d45cabde9907.jpg)

Going into day 2, only 3 players remain undefeated. They are the two players facing off here, alongside Ralf Santos.

Ken of Malaysia is clearly the underdog in this round, with Makihito being the 2006 World Champion, while he flew to Manila without any byes and only picked them up at the grinders on Friday.

**Game 1**

The game was a close damage race that hinged on a [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) tussle, but one in which Ken eventually edged away due to the card advantage he started the game with (*Makihito mulliganed down to 6 cards*), alongside some very powerful top decks.

The jostling for board supremacy started with Makihito’s [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona) meeting [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions), but Makihito was determined to set the pace of the game and drew first blood by attacking with [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault).

A [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) from Makihito revealed [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion), [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command), [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique), [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) and two lands, and he took the creature kill before proceeding to have his land beat at Ken.

However, Ken was quite the ‘miser’ and dropped [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) the very next turn, eliciting a wry smile from Makihito. Ken had now cast Makihito as the beatdown deck in this mirror match, posing the question if Makihito could deal him enough damage before the card advantage generated by [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) won the game.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/6a/c5/6ac5329f2dbc2b94b3fb358222a052db12afa39fcadf499d71839f8c020de656.jpg)

The [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) Ken played the following turn threatened to end the game for Makihito, but Makihito had an answer in [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions). Ken wasn’t too frustrated about the counter though, as the clash revealed a second [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) at the top of this deck. Amazingly, the subsequent turn repeated this sequence of events exactly. That’s three [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique)s in a row!

[Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) finally resolved, but Makihito was able to float mana into draw, turn [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) into a creature, and play a [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) of his own. But when Ken swung, Makihito blocked a token and played right into the [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) Ken had drawn.

Makihito still had outs however, and took [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) with [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation). This was where Ken’s [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) came in useful, as he returned the Clique to his hand and drew a card.

Makihito’s attempt to get back into the game with [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) failed as [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) came into play, but Ken’s own [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) was ticking down his life and he had to catch up by swinging with everything he had to tie the life totals at 5.

The replayed [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) met a mirror from Makihito, but that was sufficient for Ken to bring Makihito within range of lethal damage in two turns with [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) on the ready for any shenanigans his opponent could attempt.

**Game 2**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ab/a1/aba1762a4d96db50f014a7d0937717f13e24070000b7ff7bd481ec7d15a77628.jpg)

Makihito took the lead in the damage race again, by laying [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) on turn 1 and attacking on turn 2. However, he soon spluttered out as he stalled on lands and Ken played the game breaking [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom). Makihito lost even more ground as a [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) was allowed into play, only to be [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke)‘d at the end of turn.

Ken’s Vendillion Clique revealed [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation), [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke), [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize), and twin [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) and [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), from which he chose [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation). This showed that Makihito’s problem was not so much mana screw as colour screw, but the swamp he drew off Vendillion Clique came too late as his last hope was fizzled by [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite).

Ken wins 2-0 and remains undefeated.

In the post-mortem, Ken mentions that the only reason he came was to accompany a friend who had 3 byes. He attributes his performance to having four copies of [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke) mainboard, which he says is really good in this aggro format and at worst cycles itself. Well, perhaps that... but having his luck certainly helps too.

  

## Sunday, August 31: 10:13a.m. – Round 10: Terry Soh vs Ogie Jaro

*by Ray "blisterguy" Walkinshaw*

It gets to the point where, if you have a card with your face on it, you don’t need an introduction. So it is with Terry Soh, who has not only a Pro Player card, but a Magic card as well, Rakdos Augermage. Ogie Jaro is a successful Magic player in his own right too, having represented the Philippines at the 2006 World Champs in Paris.

When Soh’s first play was a [Vivid Creek](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vivid+Creek), my heart jumped for joy, he wasn’t playing Faeries! A common misconception is that to do well in this format, you need to be playing either Faeries, or Kithkin, as Jaro seemed to be, when he started the game with back to back to [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart)s. Soh’s five color control deck, usually described at Quick ‘n’ Toast, defended early with a [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) as Jaro poured more and more men onto the board. A [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) from Soh stopped Jaro’s attempt to [Unmake](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unmake) the Finks, and it looked like he was making very little headway on Soh’s position. A [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout) cleared the board, and a second [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) came into play.

Jaro wasn’t out yet though, as his land base now featured a pair of [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault)s, while a [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession) filled out the rest of his board. When he attacked again with his three flying spirits, a [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher) came down and threatened to wipe them out. With the ‘Thresher’s come into play ability on the stack, he [Mirrorweave](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirrorweave)d it, making his three attackers 7/7 monstrosities. Suddenly Soh had lost what had seemed to be a game he couldn’t lose.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/89/62/8962f5f422aa8b74deab8de80480f1e54ef6dfd049e6ab08e33ca625127ef19b.jpg)*Jaro attacks for 3... Soh taps out for the Cloudthresher... Mirrorweave baby, yeah!*

After a Mulligan in Game 2, Jaro then stumbled on his land drops and found his [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) eaten by a surprise [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil). Meanwhile, Soh was tearing ahead on the card count, evoking and then getting back a [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter) with a [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin), and keeping his life total up with a [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks). Jaro removed the [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil) with an [Unmake](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unmake), but Soh then just cleared the board with an [Austere Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Austere+Command), electing to destroy all small men and artifacts, so he would keep his [Mulldrifter](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mulldrifter). The [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) did what they do best of course, and persisted, bumping Soh life total up another notch.

## Saturday, August 30: 11.24 a.m. – Round 11 Shingou Kurihara vs Tsuyoshi Ikeda

*by Noel Neo*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/2f/f1/2ff1e13b41d017bc690f215834dbf2ab3a92bc74873b170baa16cc7e45348a58.jpg)*Tsuyoshi Ikeda*Tsuyoshi recently made the top 8 at Grand Prix Kobe with this very deck, while Shingou has been making a name for himself on the Asia ex Japan circuit, with his 1st runner up finishes at Grand Prix Singapore and Grand Prix Bangkok in 2007. Old friends, the two players chatted happily as they waited for the round to start.

Tsuyoshi won the die roll and had to mulligan his first hand. As he was shuffling up however, he heard a very welcome announcement, “players, please stop what you are doing and check the board for your re-pairings”.

Though a judge popped by to say that they were still paired against each other, it meant that they had to shuffle up again and draw back up to 7 cards. This was when the players burst out laughing. In between guffaws, Tsuyoshi explained that they had also met in a feature match at Grand Prix Kobe, where they were also re-paired against one another.

**Game 1**

The game started slowly, a bad sign for Tsuyoshi who is playing the aggressive Elemental Red deck against Shingou’s Faeries. The first play was [Smokebraider](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Smokebraider) on turn 4, after which [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) swung into Shingou.

Shingou quickly capitalised on his opponent being tapped out to play [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona) end of turn, followed by another on his turn, making all his faeries untargetable.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/11/c4/11c413d17c5bf6bc13fdcfeca9e130654f3522c8483d571476a2efe6ad0a861e.jpg)*Shingou Kurihara*Though Tsuyoshi had a strong turn in which he played [Vexing Shusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vexing+Shusher) and made [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) a creature before casting [Rage Forger](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rage+Forger) and swinging into Shingou, it wasn’t quite enough as Shingou’s [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation)s hit play and stole [Vexing Shusher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vexing+Shusher). This caused Tsuyoshi to think long and hard as [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation)s was a titanic 4/4, which none of his creatures could take down.

Tsuyoshi stumbled on his opening, only managing [Flamekin Harbinger](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Flamekin+Harbinger) before stalling on 1 land even after (*or perhaps because of*) mulliganing twice. ## Sunday, August 31: 12:26p.m. – Round 13: Deck Tech with Shuuhei Nakamura

*by Ray “blisterguy” Walkinshaw*

Four weeks ago at Grand Prix Kobe, we approached loveable rogue Shuuhei Nakamura for a quick word about his interesting Merfolk deck. Unlike the purely Blue/White Merfolk decks that had been rattling around Standard for the last few months, Nakamura and his chums had gone to town on his mana base, adding Black for [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) and [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke). When quizzed about this, Nakamura revealed that the deck had actually started out Black and White, and the Blue had been added later. The key interaction being around [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) and [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity). Sadly, he didn’t do as well as he had hoped, and finished a disappointing 56th.

Fast forward to the following week, and Kenny Castor finished fourth at Grand Prix Denver with a similar deck, this one instead running [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus) over [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), perhaps drawing inspiration from the Blue/Green Merfolk designs proffered by Paulo Vitor Damo de Rosa back when Eventide was released.

Apparently the Reejereys, [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing)s and [Crib Swap](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crib+Swap)s were very underwhelming for him, and he’s found that he sideboards in the Jaces, [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout)s and [Recumbent Bliss](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Recumbent+Bliss)es more often than not.

With two rounds to go, Nakamura is only two wins off adding another Top 8 to his name. Can he take the Merfolk all the way this time? Stay tuned and we shall see!

  

## Sunday, August 31: 1.32 p.m. – Round 13 Terry Soh vs Shuuhei Nakamura

*by Noel Neo*

The two veteran Pros wasted little time on niceties and briskly got into the thick of things.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/81/eb/81eb0a9a466044207cd415c1a804bbba024e9101f50067d340c0cfa8848e265f.jpg) Shuuhei hesitated before keeping his opening hand, the reason for which was soon revealed as he stalled on one land. Perhaps he felt he had a decent chance of drawing into a land as Terry had opted to play.

The game rapidly deteriorated for Shuuhei as a [Runed Halo](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Runed+Halo) (*naming [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus)*), then twin [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) hit play for Terry, as Shuuhei struggled to find his second land.

When Shuuhei finally found his land and played [Silvergill Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvergill+Adept), the merfolk merely fed [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw). The Adept’s companion was a little too late as Terry had long reached critical mass.

**Game 2**

This game revealed that the matchup is all about the card drawing. By virtue of mainboard alone, Shuuhei has the upperhand. Throw in the sideboard of [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) against Terry’s Oona’s Blessing, then you have all the makings of inevitability.

Nonetheless, the inevitability was long in the making as Terry’s turn 2 [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) took [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey) from amongst [Oona, Queen of the Fae](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oona%2C+Queen+of+the+Fae), [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher), [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing), [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey), [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) and land.

A quick scuffle for early damage ensued as Shuuhei negated the [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil) standing in the way of [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity) with [Recumbent Bliss](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Recumbent+Bliss). However, Terry’s [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command), which netted him a card, elicited a wince from Shuuhei.

Terry replayed [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil) into [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing) when Shuuhei next attacked, signalling the possibility of wanting to resolve a more important spell on his turn. But he merely passed and returned the defender with [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin) during combat. Shuuhei allowed combat to go against him, choosing to spend the turn playing [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret) and [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) instead.

Shuuhei defended his Planeswalker from the threatening [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) with [Crib Swap](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Crib+Swap), while a pair of his Bannerets met with [Firespout](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Firespout). However, with the obscene amount of cards he was drawing, Shuuhei was able to recover very quickly with a pair of [Silvergill Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvergill+Adept)s.

The game soon settled into a predictable pattern with Shuuhei drawing two cards then having both players draw a card, and repeating the whole sequence again. The monotony was broken with Shuuhei playing [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey), to which Terry responded with his own card drawing spell – [Oona’s Grace](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oona%E2%80%99s+Grace).

This was when the tempo begun to pick up with [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) for Terry and [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret) allowing Shuuhei to target [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil) and break the hold of [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin). Fortunately Terry had a second [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin), which allowed [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) and [Plumeveil](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Plumeveil) to take down the attacking Adepts, before Shuuhei used [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus) to break the hold of [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin) a second time.

Terry tried to stay in the game with [Hallowed Burial](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hallowed+Burial), but Shuuhei just dropped another [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus). Terry’s last play of the game was a [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize), just to check out Shuuhei’s hand, before he conceded.

**Game 3**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ec/81/ec81f4645363344636da49ef3a86156dbf2db5cfa554f5f8aaeaf0f39504efc1.jpg)

This game was relatively quick as Shuuhei had [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) in play by turn 3, with [Silvergill Adept](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silvergill+Adept) to defend the Planeswalker from [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks). More defenders in the form of [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret) and a second Adept followed, but Terry posed a different question in the form of [Archon of Justice](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Archon+of+Justice).

Shuuhei had the perfect answer for that too, with Crib Swipe and a very threatening [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus).

Terry’s [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) revealed a hand full of goodness: [Reflecting Pool](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Reflecting+Pool), [Oona, Queen of the Fae](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oona%2C+Queen+of+the+Fae), [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret), [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher), [Jace Beleren](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Jace+Beleren) and [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command), from which Terry chose to take the Command.

[Hallowed Burial](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hallowed+Burial) cleared the board, but Shuuhei continued to take huge chucks out of Terry’s life total with an end of turn [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher). Terry trend warily around [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing) by invoking [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw), and rightfully so, as his following [Kitchen Finks](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Kitchen+Finks) was countered by said spell.

In spite of having his attack blunted, Shuuhei continued to race against the clock with his Banneret and [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault).

Terry then cast a flurry of answers with [Makeshift Mannequin](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Makeshift+Mannequin) for [Shriekmaw](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Shriekmaw) taking out [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret), then [Hallowed Burial](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hallowed+Burial) removing [Oona, Queen of the Fae](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Oona%2C+Queen+of+the+Fae), and [Runed Halo](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Runed+Halo) guarding against [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault).

Shuuhei’s next threat was [Mirror Entity](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mirror+Entity), which he brought into play with [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing) countering [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command). However when he attacked and activated for the lethal 6 points, a second [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) bounced it back to his hand and Terry had [Hallowed Burial](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hallowed+Burial) when he replayed it.

The end game came when Shuuhei casted [Merrow Reejerey](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Merrow+Reejerey) and [Stonybrook Banneret](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stonybrook+Banneret), with [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) and [Sage’s Dousing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sage%E2%80%99s+Dousing) in support. Unable to stop the merfolk, Terry could only watch as a shot at the top 8 slipped away from him.

Shuuhei Nakamura wins 2-1.

  

## Sunday, August 31: 12:26p.m. – Round 14: Shingou Kurihara vs Chikara Nakajima

*by Ray “blisterguy” Walkinshaw*

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f1/f3/f1f330563378bb3201a265312bdb2be34577f48759dfdd0e10b4579bb3985a69.jpg)*Chikara Nakajima looking to the Chameleon Club for support*Nobody likes being in this position, friends needing to win out for the top 8 and being paired against each other. Both players are no stranger to the top 8 stage either, but to make matters worse, while Shingou Kurihara is in with a win, Chikara Nakajima could end up in ninth or tenth place if he wins. Nakajima gave some thought to conceding to his friend, but between them they decided it was worth playing out, as Nakajima’s chances weren’t bad enough to write them off outright.

They wished each other “good luck!” with a laugh before looking at their opening hands. Kurihara wasted no time in throwing his back for a better six, before leading with an [Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Island). Was he fishing for the turn two [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) draw? If he was, he didn’t hit it, only having a [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) on turn two. Nakajima went for a [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) off of a [Vivid Grove](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Vivid+Grove), but Kurihara had a [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite). His next turn, he had the [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) for Nakajima, who was stuck on two lands, choosing to take the [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) over two [Doran, the Siege Tower](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Doran%2C+the+Siege+Tower), two [Chameleon Colossus](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Chameleon+Colossus), a [Scarblade Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scarblade+Elite) and a [Profane Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Profane+Command). When Nakajima failed to find a third land before Kurihara started dropping back to back [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique)s, they were very quickly on to Game 2.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f8/8d/f88d741d6878c0d2e8a94f192af5d50220f6543f0ed8a35186208478e6feeb17.jpg)*Shingou Kurihara flies into yet another Grand Prix Top 8* This time Nakajima threw away his opening seven, and before looking at his next six, donned his peppermint green “Chameleon Club” shirt for good luck. He didn’t have a [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) in his first few turns, but Kurihara didn’t have a turn two [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) either, and could only end of turn a [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) when Nakajima played a third turn Doran. Things improved suddenly for Kurihara, who had found a [Sunken Ruins](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sunken+Ruins) to allow him to play a [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize), taking a [Cloudthresher](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudthresher) and leaving Nakajima with another three land, and then powering out the infamous [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom). Then came down a [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation), taking Nakajima’s topdecked [Scarblade Elite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scarblade+Elite). The Elite chump blocking the Doran, while the Faeries started gathering in the Red Zone.

Nakajima found and played a [Stillmoon Cavalier](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Stillmoon+Cavalier), and was pleasantly surprised when Kurihara allowed it to resolve, instead choosing to flash in a [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona) at the end of turn. Nakajima was facing a fair amount of damage in the air, especially when his Doran was powering down his Cavalier. During his next upkeep, a [Consign to Dream](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Consign+to+Dream) “time walked” Nakajima by throwing his Doran on top of his library. The Faeries swung in again, the Cavalier having to chump block the 3/3 Sower. Content to sit on his mana with a Faerie Token defending the base from Doran, Kurihara calmly passed the turn back. Nakajima drew his card, gave it some thought and steamrolled over that Faerie Token, before packing up his cards with a chuckle.

Shingou Kurihara defeats Chikara Nakajima 2-0

## Saturday, August 30: 3.45 p.m. – Quarterfinals: Shouta Yasooka vs Raphael Levy

*by Ray "blisterguy" Walkinshaw*

Both players wished each other “good luck!” with a hearty hand shake, none of this weak-wrist’d wet-fish hand shaking here, these guys are manly men. Neither Raphael Levy nor Shouta Yasooka are strangers to the top 8 stage of the Grand Prix circuit, or even the Pro Tour for that matter. Unfortunately for us, both Levy and Yasooka, and most of the players in the top 8 for that matter, are playing Faeries. This could get a little confusing to follow, so bare with me.

Levy won the roll and elected to play first. Neither player played a [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) on their second turn. It seemed that Levy had wanted to leave his mana open to threaten a [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions) instead, however Yasooka declined to take the bait, and passed the turn back. Safe from Yasooka’s Ambitions, Levy dropped his [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) into play on his third turn. With Levy’s shields now down, Yasooka naturally matched it with one of his own.

Both players then settled into a routine of “make a Faerie, play a land, go” for a few turns, before starting to trade [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke)s in an effort to gain tempo, and fighting over them with [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite)s. You’d think they’d had the exact same draw, if not for the fact that Levy had failed to play any land after his fourth, allowing Yasooka to win the [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke)-off with a [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions).

Faerie Tokens tentatively took to the Red Zone, with both players pondering the ramifications of taking any more damage than they already were from their [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom)s. Yasooka was the first to use unreasonable force, attempting a [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona) to effectively counter a [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion). Levy considered his play, before letting the Scion resolve. He untapped and played another [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion), this time on the Scion, who threatened to blow the game open if unanswered. Yasooka went for a [Broken Ambitions](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Broken+Ambitions), but was trumped by the same from Levy. Yasooka untapped, [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke)d Levy’s blocker, and player a backup Scion. Levy could only raise his eyebrows and pick up his cards for Game 2.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/f3/b8/f3b806297b7e79d196ef9ec8476a97d1759e3451d9b42070190b9b30a998fc2b.jpg)*Shouta Yasooka calmly riding his Mistbind Clique to victory*

Levy began Game 2 by swinging with a [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) as soon as he could. ## Saturday, August 30: 3.45 p.m. – Quarterfinals Ken Lim vs Luis Antonio Magisa

*by Noel Neo*

Ken and Luis are both underdogs in the top 8.

As highlighted in the swiss round feature matches, Ken came to Manila without a single bye, yet won the very first grinder he participated in and went on to finish day 1 undefeated.

Similarly, Luis came to this event without a significant top 8 under his belt, yet finished the swiss rounds at the top of chart. Magisa means ‘alone’ in Tagalog, an apt name for the sole Filipino in the top 8.

**Game 1**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/55/85/5585b0d08ff14342ba50b9e6f3be01c3fd20f02ffe54b75ba6a2b8621f3ab2be.jpg)

Ken led with [Ponder](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ponder) and [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), against Luis’s Figure of Destiny and [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn). Without an answer to a grown Figure of Destiny in hand, Ken blocked Figure of Destiny with a faerie token, then chose to Broken Ambition [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) for a single point, which Luis happily paid, before killing Figure of Destiny with [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke).

The game was decided when during Luis’s next attack, Ken played [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) and removed a token. Luis simply floated mana and when the faerie blocked, played Mirrorweave targeting [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn), saving both his creatures whilst simultaneously leaving Ken with none.

Ken was able to counter a subsequent Knight of Meadowgold, but one slipped through, and the band of Kithkin worked in hand with Ken’s own enchantment to whittle his life total. Though Ken was able to gain some card and tempo advantage by playing [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) in response to [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn), he was too far back in the damage race. Drawing nothing but lands for the remainder of the game did nothing to help his cause.

**Game 2**

![](https://i.ormos.uk/cd/4b/cd4b079af5c7677b838c261b4ca264e7716334d93137f5a0d7afcd5d696dcdf5.jpg)

Luis had another strong start, with [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) and [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn).

However, Ken had the early [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), was able to gain some early tempo advantage by countering the second [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) with [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) on turn 3, and started to edge away when he had [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) at Luis’s fourth upkeep.

Though Luis still swung in with everything, bringing Ken down to 7 life, Ken proceeded to steal [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn) with [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation)s and begin the beatdown.

Luis attempted to stall for time with back-to-back Spectral Processions, hoping that Ken would die to his own [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), but when a second [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation)s hit play, it was all over.

With [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) in play, Ken’s turn 1 [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) revealed [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart), Wilt-Leaf Liege, Unmake, [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn), Spectral Procession and no lands!

By the time Ken could play a vanilla [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite), he was facing a horde of spirits and two Kithkin. Though Ken struggled valiantly, ripping Unmake out of Luis’s hands with [Thoughtseize](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thoughtseize) and stalling for time with [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command), the outcome of the battle had long since been decided.

Luis wins 2-1 and progresses to the semi-finals.

  

## Semi-Finals: Shingou Kurihara vs Hironobu Sugaya

*by Ray "blisterguy" Walkinshaw*

While they began the top 8 out numbered by the Fae six to two, both Kithkin decks have advanced to the semi finals, evening the score to two on two. Championing the cause for the Small White Men on the table closest to the power outlet (hey, we gotta choose our matches somehow, right?) is Sugaya Hironobu, looking to knock down Grand Prix Top 8 veteran, Shingou Kurihara.

However, Hironobu lost the roll, and was then for forced to mulligan his first hand, losing his first play to a Broken Ambitions. His Spectral Procession resolved though, and he began the fight from twenty to zero by swinging in for 3. Kurihara flashed out a Spellstutter Sprite at the end of Hironobu’s turn, and took one of the Spirit Tokens with a Sower of Temptation. Hironobu exploded, dropping a Thistledown Liege at the end of Kurihara’s turn, before untapping and slamming down an Ajani Goldmane, powering up his rapidly growing army. Looking to stabilize quickly, Kurihara tapped out for Oona, Queen of the Fae. Hironobu dropped another Thistledown Liege, and wound Ajani down even further, forcing Kurihara to chump with his stolen Token and the Spellstutter Sprite.

Hironobu started moving his men about the board, trying to figure out how many blockers Kurihara could spin out with his Oona and what that might do to his army. He played a Wizened Cenn, which resolved. He animated his Mutavault and spread a third round of +1/+1 counters over his board, before Kurihara tapped them all down with a Cryptic Command. Kurihara attacked back with Oona, the Sower and the Mutavault, sending them all directly at Hironobu, and then threw down another Command to tap Hironobu’s attackers a second time. Hironobu used this window to Unmake Oona, and when Kurihara’s draw step didn’t deliver him any answers to Hironobu’s oversized attack force, he scooped up his cards.

Kurihara led Game 2 with a Thoughtseize, denying Hironobu a two-drop by taking a Knight of Meadowgrain. Another Knight came down, followed by a Moonglove Extract, which Hironobu no doubt sided in to deal with Kurihara’s Stillmoon Cavaliers. Meanwhile, Kurihara was swinging back with a Spellstutter Sprite, before tapping Hironobu down with a Mistbind Clique.

![](https://i.ormos.uk/c6/73/c673474e2a97d443d0715158557db6ecf677d7f1236157fe83ee2b8e04d047be.jpg)*Sugaya Hironobu gets his lands tapped by Mistbind Clique*

Kurihara then passed the turn back, and Hironobu merely played a Goldmeadow Stalwart, revealing a Thistledown Liege. Kurihara dropped a Scion of Oona at the end of his turn, untapped and played a Sower of Temptation. Suddenly, the board was looking very one sided in his favor. Hironobu played his Liege at the end of turn, untapped and played a Wizened Cenn, and the Liege and Stalwart swung into the Red Zone. Kurihara chumped the Stalwart with his newly acquired Knight of Meadowgrain, before swinging back with his team. The Moonglove Extract took down the Scion, taking some of the sting out of the attack, but dropping Hironobu quite low anyway. Kurihara then played another Sower, taking the Cenn, and played a Stillmoon Cavalier. When Hironobu tried to swing back to play an Unmake from under his Windbrisk Heights, Kurihara had the Spellstutter Sprite to stop it, taking the match to the third game.

While they sideboard, allow me to shift into present tense for a moment. We’re on the top floor of this mall, and over the last 10 minutes it’s become increasingly apparently that it’s raining outside. Raining *hard*. Just as Kurihara delivers that killing blow, thunder rumbles overhead and most of the lights in the room go out. Now if *that’s* not intimidating, I don’t know what is.

If Hironobu was intimidated, he sure didn’t show it. Other than missing his second turn drop, he came out the gates with a Spectral Procession, to which Kurihara had nothing. He then powered out a Thistledown Liege from under his Windbrisk Heights, to which Kurihara had nothing. He followed it up with a Stillmoon Cavalier, and again Kurihara had nothing. He could only shake his head and scoop up his cards with a smile.

Sugaya Hironobu defeats Shingou Kurihara 2-1

## Sunday, August 31: 6.15 p.m. – Finals Shouta Yasooka vs Sugaya Hironobu

![](https://i.ormos.uk/49/e6/49e63bdac31ec2324314b3d23ab1c5771e594c6befb7f76b6e7f4e814c9c1fec.jpg)*Shouta Yasooka*With more than half the top 8 being Japanese, it is not surprising that it would come to this – a pure Japanese finals between two of the most popular archetypes in the format. Regardless of the outcome, this day would go down in history as the first all Japanese Grand Prix Manila finals.

Despite Shouta being on the play, it was Hironobu who started strong with [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain), followed by [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn). However, his early advantage was eroded when an attempted [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) was countered by [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) and twin [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke)s destroyed [Wizened Cenn](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Wizened+Cenn).

Hironobu wasn’t too happy with the game progressing with [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain) being the sole attacker, but thought for a long while before playing [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger). Shouta allowed it, then [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke)‘d a token and played [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona), before another saw play on his turn.

With all faeries now untargetable, Hironobu attempted to turn the tide by attacking with all his creatures and surprising his opponent with [Thistledown Liege](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Thistledown+Liege). However, Shouta had [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) on the ready, and it was Hironobu who lost half his army in the failed gambit.

With the ball firmly in his court, Souta started to go for the kill. A [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) coupled with [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) on [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger) neutered any resistance and a second one sealed the game.

**Game 2**

The first skirmish of the game occurred with [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) blocking an early [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) with [Peppersmoke](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Peppersmoke) to shrink the Kithkin. Hironobu let the Stalwart go and continued to put the pressure on Shouta with [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession).

The balance swung back and forth as [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) countered [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger) and [Unmake](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unmake) retrieved a token converted by [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation)s. Nonetheless, Shouta was continuing to take damage from the Spectral tokens and sucked in an audibly loud breathe when he allowed [Ajani Goldmane](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Goldmane) to resolve.

Turning [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) into a creature, Hironobu had his Planeswalker give his creatures a boost. However, Shouta had [Mistbind Clique](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mistbind+Clique) to effectiveness shut Hironobu out of combat for the turn.

The respite was only momentary however, as Hironobu gave his creatures another boost and attacked the following turn. Hironobu reinforced his blocked token with [Rustic Clachan](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rustic+Clachan), but its effect was negated by [Scion of Oona](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Scion+of+Oona) and Shouta had [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) for [Unmake](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unmake).

With the board relatively more gummed up, a lone [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) tried to take down Ajani, a notion which was quickly quashed by a spirit token trading with the land. But Hironobu still had gas in his hand, gas in the form of [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) and [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) that quickly got pumped by Ajani.

Out of steam, Shouta could only watch as [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) grew again at the end of his turn and all of Hironobu’s creatures went into the red zone after Ajani sacrificed his last token to give them a leg up. At the end of combat, Shouta’s board was cleared of creatures while Hironobu only lost a land. Shouta didn’t need to wait for the next turn to know the game was over.

**Game 3**

Shouta got off to a bad start having to throw back his initial seven, but his second hand was much better, giving him a [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) with which to contend with [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart) and [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny).

Both players then started to wait for the other to make a mistake, a period which Hironobu utilised to grow [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) to a sizable 4/4. Finally, Shouta broke and traded [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) for [Goldmeadow Stalwart](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Goldmeadow+Stalwart), going down to three lands. When Hironobu proceeded to play [Ajani Goldmane](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Ajani+Goldmane), Shouta was forced to play [Spellstutter Sprite](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spellstutter+Sprite) at the end of turn to attrite to Planewalker’s loyalty tokens.

With a last token remaining on Ajani, Hironobu turned [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault) into a creature and expanded the last of the Planswalker. The stalemate of Shouta relying on Faerie tokens to chump block Hironobu’s attackers continued for a while, but Shouta eventually found [Nameless Inversion](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Nameless+Inversion) for [Mutavault](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Mutavault).

In the interim however, Shouta had also found two [Knight of Meadowgrain](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Knight+of+Meadowgrain)s and now played them. A fourth land for Shouta saw [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation)s stealing [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny), but Hironobu was quick to regain control of the game with [Unmake](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Unmake).

Now facing three creatures with just a token and a sprite, Shouta was in dire straits, a situation he tried to get out of with a second [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation). With the betraying [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) still tapped, Shouta’s two 1/1s had to chump block the attacking Knights. However, Shouta’s next play of Soulsnuffers was a very effective counter to the [Cloudgoat Ranger](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cloudgoat+Ranger) Hironobu had played.

With [Figure of Destiny](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Figure+of+Destiny) untapped, it seemed as though Shouta had the board gummed up and Hironobu had run out of steam. However Shouta had only 3 life left and [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom) was ticking down for him. In contrast, Hironobu was at a very healthy 27 life.

Shouta then started to use [Sower of Temptation](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Sower+of+Temptation) to chip away at Hironobu’s life total, but both players knew that Shouta would not have enough damage to win the game before he lost to [Bitterblossom](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Bitterblossom), and that the real question was whether he could find either Mindbind Clique or [Cryptic Command](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Cryptic+Command) in time.

Both players continued to go through the motions of the game, with Shouta especially intense on each of his two remaining draws. Finally, with just one life left, Shouta swung with everything and passed the turn.

A gleam of triumph appeared in Hironobu’s eyes, but he wasn’t sure... not yet. Hironobu just played [Spectral Procession](https://web.archive.org/web/20200818202116/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Spectral+Procession) and passed the turn back to Shouta... and the hall exploded into cheers as he ruefully conceded the game.

Hironobu is the Grand Prix Manila 2008 Champion!!
