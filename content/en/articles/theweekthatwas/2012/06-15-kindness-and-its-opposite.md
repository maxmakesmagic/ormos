What do you want to hear first: a story about human kindness and national pride or look at a decidedly unkind preview card that will be appearing in **Duels of the Planeswalkers** 2013 as well as ***Magic** 2013*?

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/twtw/twtw199_cradle.jpg)

[Gaea's Cradle](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Gaea%27s+Cradle) | Art by [Mark Zug](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Mark+Zug%22%5D)

Just as I thought... human kindness it is. Our story starts back before the 2011 **Magic** World Championships and—

—oh? The preview card you say? It is the very opposite of kindness. Don't say I didn't warn you.

I am writing the official preview for this card and I still can't believe it. I visit the Wizards offices a couple times a year to pore over old photos and videos and sometimes will get in an Ramp;D draft or two. Sometimes—and please don't tell anyone this because I would not like to jeopardize the small amount of access I have—I will overhear people talking about upcoming cards. Not often, mind you. They run a tight ship there; I am not even certain what floor they are on since I have to be blindfolded and spun around ten times before they will take me to the Pit.

I remember hearing someone talking about my preview card for this week—this was a couple years ago—in an FFL game going on in the background. I was floored. This was an aggro-defining card and a Limited powerhouse. It couldn't be coming back... could it? I waited and waited for the shoe to drop on the **Magic** community. I was certain if it was coming it would be in *New Phyrexia*, where it could wreak havoc with infect decks. But the block came and went without any sign of it.

When it wasn't there and not in any of the *Innistrad* block sets I let my guard down and figured that cooler heads had prevailed. Oh was I wrong...

![](https://i.ormos.uk/ca/a5/caa588c5277c5f4e09792d32056ae3dba28d2fe1704b744efc4155a6a134d0e2.jpg)  

This card is the actual beatstick talked about in hushed whispers at underground Pauper fight clubs. Let's take a look at one of the decks that used this card to great effect at the highest levels of competition.

My all-time favorite use for [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) was always in a combo Enchantress deck that included [Auratog](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Auratog) as a way to continually sacrifice and get back the [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) while drawing cards if you happened to have an Enchantress or similar effect in play. Every time you sacrifice the [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) to the [Auratog](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Auratog) it would get bigger by +2/+2, which meant you could give the 'tog super [Forest-](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Forest-)breathing for every green mana source you had in play. With the final green mana you would replay the [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) and give the 'tog an extra +2/+0 and trample.

A couple of quick things to note about [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) and its impending arrival in the current Standard format:

<table><tbody><tr><td><div><div><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Titanic+Growth"><img alt="" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Titanic+Growth"></a></div></div></td><td><div><div><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glistener+Elf"><img alt="" src="http://gatherer.wizards.com/Handlers/Image.ashx?type=card&amp;name=Glistener+Elf"></a></div></div></td></tr></tbody></table>

[Glistener Elf](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Glistener+Elf) is still out there and looking for work. This is exactly the card infect decks were looking for. I know I played a blue-green version of Infect last year that sported [Livewire Lash](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Livewire+Lash), but I would jettison that card and all its upside in a heartbeat to put four copies of [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) in that deck. Talk about inevitability; the trample component of [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) means all those clumsy [Titanic Growth](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Titanic+Growth)s can assuredly punch through just about any defense your opponent can muster. You can also accelerate your [Inkmoth Nexus](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Inkmoth+Nexus) clock considerably since it comes back to your hand every turn when it falls off the unanimated land. To paraphrase someone else talking about this card elsewhere—imagine if [Silver-Inlaid Dagger](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Silver-Inlaid+Dagger) had a cost to both cast and equip at a whopping total cost of ![](http://magic.wizards.com/sites/all/modules/custom/wiz_autocard/wiz_manacost/manasymbols/green.gif) AND gave trample as a bonus. For people looking to play more "fair" with their [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor), you should expect to see this card on a lot of unkind [Invisible Stalker](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Invisible+Stalker)s.

I will pass along to you a lesson I wish someone had passed along to me prior to the *Urza's Legacy* Prerelease. If you open a [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor) please take it—or at least pass it to me. The card has been moved from common to uncommon, so you can't play fast and loose and expect another one to come along. I have a hard time imagining that for the purposes of Limited this won't be the best card in that pack.

And now for the opposite of [Rancor](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Rancor)...

I have been talking about the impending World **Magic** Cup [over the past couple of columns](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://archive.wizards.com/Magic/Magazine/Archive.aspx?tag=The%20Week%20That%20Wasamp;description=The%20Week%20That%20Was) and reporting on some of the rosters as they have been filling out. Last week, I interviewed Lucas Siow as he joined Alexander Hayne and Jamie Blanchette to form 75% of the Canadian National team. What I did not fully realize was that along his way to the slot, Siow had upended last year's Canadian National Champion, Marc Anderson, in the Top 8 to get there.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/twtw/twtw199_three.jpg)

*Alexander Hayne, Lucas Siow, and Jamie Blanchette*

Anderson broke out in 2011 with his [win at Nationals](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/magazine/article.aspx?x=mtg/daily/eventcoverage/canat11/welcome) and just missed the [Top 8 of Worlds](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/magazine/article.aspx?x=mtg/daily/eventcoverage/worlds11/welcome) on mere tiebreakers. His next two events did not go quite as well, but heading into [Pro Tour *Avacyn Restored*](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://archive.wizards.com/magic/magazine/article.aspx?x=mtg/daily/eventcoverage/ptavr12/welcome) he was comfortably ahead of Alexander Hayne. Hayne needed a Top 25 finish to pass him and we all know how that turned out. Suddenly, Anderson found himself falling from potential National Champion to one of many faces in the crowd vying for a spot on the team. He admits to being a little dejected—he knew he could have played harder down the stretch of the 2011–12 season—at not playing in the first WMCQ. He snapped himself out of his funk with a little urging from his teammates and went to Toronto only to lose to Siow in the semifinals.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/twtw/twtw199_hornet.jpg)

[Hornet Sting](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?name=Hornet+Sting) | Art by [Matt Stewart](https://web.archive.org/web/20120615000000/http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Search/Default.aspx?output=spoileramp;method=visualamp;action=advancedamp;artist=+%5B%22Matt+Stewart%22%5D)

I have talked recently about the resurgent **Magic** community in Canada and the team captain Alexander Hayne knew there were some players he wanted to make the trip to Calgary for the final qualifier this past weekend.

![](http://media.wizards.com/images/magic/daily/twtw/twtw199_marc.jpg)

"The following week, completely out of nowhere, Hayne posted a (Facebook) status asking if anyone would make donations to send people to Alberta," explained Anderson of an ad hoc Kickstarter by the National Champion. "I was very touched to find out that $250, plus cards, were waiting for me if I decided to make the trip."

Anderson was able to find a flight within his budget thanks to the boost from those funds and was off to try one last time to get back onto the National team he thought he had locked up prior to Spain. One of the players who had contributed to the fund was Doug Potter—if you just listen for the loudest voice singing after Alexander Hayne won, that is Potter—and Anderson had to face him in the quarterfinals.

Anderson explained that he felt guilty beating someone who had contributed to him getting there but Potter would have none of it. The reason he had donated was because he felt the Canadian team would be stronger if Anderson was on it and let the better man win.
