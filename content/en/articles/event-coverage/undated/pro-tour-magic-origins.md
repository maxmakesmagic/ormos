## PRO TOUR MAGIC ORIGINS

SUNDAY COVERAGE

Being in the feature match area for the finals of a Pro Tour in Canada is not a first for Sweden's Joel Larsson, who found himself in a similar situation during Pro Tour *Gatecrash* in 2013. There, he ultimately succumbed, the trophy just out of reach.

Pro Tour *Magic Origins* is a different story for him. Coming into this tournament as a long-shot to obtain even Platinum status in the Pro Tour Players Club, Larsson managed to burn through opponents all weekend with his Red Aggro deck, showcasing the swift power of cheap red creatures including new powerhouse Abbot of Keral Keep. Winning his quarterfinals ensured Platinum status, but Larsson didn't stop there. He went on to dispatch Matthew Sperling in the semifinals, and then overcame 2014-15 Player of the Year Mike Sigrist in the fifth game of a best-of-five match to take home the title. Larsson's win ensured his seat in the 2015 World Championship.

Pro Tour *Magic Origins* determined many outcomes: the twenty-four competitors in the 2015 World Championship taking place later this month, the national champions and team captains for the World *Magic* Cup in December, and the Pro Tour Players Club levels for the 2015-16 Permier Play season.

But for now, it's all about Joel. The young Swede's *Magic* career has ignited in Vancouver.

The Pro Tour *Magic Origins* Top 8 has been set, featuring five players with prior Sunday stage experience including Kentaro Yamamoto, Patrick Cox, Matthew Sperling, Joel Larsson, and Mike Sigrist! Joining the veterans are Stephen Neal, Stephen Berrios, and Paul Jackson in their first big Sunday stage appearance. 

For Mike Sigrist in particular, tomorrow has the most on the line. Not only is a $40,000 check and a trophy up for grabs. A finals appearance will allow him to leap past Eric Froehlich to take Player of the Year and captaincy of the United States national team for the World *Magic* Cup.

FRIDAY COVERAGE

The dust has settled at the end of Day One here at Pro Tour Magic Origins in Vancouver. Only two players have emerged with undefeated records at the end of Friday. Canada's Rich Hoaen and Japan's Kentaro Yamamoto have showcased solid play in both Booster Draft and Standard Constructed to earn their 8-0 records.

However, the tournament isn't over yet, and plenty of solid players are not far behind. With World Championship seats, World Magic Cup captaincies, Pro Tour Players Club levels, and a Pro Tour trophy all on the line this weekend, you better believe that there will be plenty of exciting matches to follow all throughout Day Two.
